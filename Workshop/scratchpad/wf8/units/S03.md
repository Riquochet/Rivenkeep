## I.5 · *VOLLOL ET TRESKAN*, THE NAMING OF THE RIVENMEN

### Title

> ## *Vollol et Treskan*

**I.5 · The Naming of the Rivenmen**

*The naming of the Treskan.* (Lexicon O2777. *Treskan*, "the cleft-folk", is the canon's Rivenmen; a verbal noun heading a construct takes no article.)

### Seren's headnote

> Re hadh Kesterd Voss, Voss sa re vess, ul hedh et orrol dem vimm dem et cummath. Re vess o et pessyl ul et grem hosast et tynur, eth re dhovv o gor orl et gorrol dem o dhessyl.

*Laid by Corporal Voss, Voss who asked, on the eve of the going-down to the havens. He asked his question in the first winter of the siege, and waited the whole war to answer it.*

Word for word: *Laid Corporal Voss, Voss who asked, on the eve of the going-down to the havens. He asked the question in the first winter of the siege, and he waited every day of the war for his answering of it.*

- I.1's frame, *Re hadh [rank] [name], [epithet], ul …*, with *Kesterd*, the lexicon's "corporal" ('company-one'), as the pilot has *Crennel* for Kael's sergeant.
- *Voss sa re vess* is the canon epithet (§3.10). *re vess* "asked" and *vess* "night" are the canon's own homophones, as in I.1's headnote.
- *gor orl et gorrol*, "every day of the war", is Kael's *gor orl et hollarol sell* moved to Voss's war. *dem o dhessyl*, "for its answering": the object of a verbal noun is its possessor, as IV.4's *o wrodhol*.
- *ul et grem hosast*: the article keeps *ul* from bedding *grem* into *nrem*, an onset §2.5 does not allow; I.1's *ul et vess hosast* is the model.

### Voss's telling

Voss's voice is wry and plain: he plays on his words (*tessym tessyl*, "I owe an answer"; *hosen sy*, "like this"), and at the end he hands the hearth the weighing (*doss o nydhyl los*).

**¶1**

> Hebb et tolm lom. Tessym tessyl lo et odh sy hy fedh sell.

Give me the stone. I have owed this hearth an answer a long while.

Word for word: *Give the stone at me. I owe an answer at this hearth, from a long time.*

- *Hebb et tolm lom*: I.1's opening, exactly.
- ***Tessym tessyl***: canon *tess* is both "answer" and "stand surety for, owe", so Voss owes an answer with the verb of answering; the hearth's own answer in the Invocation (*es dess et odh*) is the same word (S03-14).
- "a long while" is *hy fedh sell*, "from a long time": the present with "from" for a debt still owing, where English says "have owed".

**¶2**

> Ul et grem hosast re vessym pessyl lo et vorr sy, eth re yalant merr hyos lo sy, eth re hunnant o. Re vessym: Hy vodh gorrar dem dolm eth crest? Re yalom brid, eth re lammom memm ul vimm stell um et lern rerddhalden, hy vodh nath re vellor paskard et trun; eth re haeverym o hosen pessyl duss. Re vysk tolmard lom: Nel et crest sa yorrom demo. Ew ull tessyl gell, veth ew o dhessyl. El sy en dessyl, eth re rytym o gor orl et gorrol.

In the first winter I asked a question at this fire, and some of you were here to hear it. I asked why we fight for ice and stone. I was cold, and I had buried a friend under a cairn on the east face because the ground would not take a spade, and I meant it as a hard question. A mason told me it was not the ice he was fighting for. That was a good answer, but it was his. This one is mine, and it has taken me the whole war to cut it.

Word for word: *In the first winter I asked a question at this fire, and some of you were here, and heard it. I asked: Why do we fight for stone and ice? I was cold, and I had buried a friend under a cairn on the eastern face, because a spade did not enter the ground; and I meant it as a hard question. A mason said to me: It is not the ice that I fight for. That was a good answer, but it was his answer. This is my answer, and I cut it every day of the war.*

- The question and the mason's answer are quoted, not reported: Orrowen has no "that" or "whether" before a clause (I.1's cries are the model).
- ***dem dolm eth crest***, "for stone and ice": *dem crest* would bed *crest* into *grest*, "harm", and the question would ask why we fight "for harm". The stone goes first, where the bedding is harmless (*tolm* > *dolm*). Voss says it the same way both times (S03-16).
- *nath re vellor paskard et trun*, "a spade did not enter the ground": *mellor* "come in, enter", I.1's verb for the column coming in.
- *sa yorrom demo*, "that I fight for": the conjugated preposition stays behind in the relative (pilot I.1 G5).
- *ew o dhessyl*, "it was his answer": the bite on *dhessyl* shows the possessive (§3.5), where a bare "his" could not.
- ***re rytym o***, "I cut it": *ryt* is "cut letters; swear". Voss's answer is cut as a vow is, and at the end of ¶5 it is one (*El ryt o*).

**¶3**

> Ew et dynd ul sestow Dhernil Yorndholm. Re heth Crenn Hovv Treskat et hulter hos orl, eth re hadh o o, hosen seskys. Ul et dynd ull re harma Halyna et Kethow tunn dem et parow, hesperdeth eth Stonwrytan eth lunt et trenn olen, eth re stonant ul vimm et covv treskat, eth re vysk hos, eth re dhunn ullen:

It was the week after the Rite of the Cornerstone. The Captain of the Torn Cloak had held the staff for a day and set it down, as you know. In that week Halyna called the whole Keep into the ward, soldiers and Stonewrights and the people of the column together, and they stood beneath the torn cloak, and one of them said, and the other finished:

Word for word: *It was the week after the Rite of the Cornerstone. The Captain of the Torn Cloak held the staff one day, and he laid it down, as you know. In that week Halyna called the whole Keep to the ward, soldiers and Stonewrights and the people of the column together, and they stood under the torn cloak, and one said, and the other finished:*

- *ul sestow Dhernil Yorndholm*: the Rite's name (O2500) softened again as the possessor of *ul sestow*, "after", as IV.4 softens *Gebb et Pard* to *Yebb et Pard*.
- ***Crenn Hovv Treskat***, "the Captain of the Torn Cloak": the construct, *Covv* softened as *Crenn* is to *Hrenn* (S03-01). He is never named.
- *re harma Halyna*: the dual, as the pair always takes (§3.9).
- ***re vysk hos, eth re dhunn ullen***, "one said, and the other finished": *tunn*, "finish, make whole", with the *hos … ullen* of IV.4's *hos eth ullen*. Added as *tunn ullen* (S03-03), for the foreword and V.1, which use the same English.

> Re yorr et Ketherd.
>
> Re yald et Stonwrytan.

"The Commander fought."

"The Stonewrights built."

- The canon's own two lines (`orrowen_v2` §11.11, "the Naming of the Rivenmen"), unchanged: *re* softens *gorr* and *gald* into *yorr* and *yald*. Voss tells in Seren's one ink; Halyna's two voices are the two lines.

**¶4**

> Ew ull et brodan tunn. Re yal loskat. Re vesker hos um ullen amm ull, hesperd eth tolmard, eth re vesker o: new pa lunt ol, sa re saede kaelir. Re hyler ul hos, hosen kyle pa dhrenn ul hos hald amm rhyn et lodh. Re yal lunt clennet ul et parow ull, hy et gorrol eth et galdol pana; eth nath re yal stonol lo saed hosel.

That was all the speech there was. It was enough. We looked at one another then, soldier and mason, and saw that we were not two peoples sharing a ruin. We had become one, as two courses become one wall when the mortar takes. A people was remade in that ward, out of the fighting and the building both, and neither half would have stood without the other.

Word for word: *That was the whole speech. It was enough. We looked one at the other then, soldier and mason, and we saw it: we were not two peoples who shared a ruin. We became one, as two courses become one wall when the mortar takes hold. A people was made new in that ward, out of the fighting and the building both; and standing was not at a lone half.*

- *pa lunt … sa re saede kaelir*: "two peoples" is the dual, and the relative agrees with it (*saede*, 3du): the two peoples are a pair, as the ward is about to seal them.
- ***amm rhyn et lodh***, "when the mortar takes": *rhyn*, "set fast, of mortar", is the root of *Rhyna*; a Rivenman hears her name in it.
- *Re yal lunt clennet*, "a people was made new": *clenn* "new; make new, mend" for "remade".
- ***nath re yal stonol lo saed hosel***, "standing was not at a lone half": the "at" idiom of ability (§3.7) in the past, for "neither half would have stood without the other".
- *hos um ullen*, "one upon the other", is the reciprocal "at one another" (S03-07).

**¶5**

> Hy ull re hebber voll et Kethow lo et gaed. Kethow Dhresk, re vysker: et gaed tunn, hy et sorth susk dem et tavow ommat deld sost. El Kethow Dhresk et cummath sa hethent et wemmeth myst. El Kethow Dhresk gor orrol ul so lodh, eth el cless et Kethow cless et wadh. Myskym o hos fedh hosen sy, eth es hyrrilys o gor fedh. Amm mysker Kethow Dhresk, kaeverer et kethow sy um et sorth, eth kaeverer ol naed tunn; eth nel ull pa voll wysket ul hos. El ryt o.

So we gave the name of the Keep to the land. Rivenkeep, we said: all of it, from the high ridge to the farthest fallen harbour. Every haven the grey sails hold is Rivenkeep. Every road between them is Rivenkeep, and the edge of the sea is the edge of the Keep. I say it once so, and you will understand it ever after. When we say Rivenkeep we mean this hold on its ridge, and we mean the whole of our land, and that is not two names tangled into one. It is a vow.

Word for word: *So we gave the name of the Keep to the land. Rivenkeep, we said: the whole land, from the high ridge to the fallen harbour farthest of all. The havens that the grey sails hold are Rivenkeep. Every road between them is Rivenkeep, and the edge of the sea is the edge of the Keep. I say it once, like this, and you will understand it for always. When we say Rivenkeep, we mean this keep on the ridge, and we mean our whole land; and that is not two names knotted into one. It is a vow.*

- **The copula puts Rivenkeep first.** The predicate comes before the subject (§3.4), so Voss's claims open on the name: *El Kethow Dhresk et cummath … El Kethow Dhresk gor orrol …*. The English puts it last; the Orrowen sets it at the head, twice running.
- *gor orrol ul so lodh*, "every road in their mortar": *ul lodh*, "between", is "in the mortar of"; the roads are the mortar between the havens (S03-24).
- *et gaed* is "the dry land", the land against the sea, so the edge of the one is the edge of the other. *ol naed tunn*, "our whole land", shows the bedding of *ol* (g > n).
- *pa voll wysket*: an adjective after a dual softens (§3.3), so *bysket* "knotted" is *wysket*.
- ***El ryt o***, "it is a vow": *ryt*, the word of Voss's cutting in ¶2.
- "Every haven" is the plural, "the havens": *gor cumm* would soften to *gor humm*, which is also "every husband" (lexicon §8.2).

**¶6**

> Eth re vollar ol sost Treskan, lunt et tolm treskat, pardath et kethow sa nath re omm. Amm brodhar um ol nedheth, vollar so: Orrowan, hy vodh ew Orrowan so; eth vollar ol sost: ol. El ull ternil et odh sy, eth amm sy seskys ul vodh re dhald o.

And we named ourselves the Rivenmen, the people of the riven stone, the keepers of the keep that would not fall. When we tell of our fathers we call them Shorelanders, for so they were, and we call ourselves we. That is the custom of this hearth, and now you know where it began.

Word for word: *And we named ourselves Treskan, the people of the riven stone, the wardens of the keep that did not fall. When we speak of our fathers, we name them Orrowan, for Orrowan were they; and we name ourselves: We. That is the rite of this hearth, and now you know where it rose.*

- *lunt et tolm treskat*: *treskat* is the Torn Cloak's word and the Keep's (*Covv Treskat*, *Kethow Dhresk*): riven stone, riven cloak, riven keep.
- *sa nath re omm*, "that did not fall": *nath omm* would bed the vowel into *momm*; the negative past (*nath re*, pilot I.1 G1) leaves the verb clear.
- ***vollar ol sost: Ol***, "we name ourselves: We": the pronoun said as a name.
- *ternil* is "a rite" and "a custom" at once: the custom of the hearth is a rite.
- "where it began" is *ul vodh re dhald o*, "where it rose": *mosk* "begin" is the Creed trap (lexicon §8.2), as I.1 found (*re dhald et carmol*).
- *ol nedheth*, "our fathers": *ol* beds *gedh* (g > n).

**¶7**

> Hy ull re hadh gor hos hyor garl um visk et hagess ul vodh dhenth et movenn, hos eth hos, lo dhrenn et orl ull dem vess; eth re ryter umo: Es gether pawast gor dholm. Nel hos tolm sa nath re yal lor. Veth gor hos sa re yal.

Then every one of us laid a hand on the foot of the pike where the banner flies, one after another, all that day until dark, and swore on it to take back every stone. Not one stone that was not ours. But every one that was.

Word for word: *Then each one of us laid a hand upon the foot of the pike where the banner flies, one by one, through that day until night; and we swore upon it: We will hold every stone again. It is not one stone that was not ours. But every one that was.*

- *um visk et hagess*: *um* softens *misk* "foot", the word I.1 uses for the foot of the wall (*lo visk*).
- ***Es gether pawast gor dholm***, "we will hold every stone again", for "take back every stone": *keth* "hold" is the Keep's own verb (*kethow*, the holding-place; *Ketherd*, the holder). *grik* "take" would bed to *nrik* after *es* (S03-15).
- The vow is quoted as sworn; its terms follow. *Nel hos tolm sa nath re yal lor* is the negative cleft, "it is not one stone that was not ours"; *lor*, "at us", is "ours" (§3.7).
- No *Ston.* closes it: Voss reports the vow of many and does not swear it here, and the English carries none (compare I.1's note 1).

**¶8**

> Nath re vallant et hemmeth et voll clenn, eth nath re vyskent o. Hunnos so tul lo et vorr ul medhyl: Orrow, amm kaeverent rern nahos. Re vessym essa hemm hos fedh hy vodh gald ey o; eth re vysk ey: El voll dem mymmyl o, nayess, amm es ston et hald dasken, eth es firr et Crenn Myst dasken cresket. Nath re hyrrilym ey amm ull. Kaeverym: hyrrilym amm sy.

The eldest did not like the new name, and did not say so. You still hear them murmur "the Shorelands" at the fire when they think no one is listening. I asked an old woman once why she did it, and she said it was a name to return to, perhaps, when the last wall stood and the last Mystarch lay broken. I did not understand her then. I think I do now.

Word for word: *The elders did not love the new name, and did not say it. You hear them still at the fire, murmuring: The Shore, when they think that no one listens. I asked an old woman once why she does it; and she said: It is a name for the homecoming, perhaps, when the last wall will stand, and the last Mystarch will lie broken. I did not understand her then. I think: I understand now.*

- *Back-translation fix.* ***et hemmeth***, "the elders": *hemm* "an elder" is slender, so its plural is *hemmeth*, as S01 and S10 say it. The first draft had *et hemmath*, which by the lexicon's own rules is the plural of *hemma* "Elderess" (an old *ā*-stem drops its *-a* and goes broad, as *essa* > *essath*); the analyzer passed it on that reading, and the blind reading gave "the Elderesses".
- ***Orrow***, "the Shore", is "the Shorelands", and the tongue is named for it (*Orrowen*, "of the Shore"): the elders murmur the word their speech is named from.
- ***voll dem mymmyl***, "a name for the homecoming" (S03-17): *mymmyl* is also *the Homecomings* of III.1.
- *amm kaeverent rern nahos*, "when they think no one listens": *nahos* as the subject, as in *Eth re dhess nahos*.
- ***Kaeverym: hyrrilym amm sy.*** "I think I do now" is the yes-rule (§3.4): Voss says the verb again.
- *gald* is "build; do, make": *hy vodh gald ey o*, "why she does it".

**¶9**

> Hy ull, doss en dessyl lo sy, eth doss o nydhyl los. Re vyskym galat hos fedh um et hald rerddhalden, sa vyskent uldath lom pawast tul: Nath norrar hy vodh el venn ol. Gorrar hy vodh doss ol devath ul sestow et haldath sy. El venn o, eth nel o tunn. Nath norrar dem dolm eth crest. Gorrar hy vodh el Kethow Dhresk gor dholm hyo, eth el varn Kethow Dhresk, eth re ryter um et movenn: Es lymmer o dem varn. Orra dem susk et taldow rerddhalden ul ryst baeg, eth veska lo dhrenn et orrow. Es veskys et virr sost hy ol gethowath, eth dhidh et fennet ul neld ull; eth ul neld et kethowath sy, ul vodh nath noss mellorol lo veskow, stonant hos eth hos lo dhrenn et noth cummow, eth gever lorr umo, gor hos. Re yaldar gor hos hyont ul et gorrol sy, tolm um dholm, eth re woskant et wemmeth myst gor hos hyont, eth ston gor hos tul. Veth nath dovvar ul gethow dem et dask. Orrar hyo. Amm sy resk Crenn Myst, hos hy reskowath strom et myst, ul et noth cumm, gor hos, eth tovv o.

Here, then, is my answer, and you may weigh it. I said a thing once on the east wall that men still say back to me: "We do not fight because we are right. We fight because our children are behind these walls." It is true, and it is not the whole of it. We do not fight for ice and stone. We fight because every stone of it is Rivenkeep, and Rivenkeep is home, and we swore on the banner to bring it home. Go up the east tower on a clear morning and look along the coast. You will see the nearest of our keeps, and the smoke of the next beyond it; and past those, farther than any eye can reach, they stand one after another across all ten haven-lands, with a crimson pennant on every one. We raised each of them in this war, stone upon stone, and the grey sails have come against each of them, and each still stands. But a keep is not a place to stay in forever. It is a place to go out from. In every one of the ten havens a Mystarch sits now, one of the great Thrones of the grey, and waits.

Word for word: *So, my answer is here, and its weighing is yours. I said a thing once upon the eastern wall, which men say back to me still: We do not fight because we are in the right. We fight because our children are behind these walls. It is true, and it is not whole. We do not fight for stone and ice. We fight because every stone of it is Rivenkeep, and Rivenkeep is home, and we swore upon the banner: We will bring it home. Go up the eastern tower on a clear morning, and look along the shore. You will see the nearest of our keeps, and the smoke of the next beyond that; and beyond these keeps, where reaching is not at any eye, they stand one by one along the ten haven-lands, and a crimson pennant upon each. We raised each one of them in this war, stone upon stone, and the grey sails attacked each one of them, and each one stands still. But we do not stay in a keep until the end. We go out of it. Now a Mystarch, one of the great thrones of the grey, sits in the ten havens, one apiece, and he waits.*

- ***doss o nydhyl los***, "its weighing is at you": *nydh* is "count, reckon, weigh", Kael the Counter's word; the hearth is asked to count it.
- The east-wall saying is Jack's line, kept whole: *Nath norrar hy vodh el venn ol. Gorrar hy vodh doss ol devath ul sestow et haldath sy.* (*nath* beds *gorrar* into *norrar*; *ol* beds *tevath* into *devath*.)
- ***El venn o, eth nel o tunn***, "it is true, and it is not whole": *tunn*, the word of Halyna's finishing in ¶3.
- *eth re ryter um et movenn: Es lymmer o dem varn*: the vow of ¶7 in its other words, as Voss gives it in English ("to take back every stone" there, "to bring it home" here).
- ***tolm um dholm***, "stone upon stone", is the Cry's own line (§11.2).
- *re woskant*, "attacked" (*bosk*, the Creed's word), for "have come against".
- ***Veth nath dovvar ul gethow dem et dask. Orrar hyo.*** "But we do not stay in a keep forever. We go out of it." Voss says it of *us* where the English says it of *a keep*: a copula line with two bare nouns would not show which is the keep and which the place. *orr hy*, "go out from", is also "leave" (O1875).
- *Back-translation fix.* ***resk Crenn Myst, hos hy reskowath strom et myst, …***: the apposition now stands right after *Crenn Myst*, so it says what the Mystarch *is*. The first draft set it after *gor hos*, where the blind reading took it for the seat he sits on ("on one of the great seats of the grey"); the Thrones are the grey's own rank (lexicon O2093, III.2 *Reskowath strom sa orrant um wadh*). The pun stays: the Mystarch *sits* (*resk*), and the Thrones are "great seats" (*reskow strom*, O2092).
- *ul neld et kethowath sy, ul vodh nath noss mellorol lo veskow*: "beyond these keeps, where no eye can reach" (the "cannot" idiom, §3.7). *gor hos*, "apiece" (O0763), keeps *gor* off a word after a trigger.

## II.1 · *KETHOW SA RE YAL NADHUMAT*, OF THE KEEP THAT WAS FORGOTTEN

### Title

> ## *Kethow sa re yal nadhumat*

**II.1 · Of the Keep That Was Forgotten**

*The Keep that was un-remembered.* (Lexicon O1118: *nadhumat*, "forgotten", is *na-* on the participle of *tum*, the Title's "remember".)

### Seren's headnote

> Re hadha Halyna ul morrol ryteth surr hemm et Stonwrytan, ul ba luth, hos eth ullen, ul et syst hosast et tynur. El flenn et tolm um et pa varn, eth doss et flenn ul lern lo et mesk.

*Laid by Halyna in the manner of the old annals of the guild, in two inks, by turns, in the first summer of the siege. It is the stone leaf of the two homes, and its facing leaf belongs to the wood.*

Word for word: *Laid Halyna in the manner of the old annals of the guild, in two inks, the one and the other, in the first summer of the siege. It is the leaf of the stone about the two homes, and the leaf in front is at the wood.*

- *Re hadha Halyna*: the headnote's frame, with the dual.
- *ul ba luth, hos eth ullen*: IV.4's two formula words for "in two inks, by turns", in the order of this headnote's English.
- *ul morrol*, "in the manner of": *orrol* "a going; a manner, a way", bedded after *ul* (I.1's *ul morrol*). *ryteth surr*, "the annals" (O2167), are "the cuttings of the years".
- *El flenn et tolm um et pa varn*, "It is the stone leaf of the two homes": the headnotes' one formula for "It is the stone leaf of X" (*El flenn et tolm um et X*, III.1, IV.1, V.5). *Pa Varn* is also the name of VI.1's song, *The Two Homes*, and the chime is right. *(Merge fix: the first draft had* El flenn tolmen et pa varn o*, "it is the stony leaf of the two homes"; the merge keeps one rendering of the recurring English, `wf8/merge_report.md`.)*
- *doss et flenn ul lern lo et mesk*, "the facing leaf is at the wood": the having idiom for "belongs to" (§3.7); *et flenn ul lern* is I.1's facing leaf.

### Halyna's telling

*Halyna tell it "in the manner of the old annals": they speak of themselves by name, in the dual, "from far off, as the annals speak of the dead" (I.4's headnote), and they say "we" (the plural) only of the whole people who forgot. The body paragraphs alternate inks, as every Halyna telling does.*

**¶1 · *ink I***

> Hunna ryt surr et Kethow, hosen re heth et Stonwrytan o, eth hosen nath re hether o.

Hear the annal of the Keep, as the guild kept it, and as we did not keep it.

Word for word: *Hear the annal of the Keep, as the guild kept it, and as we did not keep it.*

- *keth*, "hold, keep", is the root of *Kethow*, "the holding-place": the annal of the Keep is the annal of what was kept, and not.
- *Hunna*, the plural imperative, to the hearth. The "we" who did not keep it is the people (1pl *hether*), not the pair.

**¶2 · *ink II***

> Ul et begess ul lern et cummath, amm nath re yal hinnar tul um et orrowath rerddhalden, eth nath re yal orrol ul lodh et brunnath eth et wadh, re vellor et Stonwrytan hosast dem susk um et sorthath susk. Re weskent reskow ul vodh doss et sorth treskat ul ba, hosen amm re omm hesp strom umo; eth ul et tresk re yaldant kethow hy dholm terril et sorth sost. Ew ull Kethow Dhresk, galdat hosast et galdardath hosast. Vollant Tavow Hemm et cumm hemm sost, eth venn; veth nel et hemm sost o veth ul molt et cummath, hy vodh ew hemm et Kethow amm nath re yal tolm hosast Dhavow Hemm stannat tul.

In the age before the havens, when there was no city yet on the eastern coasts and no road between the mountains and the sea, the first Stonewrights came up onto the high ridges. They found a place where the ridge is riven in two, as though a great blade had come down on it, and in the cleft they raised a hold out of the ridge's own grey stone. This was Rivenkeep, the first work of the first builders. Eldhythe is called the Eldest Haven, and rightly; but it is the eldest only of the havens, for the Keep was old before the first stone of Eldhythe was cut.

Word for word: *In the age before the havens, when there was not yet a walled town upon the eastern coasts, and there was not a road between the mountains and the sea, the first guild came in, up onto the high ridges. They found a place where the ridge is riven in two, as when a great sword had fallen upon it; and in the cleft they raised a keep out of the grey stone of the ridge itself. That was Rivenkeep, the first work of the first builders. They call the Old Landing the eldest haven, and truly; but it is not the eldest, except among the havens, for the Keep was old when the first stone of the Old Landing was not yet quarried.*

- *amm nath re yal hinnar tul*: *nath … tul* is "not yet" (§3.8), Seren's own words; the gloss's hint *amm nath* "unless" does not apply where *tul* closes the clause.
- ***hy dholm terril et sorth sost***, "out of the ridge's own grey stone": *terril*, "grey as ash". *tolm myst* is the canon's Myststone (S03-18).
- *hosen amm*, "as when", is "as though" (O2863); *hesp strom*, "a great sword", for "a great blade".
- ***Vollant Tavow Hemm et cumm hemm sost***: Eldhythe's Orrowen name is *Tavow Hemm*, "the old landing", so "the Eldest Haven" is said of "the Old Landing" (S03-11). "The eldest only of the havens" is *nel … veth ul molt et cummath*, "not … but among the havens" (pilot I.1 G8).
- *stannat*, "quarried": *stann* is "quarry, cut stone from the bed", the quarry-wardens' verb in the next paragraph.

**¶3 · *ink I***

> Re stannant stannowvardath et sorth susk o dholm, eth re rytent so vollath um hosk et bystir rerdsennen, hos ul vimm ullen, tesk eth tesk, somm re yal et stannow ul stannol. Doss Halvard Tolmvard hy so dhrenn. Eth re hadha Aldwena, ganna lodhat hy et galdardath hosast, hosk et ganna ulvenn lo sona yemm garl sost; eth doss Rhyna Yanna Ulvenn hy sona dhrenn. Hy ull et ganna sa re velloros vess sy: re stannant o yedheth o, eth re hadhant ey vadullath o; eth ulo re hyle pa dhrenn ul hos, begess ul lern et orl amm re yala Halvard eth Rhyna cadhat ul hos tevow.

The quarry-wardens of the high ridge cut its stone, and their names are cut in the lintel of the western stair, one beneath another, for as many generations as the quarry was worked. Halvard Stone-Warden is of their line. And Aldwena, a bonded pair of the first builders, laid the capstone of the inner gate with their own four hands, and Rhyna of the Inner Gate is of their line. So the gate you came under tonight was cut by his fathers and laid by her forebears, and it was two lines made one long before Halvard and Rhyna were laid in their one cradle.

Word for word: *The quarry-wardens of the high ridge quarried its stone, and they cut their names upon the lintel of the western stair, one beneath another, generation and generation, while the quarry was in the working. Halvard Stone-Warden is of their line. And Aldwena, a bonded pair of the first builders, laid the capstone of the inner gate with their own four hands; and Rhyna of the Inner Gate is of their two's line. So, the gate that you came in by tonight: his fathers quarried it, and her forebears laid it; and in it two lines became one, an age before the day when Halvard and Rhyna were laid in one cradle.*

- *hosk et bystir rerdsennen*, "the lintel of the western stair": I.1's words (Kael swore on it).
- ***hy so dhrenn***, "of their line": a line of descent is *trenn*, a course, for the names cut one beneath another on the lintel *are* a course (S03-20, S03-21). So "two lines made one" is ***re hyle pa dhrenn ul hos***, "two courses became one", the words Voss uses in I.5 of soldier and mason (*hosen kyle pa dhrenn ul hos hald*). The two leaves stand side by side in the Book and say it the same way.
- *ganna lodhat*, "a bonded pair": a pair is a gate (S03-08). Aldwena take the dual (*re hadha*).
- *lo sona yemm garl sost*, "with their own four hands": *sona* "their two" softens *gemm* "four" (g > y).
- *o yedheth*, "his fathers" (*o* softens *gedh*), *ey vadullath*, "her forebears" (*ey* never mutates): the English's two words stay two (*gedh* / *vadull*).
- *cadhat ul hos tevow*, "laid in one cradle" (S03-09).

**¶4 · *ink II***

> Ul vimm et hosk ull, hosen ul vimm rivull yaldatath strom et Stonwrytan, re rytent et Stonwryt. El ryt o, hoskat ul hal, nethilet lo vyskorn eth prassoth, tragul et prass: Es ston et hald. Nath lenull uld o. Nath nethil hos o veth et garlath sa re hadhant et hald; eth doss o rytet ul vodh es vesk nahos pawast o, amm nath momm et hald.

Under that capstone, as under the foundation of every great work of the guild, they cut the Stonwryt. It is a vow sealed in living rock, earned by toil and skill: a master's promise that the wall will hold. It cannot be bought. It can only be earned, by the hands that laid the wall, and it is cut where no eye will see it again unless the wall falls.

Word for word: *Under that capstone, as under the foundation of the great works of the guild, they cut the Stonwryt. It is a vow, sealed in living rock, earned by toil and mastery, the promise of the master: The wall will stand. A man does not buy it. No one earns it but the hands that laid the wall; and it is cut where no one will see it again, unless the wall falls.*

- ***hoskat ul hal***, "sealed in living rock": *hal* is canon "bedrock, the living rock of the ridge", and *hosk* "seal" is "lay the lintel": the vow is lintelled in the rock.
- ***tragul et prass: Es ston et hald***: the master's promise is quoted, and its words are the Stonwryt's own first clause (§11.6, *Es ston et hald sy*) (S03-13).
- *Nath lenull uld o*, "a man does not buy it", for "it cannot be bought". *Nath nethil hos o veth et garlath …*, "no one earns it but the hands …": "only" of a clause is *nath … veth* (G8).
- *amm nath momm et hald*, "unless the wall falls": *amm nath* is "unless" (lexicon §5.2), and *nath* beds the vowel of *omm* into *momm* (§3.3: *m-* before a broad vowel).
- *es vesk nahos pawast o*: the object pronoun stands after *pawast*, so it cannot be read as "his second".
- "every great work" is "the great works": *gor* after a construct head would have to soften, which the grammar does not settle (pilot I.1 §3).

**¶5 · *ink I***

> Ul et orlath desir, amm re yal et cummath galdat, eth re hylent ul nyll, re voll et Orrowan et serull hyo. Re yal et serull Stonwryt saed styvil eth saed tolm domm, eth nath re runn o veth prassyannath lodhat et Stonwrytan, ul byskornowath sa re vellor besk nafedh. Hy ull re stonant gylornath et cummath eth lesteth Vardh um hos lodh gunn: osk, hoskat ul dolm.

In later days, when the havens were raised and grew rich, the Shorelanders named their coin for it. The Stonwryt coin was part gold and part black stone, and it was minted only by bonded master-pairs of the guild, in workshops no outsider had ever entered. So the markets of the havens stood on the same footing as their temples: trust, sealed in stone.

Word for word: *In the later days, when the havens were built and changed into rich, the Shorelanders named the coin from it. The Stonwryt coin was half gold and half black stone, and none struck it but the bonded master-pairs of the guild, in workshops that a stranger never entered. So the markets of the havens and the houses of God stood upon one footing: trust, sealed in stone.*

- *re hylent ul nyll*, "they became rich": *kyl ul*, "become" (O1129), and *ul* beds *dyll* into *nyll*.
- *serull Stonwryt*, "the Stonwryt coin" (S03-10); *re runn*, "struck": *drunn*, the mallet, also "mint, strike (a coin)", softened (*dr-* > *r-*, lexicon §4.3).
- ***um hos lodh gunn***, "upon one footing": *lodh gunn* is the canon's "footing, the deep mortar" (S03-19).
- *lesteth Vardh*, "the temples", takes no article: *Vardh* (God) is a definite possessor, and an article on *lesteth* would be the Last Carver's error (§11.9).
- *hoskat ul dolm*, "sealed in stone": *ul* beds *tolm*.

**¶6 · *ink II***

> Hy ull re orr et lunt dem vimm. Re yal et sorthath brid, eth et tavowath dhaedh, eth re vellor et serdul lo dhrenn et orrowath, eth nath um et brunnath. Re fenn et Stonwrytan et lunt, hy vodh re vaver et lunt lesteth Vardh eth haldath. Tesk eth tesk re yal et Kethow luskat, dem et fedh amm ew trenn mest hy dhumol o: et kethow sa re omm nafedh, voll ul et velleth, reskow sa re yal nafedh, hosen re raevarant tevath et cummath.

Then the folk went down. The ridges were cold and the harbours were warm, and trade came up the coasts and not over the mountains. The Stonewrights followed the folk, for the folk needed temples and walls. Generation by generation the Keep was left, until it was more myth than memory: the castle that had never fallen, a name in the songs, a place the children of the havens believed had never been.

Word for word: *Then the people went down. The ridges were cold, and the harbours warm, and trade came in along the coasts, and not over the mountains. The guild followed the people, for the people needed temples and walls. Generation and generation the Keep was let be, until the time when it was myth more than memory: the keep that never fell, a name in the songs, a place that never was, as the children of the havens believed.*

- ***Tesk eth tesk re yal et Kethow luskat***: "left" is *lusk*, "loose, let go, let be", the Captain's third call; the Keep was *loosed* (S03-04).
- *trenn mest hy dhumol*, "myth more than memory": *trenn* is "a course; a tale; a legend; a myth", so the Keep became a tale; *mest hy* is "more than" (S03-12), and *hy* softens *tumol*.
- *reskow sa re yal nafedh, hosen re raevarant tevath et cummath*, "a place that never was, as the children of the havens believed", for "a place the children … believed had never been".
- *re vaver*, "needed": *maver*, "want; need; require".

**¶7 · *ink I***

> Re heth nahos o. Re yyst nahos hy et Stonwrytan dem susk, dem glennol et brellirlodh, ell dem lunnol et sannornath. Re vellor et helvnynth ul et brellireth, eth re vellor et frenn ul sestow et helvnynth, eth grem eth grem re dhresk et crest galat sa re lusk et helvnynth. Re omm o nafedh. Nath re yal o veth luskat.

No one kept it. No Stonewright climbed up to point the mortar or to clear the drains. The rain came into the joints, and the frost came after the rain, and every winter the ice split what the rain had loosened. It had never fallen. It had only been left.

Word for word: *No one kept it. None of the guild climbed up, to mend the pointing or to open the drains. The rain came into the joints, and the frost came after the rain, and winter and winter the ice rived what the rain had loosed. It never fell. It was not but let be.*

- *Re heth nahos o*: "no one" is the subject, as in the lexicon's *Eth re dhess nahos*.
- *dem glennol et brellirlodh*, "to point the mortar": *brellirlodh* is "the mortar of a joint, pointing"; *clenn* "mend" beds after *dem* (the canon's *ul glennol*). *dem lunnol et sannornath*, "to open the drains".
- ***grem eth grem***, "winter and winter" (S03-05): *gor grem* would soften to *gor rem*.
- *re dhresk et crest*: the ice *rives*, the Keep's own word (*Kethow Dhresk*).
- ***Nath re yal o veth luskat.*** "It had only been left": *nath … veth* (G8), and *lusk* again.

**¶8 · *ink II***

> Amm re ommant et cummath hos eth hos, re hollar et lunt sa re yal hossen dem susk lo dhrenn orrolath et brunnath, dem et reskow hosel sa es geth, hosen re vyskent et velleth. Ew dhenn sa re weskent: gannath cresket, haldath cresket, hellur, eth o hrisur ul firryl ul et odh. Galat sa nath re yal o nayaldol lo uld, re wroc et begess o. Re yald et nadhumol galat sa nath re yal o yaldol lo wynd fedh.

When the havens fell one by one, the folk who lived fled up the mountain roads to the one place the songs said would hold. What they found was a shadow: broken gates, crumbled walls, a hall with its roof lying in its hearth. What man could not destroy, time had withered. Forgetting had done what no enemy ever could.

Word for word: *When the havens fell one by one, the people who were alive fled up along the roads of the mountains, to the only place that would hold, as the songs said. It was a shadow that they found: broken posts, broken walls, a hall, and its roof lying in the hearth. A thing whose destroying was not at man, the ages withered it. Forgetting did a thing whose doing was never at an enemy.*

- *dem et reskow hosel sa es geth*, "to the one place that would hold": *keth*, the Keep's verb, in the future (*es* beds it to *geth*); IV.4 uses *es* for "would".
- *gannath cresket*: *gannath* is both "posts" and the plural of *ganna* "gate" (an old dual that drops its *-a* before *-ath*): broken gate-posts either way.
- *o hrisur ul firryl ul et odh*, "its roof lying in the hearth": *o* softens *crisur* (c > h), so the possessive shows.
- *nayaldol*, "destroying" (canon *nayald*, "un-build"); *re wroc … o*, "withered it" (*broc*, "dry; wither"); *et begess*, "the age, the long time", for "time".
- ***Re yald et nadhumol …***: "forgetting" is *nadhumol*, "un-remembering": the Title's *tumol* turned by *na-*.

**¶9 · *ink I***

> Re vellora Halyna ul vimm et ganna ulvenn ul hedh, eth et trenn ul sona sestow, eth re weske et hosk ul firryl ul et ganna. Re omm o ul et surrath nadhumat, eth re hunn nahos o ul mommol. Ew tunn o. Re lumma Halyna, eth re hyle o olen, hy vodh el tolm o sa vaver pa; eth um et vimmlern re yal Stonwryt et galdardath hosast, gand hosen ul et orl amm re yal o rytet, eth lo sest dest wresketh et stannowvardath.

Halyna came under the inner gate at dusk, with the column at their backs, and found the capstone lying in the gateway. It had fallen in the forgotten years, and no one had heard it fall. It was whole. They knelt and turned it over together, for it is a stone that takes two, and on its underside was the Stonwryt of the first builders, as sharp as on the day it was cut, and beside it the bite of the quarry-wardens' chisels.

Word for word: *Halyna came in under the inner gate at dusk, and the column at their back, and they found the capstone lying in the gateway. It fell in the forgotten years, and no one heard it in falling. It was whole. Halyna knelt, and they turned it together, for it is a stone that needs two; and upon the underside was the Stonwryt of the first builders, sharp as on the day when it was cut, and beside it the bite of the chisels of the quarry-wardens.*

- Halyna by name and in the dual throughout (*re vellora*, *re weske*, *re lumma*, *re hyle*).
- *ul hedh*, "at dusk": I.1's words for the column's coming. (*lo hedh* would also read *lo* + S·*cedh*, "at who".)
- *ul mommol*, "in falling": *ul* beds *ommol*, as I.1's *ul morrol*.
- ***el tolm o sa vaver pa***, "it is a stone that needs two": *pa*, "two", standing alone, as Kael's *Delv*.
- *dest wresketh et stannowvardath*, "the bite of the quarry-wardens' chisels": *dest* is "a tooth; bite". *ganna* is "gate" and "gateway" in one word (*ul et ganna*).

**¶10 · *ink II***

> Re heth et tolm sost o dhragul. Ew ol sa nath re hether ol dragul.

The stone kept its vow. It was we who did not keep ours.

Word for word: *The stone itself kept its promise. It was we who did not keep our promise.*

- **"vow" is *tragul*, "promise", here**, the word ¶4 gave the Stonwryt (*tragul et prass*). With *ryt* the possessive could not show (*o ryt*, *ol ryt*: *r* takes no bite, §3.5). *tragul* shows both mutations, softened for the stone (*o dhragul*) and bedded for us (*ol dragul*): the two lines differ by one letter.
- *et tolm sost*, "the stone itself": without *sost* the analyzer reads *et tolm o dhragul* as a construct with an article on its head (the Carver's error, §11.9). *sost* parts them and gives the English's stress.

**¶11 · *ink I***

> Hy ull re stanna Halyna hosk clenn dem et ganna, eth amm re yal et Ternil tunnat, re yalda o, eth re hadha o ul o reskow, olen. Et tolm hemm, re lymme o ul et hellur sy, eth re hadha o ul baltur hald odh. El o et tolm sa hethys amm brodhos. Gor sa heth o, keth ryt et galdardath hosast, eth kaemul so dhevath.

Then they cut a new capstone for the gate, and when the Rite was done they raised it and set it in its place together. The old stone they carried into this hall and set in the niche of the hearth-wall. It is the stone you hold when you tell. Whoever holds it holds the vow of the first builders, and the shame of their children.

Word for word: *Then Halyna quarried a new capstone for the gate, and when the Rite was completed they raised it, and they laid it in its place, together. The old stone, they carried it into this hall, and they laid it in the niche of the hearth-wall. It is the stone that you hold when you tell. Whoever holds it holds the vow of the first builders, and the shame of their children.*

- *re yalda o … re hadha o … olen*: *gald* "raise a work" and *cadh* "lay", in the dual, and *olen*, "together".
- *Et tolm hemm, re lymme o …*: the fronted object, taken up by *o* (pilot I.1 G6).
- *ul baltur hald odh*: *paltur*, "a niche", bedded after *ul*; *hald odh*, "the hearth-wall" (O0854).
- *Gor sa heth o*, "whoever holds it": *gor* + the relative (lexicon §5.3).
- *kaemul so dhevath*, "the shame of their children": *so* softens *tevath*.

**The close** *(the close: no ink of its own; the Legends builder sets the laid formula apart, outside the alternation · merge)*

Word for word: *We two lay it as it was laid at us two.*

### 2. Collisions stepped around

- ***ul grem*** would give ***nrem***, and ***es grik*** would give ***nrik***: N·*gr-* has no repair rule (lexicon §8 item 3). Both avoided (*ul et grem*, *keth pawast*).
- ***gor cumm*** gives ***gor humm***, which is also "every husband"; ***gor grem*** gives ***gor rem***. Both avoided (a plural; *grem eth grem*).
- ***tolm myst*** is Myststone: the Keep's grey stone is *tolm terril*.
- ***lo hedh*** reads as *lo* + S·*cedh*: "at dusk" is *ul hedh*, as I.1.
- ***o hemm*** after the copula reads as S·*cemm*, "meet": *nel et hemm sost o*.
- ***hemmath*** is the plural of *hemma* "Elderess" (an old *ā*-stem goes broad), not of *hemm* "an elder": "the elders" is *hemmeth*, or the collective *hemman*. (Back-translation.)
- ***o + a word that cannot show a bite*** (§3.5): the possessor is made to show (*o dhessyl*, *o hrisur*, *o yedheth*, *ol naed*), said with the article, or recast.

### 3. What a blind reader may trip on (for the back-translation)

- *amm nath re yal … tul* (II.1 ¶2, twice) is "when … not yet"; the analyzer's phrase hint shows *amm nath* "unless", which is right only in ¶4 (*amm nath momm et hald*).
- *et hosk* (the capstone) triggers the phrase hint *et Hosk*, "the Title"; context settles it.
- *hos um ullen*: the analyzer glosses *ullen* after *um* as S·*hullen* "of rivers"; the phrase row S03-07 now shows the reciprocal.
- *dem o dhessyl* (I.5 headnote) is "for its answering", Voss answering his own question; ¶2's *ew o dhessyl* is "it was his answer", the mason's. The blind reading gave "he waited … for his answer" (scored close: ¶1 and ¶2 settle whose answer it is).
- *reskowath strom*, "great seats", is also the Thrones, the grey's rank (O2093): said beside *Crenn Myst* it names him.
- *hethent* and *heth* (S·*keth*) sit beside the reserve *heth* "spring" (lexicon §8.2); *runn* (S·*drunn*) glosses "mallet" first, *vaver* (S·*maver*) "wish" first, *nydhyl* "catalogue" first.
