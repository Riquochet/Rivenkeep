## 1 · SEREN'S FRAME, IN ORROWEN (THE LEAF-HAND, HER ONE INK)

### 1.1 The title

> **Redul ul vimm et Helv Bynt**

| Redul | ul | vimm | et | Helv | Bynt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| council | in(+N) | low | the | sky | narrow |

*The Council Under the Thin Sky.* This is the lexicon's title, O2048, used exactly. *ul vimm*, "in the low of", is the tongue's "under" (lexicon §5.1). *bynt* is "narrow; fine, thin".

### 1.2 The headnote

> **Hosen tum Mesk Myst: kethet hy volt vovennerd Hyll Lodhat, hy et meskdhrenn sa vollar Lennsast, sa re dhev hy stinyl hemman lenn so redulveskan; hy vodh ew meskan so redulhellur, eth re hunn et meskan et naelurol.**

as · remember · Mystwood: held (known) · from · S·heart · S·Standard-Bearer · Tide · Joined, from · the · grain · REL · name-1PL · Silverbark, REL · PST · S·grow · from · cutting · elders · silver · their · council-grove; from · what · was · grove · their · council-hall, and · PST · S·hear · the · grove · the · vote.

*As the Mystwood remembers: known from the heart of a Standard-Bearer of the Joined Tide, of the grain we call Silverbark, grown from a cutting of the silver elders of their council-grove; for their council-hall was a grove, and the grove heard the vote.*

- **The opening is the formula**, exactly as IV.4 has it: *Hosen tum Mesk Myst: kethet hy …*, "as the Mystwood remembers: held from …". *kethet* is the canon's verb for knowing wood.
- *volt vovennerd Hyll Lodhat*, "the heart of a Standard-Bearer of the Joined Tide":
  - *molt* "heart" is softened after *hy*;
  - *movennerd*, "banner-one", is softened as the possessor of a construct (§3.2);
  - *Hyll Lodhat* is the lexicon's name for the Joined Tide. Seren writes the wall's name, not the loan *Aelthae*.
- *hy et meskdhrenn sa vollar Lennsast*, "of the grain that we name Silverbark", in the one form the wood headnotes give "of the grain we call X" (IV.2's *hy et meskdhrenn sa vollar Dommwrint*):
  - *meskdhrenn*, "the wood's course", takes the article, the grain being a known one; *hy* is also "made of". *(Merge fix, `wf8/merge_report.md`: the first draft had* hy veskdhrenn*, without the article.)*
  - *Lennsast*, "silver-skin", is the wall's own name for the grain.
- *sa re dhev*, "that grew", carries "grown". It is *tev* softened after *re*: the canon's child-word, whose root *\*taw-* is "sprout, grow", used as a verb, as the lexicon allows.
- *stinyl hemman lenn so redulveskan*, "a cutting of the silver elders of their council-grove", is two constructs, one inside the other. *hemman* is "the elders" (the collective), and *redulveskan* is the lexicon's "council-grove".
- *ew meskan so redulhellur*: the copula sets its predicate first, "[it] was a grove, their council-hall", as *El tolm et hald* does (§3.4).
- *re hunn et meskan et naelurol*, "the grove heard the vote": *naelurol* is "a choosing; a vote". The facing leaf takes up the same word (§1.4).

> **Re hadh Rhyna garl umo lo et dhysal, eth re nahadh ey o pynt sost; eth re rarr Halvard dem susk hy et nelter, eth re yal lern thenth lo.**

PST · S·lay · Rhyna · hand · upon-it · at · the · shingle, and · PST · unlay · she · it · quick · very; and · PST · S·come · Halvard · to-the-height · from · the · vault, and · PST · S·be · face · wet · at-him.

*Rhyna laid a hand on it on the shingle and took it away almost at once, and Halvard came up from the vault with his face wet.*

Word for word: *Rhyna laid a hand upon it at the shingle, and she unlaid it very quickly; and Halvard came up from the vault, and a wet face was at him.*

- ***re nahadh ey o***, "she unlaid it". This is *na-* with *cadh* softened: the reversative, "take down, unlay" (lexicon). The hand she laid, she unlays.
- **"Almost at once" is *pynt sost***, "very quickly". The first draft said *virr pynt*, "nearly quick". After the pronoun *o*, though, the analyzer, and an ear, heard *virr* as *mirr* "weak" softened by a possessive, so the line said "its weakness". *pynt* cannot be the softened form of anything (a *p* softens to *v*), so *o pynt* is plainly "it, quickly".
- ***re yal lern thenth lo***, "a wet face was at him", is the "at" idiom for having (§3.7). *o lern* would not show the possessive, because *l* does not soften (§3.5, the I.1 back-translation). So Seren says it the tongue's way.
- *re hadh*, *re rarr* are *cadh* and *darr* ("come") softened after *re*, as in IV.4. *dhysal* is "shingle", and *nelter* is "a vault, an arched room below".

> **Re yal et kethyl daskat ul glemel.**

PST · S·be · the · holding (knowing) · ended · in · N·moment.

*The knowing was over in a moment.*

- *kethyl* is the canon's word, "a holding; (of the wood) a knowing". *daskat* is "ended".
- *ul glemel* is *clemel*, "a small hour, a moment", bedded after *ul*.

> **Re yal et brodhol um Halyna gor orl et hyll ull, hy vodh doss prenereth mest ul redul, eth re yal hos ganna wrodow lonta.**

PST · S·be · the · telling · upon · Halyna · every · day · the · tide · that, from · what · is · minds · many · in · council, and · PST · S·be · one · gate (pair) · S·mouth · at-3DU.

*The telling took them the rest of that Tide, for a council is many minds, and they had one pair of mouths to tell it.*

Word for word: *The telling was upon Halyna every day of that tide, for many minds are in a council, and one pair of mouths was at the two of them.*

- **"Took them" is the "upon" idiom.** The telling lay upon Halyna as a need or a grief is laid on a person (§3.7; *doss maver um Y*, "must", in IV.4).
  - *gor orl et hyll ull*, "every day of that tide", is I.1's *gor orl et hollarol sell*.
  - "The rest of" is left to the day-count, since the knowing had taken one day of it. This is a small loss, noted: the tongue's *dem et dask* ("until the end") cannot take a possessor without the Last Carver's error (§11.9), and *dem dask* beds into *nask*, "eat" (lexicon §8.2).
- **"A council is many minds" is *doss prenereth mest ul redul***, "many minds are in a council". It uses the state verb, where a thing is. The copula would have set *prenereth mest redul* side by side, which reads as a construct: "the many minds of a council".
- **"One pair of mouths" is *hos ganna wrodow***, "one gate of mouths". *ganna*, the old dual "the two posts", is the canon's word for a pair ("a pair is a gate"), and the Bonded are named as a gate is made (the pair-name, §3.10).
  - *lonta* is "at them two", the dual for Halyna (§3.9).
  - "To tell it" is left to the sentence's own *brodhol*, "the telling": an ear hears the mouths as the telling's.

> **Re wrodha Halyna o, hos eth ullen, ul ba luth; re hadh Seren o.**

PST · S·tell-3DU · Halyna · it, one · and · other, in · N·two · ink; PST · S·lay · Seren · it.

*Told by Halyna, by turns, in two inks; set down by Seren.*

- **The formula, exactly as IV.4 has it.** Halyna take the dual (*wrodha*). *Hos eth ullen*, "by turns", is O2858. *Ul ba luth* is Seren's epithet bedded after *ul*. *Halyna* carries its sealing lintel in the leaf-hand; *Seren*, "one name is a mourning", has none.

> **Amm re yal et mesk cresket, ell re omm et brodhol, doss o rellorat eth nahlennet.**

when · PST · S·be · the · wood · broken, or · PST · fail · the · telling, is · it · marked · and · unmended.

*Where the wood was broken, or the telling failed, it is marked, and not mended.*

- **The formula, exactly as IV.4 has it.** V.4 has no such gap; the promise stands on every wood leaf.

### 1.4 The facing leaf, in Seren's hand

> **Et flenn ul lern, sa re ryt Seren:**

the · leaf · in · face, REL · PST · write · Seren:

*The facing leaf, in Seren's hand:*

Word for word: *The leaf in front, which Seren wrote:*. *et flenn ul lern* is I.1's facing leaf, and *ryt* is "cut letters, write". In the Book this line is the leaf's own caption, set in Seren's ink with the rest.

> **Nath noss ol drenn lo sy.**

NEG · N·be · our · N·tale · at · this.

*Here there is no tale of ours.*

- *ol drenn* is "our tale": *trenn*, bedded after *ol*, is "a tale laid in one night", the Shoreland's word for a stone tale.
- *Nath noss … lo sy* is "is not … here". It can also be heard as the at-idiom, "this has no tale of ours", and both readings are true of the leaf.

> **Nath re yalar lo ull, eth nath re sesker galat um naelurol.**

NEG · PST · S·be-1PL · at · that, and · NEG · PST · know-1PL · thing · upon · deciding.

*We were not there, and we did not know that anyone was deciding anything.*

Word for word: *We were not there, and we did not know a thing about a deciding.*

- *nath re* is the negative past (lexicon O2861), as I.1 uses it (*nath re lusk*, *nath re hylym*).
- **"That anyone was deciding anything."** Orrowen has no "that" before a clause (I.1), so what is not known is a thing: *galat um naelurol*, "a thing about any choosing". *um* is "about, concerning (what a thing is told upon)".
  - *naelurol* is the headnote's word: the grove heard the vote, and the wall did not know there was one.
- *sesk* is "know a fact". It is not *keth*, because this is a fact unknown, not a wood unheld.
- *sesker*, not *seskar*: the analyzer caught the harmony slip in the draft.

> **Re luskom et flenn sy pondul, eth pessym gor sa heth et Flennath sy ul en sestow: Lusk o hosen sy.**

PST · leave-1SG · the · leaf · this · empty, and · ask-1SG · every · REL · S·keep · the · Book · this · in · my · back: leave · it · like · this.

*I have left this leaf empty, and I ask whoever keeps this Book after me to leave it so.*

Word for word: *I left this leaf empty, and I ask whoever keeps this Book after me: Leave it like this.*

- ***lusk*** is "leave, let be", used both times: what Seren left, the keeper is to leave.
- *gor sa heth*, "whoever keeps", is *gor* with the relative (lexicon §5.3).
  - *keth* "hold, keep" is the verb of the wood and of the Rite (*Ketherd*): whoever *holds* the Book.
  - *et Flennath* is the canon's "the Book".
- *ul en sestow*, "in my back": after me. It is *ul sestow*, "after, behind", with the possessive pronoun, which the analyzer reads unmutated after the bedding trigger, as IV.4's *ul o rask*. IV.4 flagged the point for Jack.
- **The request is quoted**, in the singular imperative, as I.1 gives the crowd's cries: the tongue has no "that" before a clause. *pessym*, not *pessom*: the analyzer caught the harmony slip in the draft.

## 3 · WHAT THE TRANSLATION DID, AND WHY

**The grain.**

1. **Every sentence is carved, once** (§10.11).
   - There is one mark to a content word, and a referent already cut is pointed at, never cut again.
   - **Files are the pronouns.** We are file 12, the council 9, the grove 8, the elders 7, the young 5 and the Guest 1. Nine runners end on a file referent (`@8`, `@9`, `@7`, `@1`, `@0`, `@2`).
2. **The heart stays plain** (§3, A9). V.4's opening movement is one ring, and it carries no fringe, pocket, braid, bind or break: "all" is `×3`, and "only" is `LONE+X`. The first pocket is in ring 4, and the break and the braid are in ring 12.
3. **Three movements, as the Book has them.**
   - The heart is the council under its grove.
   - The sapwood is the long argument: the thinning sky; the counsel of patience; the Guest's bones; the plea; the answer; the wood's judgement of itself; the vote.
   - The outer rings are the carving and what it did.
   - Intricacy grows outward, exactly as Jack's "anchor in the middle … details in the outer rings" asks.
4. **Every said thing is a said-pocket**:
   - the elders' counsel (ring 4);
   - the elders' plea (ring 7);
   - the young's answer (ring 8);
   - the two questions no one asked (ring 10).
   - Inside them, runners among the items give the doers (A2). No memory ray or file referent is used inside a pocket (A2, A14): "never" there is "not once" (`#1`).
5. **The carving is cited, not re-cut** (B11). *Grow until the shore is silent* is a cut-pocket holding `GROW½`, and the council's round is drawn beside ring 13.
6. **The break and the braid** stand where B12 put them, both in ring 12. The council's choosing, carried by the young's anger and grief braided `ab`, breaks the elders' plea for patience, cut again there.
7. **Echoes carried by one mark or one runner.** The grain can say "the same" without a word.
   - The elders' "send another" (`>GO{again}`, ring 7) comes back as "we did not send a second" (`!>GO{again}`, ring 9).
   - Their "when the iron stops" (`AXE+STOP`, ring 4) comes true in ring 14 with the same mark.
   - "The very wound" is two because-runners meeting on one WOUND (ring 4).
   - Four rays join the same war, the same elders, the same carving and the same grove across rings.
8. **Truth or nothing.**
   - "We could have learned more" is hollow (A6).
   - What the wood does not know it does not cut. "Was killed" names no hand: the killing is the shore's (file 0), and ring 10's said-pocket asks the question no one asked.

**Findings for the spec and the validator** (none blocks anything; they are also in `wf8/additions/W06_grain.md`).

- **§7.1's V.4 example should follow B12.** §7.1 still says the young's runner breaks the elders' plea. B12 moved the break inside ring 12, and there the runner that breaks is the council's choosing, carried by the young's two braided strands. The wording could say so.

- **The validator glosses MOUTH, THIN and CARVE first as verbs or "thin wood".** So `~MOUTH` (the jaw of ring 2) reads "seem to speak", and the sky's `THIN` reads "thin one (thin wood)". The reading is only a gloss (§13.5); a noun-first gloss for a mark standing alone would help a blind decoder.
- **§13.4 does not order rays among themselves.** This leaf writes them by file.
- **A16 names no feeling and no edge of a place as a belonging** (from the blind back-translation, `wf8/backtrans/W06.md`; findings 9 and 10 in `W06_grain.md`). A lone `ANGER` on the young's file, or an `EDGE` on the grove's, names a new referent by §11.2. The leaf now carves around both, with `ANGER+SAPLING×3` in ring 11 and `ray 8` in ring 14. Adding them to A16 is proposed for the spec's owner.
- **The validator's reading does not follow a ray when it names a file's referent.** It prints the ray ("what is cut there in ring 1 is the same one as in ring 14") but still labels ring 14's file 8 "edge" when the `# file` tags are taken away. A reader who follows §11.2 ("a ray says that marks in different rings are the same one") gets the grove.

**Orrowen.**

- **No new word was needed.** Every word of the frame is already in the lexicon, among them:
  - the reserve words *dhysal* shingle, *nelter* vault, *thenth* wet, *prener* mind, *mest* many, *pondul* empty and *naelur* choose;

  - the title, O2048.

- **"Took them" is the "upon" idiom** (*et brodhol um Halyna*). "A council is many minds" uses the state verb, which avoids a copula that would read as a construct.
- **Three traps were avoided, each the analyzer's or the spec's own**:
  - *virr* after the pronoun *o* (heard as *mirr*, "weak", softened);
  - *o lern* for "his face" (the possessive cannot show before *l*, §3.5);
  - *dem dask* for "until the end" (it beds into *nask*, "eat", lexicon §8.2).
- ***ul en sestow***, "after me", puts a possessive pronoun after a bedding trigger, unmutated. This follows the analyzer and IV.4's *ul o rask*, and IV.4 flagged the point for Jack.
- **The facing leaf uses *nath re*** (O2861), the negative past, as I.1 does.
