## 1 · VI.1 · THE KNOWING OF THE GREEN WOOD

### 1.1 The book heading and the title

> **Flennath et Mesk Lurrel**
>
> *Et Kylyl.*

**BOOK SIX · THE BOOK OF THE GREEN WOOD** · *The turn.*

- *Flennath et Mesk Lurrel* is the lexicon's (O0624). *Et Kylyl*, "the turning" (*kyl* + *-Ol*), is added as the subtitle (S10-03).

> **Kethyl et Mesk Lurrel**

**VI.1 · The Knowing of the Green Wood** (O1125: "the holding of the green wood"; to know the wood is to hold it).

### 1.2 Seren's headnote

> *Re hadha Halyna, hos eth ullen, ul ba luth, ul et vess sa re orr et sulter hyo, pynt ul sestow ommol et reskow strom nothast. Doss o rask ul garm et mesk, eth re hadhom et saed ull hosen re wrodha sona o.*

*Laid by Halyna, by turns, in two inks, on the night the boat went out, not long after the tenth Throne fell. It ends in the wood's voice, and I have set that part down as they told it.*

Word for word: *Halyna laid [it], the one and the other, in two inks, on the night that the boat went out, soon after the falling of the tenth great seat. Its end is in the voice of the wood, and I laid that part as they two told it.*

- **"Laid by Halyna" is active**, as in I.1 (*Re hadh Crennel Kael*) and IV.4 (*Re wrodha Halyna o*): *re hadha*, 3du. *hos eth ullen* (O2858) and *ul ba luth* are IV.4's.
- **"Throne"** is *reskow strom*, the word of III.2's title (*Reskowath strom sa orrant um wadh*). "Not long after" is *pynt ul sestow*, "soon after".
- ***re wrodha sona o***: a broad verb's 3du in *-a* sounds as its imperative plural (§3.4), and "a dual verb always has a subject", so Seren gives it one, *sona*, "they two".

### 1.3 Halyna's telling

Word for word: *We two hold the stone together; and this night we two hold it long, until our-two speaking.*

Word for word: *{X} was the tenth Mystarch that fell. What stayed of it lay where it came to rest, as the other nine had lain: its grain black, and riven open. - **The copula puts its predicate first** (§3.4): *Ew X et Crenn Myst nothast sa re omm*, "X was the tenth Mystarch that fell".
- **The Captain's word is quoted**, not reported (Orrowen has no "that" before a clause: I.1), and in the plural, the respect due a Bonded pair (§3.9 item 4). "Know its heart" is *keth*, the wood's verb.
- **"its name" is "the name that is in it"**, *et voll sa ross ulo*: after an imperative, *o voll* would read "it, name" (*v* does not soften, G9). ***sa ross***: the relative softens the state verb, *d > r*, as *na-* does in the lexicon's *naross*.
- *domm o veskdhrenn*, "its grain black": *o* softens *meskdhrenn*, so the possessive shows.

- **"aware" is *sesk***, knowing a fact, in the progressive (*ul seskyl*): the sliver is aware; it does not *keth*, which is the Bonded's knowing of wood.
- **The two progressives** (*re yal o ul devyl … hosen doss gendel ul devyl*) lay the sliver and the seedling side by side. The thought is quoted (*Nath re haever hos: doss galat hossen tul …*), because there is no "that".

- **"wood that knows the sea knows the way home" is a proverb**, and Orrowen proverbs are verbless (§11.7): *mesk sa heth et wadh, mesk sa heth et orrol dem varn*. With a verb it read "spring" for *heth* (the lexicon §8.2 trap).
- **"caulked" is *lodh***, "mortar the seams": the fisher-folk lay a boat as masons lay a wall, and Halyna *key* it (*roskur*, "key a joint, lock fast"). "so that no sea could work it loose" is the "at" idiom in the past, "loosing a joint was not at the sea".
- **"sound" is *tunn***, whole: the word of *Ryt Dhunnoth*, the Covenant of Completion.

*ink b*

> Re hadha Halyna et saedel lurrel lo lern et sulter, um lodull feth. Lo sest et saedel re hadha sona Stonwryt, saed styvil eth saed tolm domm: osk hoskat ul dolm; et hebbet peness sost sa re yal lo Orrow fedh, hosen re yal mesk domm et Arra et peness sost lont.

They laid the green sliver in its bow on a bed of moss. Beside it they set a Stonwryt coin, part gold and part black stone, trust sealed in stone: the holiest gift the Shorelands ever had, as the dark wood of the Guest had been the holiest of theirs.

Word for word: *Halyna laid the green sliver at the bow of the boat, upon a bed of moss. At the shoulder of the sliver they two laid a Stonwryt, half gold and half black stone: trust lintelled in stone; the holiest gift that the Shore ever had, as the black wood of the Guest was the holiest at them.*

- ***Stonwryt*** is the coin itself (O2393: "the Stonwryt coin; trust"), and "trust sealed in stone" is its gloss said back: *osk hoskat ul dolm*, trust with its lintel laid, in stone.
- **"the bow" is *lern***, the face of a hull; *lo lern* ("at the face") keeps it apart from the preposition *ul lern*, "before".

*ink a*

> Hy ull re lumma Halyna olen um dhysal ul lern et sulter. El lusul hosast dhernil et Arra ull: et hebbet eth et lummol; eth et hos lusul sa ross o yaldol lo hos lunt hosel. Doss maver et lunt ullen um et ullen.

Then they knelt down together on the shingle before it. That is the first step of the Guest's rite, the gift and the kneeling, and the only step one people can take alone. The rest needs the other.

Word for word: *Then Halyna knelt together upon the shingle before the boat. That is the first step of the rite of the Guest: the gift and the kneeling; and the one step whose making is at one people alone. The need of the other people is upon the rest.*

- "can take" is the "at" idiom (§3.7), with the verbal noun's object as its possessor (*o yaldol*, visible). "The rest needs the other" is the "upon" idiom, *doss maver … um* (O0488).

*ink b*

> Re hasta sona o, eth re lymm et hyll o dem neld. Amm re yal o ul neld, re hyl o, ul lern et helvhoss eth ul lern et kess, hosen re hyl et Aelvaren begess, Lymmerd et Lodh, eth re orr o dem neld ul myst; eth fedh sy nath re lymm o ledal, veth hebbet.

They pushed it off, and the tide took it out. When it was well out it turned, into the wind and against the current, as the Aelvaren, the Bearer of the Binding, turned long ago, and went away into the grey; and this time it carried a gift and not a grave.

Word for word: *They two pushed it, and the tide carried it away. When it was far out, it turned, against the wind and against the current, as the Aelvaren turned an age ago, the Bearer of the Bond, and it went away into the grey; and this time it did not carry a grave, but a gift.*

- **"long ago" is the past with a count of time**, *re hyl … begess* (lexicon §5.3, "ago"). "the Bearer of the Binding" is Seren's shore-gloss of the name, *Lymmerd et Lodh* (S10-11).
- **"a gift and not a grave" is *nath … veth***: "it did not carry a grave, but a gift". *Amm re yal o deld* was tried and read "its far" (*o* before *d* asks for the bite); *ul neld* is "out beyond".

*ink a*

> Re yalant et higileth hemm ul ol molt um dhysal, eth re nendent o ul morrol. Nath ludalant galat um et wadh tunn hosen sulter myst sa orr hyo ul lern et helvhoss; eth nath re hylent dem neld.

The old ship-masters among us stood on the shingle and watched it go. A grey boat going out against the wind is the thing they fear most on all the water, and they did not look away.

Word for word: *The old ship-masters in our midst were on the shingle, and they watched it in going. They fear no thing on the whole sea as a grey boat that goes out against the wind; and they did not turn away.*

- **"the thing they fear most" is "they fear nothing … as"**, the counter's way of saying *most*. *sulter myst* is also the name of the harbour rhyme, *The Grey Boat* (O2424, IV.5): the ship-masters' fear has a song.

### 1.5 Seren's frame (`HALYNA END`)

> Re yal et odh velv sell, eth re vorr et odhvorr dem vimm, eth nath re hadh hos mesk umo. Hy ull re vysk tev o, virr ul lern, hos hy dhevath sugornan, hy et kethow rerddhalden, velv: *Tumar.* Eth hy ull re vysk gor uld o.

The hearth was silent a long while, and the fire burned down, and no one put wood on it. Then a child near the front, one of the fisher-children from the east keep, said it, not loudly: "We remember." And then everyone said it.

Word for word: *The hearth was quiet long, and the hearth-fire burned down, and no one laid wood upon it. Then a child said it, near in front, one of the fisher-children, from the east keep, quietly: We remember. And then everyone said it.*

*Then, for the first time, the hearth sang in the wood's way. The Rivenmen sing it still, and call it* The Two Homes:

- *Pa Varn* is the lexicon's (O1896): "two homes", the dual.
- **"in the wood's way" is *hosen et mesk***, "like the wood". The first version, *ul morrol et mesk* ("in the manner of the wood", *orrol* "a going; a manner"), was read "as the wood went" in the blind back-translation (`wf8/backtrans/S10.md`): *ul* with a verbal noun is the progressive (§3.4), this leaf's own *re nendent o ul morrol* is "watched it going", and the telling is laid on the night the boat went out, so nothing ruled that reading out.

### 1.6 *Pa Varn*, The Two Homes

> *Re yal ol varn dannat hy hal;*
> *re nadhumar o, eth re wesker o, eth re hether o ul lorr.*
> *(line 3: the wood's, cut on the chip below)*
> *Keth tolm et ryt, eth keth meskdhrenn et vennoth.*
> *Tumar ol varn. Tumant so varn eth.*

> *Our home was hewn from the mountain's bone;*
> *we forgot it, and found it, and held it in blood.*
> *Their home was breathed from the hearts of the trees, and the axes came, and the breath grew thin, and they have not found it, and they cannot hold it.*
> *Stone keeps the vow, and the grain keeps the truth.*
> *We remember our home. They remember theirs too.*

Word for word:

> *Our home was hewn from the bedrock;*
> *we forgot it, and we found it, and we held it in blood.*
> *…*
> *Stone holds the vow, and grain holds the truth.*
> *We remember our home. They remember home too.*

- **"the mountain's bone" is *hal***, the bedrock, "the living rock of the ridge", and the first half of Halvard's name. Seren's English gives the image; the song says the stone.
- **The last line is the hearth's yes, sung**: *Tumar ol varn*, and the other side answering in the same verb, *Tumant so varn eth*. It is the sliver's sentence in Orrowen, *eth* ("too") at the end, where the wood folds its "too" in (§5.1).
- **The form.** First-syllable stress. Lines 4 and 5 close on the same consonants, *vennoth · eth*, the consonant rhyme of I.1's verse; the English pairs its lines by their first sounds (*bone · blood*, *truth · too*). Line 2 keeps the English's three verbs and its *and … and*. **[Jack]** if the song should rhyme by vowels.
- *Keth tolm et ryt*: the Stonwryt holds the vow, and the grain the truth. Both halves use *keth*, the verb that is "hold", "keep" and "know the wood".

**Line 3, the wood's: the chip.** A sung line has no tree of its own, so it is cut on a chip of end-grain at text height, as the NAELEAR chip is (mystaeri_spec §5.2), `wood: plain`. Its root is what the line is of, HOME.

### 2.1 The heading and the bound-facing note

> **Trenn Rask · bysket ul lern et trenn hosast**

**EPILOGUE · BOUND FACING THE FIRST TALE**

> *Ul flennath doss et flenn sy bysket ul lern et trenn hosast,* Covv Treskat. *El et flenn hosast sa vesk veskerd o, eth et flenn dasken sa re yal loskat.*

*In the Book this leaf is bound facing I.1,* The Torn Cloak. *It is the first leaf a reader sees, and the last to be filled.*

Word for word: *In the Book this leaf is bound facing the first tale, The Torn Cloak. It is the first leaf that a reader sees, and the last leaf that was filled.*

- *Trenn Rask* is the lexicon's (O2580, "the tale of the end"); the heading's second half is added as a phrase (S10-04). "I.1" is "the first tale", and *Covv Treskat* stands beside it in apposition, unmutated.

### 2.2 The title and the headnote

> **Tolm hy et Myst**

**The Stone out of the Grey** (O2554).

> *Re ryt Seren Pa Luth o ul et surrath sa re fennent, um et flenn sa ross ul lern dhrenn hosast et flennath sy. Re yal et flenn pondul dem et orl sy, veth meskdhrenn umo eth hos trenn:* Nawrodhat. Nath noss tul kethyl et gunn sy lo Halyna. *Ul et dask, nath re losk hos kethyl Halyna o.*

*Written by Seren Two-Inks in the years after, on the leaf that faces the first tale of this Book. It stood empty until now, with the grain drawn on it and one line:* Untold. Halyna cannot yet hold this deep. *In the end no knowing of Halyna's filled it.*

Word for word: *Seren Two-Inks wrote it in the years that followed, upon the leaf that is facing the first tale of this Book. The leaf was empty until this day, but for the grain upon it and one line: Untold. Halyna cannot yet hold this deep. In the end, not one knowing of Halyna's filled it.*

- **Seren's line is canon** (§11.4), written as it stands on the facing leaf, lintel on *Halyna*.
- "until now" is *dem et orl sy*, "until this day": *dem amm* would bed into *dem mamm*, the reserve "lose" (I.1 §3). "with the grain … and one line" is *veth*, "but for": empty, but for the grain and one line. *Re yal o pondul* read "its empty" (*o* before *p*), so the leaf is named.

### 2.3 Seren's telling (one ink)

> Rytym sy hosel, eth rytym venn; hy vodh nath noss hos amm sy sa heth et mesk eth sa wrodh o lom.

I write this alone, and I write it plain, for there is no one now to know the wood for me.

Word for word: *I write this alone, and I write plain; for there is not one now who holds the wood, and who tells it to me.*

- ***rytym*** is the regular 1sg of *ryt*; the canon's *Rytom* belongs to the oath (one of the canon's three harmony slips, lexicon §7.2). Seren writes; she does not swear. *hosel* after the verb reads "alone", which here is meant. *rytym o venn* read "its breast" (S·*menn*), so "plain" stands alone after the verb, as an adverb.

> Dossa Halyna sennet. Re senne ul hos vess, ul et grem sa re fenn ommol et reskow strom nothast, hosen senn et Lodhan: Rhyna hosast, ul naedh, eth Halvard somm re vorr et greller tul, hy vodh re sesk o. Firre ul vimm hosk clenn et ganna ulvenn, ul hos ledal, lo hos tolm, eth hos voll umo.

Halyna are gone. They went in one night, the winter after the tenth Throne fell, as the Bonded go: Rhyna first, in her sleep, and Halvard before the lamp was out, for he knew. They lie under the new capstone of the inner gate, in one grave, with one stone, and one name on it.

Word for word: *Halyna are gone out. They two went out in one night, in the winter that followed the falling of the tenth great seat, as the Bonded go out: Rhyna first, in sleep, and Halvard while the lamp still burned, for he knew. They two lie under the new capstone of the inner gate, in one grave, with one stone, and one name upon it.*

- **"gone" is *senn***, "go out, as a fire in peace": the tongue's word for a death that is not a killing, and the Bonded's death is the gentlest in the Book (II.3). The lamp is the other fire in the sentence: Halvard went "while the lamp still burned".
- **"he knew"** is *sesk*, a fact, not *keth*. *hosk* is capstone and lintel at once: the one name lies under the lintel of the gate, as a pair-name is a lintel over two posts (§3.10).
- "under the new capstone of the inner gate" is *ul vimm hosk clenn et ganna ulvenn*: the Book's recurring phrase in the one form the merge keeps (I.4, II.1 and the foreword say *hosk … et ganna ulvenn*); with the article on the possessor the adjective may stand after the head, and the analyzer reads the chain clean. *(Merge fix, `wf8/merge_report.md`: the first draft had* ul vimm et hosk clenn sa ross um yanna ulvenn*, "the new capstone that stands upon the inner gate".)*

> Nath re orrom lonta. El kaeveret hossol nalodhat ull, re wrodha sona lom hos fedh, eth nath re raevarom sona dem et ryst fennet. El ryterd hosast et Stonwrytan sa hoss et brodhardath en, eth hy ull ser et flennath.

I did not go with them. That is what an unbonded life is for, they told me once, and I did not believe them until the morning after. I am the first scribe of the guild to outlive the tellers, and so the Book goes on.

Word for word: *I did not go with them two. That is the meaning of an unbonded life (what it is meant for), they two told me once, and I did not believe them until the next morning. I am the first scribe of the guild who outlives the tellers, and so the Book goes on.*

- **"what an unbonded life is for" is *kaeveret***, "what is meant; a meaning" (O1097: *kaever* 'think; mean, intend', O1095, + *-At*): the same word as the Carver's stone "that would carry a meaning", *sa es lymm kaeveret*. The first version, *El dask hossol nalodhat ull*, kept the echo of *dask*, "the end" and "a purpose" (O0365), but the blind back-translation (`wf8/backtrans/S10.md`) read it "That is the end of an unbonded life": in a construct with a life, *dask* is its end, and the purpose was lost. *hoss* is "breathe; live; outlive".
- ***ser*** "go on, carry forward" is the word the hearth hears in *Seren* (a folk etymology, §5.8): the Book goes on in her name. *El … en*, the pronoun last, is I.1's *El nydherd yalatath en*.

> Ul heth re vellor sulter lyss myst lo hyll ul vimm et kethow, eth re dhav o sost um dhysal, hosen re dhav et Aelvaren hos fedh ul lern redulhellur et Mystaeri. Nath re yal hos ulo, eth nath re luss hos o. Nath re orrant et higileth hemm dem vimm, dem et sulter, hy vodh sulter myst sa vellor lo hyll, sulter sa vellor loth. Ew et tevath sa re orrant dem vimm. Lo lern et sulter re firr tolm.

In the spring a small grey boat came in on the tide below the Keep and beached itself on the shingle, as the Aelvaren once beached before the council-hall of the Mystaeri. No one was in it, and no one had rowed it. The old ship-masters would not go down to it, for a grey boat that comes in on the tide is the boat that comes for you. It was the children who went down. In its bow lay a stone.

Word for word: *In spring a small grey boat came in at the tide below the keep, and it beached itself upon the shingle, as the Aelvaren once beached before the council-hall of the Mystaeri. There was no one in it, and no one rowed it. The old ship-masters did not go down, to the boat, for a grey boat that comes in at the tide: a boat that comes in to you. It was the children who went down. At the bow of the boat lay a stone.*

- **The ship-masters' saying is verbless**, as the tongue's proverbs are (§11.7): *sulter myst sa vellor lo hyll, sulter sa vellor loth*. *tav* is the canon's "come ashore; beach a boat".
- "It was the children who went down" is the cleft (§3.1). "In its bow" is VI.1's *lo lern et sulter*: the boat returns to the leaf in the same words that sent it.

> New tolm o. Ell ew tolm o sa re yal mesk: myst eth domm, eth nalelv, eth meskdhrenn ulo tul, hosen hebbet et Arra sa re firr sell sost ul vestul sa re lymmym dem susk et brunn. Tolm Myst re voll et Treskan o; eth mysk: tum o gor yalat. El et hos tolm sa wrodh. Nath noss brodlymmyl lo dholm, eth el venn ull tul; veth new hosel tolm o fedh.

It was not stone. Or it was stone that had been wood: grey-black and heavy, with the grain still in it, like the Guest's gift that lay so long in the chest I carried up the mountain. The Rivenmen have taken to calling it Myststone, and they say it remembers all. It is the one stone that speaks. Stone does not translate, and that is still true; but this was never only stone.

Word for word: *It was not stone. Or it was stone that had been wood: grey and black, and heavy, and the grain in it still, like the gift of the Guest that lay so long in the chest that I carried up the mountain. Myststone the Rivenmen named it; and they say: it remembers everything. It is the one stone that speaks. Translating is not at stone, and that is true still; but it was not ever only stone.*

- ***El et hos tolm sa wrodh*** is built as the canon's own cleft, *El et tolm sa heth*, "It is the stone that holds" (§3.1).
- **"Stone does not translate"** is the "at" idiom, "translating is not at stone": *nath* before *brodlymm* would bed it into *mrodlymm*, an onset the tongue does not allow (§2.5). IV.1 has the same line. *Tolm Myst* is canon; it is fronted, *re voll* keeping the verb a verb (*voll et Treskan* read as "the name of the Rivenmen").
- "never only stone" is I.1's *hosel covv hesperd* shape, *new hosel tolm o fedh*, "it was not ever only stone".

> Eth re yal o rytet lo wresk. Seskym breskyaldat. Re ryt garl o sa re rystull et bresk desir, eth gell: tevel hosen nydhent, kaeverym, eth fedhet hosen nydher. Re yal o rytet ul ol dolmath sost, tolmath sa vesk gor uld; eth nath re hadhom en narlremullath umo. Re veskym o lo en veskowath. Re yal o rytet dem hos sa vesk hosel.

And it had been cut with a chisel. I know chisel-work. A hand had cut it that learned the chisel late and learned it well, young by their reckoning, I think, and seasoned by ours. It was cut in our own letters, so that anyone might read it, and I did not lay my palms on it. I read it with my eyes. It was cut so that one could read it alone.

Word for word: *And it had been cut with a chisel. I know chisel-work. A hand cut it that learned the chisel late, and well: young as they count, I think, and seasoned as we count. It was cut in our own letters, letters that everyone reads; and I did not lay my palms upon it. I read it with my eyes. It was cut for one who reads alone.*

- **Purpose without a "so that"**: "so that anyone might read it" is "letters that everyone reads"; "so that one could read it alone" is "for one who reads alone". *tolm* is a stone and a letter: the Carver cut stones on a stone.
- "learned it well" is "late, and well": *re rystull o gell* read "its good" (*o* before *g*).

**The Last Carver's letters** (`<!-- MIXED -->`), canon §11.9, cut in the course-hand across the round, as he cut them:

> *Ul et dumol ol varn, ol theldeth…*

*In the memory of our home, our families...*

- **His error is kept**: *et*, the article on the head of a construct, the one word too many. The analyzer reports both of his tells, as it should.

> Hess et ryt lo ull, ul molt ol Hosk, eth stinel dasken et bresk gand tul lo rask et trenn. Vesseth mest hy ull re reskym lo, eth re haeverym um et hos sa re ryt o. Nath re vesk o ol govv treskat fedh, kaeverym, hy vodh nath re farr o fedh, eth nath re farr hos hyont fedh ul scetheth ull. Veth re vesk gor sceth sa re vellor ul lern ol hald o ul dhenthyl, eth re orr galat sa re veskent et scetheth dem varn, lo dhrenn et meskrivullath, dem et meskanath; eth re heth o o lo ull, hosen heth et mesk hald. Re lymm o o wrodath ulo gor surr et gorrol, eth re rystull o ol dolmath hyont, hy vodh nath re yal galat ullen orrowen lo dem rystullol hyo; eth ul et dask re ryt o so ul et hos tolm sa re yal lo Naelear, sa es lymm kaeveret. Re vollom o ulom Ryterd Dasken, hy vodh kaeverym: amm re ryt o sy, nath re yal ullen. Eth re haeverym: doss et brodel navenn ulo, et hos brod sa nath re vaver hos, hosen sull brod et Arra lo Yebb et Pard: et galat venn, sa re vysk hos ul vennoth, sa nath re sesk tul hosen vodh mysker o.

The cutting stops there, in the middle of our Title, with the chisel's last stroke still sharp at the end of the line. I have sat with it many nights since, and thought of the one who cut it. He never saw our torn cloak, I think, for he never sailed, and no one of theirs ever sailed in those hulls. But every hull that came against our wall saw it flying, and what the hulls saw went home through the roots to the groves, and he knew it there, as the wood knows a wall. He carried its words in him through all the years of the war, and learned our letters from them, for there was nothing else of ours to learn them from; and at the last he cut them in the one stone his people had that would carry a meaning. I have called him, to myself, the Last Carver; for I think that when he cut this there was no other. And I have thought that the small wrong word in it, the one word too many, is like the Guest's three words at the warden's door: the right thing, said truly, by one who did not yet know how we say it.

Word for word: *The cutting stops there, in the middle of our Title, and the last stroke of the chisel sharp still at the end of the line. Many nights since then I have sat by it, and thought upon the one who cut it. He did not ever see our torn cloak, I think, for he did not ever sail, and not one of them ever sailed in those hulls. But every hull that came in against our wall saw it in flying, and the thing that the hulls saw went home, along the course of the roots, to the groves; and he held it there, as the wood holds a wall. He carried its words in him every year of the war, and learned our letters from them, for there was no other thing of the Shore at him, for learning from; and in the end he cut them in the one stone that the Naelear had, that would carry a meaning. I have named him in myself the Last Carver, for I think: when he cut this, there was not another. And I have thought: the small false word in it, the one word that no one wanted, is like the three words of the Guest at the Warden's Door: the true thing, that one said in truth, who did not yet know how we say it.*

- **"he knew it there" is *keth***, and "as the wood knows a wall" is *hosen heth et mesk hald*: the Carver knew the Title as the Bonded know wood, by holding.
- **"his people" is "the Naelear"**: *o lunt* would not show its possessive (G9). *Ryterd Dasken* is the lexicon's (O2165).
- **"the one word too many" is "the one word that no one wanted"** (*maver*, "wish; need; require", read here in its first sense on purpose). *brodel* is "a small word; a syllable". "the warden's door" is IV.3's title, softened after *lo* (*lo Yebb et Pard*, as IV.4's *ul lern Yebb et Pard*).
- "through all the years" is *gor surr et gorrol*, "every year of the war", with no trigger before *gor*.

> Re rarr o hosel, eth nayess el podul ull. Nath re nethiler arra tul.

No one came with it, and perhaps that is wise. We have not yet earned a guest.

Word for word: *It came alone, and perhaps that is wise. We did not yet earn a guest.*

- **"No one came with it" is "it came alone"**, *re rarr o hosel* (*hosel* after the verb reads "alone", §3.8; *o* before *h* shows no bite, so it is the subject). The first version, *Nath re rarr hos lo*, was read "No one has come for it" in the blind back-translation (`wf8/backtrans/S10.md`): *lo* is the "at, by, with" of having and being able (§5.10), and "come at it" is heard as "come for it".
- *nath … tul* is "not yet", as Seren's own line has it (§3.8). *nethil* is "earn, pay for": a guest is earned, and the Aelthar's price is known. *arra* is bare, "a guest", not the Guest.

> El vadullen lomm, eth doss o nethilet dem devath. Medh et wadh tul um dhysal ul vimm et hald. Dhenth et Covv Treskat tul um yanna ulvenn, eth ul vess darr et helvhoss um sorthath susk, ul vodh ston Kethow Dhresk tul; eth myskent et hemmeth: brodh o et Hosk somm orr o.

Grief is an inheritance, and it is paid out to the children. The sea still whispers on the shingle below the wall. The torn cloak still flies above the inner gate, and at night the wind comes over the high ridges where Rivenkeep still stands, and the old ones say it speaks the Title as it goes.

Word for word: *Grief is an inheritance, and it is paid to the children. The sea whispers still upon the shingle below the wall. The Torn Cloak flies still upon the inner gate, and in the night the wind comes over the high ridges, where Rivenkeep still stands; and the old ones say: it speaks the Title while it goes.*

- ***El vadullen lomm***, the copula's proverb order, predicate first; *nethilet* "paid" is the word of "we have not yet earned a guest": grief is the price the children are paid.
- ***ston***, "stands", is the word that closes every oath: Rivenkeep's standing is its vow. "above the inner gate" is *um yanna ulvenn*, "upon the inner gate" (*ul susk* + a softened possessor is not a construct to the analyzer).

> Galat sa es luskar ul dessyl, nath seskym o. Nath seskym galat veth sy: doss maver luskol et tessyl umor, ul lummol.

What we will send back, I do not know. I know only that it must be sent kneeling.

Word for word: *The thing that we will send in answer, I do not know it. I know nothing but this: the sending of the answer is a need upon us, in kneeling.*

- **"only that" is *nath … veth*** (§3.8). **The last word of the Book is the answer, *tessyl***, the hearth's word, and the kneeling, *lummol*, the Guest's first step: the Epilogue ends on the two things VI.1 gave.
- The fronted object is taken up again by *o* (G6).
