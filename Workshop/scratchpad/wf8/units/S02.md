# I.3 · THE CAPTAIN'S LONG BREATH

## Title

> ## *Hoss Sell et Crenn*

**I.3 · The Captain's Long Breath**

*The long breath of the Captain.* This is the lexicon's title row (O1006). The breath comes back once in the telling, let out as *hoss kethet*, "a held breath" (P8).

## Seren's headnote

> *Re hadh Kestel Hale, Hale Haskard, sa lymm brod et galat vesket lo dhrenn et hald ul et kethyl, ul surr hosast et tynur. El brodhat et odh, eth cadh o hosen vodh re dhald; clenn Kael Nydherd o lo et dask, hosen clenn virr gor yalat.*

*Laid by Private Hale, Hale the runner, who carries word of what is seen along the wall in the holding, in the first year of the siege. It is a saying of the hearth, and he tells where it came from; Kael the Counter mends it at the end, as he mends most things.*

Word for word: *Laid Private Hale, Hale the Runner, who carries word of the thing seen along the wall in the holding, in the first year of the siege. It is a saying of the hearth, and he lays how it rose; Kael the Counter mends it at the end, as [he] mends almost everything.*

- **The frame is I.1's.** It opens with the active *Re hadh* + rank + name + epithet, as in *Re hadh Crennel Kael, Kael Nydherd, …*
  - *Kestel* is the lexicon's "a private" ("one of the company").
  - *Hale Haskard* is apposition, as in *Kael Nydherd* (addition S02-03).
- ***et galat vesket***, "the thing seen". The bare noun *vesket* is "a colour, a look" in I.1 (*El vesket et covv ull*), so "what is seen" names its thing.
- ***ul et kethyl***, "in the holding": the siege day's third hour, always with its article (additions S02-06 to S02-08).
- ***ul surr hosast et tynur***, "in the first year of the siege". This is a construct whose head carries an ordinal and takes no article, as the Title's *dumol* takes none.
- ***cadh o hosen vodh re dhald***, "he lays how it rose": to tell is to lay, and a saying *rises* (*tald*) as the crying rose in I.1. *Hosen vodh*, "how", is the lexicon's; the leaf's own *Re wrodh o hosen vodh …* (I.4 P10) has the same shape. *(Back-translation fix.)* The first draft had *cadh o ul vodh re dhald*, "he lays where it rose". A blind reader heard "he lays it where he rose": *o* after a 3sg verb is "he" or "it", and *ul vodh* is "where" as a place as well as the object of *cadh*. *Hosen vodh* can only be what is told. The English "where it came from" is its origin, which "how it rose" still says (Notes 8).
- ***clenn … virr gor yalat***, "mends … almost everything". *Clenn* is "make new, mend", the canon's own verb for a breach.

## Hale's telling

> Hebb et tolm lom, hy ull. Nel brodhard en. Haskom.

Give me the stone, then. I am not a teller. I run.

Word for word: *Give the stone at me, then. Not a teller am I. I run.*

- ***Hebb et tolm lom*** is the lexicon's formula (O0897), as Kael opens I.1. *Hy ull*, "then, so", is added for the English "then".
- ***Nel brodhard en***: the copula puts the predicate first, as in Kael's *El nydherd yalatath en, nel vellerd*. Hale is plain; his whole creed is one verb, *Haskom*.

> Doss brodhat lo et odh sy sa es hunnos somm nath noss tul bress los um et hald. Kaeverym re hadhom o dholm hosast, veth mysk Voss: Doss et brodhat hemm hyoth.

There is a saying at this hearth that you will hear before you have been a month on the wall. I think it began with me, though Voss says it is older.

Word for word: *There is a saying at this hearth that you will hear while there is not yet a month at you upon the wall. I think I laid its first stone, but Voss says: The saying is older than you.*

- ***somm nath noss tul … los***, "while not yet is a month at you". "Before" as a conjunction is *nath … tul* ("not yet", G10) inside *somm* ("while"). *Amm nath* would be heard as the lexicon's "unless".
- ***re hadhom o dholm hosast***, "I laid its first stone": IV.4's *cadh et tolm hosast* ("begin"), with the possessive (*o* + S·*tolm*).
- **Voss is wry, in his own words**: *hemm hyoth*, "older than you". Orrowen quotes rather than reports (I.1), so his "it is older" comes with its own comparison; Seren's English keeps only "older". *Doss et brodhat* names its subject: after a bare *o*, *hemm* would be heard as S·*cemm* "meet".

> Doss pa relv clem lo orl, doss tynur ell nath noss. Ul et cadhol orrant niss, hy vodh doss tolm nalelv. Ul et caldol orrant niss, hy vodh doss brammath nalelv hy dholm. Veth ul et kethyl, amm mellorant et wemmeth myst, orr clem ul moltrunn, eth orl tunn hosen pa relv hyont; eth nath noss kethyl hos clem sol lo uld, dem gaeveryl ulo. Ew hosen ull ul en gethyl hosast. Re rarrant et wemmeth hy et myst, pynt hy en nydhyl, eth re wrodhant et brammath, eth re rarr et dhidh dem vimm; eth hy ull nath gadhom los amm re orr clem, ell ryst.

A day has four-and-twenty hours in it, siege or no siege. In the laying they go slowly, for stone is heavy. In the hauling they go slowly, for guns are heavier. But in the holding, when the grey sails come in, an hour goes by in a heartbeat, and a whole day in four-and-twenty of them, and a man cannot hold one hour still long enough to think in it. So it was in my first holding. The sails came out of the grey faster than I could count them, and the guns spoke, and the smoke came down, and afterward I could not have told you whether an hour had gone or a morning.

Word for word: *There are two-twelve hours at a day, [whether] there is a siege or there is not. In the laying they go slowly, for stone is heavy. In the hauling they go slowly, for guns are heavier than stone. But in the holding, when the grey sails come in, an hour goes in a heartbeat, and a whole day like two-twelve of them; and there is not at a man the holding of one hour still, for thinking in it. It was like that in my first holding. The sails came from the grey, quicker than my counting, and the guns spoke, and the smoke came down; and afterward I do not tell you if an hour had gone, or a morning.*

- ***pa relv clem***, "four-and-twenty hours", is the canon's guild count (§5.5, *clem*). A day "has" them by the "at" idiom: *Doss … lo orl*.
- ***doss tynur ell nath noss***, "siege or no siege": *there is a siege, or there is not*. Orrowen's yes and no are the verb said back.
- **The three hours are *et cadhol, et caldol, et kethyl***: the laying, the hauling, the holding (additions S02-06 to S02-08). They always keep their article. After *ul*, a bare *caldol* would bed to *galdol*, "building".
- **Qualities take the state verb:** *doss tolm nalelv*, "stone is heavy". *Doss* is "where, how, whether a thing is" (§3.4), and heaviness is a how. *Nalelv* is "un-light". Guns are heavier *hy dholm*, "than stone", the tongue's own comparison.
- ***hosen pa relv hyont***, "like two-twelve of them". The analyzer does not read a bedded *pa* (*ul ba*) as a softening trigger, so the grammatical *ul ba relv* is reported (Notes 4). *Hosen* leaves *pa* bare.
- ***nath noss kethyl hos clem sol lo uld***: "a man cannot hold one hour still" is the "at" idiom of §3.7. *Keth* is the Captain's own verb (*Ketherd*, "the holder"), and it names the holding.
- ***ul en gethyl hosast***, "in my first holding": *en* beds *kethyl*.
- ***re wrodhant et brammath***, "the guns spoke": *brodh*, "put into words", softened after *re*.
- ***nath gadhom los amm …***, "I do not tell you if …". This is Kael's *nath gadhom o los* from I.1, the habitual "cannot tell you". *Amm*, "when, if", serves for "whether".

> Doss gor wramm um et hald sy ul vodh re hadh et Crenn o, eth gor wramman, hos vennuld et Crenn ul susk. Somm nath mellorant et wemmeth tul, mysk et Crenn dem o vennuldath, hos eth hos, et galat sa haever o: ul vodh es nend o, pedh vodh es gadh o, eth amm vodh es geth o. Ul et kethyl gorr gor vennuld lo o wramman hy o dhesset sost, hy vodh kethent kaever et Crenn; hy ull gorr gor wramman hosel. Nath lusk et Crenn brod lo dhrenn et hald. Amm doss maver umo, carm et Crenn sost o, lo et gomm, dem et brammanath, hos eth hos. Doss sull carm, eth sesk gor vennuld so. *Marr tho dhrun*: el et nynth sa nend et bramman, eth nel et tolmath sa ston o umont. *Kyl tho vedh*: dem et nynth, ell dem et orrow, ell dem et helv. Eth *Keth, ell lusk*. Nath lymm haskard hos hyont. El et galat vesket sa lymmym.

Every gun on this wall stands where the Captain set it, and every battery has one of his True Men over it. Before the sails come, the Captain tells each True Man his mind: where to watch, what shot to lay, and when to hold. In the holding each True Man fights his battery by his own judgment, for they know the Captain's mind; that is why each battery fights on its own. The Captain does not send his word along the wall. When he must, he calls it himself, by the horn, to one battery at a time. There are three calls, and every True Man knows them. *Shift your ground*: that is the water a battery watches, and not the stones it stands on. *Change your shot*: for the water, or the shore, or the sky. And *hold, or loose*. A runner carries none of these. What I carry is what is seen.

Word for word: *Every gun upon this wall is where the Captain laid it, and every battery, one True Man of the Captain's above. While the sails do not yet come in, the Captain says to his True Men, one by one, the thing that he intends: where he will watch, what shot he will lay, and when he will hold. In the holding every True Man fights at his battery from his own judgement, for they hold the Captain's thought; therefore every battery fights alone. The Captain does not send word along the wall. When there is need upon him, the Captain himself calls it, by the horn, to the batteries, one by one. There are three calls, and every True Man knows them. Shift your ground: it is the water that the battery watches, and not the stones that it stands upon. Change your shot: to the water, or to the shore, or to the sky. And Hold, or loose. A runner does not carry one of them. It is the thing seen that I carry.*

- **The three calls are the canon's, word for word** (§11.5): *Marr tho dhrun · Kyl tho vedh · Keth, ell lusk*. The gloss on the first is the canon's own note: *trun* is the water, not the stones.
- ***gor wramm***, ***gor wramman***: *gor* softens *bramm*, *bramman*. **Every battery, one True Man** is the verbless distributive of I.1's *gor vennuld hos cryrull*.
  - No mutating word may stand before *gor* (I.1 §3), so "every battery has …" is not *lo gor …*.
- ***et galat sa haever o***, "the thing he intends": *kaever* is "think; a thought; mean, intend", softened after *sa*.
  - *mysk et Crenn o haever* was avoided because it reads as a construct, "the Captain of his mind" (Notes 4).
- ***pedh vodh***, "what shot", has the shape of I.1's *ul drenn vodh*. The True Man's three matters are futures: *es nend*, *es gadh*, *es geth*.
- ***lo o wramman hy o dhesset sost***: "at his battery, from his own judgement". *Gorr* with a direct object would be "fight against". *Tesset*, "a verdict, a thing answered", is the lexicon's "judgement".
- ***kethent kaever et Crenn***, "they hold the Captain's thought": to know a mind is to keep it.
- ***hy ull gorr gor wramman hosel***, "therefore every battery fights alone": here *hosel* after the verb means "alone", which is the sense wanted (G8).
- ***carm et Crenn sost o***, "the Captain himself calls it". *Sost* is "self, very", as in *et dask sost*.
- ***dem et brammanath***: the article keeps *dem* from bedding *br-* into *mr-*, an onset the tongue does not allow.
- ***El et galat vesket sa lymmym***, "It is the thing seen that I carry": the cleft of §3.1.

> Ul et orlath hosast ull nath re yal veth hos bramman um et hald, eth re nesk Kael o. Orl ull re lusk Kael en lo yalat sa re vesk o wegorn: scethel sa re dhovv ul sestow ul et myst, ul vodh nath re dhovv hos fedh, eth re yal ul mellorol amm ull, desir, amm re yal et ullen vyssorat. Re haskom o lo dhrenn et haldorrol, bisk dem vimm, eth re vellorom dem et Crenn.

In those first days there was only one battery on the wall, and it was Kael's. That day Kael sent me with what his lookout had seen: a hull that had hung back in the grey, where none had hung before, and was coming in now, late, when the rest were spent. I ran it along the wall-walk with my head down, and came to the Captain.

Word for word: *In those first days there was not but one battery upon the wall, and Kael led it. That day Kael sent me with a thing that his lookout had seen: a hull that had waited behind, in the grey, where not one had ever waited, and was in coming-in then, late, when the rest were spent. I ran it along the wall-walk, head down, and came in to the Captain.*

- ***nath re yal veth hos bramman***, "there was not but one battery": "only" of a clause is *nath … veth* (G8).
- ***eth re nesk Kael o***, "and Kael led it", stands for "it was Kael's". **Kael's name is kept out of every mutating slot.** After *lo*, or as a construct's possessor, it would soften to *Hael*, a letter from *Hale*, the teller of this very leaf (Notes 4).
- ***lo yalat***, "with a thing" (*lo* softens *galat*). *O wegorn* is "his lookout" (S·*begorn*).
- ***re dhovv ul sestow***, "waited behind": *tovv* "wait", softened. *Hos fedh* reads "one ever": not one hull had ever waited there.
- ***bisk dem vimm***, "head down", is verbless, like I.1's *eth so wresketh ul so simmeth*. "My head" is not written *en misk*: *en* would bed *bisk* into *misk*, which is the word for "foot".

> Re ston o sol. Tumom ull hosast. Re yal gor yalat um et hald ull ul morrol; nath re yal o. Re yal o yarl um hosk et hald ul susk et ganna, eth et gomm ul et garl ullen; eth re nend o hos scethel ul molt relv, et galat mystwemm sa re yal ul mellorol desir um et hyll; eth nath re vesk o umom. Re hunn et Crenn gor yalat sa re vyskym. Hy ull re wemm o et gomm.

He was standing still. That is what I remember first. Everything on that wall was moving but him. He had one hand on the parapet above the gate and the horn in the other, and he was watching one hull among a dozen, the grey-sailed thing coming in late on the tide, and he did not look round at me. He heard me out. Then he lifted the horn.

Word for word: *He stood still. That I remember first. Everything upon that wall was in going; he was not. His hand was upon the capstone of the wall above the gate, and the horn in the other hand; and he watched one hull among twelve, the grey-sailed thing that was coming in late upon the tide; and he did not look upon me. The Captain heard everything that I said. Then he lifted the horn.*

- ***Re yal gor yalat … ul morrol; nath re yal o***: "everything was in going; he was not". The answer is the verb said back, Orrowen's own "but him".
  - *Ul marrol* ("in moving") was avoided, since after *ul* it is heard as N·*arrol*.
- ***hosk et hald***, "the capstone of the wall": the parapet, as in the lexicon's *hosk hald*. With the article on the possessor, the head takes none.
- ***re nend o hos scethel ul molt relv***, "he watched one hull among twelve". *Delv* is softened as the possessor of *ul molt*. The simple past replaces *ul nendyl*, which after *ul* reads as N·*endyl*.
- ***mystwemm*** is the lexicon's "grey-sailed".
- ***Re hunn et Crenn gor yalat sa re vyskym***, "The Captain heard all I said": this is "he heard me out". *Dem et dask*, "to the end", is the lexicon's "forever".
- ***re wemm o et gomm***: *bemm* "lift", softened after *re*. After *re*, the verb reading wins over *wemm* "sail".

> Eth re yal et clem ul sellyl. Nath re hess o pynt, hosen hess saevar ul lern dholm; re orr o niss, hosen orr saevar niss um wrunnel sell, eth amm ull virr nath re orr o. Nath noss cadhol gell hy sy lom. Re stonom lo o sest, eth re sell et clem umo; eth re yal veskyl et nynth lom, ul vidhyl hy lern et scethel ull; eth re yal nydhyl fodheleth lom, ul morrol dem vimm lo dhrenn et wemm, hos eth pa eth sull, hosen nydhys scaevurath ul et mesk stinet; eth ew clemel gor fodhel, kaeverym. Re yal fedh ludalol lom. Hy ull re yal fedh haemul lom um ull. Hy ull re yal fedh hessyl lom.

And the hour began to stretch. It did not stop short, as a cart stops against a stone; it slowed, as a cart slows on a long hill, until it hardly moved at all. I have no better way to say it. I stood at his shoulder and the hour went long around him, and I could see the water sheet off that hull's bow, and I could count the ripples going down its sail, one and two and three, as you count the rings on a cut log; and each of them, I think, was a minute. I had time to be afraid. Then I had time to be ashamed of it. Then I had time to stop.

Word for word: *And the hour was in stretching. It did not stop quickly, as a cart stops against a stone; it went slowly, as a cart goes slowly upon a long hill, and then it almost did not go. There is no better laying than this at me. I stood at his shoulder, and the hour stretched upon him; and seeing the water was at me, in pouring from the bow of that hull; and counting the ripples was at me, in going down along the sail, one and two and three, as you count the rings in the cut log; and every ripple was a minute, I think. There was a time of fearing at me. Then there was a time of shame at me, about that. Then there was a time of stopping at me.*

- ***ul sellyl***, "in stretching": the progressive of §3.4 carries "began to stretch". *Sell* is "long" and "stretch", the second word of the title.
- ***ul lern dholm***, "against a stone": *ul lern* ("in the face of") with its possessor softened, the Cry's *tolm um dholm* sound. *Um wrunnel sell*, "on a long hill": *brunnel* ("a small mountain"), softened after *um*.
- ***eth amm ull virr nath re orr o***, "and then it almost did not go". "Until" is *eth amm ull*, as in I.1: *dem amm* would bed to *dem mamm*, a reserve word.
- ***Nath noss cadhol gell hy sy lom***, "there is no better laying than this at me". This is I.1's *nath noss cadhol ullen lom* ("I have no other way of telling"), made comparative.
- ***re sell et clem umo***, "the hour stretched upon him": the "upon" of feelings and burdens (§3.7).
- ***re yal veskyl … lom*** and ***re yal nydhyl … lom*** are "I could see", "I could count": the past of the "at" idiom.
  - *Lern* is both "face" and "the bow of a hull".
  - *Fodhel* is "a ripple" ("a small wave").
  - *Scaevurath* are "rings" (*scaevur*, "a ring, a round").
  - *Mesk* takes its article (*ul et mesk*), since after a bare *ul* it is heard as N·*besk*, "stranger".
- ***hosen nydhys***, "as you count", is the 2pl: the hearth is addressed as many, as Kael's *los* in I.1.
- **The three times** are the same line three ways: *Re yal fedh ludalol lom* / *fedh haemul lom um ull* / *fedh hessyl lom*, "a time of fearing, of shame about that, of stopping". *Kaemul*, "shame", is softened as the possessor of *fedh*.

> Hy ull re hebb o et carm lo et bramman sa re nesk Kael, eth re lymm et gomm o, eth niss re lusk et clem ol, hosen orr hoss kethet hyo; eth amm ull re yal et trun pawast lo o lusul sost. Re wrammant et brammath. Re hresk et scethel ul hosel reskow et nynth ull, ul vodh nath re yal grest hyo um hos, ul vodh re haever o.

Then he gave Kael's battery its call, and the horn carried it, and the hour let go of us slowly, as a held breath goes out, until the world was back at its own pace. The guns went. The hull broke up in the one stretch of that water where it could hurt no one, where he had meant it to.

Word for word: *Then he gave the call to the battery that Kael led, and the horn carried it, and slowly the hour loosed us, as a held breath goes out; and then the world was again at its own pace. The guns fired. The hull broke in the only place of that water where there was no harm from it upon anyone, where he had intended.*

- ***hebb … lo***, "give to", as in *Hebb et tolm lom*. The battery is "the one Kael led", since *bramman Hael* would have put Hale's name where Kael's belongs.
- ***niss re lusk et clem ol***, "slowly the hour loosed us": *lusk* is the Captain's third call ("loose"), and the hearth hears *lunn* (freedom) in it (§11.5). The adverb stands first so that *ol* ends its clause. After *et clem*, *ol niss* would be read as a construct, "the hour of our slow".
- ***hosen orr hoss kethet hyo***, "as a held breath goes out": the breath of the title (addition S02-11). *Hyo*, "out of it", is the lexicon's "out".
- ***et trun***, "the world": *trun* is ground, soil and world, and on the wall the water a battery watches. The world coming back to its pace is the ground coming back to the battery.
- ***ul hosel reskow et nynth ull***, "in the only place of that water". *Hosel* stands before the noun as in I.1's *hosel covv hesperd*. *Ul reskow* alone is the lexicon's "somewhere".
- ***nath re yal grest hyo um hos***, "there was no harm from it upon anyone", which is the old masters' saying turned: *Grest um hos, grest um vana* (§11.7). The Captain chose the one water where the saying had no one to fall on.
- ***re haever o***: *kaever* "mean, intend", softened.

> Hy ull myskent et uldath: *Lo et Crenn, clem sell hosen orl.*

So the men say: *For the Captain, an hour lasts as long as a day.*

Word for word: *So the men say: At the Captain, an hour long as a day.*

- **The saying is verbless, as Orrowen proverbs are** (§11.7, *Grest um hos, grest um vana*): *at the Captain* is where the hour is, and *sell*, "long", is the title's word. It is an addition (S02-05), because the saying is the wall's and will be met again: in the Whispers, the battle messages and Command Time.

> Myskent o hosen amm ew hebbet sa re hadh et Stonwrytan umo. Re vyskym o hosen ull, en sost, ul et grem hosast. Amm sy seskym gell hy ull. Nel hy vodh doss pyntyth lo. El hy vodh nath noss pyntyl umo.

They say it as though it were a gift the Stonewrights laid on him. I said it so myself, the first winter. I know better now. It is not that he is quick. It is that he will not be hurried.

Word for word: *They say it as if it were a gift that the Stonewrights laid upon him. I said it like that, myself, in the first winter. Now I know better than that. It is not because speed is at him. It is because hurrying is not upon him.*

- ***hosen amm***, "as if" (I.1's addition). *re hadh et Stonwrytan umo*: the guild (a collective, 3sg) *laid* it upon him, as a course is laid.
- ***Nel hy vodh … El hy vodh …***: the negative copula and the copula, as I.1's *New hy vodh …*, and the two halves of Hale's answer are the two idioms of §3.7.
  - Speed would be something he *has* (*doss pyntyth lo*, "speed is at him").
  - Haste would be something laid *upon* him (*nath noss pyntyl umo*), and it is not.
  - *O pyntet*, "he is hurried", was avoided: after *o*, an unsoftened *p* is heard as a fault (Notes 4).

> Gell hyom re vysk Kael o, um et vorr, eth hebbym o los hosen re vysk o: "Nel et trun sa orr niss, luld. El et Crenn sa nath bynt. Et clemel sa re naelur o, ew et clemel sa re rarr."

Kael said it better, from across the fire, and I give it to you as he said it: "It is not the world that slows, lad. It is the Captain who will not hurry. The moment he chose was the moment that came."

Word for word: *Better than I, Kael said it, across the fire, and I give it you as he said it: "It is not the world that goes slowly, lad. It is the Captain who does not hurry. The moment that he chose, it was the moment that came."*

- **Kael is plain, as ever**: three short sentences, two clefts (*Nel et trun sa …*, *El et Crenn sa …*) and a proverb-shaped close. *Luld*, "lad", is a vocative (softening leaves *l*).
- ***sa nath bynt***: *nath* beds *pynt*, "hurry".
- ***Et clemel sa re naelur o, ew et clemel sa re rarr***: the fronted phrase is taken up by the copula. *Clemel*, "a small hour", is a moment. *Naelur* is "choose". *Re rarr* is *darr* "come", softened.
- ***Gell hyom … um et vorr***: "better than I, across the fire". The comparison stands first so that no bare *o* stands before *gell*. "From across" is *um*, "across, over". *Hy neld* is not Orrowen, since *neld* is only N·*deld* ("far").

(The hearth formula, O0238, exactly.)

# I.4 · THE RITE OF THE CORNERSTONE

## Title

> ## *Ternil et Gorndholm*

**I.4 · The Rite of the Cornerstone**

*The rite of the cornerstone.* This is the lexicon's title row (O2499). *Ternil* is "a rite, a thing done in order; a custom", and the leaf's last discovery is that the hearth's custom is a *ternil* too (P10).

## Seren's headnote

> *Re hadha Halyna, Lodhan et Kethow, ul heth hosast et tynur, eth re hadhom o ul ba luth, hy vodh cadha Halyna o hos eth ullen, trenn eth trenn, hosen cadha Halyna gor dhrenn. Brodha Halyna um sona sost lo voll, eth dem neld, hosen brodhant ryteth surr um uldath sennet. Amm re vessym sona hy vodh, re vysk hos: Nel lyrness trenn; eth re vysk ullen: Nath sesk hald et garlath sa re hadhant o.*

*Laid by Halyna, the Bonded of the Keep, in the first spring of the siege, and set in two inks, for they tell it by turns, a paragraph each, as all their tellings are told. They speak of themselves by name and from far off, as the annals speak of the dead; when I asked them why, one said that a tale is not a boast, and the other said that a wall does not know whose hands laid it.*

Word for word: *Laid Halyna, the Bonded of the Keep, in the first spring of the siege, and I laid it in two inks, for Halyna lay it the one and the other, course and course, as Halyna lay every tale. Halyna speak of themselves by name, and far off, as the annals speak of dead men. When I asked them why, one said: A tale is not a boast; and the other said: A wall does not know the hands that laid it.*

- **Halyna are two, and take the dual** (§3.9 item 1), as in IV.4's *Re wrodha Halyna o*: *re hadha*, *cadha*, *brodha*. The 3du of a broad verb sounds like the imperative plural, and "a dual verb always has a subject" (§3.4), so the pair-name is said each time. That is also the leaf's own point: they speak of themselves by name.
- ***Lodhan et Kethow***, "the Bonded of the Keep" (addition S02-02): a construct, its head without the article.
- ***ul heth hosast et tynur***, "in the first spring of the siege". *Heth* "spring" cannot be S·*keth* here, because *ul* beds and does not soften.
- ***eth re hadhom o ul ba luth***, "and I laid it in two inks": Seren sets it down, as in IV.4's *re hadh Seren o*. *Ul ba luth* is IV.4's, exactly.
- ***hos eth ullen, trenn eth trenn***, "by turns, course and course". *Hos eth ullen* is IV.4's (O2858). *Trenn eth trenn* is "a paragraph each", the distributive *X eth X* of pilot I.1's *hos eth hos* (merged lexicon; S04 and S07 say it so). In Orrowen a paragraph of a telling is a course of it. *(Merge fix, `wf8/merge_report.md`: this unit first wrote* trenn um dhrenn*, "course upon course", addition S02-09; the merge keeps one form for the Book's recurring "a paragraph each".)*
- ***dem neld***, "far off", is the lexicon's phrase.
- ***ryteth surr***, "the annals" (O2167), speak *um uldath sennet*, "about dead men". *Sennet*, "gone out", is the death of a fire in peace.
- **The two answers are quoted**, one voice each, as Orrowen reports speech:
  - *Nel lyrness trenn*, "a tale is not a boast": predicate first.
  - *Nath sesk hald et garlath sa re hadhant o*, "a wall does not know the hands that laid it".

Word for word: *There are vaults beneath this hall, and beneath those vaults are others, cut down into the bedrock of the ridge while not yet was a haven built upon the Shore; and the deepest of them, a Stonewright had not entered it in the memory of the guild. On the third night after the cry, Rhyna of the Inner Gate rose from her blanket, and Halvard Stone-Warden rose in the very moment, and took up the lamp. They two went down, stair upon stair, and then the stairs stopped at the lip of a shaft where the first builders lowered their stone by ropes. She knew where it was, and he went down, and she let out the rope, and they two had not said one word. One thought was the thing that she knew and the thing that he found, as their two thoughts are one thought.*

- ***ul hal et sorth***, "into the bedrock of the ridge". *Hal* is the canon's "bedrock, the living rock of the ridge", the old register's own name, so "the living ridge" is said with the word that already means it.
- ***somm nath re yal tul cumm galdat***, "while not yet was a haven built": "before ever" is *nath … tul* (G10) inside *somm*, as in I.3.
- ***et gunn sost hyont, nath re vellor stonwrytel ulo***: a fronted phrase taken up by *ulo*, "into it" (G6).
- ***ul dumol et Stonwrytan***, "in the memory of the guild": the Title's own bedded *dumol*.
- **Halyna speak of themselves from far off**: the paragraph names Rhyna and Halvard with their canon epithets (*Rhyna Yanna Ulvenn*, *Halvard Tolmvard*) and uses *ey*, *o*, *sona*, never "we".
- ***bystir um wystir***, "stair after stair" (addition S02-10). *Lo inth vyger* is "at the lip of a shaft": *inth* is "a lip", and *myger* "a shaft cut down" is softened as possessor.
- ***re lusk ey et bunth***, "she paid out the rope": *lusk*, "loose, slacken".
- ***nath re vyske sona hos brod***: "they two had not said one word", which is "neither had said a word".
- ***hosen el hos kaever sona haevereth***, "as their two thoughts are one thought". *Sona* softens *kaevereth*; the plural is slender, since *kaever* is S.

> Lo visk et myger re sol vestul hy dholm domm, eth rellor et galdardath hosast rytet ul o waek. Ulo re yalant ryteth Dhernil et Gorndholm, rytet lyss um halflenneth bynt. Word for word: *At the foot of the shaft lay a chest of black stone, and the mark of the first builders cut in its lid. In it were the inscriptions of the Rite of the Cornerstone, cut small upon thin slates. It was the oldest rite of the guild, the rite that the first Stonewrights blessed the leaders of the Keep by, for the building of the Keep itself.*

- ***lo visk et myger***: *lo* softens *misk* ("foot"). *Hy dholm domm*, "of black stone": *hy* is "made of".
- ***rellor et galdardath hosast rytet ul o waek***: "the mark of the first builders cut in its lid", a clause with no verb (I.1). *Baek* is "lid", softened after *o*.
- ***ryteth Dhernil et Gorndholm***: the title *Ternil et Gorndholm* stands as a possessor, so its head softens (*t* > *dh*).
- ***halflenneth bynt***, "thin slates". *Halflenn*, "slate", is "rock-leaf", so the leaves of slate are said in one word. *Flennath* is not used here, because it is also "the Book".
- ***et ternil sa re veskul … lo***, "the rite that … consecrated … by": the preposition stays behind (G5). *Peskul* is "bless; consecrate", softened.
- ***neskerdeth et Kethow***, ***dem naldol et Kethow sost***: "Rivenkeep" is *et Kethow*, the Keep, as in I.1. *Dem* beds *galdol*.
  - *Dem* + a verbal noun is purpose, "to build" (lexicon O0390: "for, toward, for the sake of; to"), as in *dem stemir* and *dem gylyl vedh* (P4). A blind reader took the spec's only gloss, "until, as far as" (§5.10), and heard "back to the building of the Keep itself". The text stands; the gap is the spec's (Notes 8).

Word for word: *Halyna read the Rite-leaves by the lamp in the vault, and Halyna were troubled by the thing that they read. - ***Re veske Halyna***, ***re yala Halyna prumul***: the pair-name, the dual (*re yal-* + *-a*), and *prumul*, "troubled, heavy of heart". *Ternilflenneth*, "the Rite-leaves", is the plural of the lexicon's *ternilflenn* (O2503); the compound is slender and takes *-eth*.
- **The three secrets are cut as a mason's list, in the generic singular**:
  - *hald ferret … dem gylyl vedh*: "a wall bent back, for the turning of a shot". *Kylyl vedh* is the Captain's second call made a noun.
  - *ganna ferret sa nath noss haskol venn umo lo stromrunn*: "a crooked gate that a ram cannot run straight upon". *Stromrunn* is "great-hammer".
  - *trenn cadhat sa omm vathur ulo hyvenn …*: "a course laid that a breach in it falls outward …". The present *omm* is the habitual.
  - The plural of *ganna* would be *gannath*, the lexicon's "posts", and the list keeps one number throughout.
- ***nel ulvenn***, "not inward": *nel* does not bed. *Nath ulvenn* would give *nath mulvenn*.
- ***Delv tesk***, "twelve generations", is the canon's phrase. The guild's old teaching is quoted verbless, as a proverb: *Haldath dem sollan eth helv*, "walls for peace and weather". *Sollan* is the Title's last word, "the rest after the work", and *helv* is sky, air and weather.
- ***re yal lyrness umo***, "pride was upon it": the "upon" idiom (lexicon *doss lyrness um*).
- ***re nadhumar so***, "we forgot them": *we* is the guild here (1pl), not the pair.

Word for word: *When the people had gone to their fires, Halyna knelt at the new capstone, and they two cut a Stonwryt in its underside for his work, which was the wall, and the war. - ***re lumma Halyna***, ***re ryte sona***: the dual throughout. *Vimmlern* is the lexicon's "underside" ("low-face").
- ***Re hadh hos et bresk, eth re runn ullen***: "the one laid the chisel, and the other struck". *Drunn* ("a mallet; strike") is softened after *re*, and *dr-* > *r-* (lexicon §4.3). The sentence is split as the pair works.
- ***ul Gethow Dhresk***, "in Rivenkeep": *ul* beds *Kethow* to *Gethow*, and the nasal leaves no doubt.
- **The Stonwryt's words are not given**: the canon keeps the Captain's Stonwryt "cut where no eye will see it again unless the wall falls" (§11.6). The leaf only says that it was cut.
- ***ul et vess ull***, "in that night" (addition S02-12, *vess ull*).

Word for word: *He did not carry the staff to battle. He did not wear the oils beyond that day. - ***Nath re hovv o***: *covv*, "a cloak; wear", softened. The Captain does not "wear" the oils, and the verb is the cloak's own.
- ***hosel covv hesperd***, "only a soldier's cloak", is I.1's rendering of "a plain soldier's cloak" (its back-translation fix), reused.
- ***lo sest et vennuldath***, "beside the True Men". *Lo o sest* would be "at his shoulder", and *his* True Men are already his.
- ***Ferr et hulter ul norn et gannalest***: *ferr*, "lean", and *ul* bedding *gorn* ("corner") to *norn*. *Gannalest* is the lexicon's "gatehouse".
- ***Re varr nahos o***, "no one moved it": a positive verb with *nahos*, as in the lexicon's *Eth re dhess nahos*.

Word for word: *One leaf yet lay in the chest, at the very bottom, and Halyna did not find it but when the Rite was done. It was written by a smaller hand than the rest. It told how the first builders of the Keep sealed the day: when the stone was laid, and the tools cleaned, they sat at the hearth, and one of them laid a tale, as a course is laid upon a wall, one course every night, for the rising of the wall and the telling together. - ***hos flenn tul***, "one leaf yet": *tul*, "yet, still", for "one more".
- ***nath re weske Halyna o veth amm …***, "did not find it but when …": "until" is *nath … veth amm* (G8). *Dem amm* would bed to the reserve *mamm*.
- ***lo yarl lyss***, "by a smaller hand": *garl* is "a hand of writing" (*Garl Dhrenn*, *Garl Flenn*).
- ***re hoskant … et orl***, "they sealed the day". To end a day is to lay its lintel (*hosk*), as the gate mark ends a vow and the coping ends a tale. This is the tongue's idiom for the English "ended"; *hoskol*, "the sealing", is also the wedding.
- **The leaf's content is narrated, not reported**: Orrowen has no "that" before a clause.
  - *Hosen doss trenn cadhat um hald* is "as a course is laid upon a wall": the canon's "a tale that is laid is part of the wall" (§5.1, *trenn*).
  - *Dem daldol et hald eth et brodhol olen* is "for the rising of the wall and the telling together".
- ***doss et odh sy hemm hy et cummath***, "this hearth is older than the havens": *doss* for a quality, and *hy*, "than".
- ***Nath re yaldar o dhernil***, "we did not build its custom". *Ternil*, softened after *o*, is the same word as the Rite's: the custom of the hearth is itself a rite, found in the same chest.
  - A blind reader heard "we never made a rite of it". The softening of *dhernil* is what marks *o* as "its" (§3.5), so the reading is ruled out and the text stands.
  - *Dem daldol et hald eth et brodhol olen*, "for the rising of the wall and the telling together", is purpose *dem* again (P3 note). Read as "until", it still says the two rose together.
- **Orrowen must choose between the 1pl and the dual, and the choice is exact**: *re nadhumar o* (all of us forgot it), *eth re wesken o* (and we two found it). The English "we" covers both. **[Jack]** If a single "we" is wanted, *re wesker* (1pl) is the one change.

*(first ink)*

> Re harm et lunt o: *Ketherd.* Ul o volt sost, ew tul hosel et Crenn o.

The people called him Commander. In his own heart he was still only the Captain.

Word for word: *The people called him: Commander. In his own heart, he was still only the Captain.*

- ***Re harm et lunt o: Ketherd.*** *Carm*, "call, cry", softened. The name is given as it was called out. *Re voll et lunt o Ketherd* would set *Ketherd* after *o*, where an unsoftened *K* is heard as a fault.
- ***ew tul hosel et Crenn o***: "was still only the Captain". *Hosel* stands before the noun as I.1's "only". The last word of the telling is *o*, "he", and the last noun is *Crenn*.

### 8. From the blind back-translation (`wf8/backtrans/S02.md`)

Two gaps in `orrowen_v2.md` misled the blind reader. They are proposed here, not written into the shared spec, because other translators are working from it at the same time. **[orchestrator: fold into the spec with the other units' findings]**
- **SG1 · Indirect questions (§3.8).** The spec lists the question words and does not say how an indirect question is built. The leaves build it as the question word and its clause, standing where the object stands:
  - *Re sesk ey ul vodh re yal o*, "she knew where it was";
  - *Hunna hosen vodh …*, "hear how …";
  - *Re wrodh o hosen vodh …*, "it told how …".
  
  Proposed line: *An indirect question is the question word and its clause, in the object's place. After a bare 3sg pronoun (V o …), ul vodh is heard as the place "where", and o as "it", so there use a question word that cannot be a place (hosen vodh, amm vodh), or name the subject.*
- **SG2 · *dem* (§3.3, §3.6, §5.10).** The spec glosses *dem* only as "until, as far as". The lexicon (O0390) also gives "for, toward, for the sake of; to", and the leaves use every sense:
  - "to" a hearer: *mysk et Crenn dem o vennuldath*;
  - "for" a purpose: *dem stemir*, *dem hydull*, *dem hesperd*;
  - with a verbal noun, "in order to": *dem gylyl vedh*, *dem naldol et Kethow sost*, *dem daldol et hald*.
  
  Proposed line for §5.10: *dem (+N): until, as far as; to, for (a hearer, a purpose); dem + a verbal noun is "to V, in order to V".*
- **For the lexicon's builder.** A participle row carries only its verb's first sense: *galdat* is "built" but not "done", though *gald* is "build; do, make". It is the same builder defect that `backtrans_I1.md` §7 item 2 found for *driget*. Addition S02-13 patches *galdat* for these leaves.
