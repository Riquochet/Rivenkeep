### Title

> ## *Flennath et Cumm*

**III.1 · The Book of the Havens**

The lexicon's own title for Book Three (O0619), which this leaf shares. Like all the lexicon's book titles (*Flennath et Hald, Flennath et Hyll*) it names the thing in the generic singular: "the Book of the Haven".

### Seren's headnote

> *Re hadha Halyna o ul ba saed, hos eth ullen, cumm gor hos, hosen doss nydhyl cadhat: So Yaldol, lo wremm hosast et tynur, eth So Ommol, ul et grem ul sestow. El flenn et tolm um et cummath; doss flenn et mesk ul o lern.*

*Laid by Halyna in two parts, by turns, a haven each, as a catalogue is laid: Of Their Raising, in the first autumn of the siege, and Of Their Fall, in the winter after. It is the stone leaf of the havens; the wood's leaf faces it.*

Word for word: *Halyna (the two) laid it in two halves, the one and the other, a haven apiece, as a counting is laid: Their Raising, at the first autumn of the siege, and Their Falling, in the winter after. It is the leaf of the stone about the havens; the leaf of the wood is in its face.*

- ***Re hadha Halyna o***: the 3rd dual, as IV.4's *Re wrodha Halyna o* ("Halyna are always two", §3.9, §12). *Halyna* carries its sealing lintel in the ink; *Seren* never does.
- ***ul ba saed***, "in two halves": *pa saed* bedded after *ul*, as IV.4's *ul ba luth*. A telling laid in two parts is laid in two halves, so the parts are *Saed Hosast* and *Saed Pawast*.
- ***cumm gor hos***, "a haven apiece": the lexicon's *gor hos* (O0763). ***nydhyl***, "a catalogue", is "a counting", Kael's word: Halyna lay the havens as the Counter would.
- ***lo wremm hosast***: *bremm* "autumn" softened after *lo*. After *ul* it would bed into *mremm*, an onset §2.5 does not allow (I.1 and IV.4 avoid *mr-* the same way); *wr-* is the canon's own softened *br-* (*nawrodhat*).
- ***So Yaldol***, ***So Ommol***: "their raising", "their falling". A title leaves out the English "Of", as the lexicon's titles do (*Kethow sa re yal nadhumat*, "Of the Keep That Was Forgotten"). *Yaldol* is the softening of the canon's *Re yald et Stonwrytan*.
- ***doss flenn et mesk ul o lern***, "the wood's leaf faces it": *ul lern* "facing", with the possessive between (IV.4: *doss o ul lern Yebb et Pard*).

### Saed Hosast · So Yaldol

**Part One · Of Their Raising**

**(ink I)**

> Amm sy hunna vollath et cummath, eth ketha so, hy vodh dossant lor.

Hear now the names of the havens, and hold them, for they are ours.

Word for word: *Now hear the names of the havens, and hold them, for they are at us.*

- ***Amm sy hunna*** is I.1's "Now hear" (*Amm sy hunna cedh re dhess*). ***ketha***: to keep a thing is to hold it, the canon's *keth*. ***dossant lor***, "they are ours": the "at" idiom of having (§3.7).

**(ink II)**

> Lo dhrenn et orrowath rerddhalden ommant et brunnath eth et brunneleth susk dem vimm dem gemmyl et wadh, eth re hossant ol nedheth lo ull. Ew galdardath so, new wadhardath. Re yal ludal et wadh lunnat umont, eth re dhovvant lo et orrowath cummen; eth re hadhant orrolath strom hosen lorrdhrenneth um et brunnath, dem lodhol et reskowath sa re yaldant. Re yaldant noth cumm, sa vollant et velleth hemm: et noth tavowhinnar, eth ew galdat Stonwrytan gor hald hyont. Ul molt et Orrowan, new gor fedh tavow scetheth et cumm, veth gor reskow ul molt haldath Stonwrytan. Hunna so, hy et hemm sost.

Along the eastern coasts the mountains and the high hills come tumbling down to meet the sea, and there our fathers lived. They were builders and not seafarers. They feared the open water and kept to the sheltered shores, and they paved vast roads like veins across the mountains to join the places they had built. They raised ten havens, which the old songs call the ten harbour-cities, and every wall of them was Stonewright work; though a haven among the Shorelanders was not always a harbour for ships, but any place of shelter the Stonewrights had walled. Hear them, from the eldest.

Word for word: *Along the eastern shores the mountains and the high hills fall down to the meeting of the sea, and our fathers lived there. Builders were they, not seafarers. Fear of the open sea was upon them, and they stayed by the haven-like shores; and they laid great roads like blood-courses across the mountains, for the joining of the places that they had built. They raised ten havens, which the old songs name: the ten harbour-cities; and every wall of them was the Stonewrights' work. Among the Shorelanders the haven was not always a harbour of ships, but any place within the Stonewrights' walls. Hear them, from the very oldest.*

- ***Ew galdardath so, new wadhardath***: I.1's shape, *El nydherd yalatath en, nel vellerd* ("a counter of things am I, not a singer"), in the past.
- ***ludal … umont***, "they feared": a feeling is laid upon a person (§3.7).
- ***re hadhant orrolath***: the roads are *laid* (*cadh*, which is also "pave"), as a course and a tale are; ***lorrdhrenneth*** "veins" are "blood-courses". The whole paragraph is built of the mason's metaphor.
- ***dem gemmyl et wadh***, "to meet the sea": the verb of *Cemm Hyll*, which the next paragraph but one names.
- ***gor hald hyont***, "every wall of them": the partitive is *hy* "from", as I.1's *nahos hyor*.
- ***sa vollant et velleth hemm: et noth tavowhinnar***: the colon keeps "the ten harbour-cities" a name and not a possessor (note 4).
- The English "though …" is a sentence of its own in Orrowen. "Any place of shelter the Stonewrights had walled" is "any place within the Stonewrights' walls": *cumm* is already "any walled place of shelter", and *reskow cumm* would soften to *reskow humm* (the reserve *humm* "husband", lexicon §8.2).

**(ink I)**

> Tavow Hemm, et cumm hemm sost, sa re vorr o dhavow ul hos orl. Re orrant et prassath hosast dem vimm hy et Kethow, eth re hadhant et haldath; eth re yalant o dhavowlesteth et dyll sost ul Morrow.

Eldhythe, the Eldest Haven, whose harbour burned in a day. The first masters to come down from the Keep laid its walls, and its harbour-houses were the richest in the land.

Word for word: *The Old Landing, the very oldest haven, whose landing burned in one day. The first masters went down from the Keep and laid the walls; and its harbour-houses were the richest in the Shore.*

- **The name is the haven's own**: *Tavow Hemm*, "the old landing"; its epithet is "the eldest haven", so the hearth hears *hemm* twice.
- ***sa re vorr o dhavow***, "whose harbour burned": the relative takes its noun back up as a possessor (G5); *o* shows its bite (*tavow* > *dhavow*), so it reads "its" (G9).
- ***ul Morrow***: *Orrow* "the Shore" bedded after *ul* (a vowel takes *m-* before a broad vowel, §3.3). A name mutates like any word; the analyzer asks for it.

**(ink II)**

> Cemm Hyll, ul vodh cemm et rhull et hyll. Re ston o um pa hless ul hos fedh, eth re lodhant sull heskal strom o um et nynth.

Tidesmeet, where the river meets the tide. It stood on both banks at once, and three great spans joined it across the water.

Word for word: *The Meeting of the Tide, where the river meets the tide. It stood on two banks at one time, and three great spans joined it across the water.*

- ***Cemm Hyll, ul vodh cemm …***: the epithet says the name's own verb. ***pa hless***: the dual (*pa* + S, *cless* > *hless*). ***sull heskal*** are Tamm Sull Heskal's three spans; ***lodh*** "join" is the mortar's verb.

**(ink I)**

> Sedhnell, et hinnar sa re yal gor fedh ul felmyl. Re ston o um yannath ul et sedh, eth re orr o dem vimm ryssyth yarl ul gor hossol, eth re hadh gor dhesk trenn clenn um et trenn hemm.

Fenholm, the town that was always sinking. It stood on pilings in the fen and went down a hand's breadth in every lifetime, and every generation laid a new course on the old.

Word for word: *Fen-Islet, the town that was always in sinking. It stood upon posts in the fen, and went down a hand's breadth in every lifetime, and every generation laid a new course upon the old course.*

- ***ul felmyl***: the progressive (§3.4). ***um yannath***: *gannath* "posts, pilings" softened after *um* (the same pilings Marl's Homecoming ends on). ***ul gor hossol***: *gor* stays plain after *ul*, as in the lexicon's *ul gor reskow* (O2630). ***gor dhesk***: *gor* softens *tesk*.

**(ink II)**

> Lurrvard, et kethow ul vimm et crisur lurr, ul lurr gunn et bithlern, crisurat sost virr ul molt et mesketh, hy ull re yal orrol ul neld lo orrard.

Holtward, the keep under the canopy, in the deep woods of the south, roofed so close among the trees that a traveller could pass it by.

Word for word: *Forest-Ward, the keep under the canopy, in the deep forest of the south, roofed very close among the trees, so that going past was at a traveller.*

- ***crisur lurr***, "the canopy", is "the roof of the forest", Aske Hrisur Lurr's epithet. ***re yal orrol … lo orrard***: "a traveller could go past", the "at" idiom of being able (§3.7). ***bithlern***, "the south", is "the hot side".
- The appositive (*crisurat …*) has no verb, as the English. With one (*re yal o crisurat*), *o* before an unbitten *c* is read by the analyzer as a possessive missing its bite.

**(ink I)**

> Kethow Stellath, kethow et stelleth, stannat ul et brunn ul molt ledalstelleth et stannardath hosast, eth bont gor orl hy o runnowath.

Carnhold, the stronghold of the cairns, cut into the mountain among the burial-cairns of the first quarrymen, and loud all day with its smithies.

Word for word: *The Hold of the Cairns, the hold of the cairns, quarried into the mountain among the burial-cairns of the first quarrymen, and loud every day from its smithies.*

- **The name said again as words**, as Kael's *Stannard Sorth, et stannard*: the English epithet is exactly the name's sense, and a Rivenman hears it twice. The name keeps the canon's broad *Stellath* (a harmony slip the analyzer knows); the word takes the rule's *stelleth*. The construct head takes no article (*kethow et stelleth*, never *et kethow et …*, §3.2).
- ***stannat … stannardath***: the quarryman's verb, and Stannard's own name as a word. ***o runnowath***: *drunnowath* softened (*dr-* > *r-*), as *Garvel Runnowath*.

**(ink II)**

> Tavow Vorr, et drunnow um et orrow sa vorr, ul vodh orr dhidh et brunn dem et wadh, eth doss et trun dhaedh ul vimm misk ul et grem.

Emberhythe, the forge on the burning shore, where the mountain smokes into the sea and the ground is warm underfoot in winter.

Word for word: *The Landing of Fire, the smithy on the shore that burns, where the smoke of the mountain goes to the sea, and the ground is warm under foot in the winter.*

- ***et orrow sa vorr*** is Harl Orrow sa Vorr's shore. ***ul et grem***: the article keeps *grem* out of the bedding (*ul nrem* would open with *nr-*, §2.5).

**(ink I)**

> Grullsorth, et kethow rytet ul hald et thykir, sa nath re yal galdat, veth paskat, saler ul vimm saler ul et hal unth.

Sandreach, the citadel carved in the canyon wall, which was not built so much as dug, gallery below gallery into the red rock.

Word for word: *Sand-Ridge, the keep carved in the wall of the canyon, that was not built, but dug, gallery under gallery in the red bedrock.*

- "Not built so much as dug" is ***nath … veth***, "not … but, rather" (§3.8). ***hal*** is the living rock, the rock of *Halvard*.

**(ink II)**

> Frennvar, et par um et lynth crentet; re ston et hellur strom um et crest lo dhrenn et grem, eth grellereth ul o riskereth lo dhrenn et domm sell.

Rimewatch, the watch on the frozen mere, whose great hall stood out on the ice all winter, with lamps in its windows through the long dark.

Word for word: *Frost-Guard, the watch upon the frozen mere; the great hall stood upon the ice through the winter, and lamps in its windows through the long dark.*

- ***par***, "a watch", is the *-var* of the name. "Whose great hall" is *et hellur strom*: after a verb, *o hellur* would read "it, hall" (G9). The last clause has no verb, as I.1's *eth so wresketh ul so simmeth*. ***lo dhrenn et grem***, "all winter", is "by the course of the winter" (added).

**(ink I)**

> Taldow Helv, taldow et tolmath sa haemerent, um et gynteth sost susk, hy ull re wemment et tolmardath et trenneth um wemmerdeth, eth re veskent et tolmath ul haemeryl um et helvhoss.

Highreach, the tower of the floating stones, on crags so high that the masons lifted their courses on cranes, and the stones seemed to hang upon the wind.

Word for word: *The High Place of the Sky, the tower of the stones that float, upon the crags, very high, so that the masons lifted the courses upon lifters, and the stones seemed in floating upon the wind.*

- The name and then the word again (*Taldow … taldow*), as Carnhold. ***bemmerd***, "a crane", is "a lifter"; the reserve *stendor* ends in the banned *-dor* (lexicon §8.1). ***um et gynteth***: *um* would soften *gynteth* to *yynteth*, which I.1 avoided; the article keeps it plain. "Their courses" is *et trenneth*: *et tolmardath so dhrenneth* would read "the masons of their courses".

**(ink II)**

> Eth Sirrvell, sa re yald dhom tesk Stonwrytan, sa re rik et rerd hosen preller hrest. Ew o et galdat dasken sa re yaldar, eth et deth sost; eth re yal veskyl sirr strom et pell lo farrardath hy farrol orl, eth re rig o et rerd dem neld ul et myst.

And Glasspire, raised by seven generations of Stonewrights, that caught the sun like a blade of ice. It was the last and proudest of our works, and its great glass could be seen from a day's sail out, throwing the sun far into the grey.

Word for word: *And Glass-Spire, that seven generations of the Stonewrights raised, that caught the sun like a blade of ice. It was the last work that we raised, and the proudest; and seeing the great glass of the spire was at sailors from a day's sailing, and it threw the sun far into the grey.*

- ***preller hrest***: *crest* softened as possessor (*cr-* > *hr-*, as *Hrenn*). ***re rik***, "caught": *grik* softened (the *g* drops before a consonant). ***re rig o***: *drig* "throw" softened (*dr-* > *r-*).
- "Could be seen" is the "at" idiom: sailors could see it. "Of our works" is "that we raised": *ol galdatath* would bed into *ol naldatath*.

**(ink I)**

> Noth hinnar, noth tavow, noth lonn sa re vollant so sost hos lunt.

Ten cities, ten harbours, ten kindreds who called themselves one nation.

Word for word: *Ten towns, ten harbours, ten kindreds who named themselves one people.* (Numerals before a singular noun, §3.1.)

**(the close)** *(the close: no ink of its own; the Legends builder sets the laid formula apart, outside the alternation · merge)*

> Cadhan o hosen re yal cadhat lona.

Word for word: *We two lay it as it was laid at us two.* Leaf-hand: `k.a.dh.A.n o h.o.s.e.n r.e g^S.a.l k.a.dh.A.t l.o.n.a |`

### Saed Pawast · So Ommol

**Part Two · Of Their Fall**

**(ink I)**

> Amm sy hunna hosen vodh re yalant et cummath mammat, eth nath veska dem neld, hy vodh nath re vesken dem neld ul et brodhol.

Hear now how the havens were lost, and do not look away, for we did not look away in the telling.

Word for word: *Now hear how the havens were lost, and do not look away, for we two did not look away in the telling.*

- ***nath veska***, "do not look": *nath* before the plural imperative, as I.1's *Nath hyrrila*. ***vesken***: the 1st dual; the tellers are Halyna (note 1). ***mammat***: I.1's *mamm* "lose".

**(ink II)**

> Nath re yal scethan stonat lo ol nedheth; nath re yal scethan yorrol; nath re yal ammadol pemessen. Nath re rarr bynd hy ul neld et wadh ul lern Orrow lo dhrenn so dhesketh. Re yalant haldath ol nedheth galdat dem dernil eth dem helv; nath re yalant galdat dem sevir; eth re yal gor yanna ryss, eth re ston lunnat ul vess.

Our fathers had no standing navy, no war fleet, no defensive doctrine. No enemy from beyond the sea had come against the Shorelands in all their generations. Their walls were built for ceremony and weather, not for bombardment, and their gates were wide, and stood open at night.

Word for word: *There was no standing fleet at our fathers; there was no fleet of war; there was no teaching of defence. No foe came from beyond the sea against the Shore through their generations. The walls of our fathers were built for rite and for weather; they were not built for bombardment; and every gate was wide, and stood open in the night.*

- The English's three "no" are three ***nath re yal***, the negative past (G1) of the "at" idiom of having.
- ***bynd***, "a foe, one who comes against", carries "against"; ***re rarr***: *darr* "come" softened, as IV.4's *re rarr o*.
- "Their walls" is *haldath ol nedheth*, "the walls of our fathers": *so haldath* after a verb would read "they, walls" (G9). ***gor yanna***, "every gate": *ganna* softened after *gor*, the generic singular, as *gor hald hyont* in Part One. *(Back-translation fix.)* The first draft had *so yannath*, "their gates", meant as the plural of *ganna*; but that plural is homophonous with the lexicon's *gannath*, "posts; a palisade" (O0671), the very word this leaf uses for Fenholm's pilings, and the blind reader could reach "gates" only against the lexicon (a strict reading gives "their palisades were wide, and stood open at night"). Sibling units S03, S04 and S07 avoid the plural of *ganna* for the same reason. ***dem dernil***: *ternil* bedded after *dem*.

**(ink I)**

> Re orrant et wemmeth domm hosast hy et myst, eth nath re yal peltir. Ul et dhess re ommant um Dhavow Hemm, eth dem rerdsenn ew vorrdhuss o. Re heth et hinnar ul sestow et tavow sull bress. Nath re lusk hos cumm dem mullen dem renn, hy vodh nath re yal renn lo hos dem luskol. Hy ull re omm Cemm Hyll; eth re nend et lunt sa re yal um et orrol susk ul o susk et sull heskal ul mommol dem vimm ul et rhull, hos eth hos, eth re hunn so ul morrol. Re felm Sedhnell tunn ul hos fedh, hosen amm nath re yal et sedh veth ul dovvol. Re vorr Lurrvard ul o vesketh, eth Kethow Stellath ul o runnowath. Re heth Tavow Vorr lesk bress um et orrow sa vorr. Re yal Grullsorth griket saler eth saler, eth Frennvar um et crest, eth Taldow Helv um et gynteth. Eth re omm Sirrvell, sa re yald dhom tesk, ul hos vess ul o nyrulath sost; eth doss et domm ul o vimm lunnat tul.

The first dark sails came out of the grey without warning. At dawn they fell upon the harbour of Eldhythe, and by sunset it was ash. The city behind the harbour held three months. No haven sent to another for help, for none had any help to send. Then Tidesmeet fell, and the people on the high road above it watched its three spans go down into the river one after another, and heard them go. Fenholm went under all at once, as if the fen had only been waiting. Holtward burned in its trees and Carnhold in its smithies. Emberhythe held five months on its burning shore. Sandreach was taken gallery by gallery, and Rimewatch on its ice, and Highreach on its crags. And Glasspire, that seven generations had raised, collapsed in a single night into its own cellars, and the dark beneath it lies open still.

Word for word: *The first dark sails went out of the grey, and there was no warning. At first light they fell upon the Old Landing, and by sunset it was ash. The town behind the landing held three months. Not one haven sent to another for help, for there was no help at anyone for sending. Then the Meeting of the Tide fell; and the people who were on the high road above it watched the three spans in falling down into the river, one and one, and heard them in going. Fen-Islet sank whole at one time, as if the fen had not been but waiting. Forest-Ward burned in its trees, and the Hold of the Cairns in its smithies. The Landing of Fire held five months on the shore that burns. Sand-Ridge was taken gallery and gallery, and Frost-Guard upon the ice, and the High Place of the Sky upon the crags. And Glass-Spire, that seven generations raised, fell in one night into its very cellars; and the dark under it is open still.*

- **"The harbour of Eldhythe" is the name itself**: *Tavow Hemm* is "the old landing", softened after *um* (*Dhavow Hemm*). "The city behind the harbour" is then plainly *et hinnar ul sestow et tavow*.
- ***wemmeth domm***, "the dark sails", beside the wall's *wemmeth myst*, "the grey sails" (added).
- ***dem mullen***: *ullen* bedded after *dem*. ***lusk*** is "send (off)", the verb of *luskat* "an envoy" and *luskard* "a sender" in the next paragraph.
- ***hos eth hos***, "one after another": I.1's "one by one". ***ul mommol***, ***ul morrol***: the progressive, *ommol*, *orrol* bedded.
- "As if the fen had only been waiting" is ***hosen amm*** (I.1) with ***nath … veth*** (G8).
- The fall is told in the order of the raising, as the English, and with the plainest verbs: *omm* "fall", *felm* "sink", *vorr* "burn", *keth* "hold", *grik* "take". The gapped clauses keep the verb once, as the English does (*…, eth Kethow Stellath ul o runnowath*). ***ul o vesketh***: *mesketh* softened after *o*.

**(ink II)**

> Nath re vellor luskat sa re hyrril hos. Nath re yal veth brammol helv scid, brodhen tevet ul lurrath hemm, sa re runn eth re runn hy et myst; vollant et hesperdeth um ol hald o: et drunnatath. Eth re ston nahos um et pressireth. Re veskent ol nedheth dem grenn um et scetheth ull, eth re veskent nahos, crenn ell cryrull; eth re vollant o: scethan dhenn. Nath re seskent cedh re lusk o; eth hy vodh re orr o hy et myst sa re vollant et farrardath: Mystow, re vollant ol nedheth et luskardath: Mystaeri, et mystan. Garlar et pa voll tul.

No envoy came that anyone could understand. There was only the harsh thunder of a tongue born in ancient forests, knocking and knocking out of the grey; the soldiers on our wall call it the drums. And no one stood upon the decks. Our fathers looked for a captain on those ships and saw none, nor any crew, and they called it the ghost fleet. Who had sent it they did not know; and because it came out of the fog the sailors called the Mystlands, they named the senders the Mystaeri, the mist-folk. We use both names still.

Word for word: *No envoy came in whom anyone understood. There was nothing but a harsh thunder, a tongue born in old forests, that knocked and knocked out of the grey; the soldiers on our wall name it: the drums. And no one stood upon the decks. Our fathers looked for a captain upon those ships, and saw no one, captain or crew; and they named it: the ghost fleet. They did not know who sent it; and because it went out of the grey that the sailors named: the Mystlands, our fathers named the senders: Mystaeri, the mist-folk. We use the two names still.*

- ***brammol helv***, "thunder", is "the roaring of the sky", and *bramm* is the gun's word: a Rivenman hears guns in that tongue. It is set in apposition (*brodhen tevet …*, "a tongue born …"), so no chain of possessors piles up.
- ***re runn eth re runn***: *drunn* "knock, strike" softened (*dr-* > *r-*); ***et drunnatath***, "the drums", are "the struck things", of the same verb (added).
- ***Eth re ston nahos***, "and no one stood": the shape of the wood's closing formula, *Eth re dhess nahos*.
- **The naming colon** (note 4): *vollant … o: et drunnatath*, *re vollant o: scethan dhenn*, *et farrardath: Mystow*, *et luskardath: Mystaeri*.
- ***Mystow*** is the canon original behind "the Mystlands" (§4.7): kept. ***Mystaeri*** is the hybrid exonym, kept; ***et mystan***, "the mist-folk", is the lexicon's gloss of it.
- "Could understand" is said plainly (*sa re hyrril hos*, "whom anyone understood"): the "at" idiom with an object gap inside a verbal noun would not read.
- The *we* of *ol hald* and *garlar* is the people's plural; Halyna's own *we* is dual (note 1).

**(ink I)**

> Re yalant et wemmeth sa re rik et cummath nestorn hy et wemmeth sa orrant ul ol lern amm sy. Re orr gor wemm hosel, eth nath re yal maver ullen umont, hy vodh nath re yal galat sa re hess so.

The sails that took the havens were plainer than the sails that come against us now. They came each alone, and they needed no more, for there was nothing to stop them.

Word for word: *The sails that took the havens were humbler than the sails that come against us now. Every sail came alone, and no need of another was upon them, for there was no thing that stopped them.*

- ***nestorn***, "humble, low-set", for "plainer": *venn* is "plain" only as in "tell it plain" (lexicon O2715, after the I.1 back-translation). ***hy*** is "than" after a quality (§3.6).
- ***maver … umont***: the "upon" idiom of need (O0488). ***hess***, "stop", is the Guest's first word.

**(ink II)**

> Nath re yorr et Stonwrytan. Re orr, eth nayalat ullen; eth re yalar ul so volt.

The Stonewrights did not fight. They simply walked; and we were among them.

Word for word: *The Stonewrights did not fight. They walked, and nothing else; and we were among them.*

- ***re yorr*** is the Naming's verb (*Re yorr et Ketherd*, §11.11), turned by *nath*: the Creed kept. The guild is a collective and takes the 3rd singular (G2).
- "Simply" is "and nothing else": *hosel* after a verb reads "alone" (G8). ***ul so volt***, "among them": *ul molt* with the possessive between, *molt* softened.

**(ink I)**

> Doss hos galat ullen brodhat um vess Sirrvell, eth brodhan o hosen re yal brodhat lona. Myskent merr sa re hollarant hy et pell: Re yal dhenn vesket ul et myst ul vess ull, strom hy gor sceth, ul stonol ul neld et tavow, somm re orr et pell dem vimm. Re veskent ullen sa re yalant lo ull nayalat. Nath sesken.

One thing more is told of Glasspire's night, and we tell it as it was told to us. Some who fled the spire say a shadow was seen in the fog that night, greater than any hull, standing off beyond the harbour while the spire went down. Others who were there saw nothing. We do not know.

Word for word: *There is one other thing told of Glass-Spire's night, and we two tell it as it was told at us two. Some who fled from the spire say: A shadow was seen in the grey that night, greater than any hull, in standing beyond the landing, while the spire went down. Others who were there saw nothing. We two do not know.*

- ***brodhan … brodhat***: "tell" here is *brodh*, not *cadh*. The English says "tell", not "lay", and the line is not the formula.
- The report is quoted, as I.1 quotes the cries (Orrowen has no "that" before a clause). ***Myskent*** stands at the head of its sentence: after a bedding word *mysk* would be heard as *bysk* (I.1 note 3).
- ***Nath sesken***: the answer said with the verb (§3.4), in the dual.

**Seren's added line** *(in her own single ink; the Book's comment: it ripens when the Glasspire entry of III.2 unlocks)*

> *Cadhat ul narl Seren, ul sestow wrodhol et mesk:* Nath gemment et trenneth. Re hethym pana.

*Added in Seren's hand, after the wood was told: The tales do not agree. I have kept both.*

Word for word: *Laid in Seren's hand, after the telling of the wood: The tales do not meet. I kept both.*

- ***nath gemm***, "disagree", is "do not meet" (O1680), plural here. ***ul narl Seren***: *garl* bedded after *ul*. ***wrodhol***: *brodhol* softened as the possessor of *sestow*. The line is the foreword's ("Where the tales do not agree, I have kept both"), so it is added as a phrase row for the foreword's translator to take or overrule.

### Mymmyleth

**The Homecomings**

> *Cadhat ul vimm vollath et cummath ul et saed hosast, hos eth hos, ul et surrath sa re vymment et cummath dem varn, lo wrodath vennuld et cumm, sa re yal lo ull.*

*Added under each haven's name in Part One, one by one, in the years the havens came home, in the words of the True Man of each haven, who was there.*

Word for word: *Laid under the names of the havens in the first half, one by one, in the years that the havens came home, with the words of the True Man of the haven, who was there.*

- ***Mymmyleth***, "the Homecomings", is *mymmyl* made plural as the lexicon's *Hethyleth*, "the Knowings" (added). Its verb, *mymm* "come back", is the verb of the captions (*re vymment … dem varn*, as V.3's title *Kaelir sa re vymm dem varn*).
- ***ul et surrath sa …***, "in the years that …", is the shape of I.1's *ul et vess hosast sa re vess et odh sy trenn*.
- ***lo wrodath***, "with the words": after *ul*, *brodath* would bed into *mrodath* (§2.5), and *et* cannot stand before the head of a construct. "Each haven" is the generic singular, *et cumm*.
- **One voice each.** The ten captions are the True Men's words, not Halyna's; Seren writes them in her own ink. Each True Man is named by his I.1 epithet, exactly.

> **Tavow Hemm.** *Corlen Dhavow:* Re orrar dem vimm dem et tavow ul et dhess, eth re felm Molt Hyll ul et wadh sa re rarr o hyo, eth nath re dhald o. Stonant haldath hosast et cummath pawast.

**Eldhythe.** *Corlen of the harbour:* We came down to the harbour at first light, and the Tide-Heart sank into the sea it came from, and did not rise. The first walls of the havens stand again.

Word for word: *We went down to the harbour at first light, and the Heart of the Tide sank into the sea that it came from, and it did not rise. The first walls of the havens stand a second time.*

- ***sa re rarr o hyo***, "that it came from": the preposition stays behind with its ending (G5). ***pawast***, "again", is "a second time".

> **Cemm Hyll.** *Tamm Sull Heskal:* Re runnar et sull bisk ul et hos kethyl, eth re vymm nahos; eth re orr Lymmerd Yendeth dem vimm ul vodh re ston et heskal ul molt et sull.

**Tidesmeet.** *Tamm of the three spans:* We struck all three heads in the one holding, and none grew back, and the Bearer of Branches went down where the middle span had stood.

Word for word: *We struck the three heads in the one holding, and none came back; and the Bearer of Branches went down where the span in the middle of the three had stood.*

- ***kethyl***, "holding", is the Captain's third call, *Keth*. "Grew back" is "came back", ***mymm***, the Homecomings' own verb: *tev* "grow" would be heard first as "a child". ***Lymmerd Yendeth***, "the bearer of shoots from a stump" (added).

> **Sedhnell.** *Marl Sedhen:* Re orr o ul et sedh hosen mesk lilvet, eth re orr et lilvyl hy ol lodh ul et clem sost. Firr Meskdhrenn sa Lilv ul vodh re yalant et gannath.

**Fenholm.** *Marl the fen-born:* It went into the fen like a log gone soft, and the rot went out of our mortar the same hour. The Grain That Rots lies where the pilings were.

Word for word: *It went into the fen like a rotted log, and the rot went out of our mortar in the very hour. The Grain That Rots lies where the posts were.*

- ***lilv*** three times (*lilvet*, *lilvyl*, *sa lilv*): the soft, the rot, the one that rots. ***et clem sost***, "the same hour", is "the very hour".

> **Lurrvard.** *Aske Hrisur Lurr:* Re ston Molt Veskrivull sol ul et dask ul molt et mesketh, eth re hadh o o veskrivullath, eth nath re dhald pawast. Re luskar o ul stonol. Re vymm o dem varn.

**Holtward.** *Aske of the canopy:* The Root-Heart stood still at last among the trees, and put down its roots, and would not rise again. We left it standing. It had come home.

Word for word: *The Root-Heart stood still in the end among the trees, and it laid its roots, and did not rise a second time. We let it be, in standing. It came home.*

- ***re hadh o o veskrivullath***, "it laid its roots": subject and possessive, as IV.4's *re heth o o*. "Would not rise again" is said plainly, with no *o* before *pawast* (*o pawast* would read "its second"). ***Re vymm o dem varn***: the Homecomings' verb again.

> **Kethow Stellath.** *Garvel Runnowath:* Re hresk Meskdhrenn Dholm ul vodh re ston o, tolm ul dolm, eth re rik et stelleth o. Doss et drunnowath velv.

**Carnhold.** *Garvel of the smithies:* The Stone-Grain crumbled where it stood, stone into stone, and the cairns took it. The smithies are quiet.

Word for word: *The Stone-Grain broke where it stood, stone into stone, and the cairns took it. The smithies are quiet.*

- ***tolm ul dolm***, "stone into stone", answers the Cry's *tolm um dholm*, "stone upon stone". ***cresk*** is "break; crumble".

> **Tavow Vorr.** *Harl Orrow sa Vorr:* Re senn Vorrol um et orrow hosen drunnow navothat, eth re esk et sast domm umo. Doss et trun lo ull dhaedh tul.

**Emberhythe.** *Harl of the burning shore:* The Burning burned out on the shore like a forge left untended, and the black crust closed over it. The ground there is warm still.

Word for word: *The Burning went out on the shore like an untended forge, and the black crust closed upon it. The ground there is warm still.*

- ***senn***, "go out, as a fire in peace", is the verb of I.1's dead hearth. The last line answers Part One's *doss et trun dhaedh*.

> **Grullsorth.** *Ulden Salereth:* Re hresk Gunn ul vimm dem susk hy et grull ul vimm et kethow rerddhalden, eth re hresk o; eth re wont et thykir hyo hos clem ul sestow.

**Sandreach.** *Ulden of the galleries:* The Deep-Beneath burst up out of the sand under the east keep, and broke, and the canyon rang with it for an hour after.

Word for word: *The Deep-Beneath broke up out of the sand under the eastern keep, and it broke; and the canyon rang from it one hour after.*

- ***cresk*** is "burst" and "break" both: the soldier says the one word twice. ***re wont***: *bont* "ring, resound" softened.

> **Frennvar.** *Hesk Lynth:* Re dhald et sulv ul sorenn, eth re firr Grem Sell um et lynth, eth re rik et crest o dem vimm lo dhrenn et reskow ul vodh re ston et hellur strom.

**Rimewatch.** *Hesk of the mere:* The white lifted at noon, and the Long Winter lay out on the mere, and the ice took it down through the place where the great hall stood.

Word for word: *The white rose at noon, and the Long Winter lay down upon the mere, and the ice took it down through the place where the great hall stood.*

> **Taldow Helv.** *Lanner Gynten:* Re orr Lorr Helv dem vimm hy o henth um et gynteth, eth nath re orr o dem susk pawast.

**Highreach.** *Lanner of the crags:* The Blood of the Sky came down out of its cloud onto the crags, and did not go up again.

Word for word: *The Blood of the Sky went down out of its cloud onto the crags, and it did not go up a second time.*

- ***Lorr Helv*** chimes with *Lodh Helv*, the Aetherbond (added).

> **Sirrvell.** *Pellow Vell:* Ul nomm et nyrulath re hresk Lennet hosen vesksirr, eth re vesker ul gor saedel hyo et grellereth sa re lymmer ol sost.

**Glasspire.** *Pellow of the spire:* In the dark of the cellars the Silvered broke like a mirror, and every piece of it showed us our own lamps.

Word for word: *In the dark of the cellars the Silvered broke like a see-glass, and we saw in every piece of it the lamps that we ourselves brought.*

- The tongue's "show" (*lestir*) is "show the way, guide", so "showed us" is "we saw". "Our own lamps" is "the lamps we ourselves brought": *ol grellereth* would bed into *nrellereth* (§2.5). ***vesksirr***, "a mirror", is "a see-glass". *Sirrvell* and *Pellow Vell*: the spire twice.

## NOTES FOR JACK AND FOR THE ASSEMBLER

### 1. Halyna's hearth close is dual

`orrowen_v2` §3.9: "Where the Book gives Halyna a plural verb, the Orrowen original is dual"; §12: "Halyna are always two". The lexicon has the close in the 1st singular (O0238, Kael, reused by pilot I.1) and the 1st plural (O0234, *Cadhar o hosen re yal cadhat lor*). For Halyna this leaf uses the same frame in the dual, ***Cadhan o hosen re yal cadhat lona*** (added, S05-06). **For the assembler:** every Halyna leaf that closes "This we lay as it was laid for us" (I.4, II.1, II.3, III.1 twice, IV.1, IV.3, V.1, VI.1) should use the dual. V.7, which Seren lays with Halyna (three voices), keeps the plural O0234. If another unit used O0234 for Halyna, the two should be reconciled. Inside the telling, Halyna's *we* is dual when they mean themselves as the tellers (*nath re vesken*, *brodhan … lona*, *Nath sesken*), and plural when they mean the people (*ol nedheth*, *re yaldar*, *re yalar*, *garlar*, *ol hald*).

### 2. The Thrones in the Homecomings [Jack]

The Homecomings name the Thrones by the names the Thrones gave themselves, in Seren's English ("the Tide-Heart", "the Bearer of Branches" …; III.2: "from this leaf on I write the Thrones by the names they gave themselves"). The True Men spoke Orrowen, so the Orrowen gives those names in the wall's words:
- four were already in the lexicon: *Molt Hyll*, *Molt Veskrivull*, *Meskdhrenn Dholm*, *Gunn ul vimm*;
- six are added: *Lymmerd Yendeth* (the Bearer of Branches), *Meskdhrenn sa Lilv* (the Grain That Rots), *Vorrol* (the Burning), *Grem Sell* (the Long Winter), *Lorr Helv* (the Blood of the Sky), *Lennet* (the Silvered).

The Thrones' own sounds (*Thaesaen … Neivaere*, the lexicon's loans) belong to III.2, where the wood gives them. If Jack would rather the True Men say the loans, swap the ten names and nothing else moves.

### 3. The havens by their own names

The Book's English haven-names are translations, and `orrowen_v2` §4.7 gives each its Orrowen original, so the leaf names every haven by its original, with the epithet after it. Two epithets are the name's own sense: "Carnhold, the stronghold of the cairns"; Highreach, "the tower of …". Where that happens the Orrowen says the name and then says it again as words (*Kethow Stellath, kethow et stelleth*; *Taldow Helv, taldow et tolmath …*). It is the chime of Kael's *Stannard Sorth, et stannard*. "The harbour of Eldhythe" in Part Two is the name alone (*um Dhavow Hemm*, "upon the Old Landing").

### 5. Collisions the leaf steps around

- **The analyzer reads *o* or *so* before an unbitten mutable letter as a possessive missing its bite** (the reverse of G9). So *re yal o crisurat* became an appositive; *nath re dhald o pawast* lost its *o*; *nath re veskent so crenn* became *re veskent nahos, crenn ell cryrull*; *vollant … o drunnatath* took the naming colon.
- ***mr-*, *nr-***: see S4. ***dhess*** "dawn", which the lexicon flags as sounding like the softened *tess* "answered", is used only after *et* (*ul et dhess*), where *tess* could not be softened. ***Myskent*** heads its sentence (no bedding word before *mysk*). ***stendor*** (crane, banned *-dor*) is avoided for *bemmerd*. ***reskow cumm*** would soften to *reskow humm* ("husband"): avoided. ***ol galdatath*** would bed into *ol naldatath*: said as *sa re yaldar*. ***tev*** "grow" (first read "a child"): *mymm* "come back" instead. ***gannath***: the plural of *ganna* "gate" would sound as the lexicon's *gannath* "posts; a palisade", so "their gates" is *gor yanna*, "every gate" (back-translation fix; `wf8/backtrans/S05.md`).
