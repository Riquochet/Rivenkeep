## 1 · SEREN'S FRAME, IN ORROWEN (THE LEAF-HAND, HER ONE INK)

### 1.1 The title

> **Dannwresk ul Meskdhrenn**

| Dannwresk | ul | Meskdhrenn |
|---|---|---|
| axe | in(+N) | grain |

*The Axe in the Grain.* (The lexicon's title, O0360. *Dannwresk* "an axe" is 'hew-chisel', *dann* + softened *bresk*; *meskdhrenn* "the grain" is 'the wood's course', *mesk* + softened *trenn*. The nasalising *ul* leaves *m* unchanged.)

### 1.2 The headnote

> **Hosen tum Mesk Myst: kethet hy volt vovennerd Hyll Pawast, hy et meskdhrenn sa vollar Dommwrint, tevet hy yend yann gostat; doss o ul lern dhrenn et gostol.**

as · remember · Mystwood: held (known) · from · S·heart · S·Standard-Bearer · Tide · Second, from · the · grain · REL · name-1PL · Blackthorn, grown · from · S·stump-shoot · S·pillar · felled; is · it · in(+N) · face · S·tale · the · felling.

*As the Mystwood remembers: known from the heart of a Standard-Bearer of the Second Tide, of the grain we call Blackthorn, grown from a shoot of a felled pillar's stump; it faces the tale of the felling.*

- **The opening and the facing clause are IV.4's formulas**, word for word: *Hosen tum Mesk Myst: kethet hy …* and *doss o ul lern …*.
- *hy volt vovennerd Hyll Pawast* is a construct chain, each possessor softened: *molt* "heart" after *hy*, *movennerd* "Standard-Bearer" as its possessor (*m* > *v*), and *Hyll Pawast*, the Second Tide, whose *h* does not change.
- *hy et meskdhrenn sa vollar Dommwrint*, "of the grain we call Blackthorn": "made of" is *hy* (lexicon §5.3); *voll* "call by name" takes 1pl *-ar*; *Dommwrint* is the lexicon's Blackthorn ('black-thorn').
- *tevet hy yend yann gostat*: *gend* is "a shoot from a stump" (reserve), so the stump is in the word and is not said twice; *gann* is "a pillar (of the wood)", IV.1's own word (*Gostol et Gannath Domm*), softened as possessor; *gostat* "felled" is an adjective and does not mutate.
- *ul lern dhrenn et gostol*, "facing the tale of the felling". The English names IV.1 by what it is, not by its title (IV.4's headnote did name IV.3), so Seren does the same. *Trenn* is "a tale laid in one night" (canon), softened as the possessor of *lern*.

> **Re rik Rhyna o um et dhysal amm re vellor o, eth re yal kethyl et lern hosel lo Halyna; stonant et lerneth ull hosast.**

PST · S·pick-up · Rhyna · it · upon · the · shingle · when · PST · S·come-in · it, and · PST · S·be · holding · the · shape · alone · at · Halyna; stand-3PL · the · shapes · those · first.

*Rhyna took it up on the shingle when it came in, and Halyna could hold only its shape; those shapes stand first.*

- *rik* is *grik* "pick up" softened after *re*, as in IV.4; *vellor* is *mellor* "come in, arrive" softened.
- **"Could hold" is the "at" idiom in the past**, *re yal* + verbal noun + *lo Y* (§3.7, lexicon §5.3): "holding the shape alone was at Halyna". The object of the verbal noun is its possessor, *kethyl et lern* (§3.7's *kethyl et tolm*).
- **"Only" of a noun is *hosel* after it**, "the shape alone". After a verb *hosel* reads "alone" of the doer (G8), so it is kept on the noun.
- *lern* is the lexicon's word for "shape" (also "face; front"), plural *lerneth* (slender). *Stonant et lerneth ull hosast*, "those shapes stand first": they stand on the leaf as stones stand in a course.
- ***Et lern hosel*, "the shape only", is a set phrase** (W03-01, fix F6). *Lern*'s first sense is "the face", and the blind back-translation read "Halyna could hold only its face". As the Book's own term (the Knowing ladder's "one shape", "two shapes"; the fragments' frame, "the shape only") it is now an entry, and the analyzer's set-phrase hint gives its sense in this line and in the label (§1.3).

> **Ul Hyll Dhumol re hadh Halvard garl umo ul et nelter, Rhyna lo et odh ul molt et tevath, eth re rarr o tunn ulonta ul hos hoss.**

in(+N) · Tide · S·Remembering · PST · S·lay · Halvard · hand · upon-it · in · the · vault, Rhyna · at · the · hearth · in(+N) · heart · the · children, and · PST · S·come · it · whole · in-them-two · in · one · breath.

*In the Tide of Remembering Halvard laid a hand on it in the vault, Rhyna at the hearth with the children, and it came whole to them both in a breath.*

- The time is set first, as I.1 fronts *Amm sy …*; *Hyll Dhumol* is IV.4's.
- **"Laid a hand on it" is *cadh garl umo***: Seren's own verb of laying (*cadh*), the conjugated *umo* "upon it".
- *Rhyna lo et odh* is a clause with no verb, laid beside the other, as I.1's *eth so wresketh ul so simmeth*. "With the children" is *ul molt et tevath*, "among the children" (the lexicon's *ul molt*; *tevath* is the canon's irregular plural).
- ***ulonta*, "into them two"**: the pair takes the dual (§3.9), as IV.4's *re rarr o uloy* ("it came into her") takes the feminine. *Ul hos hoss*, "in one breath".

> **Ew sull vess et brodhol, hy vodh re yal o ulonta hosen grestet, eth nath re yal hosen trenn.**

was · three · night · the · telling, from · what · PST · S·be · it · in-them-two · as · wound, and · NEG · PST · S·be · as · tale.

*The telling took three nights, for it was in them as a wound is, and not as a tale.*

- **The copula puts its predicate first**: *Ew sull vess et brodhol*, "three nights was the telling" (§3.4; a numeral before a singular, §3.1).
- *grestet* "a wound" is the word of the Book of the Wound (*Flennath et Grest*); "and not" is the negative past *nath re* (G1).
- ***trenn* is canon "a tale laid in one night"**. A telling that took three nights, and was in them as a wound is, was not a *trenn*: the tongue says Halyna's point with its own word.

> **Re wrodha Halyna o, hos eth ullen, ul ba luth; re hadh Seren o.**

PST · S·tell-3DU · Halyna · it, one · and · other, in · N·two · ink; PST · S·lay · Seren · it.

*Told by Halyna, by turns, in two inks; set down by Seren.*

- IV.4's formula, word for word: the dual *wrodha*, *hos eth ullen* (O2858), *ul ba luth*, and Seren laying the telling. *Halyna* carries its sealing lintel in the leaf-hand; *Seren* has none.

> **Amm re yal et mesk cresket, ell re omm et brodhol, doss o rellorat eth nahlennet; eth ul vodh nath gemment o eth et scaevureleth sa hether, re hethym pana, hy vodh nadhumant et scaevureleth, eth doss cadhol nycul ulont lo yarl; eth nath noss nyculol lo et mesk, veth nath noss veskyl ludal lo.**

when · PST · S·be · the · wood · broken, or · PST · fail · the · telling, is · it · marked · and · unmended; and · in(+N) · what · NEG · N·meet-3PL · it · and · the · rolls · REL · S·keep-1PL, PST · S·keep-1SG · both, from · what · forget-3PL · the · rolls, and · is · laying · lie · in-them · at · S·hand; and · NEG · N·be · lying · at · the · wood, but · NEG · N·be · seeing · fear · at-it.

*Where the wood was broken, or the telling failed, it is marked, and not mended; and where it and our rolls disagree I have kept both, for the rolls forget and can be made to lie, and the wood cannot lie but cannot see fear.*

- **The first clause is IV.4's formula, word for word**; IV.2 goes on after the semicolon.
- **"Where" is *ul vodh***, the relative "in what" (G4), not the formula's *amm*: *amm nath* is "unless" (the analyzer's phrase hint), and would turn "where they disagree" into "unless they agree".
- **"Our rolls" is *et scaevureleth sa hether*, "the rolls that we keep"**: *ol* before an *s-* shows no bite and would read "we, rolls" (§3.5, G9), so the possession is said with the verb Seren then uses of herself, *keth* "keep": *re hethym pana*, "I kept both". *Scaevurel* is the lexicon's "a roll (a scroll)", plural *scaevureleth*; *nath gemm* is its "disagree" ('do not meet'), 3pl for the two subjects joined.
- **"Can be made to lie" is *doss cadhol nycul ulont lo yarl*, "a hand can lay a lie in them"**. Orrowen has no passive and no causative for it; it has the "at" idiom for *can* (§3.7) and the Shore's own verb for writing, *cadh*, "lay" (to write a line is to lay a course). The rolls lie because a hand lays the lie: the warden's letter of IV.3 is such a hand.
- "Cannot" is *nath noss* + verbal noun + *lo* (§3.7); *veskyl ludal*, "seeing fear", takes its object as its possessor.

## 3 · WHAT THE TRANSLATION DID, AND WHY

**The grain.**

1. **Every sentence is carved, once** (§10.11). One mark to a content word; a referent already cut is pointed at, never cut again. **Files are the pronouns**: *we* is file 12, *you* file 0, *the iron* file 4; eight runners end on a file referent (`@0`, `@4`, `@8`, `@11`, `@12`).
2. **The proof rings were reused and completed.** `coverage/tests/IV-2_proof.gn2` had rings 1, 6, 7, 8 (from "We believed") and 9. Their choices stand where they read true (the regrowth braid, the plea, the belief, the blind of place, `!GROW{last}`); six were changed on the evidence of the whole leaf: the "bank" likeness (it read *remember*); the braid's pattern (`aba`); the band orders (DARK over the bark, not the tall pillar); "it was enough" (GLAD, content, for the proof's TRUE, "so"); "it did not answer" (`!MOUTH →` our carving, for `→ @12`, whose cell the packing fills, A4); and ring 9's "the wood comes again" (our growing again, not the stumps' going). The proof's second pocket moved from files 8–9 to 9–10, to keep file 8 free for "carried it away" (`→ @8`).
3. **The heart stays plain** (§3, A9). Ring 1 carries no fringe and no pocket: "all we were for" is `LONE+HOLD`, "enough" is GLAD. The first fringe is in ring 2, the first pocket and the braid in the sapwood.
4. **The note's two marks are the grain's own**: the long ranks with a memory ray cut through them to the heart (ring 3) and the mouth, mirrored, tied back by a *then* runner across the ring-line to the carving of the plea (rings 7–8). The Second Tide's fragments are this round, partly glossed (§2.2).
5. **Two blinds, one of each kind**: the young of the long-lived weeping, and why the wood does not hold (`→bc ∅`, ring 5); the stones carried off, and whither the wood does not hold (`→ ∅`, A5, ring 8).
6. **Truth or nothing, in the conditional**: the pole that was never made is hollow; the likenesses are hollow except "as stumps will", which is the custom itself.
7. **Four memory rays**: *always* (r7), *never* (r10), and two that carry the axe and the ranks back to the heart, whose root is the axe (r3, r11).

**Findings for the spec, the register and the validator** (none blocks anything; all are in `wf8/additions/W03_grain.md` for merging).

- **A band lies over the whole of its file's year.** A band's year is its knowing's first mark's, and every mark on its files in that year takes it, including another knowing's marks that the packing puts in the same year's other cells. Two carvings had to be recut for it (the old men's white heads, the word across the water). **Proposed:** a validator note when a band covers a mark of another knowing.
- **A memory ray in a year of several cells.** `mem f: rK/Y` names no cell, so two drawings (the ray from cell 1 or from cell 2) share one canonical GN. **Proposed** (§6.4): the ray leaves the first cell's mark, and an author writes the remembered mark first. IV.2 follows it in all four rays.
- **The register's "bank" likeness** (`~ROOT+HOLD`) is the compound *remember*; **its "stump cut low"** has head and modifier reversed; **its "grain"** (CARVE) reads as a carving; **§18's braid** for "it came again; we put up more" should be `aba`. Each is corrected in the additions.
- **A4 at work.** Ring 2 is one cell wide, so "came near us" could not end in our free cell; it runs to our watching eye instead. The rule served, and the reading is the better for it.
- **The blind back-translation** (`wf8/backtrans/W03.md`) read the unit back from its Orrowen and GN alone. The frame scored 8 exact and 2 close (*lern* read "face" for "shape"); the grain 2 exact, 7 close and 4 drift (rings 1, 2, 5, 7), with one sentence wrong inside ring 7. Its fixes: F1 (the grey's file holds no thing-sign, so `→ @11` is the grey), F2 (`~LOSE` after a *then* runner for "until it is taken"), F3 (the young's own `!HOLD` beside the blind), F4 (`!TURN+HOLD` for "must not be mistaken", since `!~HOLD` draws as `~!HOLD`), F5 (the water band on file 13 alone) and F6 (the set phrase *et lern hosel*). After them, the frame re-reads 10 exact and the grain 2 exact and 11 close. The spec findings (A1 in a year, prefix order, the blind is the wood's, A16's belongings) are in `wf8/additions/W03_grain.md`.

**Orrowen.**

- **No new word was needed.** Every word of the frame is in the lexicon or made by its grammar (the plurals *lerneth* and *scaevureleth*, the conjugated *ulonta* and *ulont*, the dual *hethe*). `wf8/additions/W03.tsv` holds one set phrase, *et lern hosel* "the shape only" (W03-01), added after the blind back-translation so that *lern* reads as the Book's *shape* and not as its first sense, *face*.
- **"Where" as a relative is *ul vodh*** (G4) where the formula's *amm* would meet a negative: *amm nath* is "unless".
- **"Our" before a word that does not mutate is said with a verb**: *et scaevureleth sa hether*, "the rolls we keep" (G9).
- **"Can be made to lie" is "a hand can lay a lie in them"**: no passive, no causative; the "at" idiom and the Shore's own verb for writing.
- ***trenn* against *brodhol***: Seren's line turns on the canon's sense of *trenn*, "a tale laid in one night"; the telling that took three nights "was not as a *trenn*".
- **Labels made active** (*Hosen re hethe Halyna o hosast*): a passive *re yal o kethet* reads as a missing bite after *o*.
