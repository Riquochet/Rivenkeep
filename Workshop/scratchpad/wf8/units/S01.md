## 1 · OF THIS BOOK (*FLENN SEREN*)

### Title

> ## *Flenn Seren*

**OF THIS BOOK**

*The leaf of Seren.* The lexicon's own title for the foreword (O0616, `x_book_titles`).

### Seren's headnote

> *Re ryt Seren Pa Luth o, prassel Halyna eth sullast sona odh, ul lern et flennath sy, amm re yal saed et flennath losk tevar.*

*Written by Seren Two-Inks, apprentice to Halyna and the third of their hearth, at the front of this Book, when the Book was already half full.*

Word for word: *Seren Two-Inks wrote it, prentice of Halyna and the third of their hearth, at the front of this Book, when half the Book was full already.*

- ***Re ryt Seren Pa Luth o***: the headnote is active, as I.1's *Re hadh Crennel Kael* is. *ryt* "cut letters, write": the foreword is written, not laid (it is not a tale).
- ***sullast***, "the third", is the canon's own word for "the unbonded third of a hearth" (O2421); *sona* (their two, +S) because Halyna are two.

### Seren's foreword

> El sy Flennath Dholm Treskat. Keth o et trenneth sa hadhar lo Odh Sell ul Gethow Dhresk, ul et hellur ul sestow et ganna ulvenn, ul vimm et hald ul vodh dhenth et covv treskat. Cadhar so ul et clemeth ul sestow hiser, amm brident et brammath eth nath rhyn tul et lodh, eth doss nayalat tul lo et garlath veth so hethyl lo et vorr.

This is the Book of the Riven Stone. It holds the tales that are laid at the Long Hearth of Rivenkeep, in the hall behind the inner gate, under the wall where the torn cloak flies. They are laid in the hours after an assault, when the guns are cooling and the mortar has not yet set, and there is nothing left to do with the hands but hold them to the fire.

Word for word: *This is the Book of the Riven Stone. It holds the tales that we lay at the Long Hearth in Rivenkeep, in the hall behind the inner gate, under the wall where the torn cloak flies. We lay them in the hours after an assault, when the guns cool and the mortar does not yet set, and there is nothing still at the hands but their holding at the fire.*

- ***El sy Flennath Dholm Treskat***: the copula with the demonstrative, as I.1's *Ew ull hald nahresket …*; *Flennath Dholm Treskat* is the lexicon's title for the Book (x_book_titles).
- ***hadhar***, "we lay": the tales are laid by the hearth's *we*, and the passive English ("that are laid", "They are laid") becomes the hearth's own voice, as Orrowen prefers a doer.
- ***ul Gethow Dhresk***, "in Rivenkeep" (N·*Kethow*): the construct *Odh Sell Hethow Dhresk* would put the softened *hethow* on the page, which the analyzer reads as *heth* "spring" + *-ow* (lexicon §8.2); pilot I.1 stepped round it the same way.
- ***dhenth***, "flies" (the reserve *dhenth*, "a wing; fly; soar"): the cloak flies as a bird does.
- ***brident et brammath***, "the guns cool": *ul mridyl* ("in cooling") would begin *mr-*, which §2.5 does not allow; the plain present serves, here and in the Invocation.
- ***so hethyl***, "their holding": the object of a verbal noun is its possessor (IV.4's *o wrodhol*), so "to hold them" is "their holding".

> Nath re yaldar et ternil. Re yaldant galdardath hosast et Kethow o, eth re nadhumar o, eth re wesker et ternil pawast ul vimmow vestul ul et nelter gunn sost, hosen es veskys. Amm re yal so dholm cadhat eth so yarleleth midhet, re reskent et galdardath hosast lo et odh sy, eth gor vess re hadh hos hyont trenn. Galdar hosen re yaldant. Doss et hald ul daldol pawast, eth doss et flennath sy hosen ull.

We did not make the custom. The first builders of the Keep made it, and we forgot it, and found it again at the bottom of a chest in the deepest vault, as you will read. When their stone was set and their tools were cleaned, the first builders sat at this hearth, and each night one of them laid a tale. We do as they did. Our wall is rising again, and so is this Book.

Word for word: *We did not make the custom. The first builders of the Keep made it, and we forgot it, and we found the custom again in the bottom of a chest in the deepest vault, as you will read. When their stone was laid and their tools cleaned, the first builders sat at this hearth, and every night one of them laid a tale. We do as they did. The wall is rising again, and this Book is like that.*

- *gald* is "build; do, make" (O0655): the custom was *made* (*Nath re yaldar*), and ***Galdar hosen re yaldant*** is "we do as they did", in the word that also builds walls.
- ***re wesker et ternil pawast***: "found it again" names the custom. *re wesker o pawast* would put the object pronoun *o* before *pawast*, where the analyzer (and a reader) takes *o* as "its" and asks for *vawast*; the noun is clearer.
- ***Doss et hald***, not *Doss ol hald*: after a verb, *ol* before an *h* shows no bite and reads as "we" (`orrowen_v2` §3.5, G9). The Keep's wall is simply *the* wall.
- ***ul daldol***, "in rising": the progressive of §3.4, as *Doss et hald ul glennol*.

> Lo sest et odh, ul baltur hald odh, sol hosk hemm et ganna ulvenn. Re omm o ul et surrath nadhumat, eth re sol o ul et ganna dem et orl sa re hyle Halyna o. El Hemm eth Hemma et Stonwrytan ul et Kethow sy Halyna. Doss sona ul molt Lodhan Helv, sa nath voll et Stonwrytan veth et Lodhan; eth kethe sona hos voll, Halyna; hos eth hos, el Halvard eth Rhyna sona. Re stannant gedheth Halvard et tolm ull, eth re hadh vadullan Hyna o, eth doss Stonwryt et galdardath hosast gand tul um o vimmlern. Brodh gor sa heth o. Amm doss et trenn tunnat, cadh et brodhard o ul o valtur pawast, eth mysker: Doss et trenn cadhat; hy vodh trenn sa nath gadhar veth brodhar, doss o sennet dem et ryst, eth trenn sa hadhar, el saed et hald o.

Beside the hearth, in a niche of the hearth-wall, lies the old capstone of the inner gate. It fell in the forgotten years and lay in the gateway until Halyna turned it over. Halyna are the elders of the guild at this Keep. They are of the Aetherbonded, whom the guild calls simply the Bonded, and Halyna is the one name the two of them share; singly they are Halvard and Rhyna. Halvard's fathers cut that stone and Rhyna's forebears laid it, and the Stonwryt of the first builders is still sharp on its underside. Whoever holds it tells. When the tale is done the teller sets it back in its niche, and we say that the tale is *laid*; for a tale that is only told is gone by morning, and a tale that is laid is part of the wall.

Word for word: *At the shoulder of the hearth, in a niche of the hearth-wall, rests the old capstone of the inner gate. It fell in the forgotten years and rested in the gate until the day that Halyna turned it. The Elder and the Elderess of the guild at this Keep are Halyna. They two are among the Aetherbonded, whom the guild calls nothing but the Bonded; and they two keep one name, Halyna; one by one, they are Halvard and Rhyna. Halvard's fathers quarried that stone, and Rhyna's forebears laid it, and the Stonwryt of the first builders is sharp still on its underside. Whoever holds it tells. When the tale is finished, the teller lays it in its niche again, and we say: The tale is laid; for a tale that we do not lay but only tell, it is gone out by the morning, and a tale that we lay, it is part of the wall.*

- ***dem et orl sa re hyle Halyna o***, "until the day that Halyna turned it": *dem amm* ("until when") would nasalise to *dem mamm*, a reserve word (pilot I.1, §3); a day stands in the place of the clause, as I.1's *ul et vess hosast sa …*.
- ***Hemm eth Hemma***, "the Elder and the Elderess" (an addition): the canon's one rank in its two forms. *pa Hemm* "two Elders" would also be heard as *pa hemm*, S·*cemm*, "two meetings".
- ***Lodhan Helv***, "the Aetherbonded" (an addition): the lexicon gives *et Lodhan* for both "the Bonded" and "the Aetherbonded", and the sentence turns on the two being different names. *Lodhan Helv*, "the mortar-folk of the sky", is built as the canon's *Lodh Helv*, "the Aetherbond". ***sa nath voll et Stonwrytan veth et Lodhan*** is "whom the guild calls nothing but the Bonded", which is "simply" (`orrowen_v2` §3.8, G8).
- ***vadullan Hyna***, "Rhyna's forebears": a possessor is softened, and a name is no exception (the canon's *lest Vardh*, *Kethow Dhresk*), so *Rhyna* is *Hyna* here (*rh* > *h*). **[Jack]** if a name should keep its face as a possessor; I.2 says the same thing with the "at" idiom (*re yal vadullan lodhat loy*, "there were bonded forebears at her"), which leaves *Rhyna* whole. *Halvard* does not change (*h*).
- ***re stannant***: the fathers *quarried* the stone (*stann*, the canon's word for cutting stone from the bed, and the root of *Stannard*).
- ***Brodh gor sa heth o***, "Whoever holds it tells" (an addition, as a formula): the Invocation says it again in the same words. *brodh*, "tell", is kept apart from *cadh*, "lay", because this paragraph turns on the difference: *trenn sa nath gadhar veth brodhar*, "a tale we only tell" (*nath … veth*, "only", G8), goes out like a fire (*sennet*, the canon's *senn*, "go out, as a fire in peace"); *trenn sa hadhar*, "a tale we lay", is *saed et hald*, part of the wall.
- ***mysker: Doss et trenn cadhat***: Orrowen has no "that" before a clause; what is said is given as it is said (pilot I.1, the cries).

**The Stonwryt on the tale-stone's underside** (the `legend_stonwryt_hal` surface). It is drawn as the first builders cut it, in the Hal and in *Garl Hal*, right to left, mirrored, one unbroken course, and signed **ALD | BEN** under one lintel: `orrowen_v2` §11.6 (a), canon, unchanged.

> **ESAN STONOS ETAS XALDOS SIU · RE CADHANA OA ETHAS TESSANA OA SOŊMAS ELIS TOLMOS TOLMOS · STONOS** ⟨gate⟩ ⟨**ALD | BEN** under one lintel⟩

*This wall will stand. We two laid it, and we two answer for it, while stone is stone. It stands.* It is signed under one lintel with the pair-name of *Aldwena*, who laid the capstone.

As Halyna say it in the living tongue (§11.6 (b), canon; what the fold's reading would be in Seren's ink, if the Original tab gives one):

> *Es ston et hald sy. Re hadhan o, eth tessen o, somm el tolm tolm. Ston.*

- The caption and the Translation fold are the reader's furniture, in English, as in pilot I.1's Title block; the surface itself is the Hal. The Hal line passes the analyzer's Hal reading (`--hal`) word for word.

> Amm cadhat ol drenn, tess et odh o, olen: Tumar. Darr et tessyl hy wrodath hosast Hosk et Crenn, eth el et galat hosel lo et odh sy sa vysker hosen gor fedh.

When a tale of ours is laid, the hearth answers it, all together: *We remember.* The answer comes from the first words of the Captain's Title, and it is the only thing at this hearth that is always said the same way.

Word for word: *When our tale is laid, the hearth answers it, together: We remember. The answer comes from the first words of the Captain's Title, and it is the only thing at this hearth that we say alike every time.*

- ***Amm cadhat ol drenn***: the verbless "when [it is] laid" of the Invocation's canon close, *Amm cadhat o*.
- ***Hosk et Crenn***, "the Captain's Title": *et Hosk* is the Title (O0994), and the Captain is its possessor.

> Nath noss gor dhrenn ul et flennath sy lor. Doss merr lo et mesk. Darrant hy et kaelirvesk sa lymm et hyll ul vimm ol hald, eth hy et tolm domm sa sol ul et vestul sa re lymmym dem susk et brunn, eth hy et mesk ullen. Nath vesker et mesk, hy vodh nath noss brod ulo. Femme Halyna o, eth kethe sona o dhrenn tunn, hosen kether tumol: o voskol, eth o volt, eth o rask, ul hos fedh. El loskat femmyl hos hyonta, hy vodh galat sa heth hos, keth ullen o. Nel et galat duss et kethyl. El et brodhol. Doss kylyl lo wrodath, eth nath noss kylyl lo hethyl; hy ull brodha sona o ul et brodath merr sost eth venn sost sa weske sona, eth cadha sona gor wrod hosen rellordholm ul vodh sol et kethyl. Nath noss trenneth et mesk brodhat lo et tolm. Amm hess brodhol et mesk, nath dess et odh, hy vodh nath sesk o hosen vodh. Re rytym et velvyth ull gor fedh, hosen re yal o.

Not every tale in this Book is ours. Some are the wood's. They come from the wreck-wood that the tide brings in under our wall, and from the dark stone that lies in the chest I carried up the mountain, and from other wood besides. The wood is not read, for there are no words in it. Halyna touch it, and its tale is known to them whole, as a memory is known, the beginning and the middle and the end of it at once. One of them need only touch it, for what the one knows, the other knows. The knowing is not the hard thing. The telling is. Words can be turned, and a knowing cannot; so they tell it in the fewest and plainest words they can find, and set each word down like a marking-stone where the knowing lies. The wood's tales are not told with the stone. When a wood telling ends, the hearth does not answer, for it does not know how. I have written that silence down each time, as it was.

Word for word: *Not every tale in this Book is at us. Some are at the wood. They come from the wreck-wood that the tide brings under our wall, and from the dark stone that rests in the chest that I carried up the mountain, and from the other wood. We do not read the wood, for there is no word in it. Halyna touch it, and they two hold its tale whole, as we hold a memory: its beginning, and its heart, and its end, at one time. The touch of one of them two is enough, for what the one holds, the other holds it. The hard thing is not the knowing. It is the telling. A word can change, and a knowing cannot change; so they two tell it in the fewest and truest words that they two find, and they two lay every word like a marking-stone where the knowing rests. The wood's tales are not told at the stone. When the wood's telling stops, the hearth does not answer, for it does not know how. I wrote that silence every time, as it was.*

- **Owning is the "at" idiom** (§3.7): *Nath noss gor dhrenn … lor*, "not every tale is at us"; *Doss merr lo et mesk*, "some are at the wood".
- ***hy et mesk ullen***, "from the other wood": *hy mesk ullen* would soften *mesk* to *vesk*, the verb "see, read", in a sentence about reading wood.
- ***Nath vesker et mesk***, "we do not read the wood": the participle *vesket* is also the noun "colour" (I.1's *vesket vorrdholm*), so the passive is said with a doer.
- ***kethe sona o dhrenn tunn***: to know the wood is to hold it (*keth*, the canon's own verb); *kether tumol*, "we hold a memory", is the Title's second word. ***o voskol***, "its beginning", is the lexicon's *moskol*; after *o* it is *voskol*, and the Creed's trap (*nath mosk*) does not arise.
- ***Nel et galat duss et kethyl. El et brodhol.*** Orrowen answers by saying the verb back (§3.4): "The telling is" is simply *El*, "[it] is", with the telling.
- ***brodath merr sost eth venn sost***: *venn* is "plain" as in "tell it plain" (O2715), which is Halyna's plainness exactly; *sost* makes the most.
- ***rellordholm***, "a marking-stone" (O2061, "sign-stone"); ***velvyth***, "silence" (O2710).

> Nath gethym et mesk, en sost. Nel hos et Lodhan en: nalodhat, voll et Stonwrytan en; eth amm re orrom dem susk et brunn, re lymme Halyna en ul sona odh, hosen sullast et odh. Re hadhom en narl um et mesk hos fedh, eth re luskom, hy vodh re rarr ulom galat strom hy en gethyl. Hy ull rytym galat sa wrodha Halyna. Re yal gor ryterd et Stonwrytan demom lodhat. Hy vodh ryt nalodhat et flennath sy? Brodha Halyna o ul o reskow sost, lo et tevath.

I do not know the wood myself. I am not of the Bonded: the guild calls me unbonded, and after I came up the mountain, Halyna took me into their hearth as its third. I laid my hand on the wood once, and let go, for what came into me was too great for me to hold. So I write down what Halyna tell. Every scribe of the guild before me was one of a bonded pair; why this Book is written by one who is not, Halyna tell in its own place, to the children.

Word for word: *I do not hold the wood, I myself. I am not one of the Bonded: unbonded, the guild names me; and when I went up the mountain, Halyna brought me into their hearth, as the third of the hearth. I laid my hand on the wood one time, and let go, for there came into me a thing greater than my holding. So I write what Halyna tell. Every scribe of the guild until me was bonded. Why does an unbonded one write this Book? Halyna tell it in its own place, to the children.*

- ***en narl***, "my hand": *en* beds *garl* into *narl* (§3.3).
- ***strom hy en gethyl***, "greater than my holding": *hy* is "than" (§3.6), and it stands after *strom*, not after a verb of going.
- ***demom***, "until me": "before me" in time. *ul lern*, the lexicon's "before", is "in the face of", and would put the scribes in front of her.
- **"Why this Book is written by one who is not" becomes Seren's question**, *Hy vodh ryt nalodhat et flennath sy?* Set inside a sentence, *hy vodh* would also read "because" ("because an unbonded one writes this Book, Halyna tell it…"); asked, it can only be "why". The answer follows, as the English has it.

> Mavere Halyna trenneth et mesk ul et flennath sy. Trenn brodhat hy hos lern hald, re vysk hos, eth re dhunn ullen: saed trenn. Hy ull, ul vodh re veske pa lern hos galat, cadhar et pa wrodhol hos ul lern ullen: ol drenn lo yarl ullen, eth trenn et mesk lo yarl venn. Ul vodh nath re yal tul brodhol flenn et mesk lo Halyna, re luskom et flenn pondul, eth doss hosel meskdhrenn umo. Doss lunn lo hos sa lunn et flennath sy dem veskyl lern yalat sa nath re hyrriler tul, eth dem dovvol lo ol sest.

Halyna would have the wood's tales in this Book. "A tale told from one side of a wall," one of them said, and the other finished, "is half a tale." So where one thing was seen from both sides, the two tellings are set facing each other, ours on the left leaf and the wood's on the right. Where the wood's leaf could not yet be told, I have left it blank, with only the grain showing. Whoever opens this Book may see the shape of what we did not yet understand, and wait with us.

Word for word: *Halyna want the wood's tales in this Book. A tale told from one face of a wall, said one, and the other finished: half a tale. So, where two sides saw one thing, we lay the two tellings one in the face of the other: our tale at the left hand, and the wood's tale at the right hand. Where the telling of the wood's leaf was not yet at Halyna, I left the leaf blank, and there is only the grain upon it. Freedom is at one who opens this Book, to see the shape of what we did not yet understand, and to wait at our shoulder.*

- **The saying is verbless**, as Orrowen sayings are (*Grest um hos, grest um vana*): *Trenn brodhat hy hos lern hald, … saed trenn.* One ink runs to the wedge and the other takes the rest (§5.4). ***re vysk hos, eth re dhunn ullen***, "one said, and the other finished" (*tunn*, whole), is the Book's own formula for a line two voices share, word for word as I.4, V.1 and V.7 give it (S03, S07, S09). *(Merge fix: the first draft had* re vysk hos hyonta, eth re dhunn ullen o*, "said one of them two, and the other finished it"; the merge keeps one rendering of the recurring English, `wf8/merge_report.md`.)*
- ***lo yarl ullen*** / ***lo yarl venn***, "at the left hand" / "at the right hand" (additions *garl ullen*, *garl venn*, from the canon's own senses: *ullen* "left, the other hand", *venn* "right-hand"): the facing leaves are named by the hands, as a reader holds the Book.
- ***re veske pa lern hos galat***, "two sides saw one thing": the passive *vesket* is avoided again (it is "colour").
- ***re luskom et flenn pondul***, "I left the leaf blank" (*lusk*, "let be"), and ***doss hosel meskdhrenn umo***, "there is only the grain upon it", is I.1's facing-leaf line, *Doss hosel meskdhrenn um et flenn*.
- **"May" is *Doss lunn lo* Y** (lexicon §5.3), with *dem* and the verbal noun for the doing: *Doss lunn lo hos sa lunn et flennath sy dem veskyl …*, "freedom is at one who opens this Book, to see…". *lunn* is both "freedom" and "open"; the hearth would hear the chime. "Whoever" is *hos sa*, "one who": *lo gor sa* would put *gor* after a softening trigger, which the grammar has not settled (pilot I.1, §3).
- ***lo ol sest***, "at our shoulder": I.1's *sa re orrant … lo ol sest*, "who walked … at our shoulder".

> Amm brodha Halyna, lymme sona et trenn hos eth ullen, trenn eth trenn, hy vodh seske sona et trenneth hosen sesk hos ullen, eth nath dovv hos um ullen. Re rytym ul ba luth, luth gor hos, eth nath mavere sona rellorol vodh luth lo hos, ell vodh lo ullen. Amm brodha sona hos myskyl ul sona lodh, hos lo o dholm hosast eth ullen lo o hosk, doss ull ul ba luth hosen.

When Halyna tell, they take the tale by turns, a paragraph each, for they know the tales as they know each other, and neither waits on the other. I have used two inks, one for each of them, and they would not have me mark which is whose. When they speak a single line between them, one beginning it and the other ending it, that is in two inks as well.

Word for word: *When Halyna tell, they two carry the tale one and the other, course and course, for they two know the tales as one knows the other, and one does not wait upon the other. I wrote in two inks, an ink apiece, and they two do not want it marked which ink is at the one, or which at the other. When they two tell one saying between them, one at its first stone and the other at its lintel, that is in two inks likewise.*

- ***hos eth ullen***, "by turns", is IV.4's phrase (O2858), and ***ul ba luth***, "in two inks", IV.4's too.
- ***trenn eth trenn***, "a paragraph each" ("course and course"): *trenn* is the canon's "course; line of writing; tale", so a paragraph is a course. It is the Book's recurring "by turns, a paragraph each", *hos eth ullen, trenn eth trenn*, as I.4, II.3, IV.3 and V.1 give it. *(Merge fix, `wf8/merge_report.md`: the first draft had* trenn gor hos*, "a course apiece".)*
- **"A single line" is *hos myskyl*, "one saying"**, not *hos trenn*: *trenn* has already been spent on the paragraph (*trenn eth trenn*), and with *trenn* in both places the leaf says "a course each … one course between them", which a blind reader took as "a line each" (back-translation S01). *myskyl* is the lexicon's "saying" (*mysk* + *-Ol*), and a line two voices share is a saying: the foreword's own example, *Trenn brodhat hy hos lern hald, … saed trenn*, is one.
- ***nath dovv hos um ullen***, "one does not wait upon the other" (addition *tovv um*): I.1's *re dhovv o dem hos*, "waited for one", would give *dem mullen* here (*dem* nasalises a vowel).
- ***ul sona lodh***, "between them two" (addition *ul so lodh*): *ul lodh*, "in the mortar of", is the lexicon's "between", and the Bonded are those of the mortar.
- **"One beginning it and the other ending it" is the mason's**: *hos lo o dholm hosast eth ullen lo o hosk*, "one at its first stone and the other at its lintel". *cadh et tolm hosast* is the pilots' "begin"; the lintel ends a line as it caps a gate (the pair is a gate).

> Nath gadh et Crenn trenn. Resk o lo rask et prenth, eth o wiskhovv dem susk, eth hunn brodhol o yaldatath sost, eth mysk o nayalat veth: New hosen ull. Um et hald eth ul et haralath, el et Ketherd o, hy vodh hosen sy re voll et Ternil o. Lo et odh sy, el et Crenn o tul, hosen maver o.

The Captain does not tell. He sits at the end of the bench with his hood up and hears his own deeds told, and says nothing, unless it is to say that it was not like that. On the wall and in the orders he is the Commander, for so the Rite named him. At this hearth he is the Captain still, as he would have it.

Word for word: *The Captain lays no tale. He sits at the end of the bench, and his hood up, and hears the telling of his own deeds, and says nothing but: It was not like that. On the wall and in the orders he is the Commander, for so the Rite named him. At this hearth he is the Captain still, as he wants.*

- ***lo rask et prenth, eth o wiskhovv dem susk*** and ***New hosen ull*** are I.1's, word for word: the Captain and his one denial are the same on every leaf.
- ***Nath gadh et Crenn trenn***: "the Captain does not tell" is said in the hearth's own verb for telling a tale, *cadh*, because *nath* would bed *brodh* into *mrodh*, an onset §2.5 does not allow.
- ***mysk o nayalat veth***: "says nothing, unless it is to say" is *nayalat veth*, "nothing but", I.1's *lo nayalat veth osk*. *nath mysk* is avoided: after *nath* it reads as the nasalised *bysk*, "tie".
- ***el et Ketherd o***: the canon's *Ketherd*, "the holder", the Rite's name for him; *et Ternil*, the Rite (*Ternil et Gorndholm*).

> Amm brodhar um ol nedheth, vollar so: Orrowan, hy vodh ew Orrowan so; eth vollar ol sost: ol. Et lunt ul sestow et myst, vollar o: Mystaeri, et mystan. El ull brod orrowen demont, eth nel so wrod.

When we speak of our fathers we call them Shorelanders, for so they were, and we call ourselves *we*. The folk behind the grey we call the Mystaeri, the mist-folk. That is our word for them, and not theirs.

Word for word: *When we speak of our fathers, we name them: Shorelanders, for Shorelanders they were; and we name ourselves: we. The people behind the grey, we name them: Mystaeri, the mist-folk. That is a word of the Shore for them, and not their word.*

- **A name given is set off by a wedge** (*vollar so: Orrowan*; *vollar o: Mystaeri*): run on, *so Orrowan* and *o Mystaeri* would read "their Shorelanders" and ask for a softened *Vystaeri* (§3.5, G9).
- ***vollar ol sost: ol***: the Rivenmen's word for themselves is the plain pronoun. ***et mystan***, "the mist-folk", is the lexicon's (*myst* + the collective *-an*); *Mystaeri* is the hybrid exonym (§4.7).
- ***brod orrowen demont***, "a word of the Shore for them": "our word" would be *ol mrod*, with the forbidden *mr-*; IV.4 said "words of ours" the same way (*et brodath orrowen*). *so wrod*, "their word", shows its bite.

> Ul vodh re yal rytet cresket, ell nath re yal brodhol et kethyl lo Halyna, re vyskym o, eth nath re hlennym o lo haevereth. Ul vodh nath gemment et trenneth, re hethym pana.

Where a carving was broken, or a knowing could not be told, I have said so, and have not mended it with guesses. Where the tales do not agree, I have kept both.

Word for word: *Where a carving was broken, or the telling of the knowing was not at Halyna, I said it, and I did not mend it with guesses. Where the tales do not meet, I kept both.*

- IV.4's headnote says the same promise in its own words (*Amm re yal et mesk cresket, ell re omm et brodhol, doss o rellorat eth nahlennet*); Seren's foreword says it in the first person, as the English does: *re vyskym o*, "I said it"; *nath re hlennym o*, "I did not mend it" (G1, the negative past).
- ***nath gemment***, "do not agree": *cemm*, "meet; agree ('the tales do not meet')" (O0256), in the plural (*gemm* alone would also be "four"). The Invocation's *Ul vodh nath gemme pana, re hether pana* is the same sentence in the hearth's voice.

> El luth et flennath sy. Kaevere Halyna o rytyl ul gyvil et hellur sy, amm doss sollan loskat dem o rytyl. Dem et orl ull, kether o ul et vestul sa re lymmym, ul molt et tolmath nawrodh.

This Book is ink. Halyna mean it to be cut into the granite of this hall when there is peace enough to cut it. Until that day it is kept in the chest I carried, with the mute stones.

Word for word: *This Book is ink. Halyna intend its cutting into the granite of this hall, when there is peace enough for its cutting. Until that day we keep it in the chest that I carried, among the mute stones.*

- ***El luth et flennath sy***: the copula puts the predicate first (§3.4), "ink is this Book".
- ***kether o***, "we keep it": *doss o kethet* would put *o* before *kethet* and read "its" (asking for *hethet*).
- ***tolmath nawrodh***, "the mute stones", is the lexicon's (§5.4 of lexicon_orrowen.md).

## 2 · THE INVOCATION (*AMMELYL*)

### Title and headnote

> ## *Ammelyl*

**THE INVOCATION** (the lexicon's *ammelyl*, "an invocation, a prayer": *ammel* "pray" + *-Ol*.)

> *Mysket gor vess lo Odh Sell, ul lern et trenn hosast, eth cadhat lo sy ul lern et trenn hosast et flennath.*

*Spoken at the Long Hearth before the first tale of a night, and set here before the first tale of the Book.*

Word for word: *Said every night at the Long Hearth, before the first tale; and laid here before the first tale of the Book.*

- ***Mysket gor vess***: "before the first tale of a night" is "every night, before the first tale"; *ul lern dhrenn hosast vess* would soften the whole possessor phrase, and *gor* after a trigger is unsettled (pilot I.1, §3).

### The Title, in coal (the `legend_title_coal` surface)

Drawn as the Captain wrote it in coal (*garl brenth*: unjoined letters, no bed band), and the same words written by Seren beneath it; canon, `orrowen_v2` §11.1, unchanged. The caption and fold are the reader's furniture, in English.

> **Ul dumol ol varn, ol theldeth, ol Mardh, ol lunnath, ol sollan.**

**IN MEMORY OF OUR HOME, OUR FAMILIES, OUR GOD, OUR FREEDOMS, OUR PEACE.**

### The Invocation

> Reska, amm doss lunn, eth stona amm nath noss. Brident et brammath, eth nath rhyn et lodh ul lern vess.

Sit, if there is room, and stand if there is not. The guns are cooling, and the mortar will not set before dark.

Word for word: *Sit, when there is room, and stand when there is not. The guns cool, and the mortar does not set before night.*

- ***Reska … stona***: the imperative plural, to the whole hearth. ***amm nath noss***, "if there is not": Orrowen says no by saying the verb back (§3.4).
- ***lunn***, "room": the canon's *lunn* is "freedom", and its root is "an open space" (lexicon §4.4); the first line of the Invocation offers the hearth a seat in the word of the Title's fourth thing.
- ***nath rhyn et lodh ul lern vess***: the tongue has no negative future (§3.4 gives none); the plain negative is also the habitual, as Kael's *nath gadhom o los*. "Before dark" is "before night" (*ul lern* + *vess*).

> Doss et odh lo sy, eth doss lo sy et tolm sa re omm hy et ganna ulvenn. Brodh gor sa heth o.

Here is the hearth, and here is the stone that fell from the inner gate. Whoever holds it tells.

Word for word: *The hearth is here, and here is the stone that fell from the inner gate. Whoever holds it tells.*

- ***Brodh gor sa heth o*** is the foreword's line, the same words.

> Brodhar et trenneth sy hy vodh re nadhumar hos fedh. Re nadhumant ol nedheth kethow, eth re hresk o. Re nadhumant so yedheth grest, eth re vymm o demor ul vimm wemmeth myst. Hy ull brodhar gor yalat: et galdol eth et creskyl, eth galat sa re yaldar hosen galat sa re yal galdat umor.

We tell these tales because we forgot once. Our fathers forgot a keep, and it crumbled. Their fathers forgot a wrong, and it came back to us under grey sails. So we tell all of it: the building and the breaking, and what we did as well as what was done to us.

Word for word: *We tell these tales because we forgot once. Our fathers forgot a keep, and it broke. Their fathers forgot a wrong, and it came back to us under grey sails. So we tell everything: the building and the breaking, and what we did, like what was done upon us.*

- ***re hresk o***, "it crumbled": *cresk* is "break; burst; crumble" (O0307).
- ***re nadhumant ol nedheth***: a plural subject takes the plural (G2); *ol* beds *gedheth* into *nedheth*, and *so* softens it into *yedheth*: "our fathers", "their fathers".
- ***re vymm o demor***, "it came back to us" (*mymm*, "return"); ***ul vimm wemmeth myst***, "under grey sails", the canon's *wemmeth myst*.
- ***galat sa re yaldar hosen galat sa re yal galdat umor***: *gald* is "do"; *hosen*, "like, equal", carries "as well as": the one weighed the same as the other. What is done *to* someone is laid *upon* them (*umor*, "upon us"), as a feeling or a burden is (§3.7; *re resk o umor hosen covv thenth* in I.2). *dem* is "until, as far as", a dative only of motion (*re vymm o demor*, "it came back to us"): *galdat demor* was read "built up to our time", that is, "what was built before us" (back-translation S01).

> Doss merr hy et trenneth sy lor, trenneth rytet venn, hosen tolmath et hald. Doss merr lo et mesk, eth el duss so wrodhol, eth nath byntent. Ul vodh nath gemme pana, re hether pana.

Some of these tales are ours, and they are cut plain, like the stones of the wall. Some are the wood's, and those are hard to tell, and will not be hurried. Where the two do not agree, we have kept both.

Word for word: *Some of these tales are ours: tales cut plain, like the stones of the wall. Some are the wood's, and their telling is hard, and they do not hurry. Where the two do not agree, we kept both.*

- ***trenneth rytet venn***, "tales cut plain": in apposition, not *doss so rytet venn*, which reads "their carving is true" (*so* before *r* shows no bite). *ryt* is the chisel's verb, and *venn* is a plumb wall.
- ***el duss so wrodhol***, "their telling is hard": *so* softens *brodhol* into *wrodhol*, so the possessive shows. ***nath byntent***, "they do not hurry" (*pynt*, bedded after *nath*).
- ***Ul vodh nath gemme pana, re hether pana***: *pana*, "both, the two", takes the dual (*gemme*), and the answer is *pana* again: "where the two do not meet, we kept the two".

> Keth et tolm. Cadh et trenn. Amm cadhat o, es dess et odh.

Hold the stone. Lay the tale. When it is laid, the hearth will answer.

- Canon, `orrowen_v2` §11.10, unchanged: *trenn* is "tale" and "course" at once.

> *Eth re vysk et odh:* **Tumar.**

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

- The hearth's answer, as the lexicon and both pilots give it (O0564).

## 3 · I.2 THE CRY OF THE BONDED (*CARM ET LODHAN*)

### Title

> ## *Carm et Lodhan*

**I.2 · The Cry of the Bonded**

*The cry of the Bonded.* The lexicon's title (O0246).

### Seren's headnote

> *Re hadh Seren Pa Luth, prassel Halyna eth ryterd et flennath sy, ul et vess ul sestow et kiser hosast sa re heth et Treskan. Ew tev et Stonwrytan ey ul et orl sa wrodh ey umo, eth re resk ey ul et parow ul molt et ullen.*

*Laid by Seren Two-Inks, apprentice to Halyna and scribe of this Book, on the night after the first assault the Rivenmen held. She was a child of the guild on the day she tells of, and sat in the courtyard with the rest.*

Word for word: *Laid Seren Two-Inks, prentice of Halyna and scribe of this Book, on the night after the first assault that the Rivenmen held. She was a child of the guild on the day that she tells of, and she sat in the courtyard among the others.*

- ***Re hadh Seren Pa Luth***: I.1's headnote shape (*Re hadh Crennel Kael*), with Seren's canon epithet, *Seren Pa Luth*. She writes of herself in the third person, as the English does (*ey*, "she").
- ***sa wrodh ey umo***, "that she tells of": the relative with the conjugated preposition left behind (G5), as I.1's *sa re rytent … umo*.
- ***sa re heth et Treskan***, "that the Rivenmen held": *keth*, the Commander's verb; *Treskan* is a collective and takes the singular (G2).

### Seren's telling

> Keth et tolm, re vyske Halyna lom, amm re yalom lyss eth re yal ludal wrodhol umom lo et odh sy. Keth o, eth es geth tho. Hy ull kethym o.

"Hold the stone," Halyna told me when I was small and afraid to speak at this hearth. "Hold it, and it will hold you." So I hold it.

Word for word: *Hold the stone, Halyna told me, when I was small and the fear of speaking was upon me at this hearth. Hold it, and it will hold you. So I hold it.*

- **Halyna's words to the child are canon** (`orrowen_v2` §11.11: *Keth et tolm. Keth o, eth es geth tho.*), split round the telling as the English splits them; *tho*, the singular, to a child.
- ***re vyske Halyna lom***: Halyna are two and take the dual (*myske*), and *re* softens it to *vyske* (§3.9).
- ***re yal ludal wrodhol umom***, "the fear of speaking was upon me": feeling is the "upon" idiom (§3.7), and the lexicon's own *doss ludal um*; the verbal noun is the possessor (*brodhol* > *wrodhol*). *dem brodhol* ("to speak") would nasalise to *mrodhol*.

> Re yalom ul et parow orl ull ul molt et Stonwrytan, hy vodh re yalom carmat dem et Stonwrytan tevar, eth nath re yal prassoth lom tul veth lymmyl nynth. Re resker ul et parow hemm sa re yal linthet, ol, merr lesk murr, prassath eth prasseleth, eth et Lodhan hy ullen. Re orrom et hiskenneth lo sest luld et Stonwrytan, eth re vellorom ul et parow ull hosel. Re rarrar hy noth cumm. Re vesker gor hos hyont ul sennyl, eth re orrar dem neld hyont, hos eth hos, eth et bresketh ul ol havulath, eth re wemm nahos hyor garl, hy vodh re nalusk et daevarat o. Gald; nath nayald. Par; nath mosk. Re lymm nahos et Stonwrytan fedh hesp. Nath re yal hespeth lor veth lodh eth bresk, eth nath re yal so loskat.

I was in the courtyard that day among the Stonewrights, for I had been called to the guild already, though I had no craft yet but carrying water. We sat in the old ward where it had gone to grass, some hundreds of us, masters and prentices, and the bonded pairs a little apart. I had walked the passes beside a boy of the guild, and I came into that courtyard alone. We had come out of ten havens. We had watched every one of them die, and we had walked away from each with our chisels in our packs, and not one of us had lifted a hand, for our creed forbade it. Build, do not destroy. Protect, do not attack. No Stonewright ever bore a weapon. Mortar and chisel were our only arms, and they had not been enough.

Word for word: *I was in the courtyard that day among the Stonewrights, for I had been called to the guild already, and there was no craft at me yet but the carrying of water. We sat in the old ward that was grassed over, we, some hundreds, masters and prentices, and the Bonded apart. I walked the passes at the shoulder of a boy of the guild, and I came into that courtyard alone. We came out of ten havens. We saw every one of them in dying, and we walked away from them, one by one, and the chisels in our packs; and not one of us lifted a hand, for the creed forbade it. Build; do not destroy. Protect; do not attack. Not one of the guild ever bore a sword. There were no arms at us but mortar and chisel, and they were not enough.*

- ***re yalom carmat dem et Stonwrytan tevar***, "I had been called to the guild already": *carm*, "a call"; *tevar*, "early, before the time", which is "already" (O2517).
- ***nath re yal prassoth lom tul veth lymmyl nynth***: "no craft yet but carrying water" is the "at" idiom with *nath … veth* (G8).
- ***merr lesk murr***, "some hundreds": I.1's *merr lesk murr* ("some five-twenties"); a hundred is a phrase and takes no plural.
- ***et Lodhan hy ullen***, "the Bonded apart": "the bonded pairs" are the Bonded, who are all pairs; *gannath* ("pairs") would also be "posts, a palisade". *hy ullen* is the lexicon's "apart (from the other)", the words that say of Halyna below that they *sat apart* (*sa re reske hy ullen*). *lo sest*, "at the shoulder", with no possessor after it, was read "beside us", the opposite of "a little apart" (back-translation S01).
- ***re vellorom … hosel***: after a verb *hosel* is "alone", which is what the English says this time (the other side of I.1's G8).
- ***Re vesker gor hos hyont ul sennyl***, "we saw every one of them in dying": the progressive with *ul*, as I.1's *uldath ul greskyl*. ***re orrar dem neld hyont, hos eth hos***: "from each" is "from them, one by one"; *hy gor hos* would set *gor* after a trigger (unsettled).
- ***eth et bresketh ul ol havulath***, "and the chisels in our packs": a verbless clause laid beside the other, as I.1's *eth so wresketh ul so simmeth*. "Our chisels" would be *ol mresketh*, with *mr-*.
- ***re wemm nahos hyor garl***, "not one of us lifted a hand": *bemm*, "lift", softened after *re* (*wemm* is also "a sail", but after *re* only the verb fits); *nahos*, "no one", as I.1's *re hyrril nahos*.
- **The Creed is canon** (§11.11: *Gald; nath nayald. Par; nath mosk.*), and ***re nalusk et daevarat o***, "the creed forbade it" (*nalusk*, "un-loose"; *daevarat*, "what is held true"): "our creed" would bed *daevarat* into *naevarat*.
- ***Re lymm nahos et Stonwrytan fedh hesp***, "not one of the guild ever bore a sword": I.1's *re hyrril nahos fedh*, "no one ever understood"; *hesp* is "a sword; a weapon". ***Nath re yal hespeth lor veth lodh eth bresk***: "our only arms" is *nath … veth* again.

> Re yal et vennoth sa re ston ol lo dhrenn relv tesk umor tul, veth re resk o umor hosen covv thenth. Kaeverym re yal gor hos hy et hemmeth ul et parow ull ul gylyl hos galat duss, eth nath re vysk o ul garm: haldath sa yaldar dem sollan, doss maver fedheth so varol lo lorr. Eth amm ew hosen ull, cedh sa hebb et lorr? Eth cedh sa wemm et garl?

The faith that had sustained us through twelve generations was on us still, but it sat on us like a wet cloak. I think every elder in that courtyard was turning over the same hard thing and would not say it aloud: that walls built for peace must sometimes be defended with blood. And if that was so, whose blood? And whose hands?

Word for word: *The faith that had held us up through twelve generations was upon us still, but it sat upon us like a wet cloak. I think every one of the elders in that courtyard was in turning one hard thing, and did not say it aloud: walls that we build for peace, there is need at times of their guarding with blood. And if it was so, who gives the blood? And who lifts the hand?*

- ***sa re ston ol***, "that had sustained us": *ston*, "stand; make stand; sustain", the word of every oath's close. ***lo dhrenn relv tesk***, "through twelve generations": the canon's *delv tesk*, softened as the possessor of *lo dhrenn* (*d* > *r*, as in the canon's *pa relv*).
- ***gor hos hy et hemmeth***, "every one of the elders": *gor hemm* would also be *gor* + S·*cemm*, "every meeting".
- **The thought is given, not reported**, and verbless at its head: *haldath sa yaldar dem sollan, doss maver fedheth so varol lo lorr*. "Must" is *doss maver*, "there is need" (lexicon §5.3), and "defended" is *par*, the Creed's own *Par*, "protect": the thought turns the Creed against itself.
- **"Whose blood? And whose hands?" are asked as who gives and who lifts**: *cedh sa hebb et lorr? … cedh sa wemm et garl?* "Whose" would make *cedh* a possessor, softened into *hedh* ("dusk"); and "lifts the hand" answers "not one of us had lifted a hand".

> Hy ull re rarr et Crenn dem vimm hy et hald. Re veskym et covv ul daldol, eth re hunnom et hesperdeth ul garmol, eth nath re seskym vodh ew o demor. Re orr o dem molt et parow. New susk o. Re yal brenthromm um nindeth et Crenn. Re vesk o um et hemmeth, eth um et ganna cresket ul sestow, eth re vess o hos pessyl. El sy o wrodan hosel sa ross myskyl pawast lo et odh sy:

Then the Captain came down from the wall. I had seen the cloak go up, and heard the soldiers shout, and did not know what it had to do with us. He walked into the middle of the courtyard. He was not tall. There was coal-black on his fingers. He looked at the elders, and at the broken gate behind them, and he asked one question. It is the only speech of his that anyone at this hearth can repeat:

Word for word: *Then the Captain came down from the wall. I saw the cloak in rising, and heard the soldiers in crying out, and I did not know what it was to us. He went to the heart of the courtyard. He was not tall. There was coal-black on the Captain's fingers. He looked at the elders, and at the broken gate behind, and he asked one question. This is his only speech that this hearth can say again:*

- ***Re vesk o um et hemmeth***: I.1's *Re vesk et Crenn um et hald* ("looked at" is "looked upon").
- ***um nindeth et Crenn***, "on the Captain's fingers": *um o nindeth* would be heard as *umo nindeth*, "upon him, fingers" (*n* shows no bite). *brenthromm*, "coal-black", is the lexicon's compound (*brenth* + S·*domm*).
- ***sa ross myskyl pawast lo et odh sy***: the "can" idiom inside a relative (*sa* softens *doss* to *ross*); *mysk pawast* is the lexicon's "repeat". "Anyone at this hearth" is the hearth itself: *lo gor hos* would put *gor* after a trigger.

> Ommant va haldath. Es naldos, ell es veskys eth es sennys?

"Your walls are falling. Will you build, or will you watch and die?"

Word for word: *Your walls fall. Will you build, or will you watch and will you die?*

- **Eleven words**, the Captain's plainness. He speaks to the whole guild, so in the plural (*va*, *-os*); a True Man he would call *tho* (§3.9).
- ***Ommant va haldath***: the plural verb shows that *va* is "your", not "you" (after *doss*, *va haldath* could be either). ***es naldos … es veskys … es sennys***: *es* beds *gald* into *nald*. A question about the future has no particle of its own (*ho* is the present's); the alternative carries it, as the English does.

> Re dhess nahos o. Tumom et helvhoss ul et linth.

No one answered him. I remember the wind in the grass.

- ***Re dhess nahos o***: the lexicon's *Eth re dhess nahos*, "and no one answered", with its object. ***Tumom***, "I remember": the hearth's *Tumar*, in the singular; Seren's one line of memory is in the Title's word.

> Hy ull re dhalda Halyna. Seskys sona ul et voll ull amm sy. Re hebb et Stonwrytan o lonta ul sona hoskol, hosen hebb o hos gor fedh lo yanna lodhat, eth vollar sona ulo. Nath voll et Stonwrytan Lodhan Helv veth et Lodhan, eth el Lodhan et Kethow Halyna. Nath re seskym sona amm ull, veth hosen Hemm eth Hemma sa re reske hy ullen eth re wrodha lo nahos, hy vodh nath re yal maver lonta. Ew Hemma Rhyna ey, Rhyna Yanna Ulvenn: re yal vadullan lodhat loy, sa re hadh hosk et ganna ull sost, ul et begess ul lern et cummath. Ew Hemm Halvard o, Halvard Tolmvard, hy stannowvardath et sorth susk: re stannant gedheth Halvard et tolm ull sost. Ew humm eth verr sona, hosen el et Lodhan, eth re yala sona hos hy et tevow. Re veske sona dem susk um et covv treskat, eth re ferre sona demo ul et hos hoss, hy vodh nath dovv et Lodhan hos um ullen. Hy ull re dhalda et pa wresk hosen hos, eth re rike et baegyth dasken.

The guild calls the Aetherbonded simply the Bonded, and Halyna are the Bonded of the Keep. I did not know them then, save as two elders who sat apart and spoke to no one, for they had no need. I will name them singly this once, for the tale needs it. She was Elderess Rhyna, Rhyna of the Inner Gate, whose bonded forebears had laid the capstone of that very gate in the age before the havens. He was Elder Halvard, Halvard Stone-Warden, of the quarry-wardens of the high ridge, whose fathers had cut that same stone. They were husband and wife, as the Bonded are, and had been one since the cradle. They looked up at the torn cloak, and they bowed to it in the same breath, for the Bonded do not wait on each other. Then their two chisels went up as one and took the last of the light.

Word for word: *Then Halyna rose. You know them two by that name now. The guild gave it to them two at their sealing, as it gives one every time to a bonded pair, and we name them by it. The guild calls the Aetherbonded nothing but the Bonded, and Halyna are the Bonded of the Keep. I did not know them two then, but as an Elder and an Elderess who sat apart and spoke to no one, for there was no need at them. I will name them one by one, this time only, for the tale needs it. She was Elderess Rhyna, Rhyna of the Inner Gate: there were bonded forebears at her, who had laid the capstone of that very gate, in the age before the havens. He was Elder Halvard, Halvard Stone-Warden, of the quarry-wardens of the high ridge: Halvard's fathers quarried that very stone. They were husband and wife, as the Bonded are, and they had been one from the cradle. They looked up at the torn cloak, and they bowed to it in the one breath, for the Bonded do not wait, one upon the other. Then the two chisels rose as one, and took the last light.*

- **Halyna take the dual throughout** (*re dhalda*, *re reske*, *re wrodha*, *re veske*, *re ferre*, *re rike*): "where the Book gives Halyna a plural verb, the Orrowen original is dual" (§3.9).
- ***ul sona hoskol***, "at their sealing": *hoskol*, "the laying of the lintel", is the canon's word for the wedding (§3.10). ***lo yanna lodhat***, "to a bonded pair": a pair is a gate (*ganna*), softened after *lo*; *lo gor* is avoided again by *gor fedh*, "every time".
- ***Nath voll et Stonwrytan Lodhan Helv veth et Lodhan***: the foreword's sentence, the same words.
- ***Hemm eth Hemma***, "an Elder and an Elderess", for "two elders" (as in the foreword); ***Hemma Rhyna***, ***Hemm Halvard*** (additions): the canon's title before the name.
- ***Rhyna Yanna Ulvenn*** and ***Halvard Tolmvard*** are canon. Rhyna's forebears are said with the "at" idiom, *re yal vadullan lodhat loy*, "there were bonded forebears at her": *ey*, "her", shows no bite, and after a verb reads "she"; this way the name keeps its face (compare the foreword's *vadullan Hyna*, §5.3).
- ***stannowvardath et sorth susk***, "the quarry-wardens of the high ridge" (*stannowvard*, O2355); ***re stannant gedheth Halvard et tolm ull sost***: the foreword's *Re stannant gedheth Halvard et tolm ull*, with *sost*, "that same stone".
- ***Ew humm eth verr sona, hosen el et Lodhan***: "as the Bonded are" says the copula back (§3.4).
- ***nath dovv et Lodhan hos um ullen***: the foreword's "neither waits on the other", the same phrase.
- ***re dhalda et pa wresk hosen hos***, "the two chisels rose as one": "their two chisels" would be *sona va wresk*, with *pa* softened after the possessive into a word that is also "your"; the article serves. ***et baegyth dasken***, "the last light".

> Re rarr et carm hy vana, eth ew hos carm o. Re hadh ey o dholm hosast:

The cry came out of them both, and it was one cry. She began it:

Word for word: *The cry came from both, and it was one cry. She laid its first stone:*

- ***Re hadh ey o dholm hosast***, "she laid its first stone": the pilots' *cadh et tolm hosast*, "begin", with the cry as its possessor (*o dholm*, softened, so the possessive shows).

**The Cry** (canon, `orrowen_v2` §11.2, unchanged). Rhyna's ink to the wedge; Halvard's ink takes the three words after it. One perpend, at the very end: "there was no silence" is written into the punctuation.

> Carm et lodh hy et trun. Talda, Stonwrytan! Cadha tolm um dholm. Stona gor hald dem et dask sost,

"The mortar cries out from the ground. Rise, Stonewrights! Lay stone upon stone. Stand every wall to the very end."

> Eth re hadh o et hosk umo, eth o wresk lo sest ey bresk:

And he ended it, with his chisel beside hers:

Word for word: *And he laid the lintel upon it, and his chisel beside her chisel:*

- ***re hadh o et hosk umo***, "he ended it", "he laid the lintel upon it": ***cadh et hosk um***, "end, finish" (S09's addition, O2983 in the merged lexicon), the exact counterpart of the pilots' *cadh et tolm hosast*, "begin" (O2859), whose possessor form Rhyna's half takes above (*re hadh ey o dholm hosast*). The second voice caps the cry as a lintel caps a gate. *(Merge fix: the first draft had* re hosk o et carm*, with* hosk *as a verb, addition S01-04; the merge keeps one form for "end", `wf8/merge_report.md`.)* *o wresk lo sest ey bresk*: *o* softens *bresk*; *ey* softens nothing.

> Talda! Talda! Talda!

"Rise! Rise! Rise!"

> Brodhant merr o: Re harm ey, eth re dhess o, hosen amm re yal pa harm eth velvyth ul so lodh. Nath re yal velvyth. Ew hos carm ul ba hossow o, eth new hosast ey veth hy vodh doss maver moskol um harm ul reskow.

Some tell it that she called and he answered, as though there were two cries and a silence between them. There was no silence. It was one cry in two throats, and hers came first only because a cry must begin somewhere.

Word for word: *Some tell it: She called, and he answered; as if there were two cries and a silence between them. There was no silence. It was one cry in two throats, and she was first only because a cry needs a beginning somewhere.*

- ***Brodhant merr o: Re harm ey, eth re dhess o***: what some say is given as they say it (no "that"), as I.1's cries are.
- ***ul so lodh***, "between them" (the two cries): the mortar between two stones.
- ***new hosast ey veth hy vodh …***, "she was first only because…": *nath … veth* on the copula (*new*, "was not"), I.1's G8. Read word by word it is "she was not first, but because…", which is the tongue's "only because".
- ***ul ba hossow***, "in two throats" (*hossow*, "the breath-place"). ***moskol***, "a beginning", after *maver*, which does not mutate it; the Creed's trap is only *nath mosk*.

> Hy ull re dhald et Stonwrytan tunn, eth en ulont, veth nath dumom en daldol. Re riker et carm, eth re rikent et hesperdeth um et hald o hyor, dem et fedh sa re veskent haldath et Kethow sost ul marrol. Nath marr tolm hemm. Re ledhym o ul marrol ul vimm misk.

Then the whole guild was on its feet, and I with them, though I do not remember standing. We took up the shout, and the soldiers on the wall took it from us, until the walls of Rivenkeep itself seemed to tremble. Old stone does not tremble. I felt it do so under my feet.

Word for word: *Then the whole guild rose, and I among them, but I do not remember my rising. We took up the cry, and the soldiers on the wall took it from us, until the time that the walls of the Keep itself were seen trembling. Old stone does not tremble. I felt it trembling underfoot.*

- ***nath dumom en daldol***, "I do not remember my rising": *Tumar*'s verb again.
- ***dem et fedh sa re veskent haldath et Kethow sost ul marrol***: "until" before a clause is "until the time that" (*dem amm* would be *dem mamm*). "The walls of Rivenkeep" is "the walls of the Keep": the construct would soften *Kethow* into *Hethow*, the lexicon's trap (§8.2); *vesk* is "see; seem (be seen)".
- ***ul vimm misk***, "underfoot", the lexicon's own (O2640): *ul vimm en misketh*, "under my feet", would also read N·*bisketh*, "my heads".

> Re yal et haethil galdat ul et vess ull, ul gaelir et hellur sy, eth re yalda Halyna o. Nath lymm et Stonwrytan hespeth: re lymm o nafedh, eth es lymm o nafedh, eth nath re vess et Crenn o. Veth es nald et Stonwrytan ul lodh et sevireth, eth es glenn vathur somm carmant et brammath tul, lo vyntyth eth gandoth sa re yal lo Lodhan Helv hosel, hy vodh gald ganna lodhat hosen hos derdsast eth gemm garl. Re vysk hos hyonta et saed hosast lo et Crenn, eth ullen et saed pawast, eth nath seskym cedh ew cedh. El brod et Stonwrytan o hy amm ull: Nel et hesp, veth et tolm. Nel et hurrol, veth et kethyl.

The terms were made that night in the ruin of this hall, and Halyna made them. The Stonewrights would not take up arms. They never had and never would, and the Captain did not ask it. But they would build between the volleys, and mend a breach while the guns still spoke, with the speed and precision that only Aetherbonded pairs could achieve, for a bonded pair works as one body with four hands. One of them said the first half to the Captain and the other the rest, and I could not tell you which. It has been the guild's word ever since: "Not the sword, but the stone. Not the killing, but the keeping."

Word for word: *The terms were made that night, in the ruin of this hall, and Halyna made them. The Stonewrights do not bear arms: they never bore them, and they never will, and the Captain did not ask it. But the Stonewrights would build between the volleys, and would mend a breach while the guns still cried, with the speed and precision that were the Aetherbonded's alone, for a bonded pair works as one body and four hands. One of them said the first half to the Captain, and the other the second half, and I do not know who was who. It is the guild's word since then: Not the sword, but the stone. Not the killing, but the keeping.*

- ***haethil***, "terms (of peace); a truce" (O0844), *galdat*, made; ***ul gaelir et hellur sy***, "in the ruin of this hall" (*kaelir*, bedded after *ul*).
- **The terms are given in their own voice**, the present: *Nath lymm et Stonwrytan hespeth*; "take up arms" is "bear arms", *lymm*, because *nath* would bed *grik* ("take") into *nrik*. ***re lymm o nafedh, eth es lymm o nafedh***: "never" is *nafedh* after a plain past and a future, as IV.4's *Re femm Halvard o nafedh*.
- ***es nald … es glenn***: the future in a past frame is "would" (lexicon, *es*). *somm carmant et brammath tul*, "while the guns still cried": guns do not speak in Orrowen; they cry out, as a gun is "the roarer" (*bramm*).
- ***lo vyntyth eth gandoth***, "with speed and precision" (*pyntyth*, softened after *lo*; *gandoth*, "sharpness"): ***sa re yal lo Lodhan Helv hosel***, "that were at the Aetherbonded alone": after a noun, *hosel* is "alone", which is "only" here.
- ***hosen hos derdsast eth gemm garl***, "as one body and four hands" (*derdsast*, "bone-skin").
- ***nath seskym cedh ew cedh***, "I do not know who was who" (addition): "I could not tell you which" is a thing Seren does not know, and says so.
- **The guild's word is canon** (§11.11), one voice to each wedge: here, one ink to each.

> Hy ull re yal et tynurorl saedet, eth doss vollath lo et odh sy dem o hlemeth. Doss et cadhol, amm gald et Stonwrytan, eth clenn. Doss et caldol, amm doss et brammath caldat dem et reskow sa vaver et Crenn so. Doss et kethyl, amm mellorant wemmeth myst eth doss maver stonol um et hald, eth gorr gor wramman ul vimm hos hy vennuldath et Crenn. Gor wramm caldat, trenn nahadhat, hy vodh doss et garlath hosen um et bunthath; eth sesk et Crenn o, eth marr o bramm hosen vyssor uld vothen lenn. Eth amm veskys stonwrytel um et hald ul et kethyl, veska pawast. Es veskys pa.

There is the hauling, when the guns are dragged to where the Captain wants them. There is the holding, when the grey sails come in and the wall must stand, and every battery fights under one of the Captain's True Men. Every gun that is hauled is a course of stone not laid, for it is the same hands on the ropes; and the Captain knows it, and moves a gun as a careful man spends silver. And wherever you see a Stonewright on the wall in the holding, look again. You will see two.

Word for word: *So the siege-day was shared, and there are names at this hearth for its hours. There is the laying, when the guild builds, and mends. There is the hauling, when the guns are dragged to the place that the Captain wants them. There is the holding, when the grey sails come in and standing is needed upon the wall, and every battery fights under one of the Captain's True Men. Every hauled gun, a course unlaid, for the hands are the same on the ropes; and the Captain knows it, and moves a gun as a careful man spends silver. And when you see a Stonewright on the wall in the holding, look again. You will see two.*

- ***tynurorl***, "a siege-day" (O2627, four-and-twenty hours); *saedet*, "halved, shared".
- **The three hours are the siege's own words**: ***et cadhol***, the laying; ***et caldol***, the hauling (*cald*, "drag, haul"); ***et kethyl***, the holding, the Commander's verb and the third call's. *cald* is a reserve word that softens into *hald*, "wall", and nasalises into *gald*, "build" (lexicon §8.2): on this leaf no trigger stands before it.
- ***gor wramman ul vimm hos hy vennuldath et Crenn***: *gor* softens *bramman* ("a battery") into *wramman*.
- ***Gor wramm caldat, trenn nahadhat***, "every hauled gun, a course unlaid" (added as a saying): verbless, as the tongue's sayings are, so that it can be said on the wall as a proverb. *nahadhat*, "unlaid" (O1513).
- ***marr o bramm hosen vyssor uld vothen lenn***: *marr*, the first call's "shift"; *vyssor*, "spend"; *vothen*, "careful".
- ***amm veskys stonwrytel***: "wherever" is "when" (*amm*): the holding is a time. *stonwrytel*, "one Stonewright" (the canon's singulative), and ***Es veskys pa***, "you will see two".

> Re veller ul et vess hosast ull lo waegyth dhymorn ul dresk et ganna ulvenn somm re hadhar, hos carm dem et hessel, eth carm ullen et ullen, hosen re ammada Halyna ol:

We sang that first night by torchlight in the gap of the inner gate as we laid, one voice to the comma and the other the rest, as Halyna taught us:

Word for word: *We sang on that first night by torchlight, in the cleft of the inner gate, while we laid, one voice to the comma, and another voice the rest, as Halyna taught us:*

- ***lo waegyth dhymorn***, "by torchlight" (the lexicon's *baegyth dhymorn*, softened after *lo*); ***ul dresk et ganna ulvenn***, "in the gap of the inner gate": *tresk*, the cleft of *Kethow Dhresk* and of the Torn Cloak, as I.1's *ul dresk et hald*.
- ***hos carm dem et hessel, eth carm ullen et ullen***: *hessel*, "a comma ('a small stop')" (O0956), and *et ullen*, "the rest".

**The verse** (*Pa lo Yanna*, *Two at the Gate*, the lexicon's name for the song, O1893). One voice to the wedge and the other the rest, in every line; Seren writes it as she writes all antiphony, with the ink changing at the wedge (§5.4).

> *Tolm um dholm, eth keth et tolm vennoth;*
> *garl um yarl, eth keth et garl venn;*
> *pa lo yanna, amm cemment wemmeth myst,*
> *pa lo yanna, eth ston et ganna tunn.*

> *Stone upon stone, and the stone keeps faith;*  
> *hand over hand, and the hand holds true;*  
> *two at the gate when the grey sails gather,*  
> *two at the gate, and the gate stands through.*

Word for word:

> *Stone upon stone, and the stone holds faith;*  
> *hand upon hand, and the hand holds true;*  
> *two at the gate, when the grey sails gather,*  
> *two at the gate, and the gate stands whole.*

**The form.** First-syllable stress, five beats to the line: TOLM um DHOLM, eth KETH et TOLM VEN-noth · GARL um YARL, eth KETH et GARL VENN · PA lo YAN-na, amm CEM-ment WEM-meth MYST · PA lo YAN-na, eth STON et GAN-na TUNN. The second and fourth lines close on **the same consonants, not the same vowels**, *venn · tunn*, which is how Seren rhymed the True Men's verse in pilot I.1 (*myst · sest · vranast*); the English rhymes *true · through*.

- ***Tolm um dholm*** is the Cry's own *Cadha tolm um dholm*, and ***garl um yarl*** is built the same way: *um* softens the second of each pair, the one mutation in each line you can hear.
- ***keth et tolm vennoth***, "the stone keeps faith": *keth*, "hold, keep", and the canon's *vennoth*, "faith"; the first line's last word chimes with the second's (*vennoth · venn*).
- ***keth et garl venn***, "the hand holds true": *venn* stands where an adjective stands, after its noun, so the line can also be heard with the addition *garl venn*, "the right hand" ("and [one] holds the right hand"). The blind back-translation read it as the Book has it, with a doubt; the chime *vennoth · venn* is worth the doubt, and a hand held at the gate is no wrong reading. **[Jack]** if it should be unambiguous.
- ***amm cemment wemmeth myst***: *cemm*, "gather, come together; meet" (O0256).
- ***eth ston et ganna tunn***, "and the gate stands whole": *ston*, the oath's word; *tunn*, "whole, complete", for "stands through".

> Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

- The formula as the lexicon and pilot I.1 give it; in the leaf-hand it ends with the coping, which caps a tale (§6.7).

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

- **N + *br-* gives *mr-*** (and N + *gr-* gives *nr-*): no *ol mrod*, *ol mresketh*, *dem mrodhol*, *nath mrodh*, *nath nrik*, *ul mridyl* anywhere; the tongue's *cadh*, *lymm*, the article, or a plain verb stands in.
- ***nath mysk*** reads as N·*bysk* "tie": "says nothing but" is *mysk o nayalat veth*.
- ***vesket*** is the participle "seen" and the noun "colour": "is not read", "was seen" are said with a doer.
- ***hethow*** (S·*kethow*) reads as *heth* + *-ow*: *ul Gethow Dhresk*, *haldath et Kethow*.
- ***pa hemm***, ***gor hemm*** also read as S·*cemm* "meeting": *Hemm eth Hemma*, *gor hos hy et hemmeth*.
- ***en misketh*** also reads N·*bisketh* "my heads": *ul vimm misk*, "underfoot".
- ***hy mesk*** gives *vesk* ("see, read"): *hy et mesk ullen*.
- ***dem amm*** gives *dem mamm*; ***dem ullen*** gives *dem mullen*: *dem et orl sa …*, *dem et fedh sa …*; *tovv um ullen*.
- ***cald*** "haul" (S *hald* "wall", N *gald* "build"): no trigger before it on this leaf.
- ***dem*** + a person is "until, as far as": with no motion it reads as time (*demom*, "until me", "before me"), so "done to us" is *galdat umor*, "done upon us" (back-translation S01).
- ***lo sest*** with no possessor reads "beside [us]" as readily as "aside": "apart" is *hy ullen* (back-translation S01).
- ***trenn*** is both the paragraph and the line: where the two are set against each other, the line is *myskyl*, "a saying" (back-translation S01).
- ***Hyna*** (S·*Rhyna*): see S6.

### 5.4 The inks

None of the three texts is a Halyna telling, so no paragraph alternates inks. The ink changes only at the wedge of an antiphonal line (`orrowen_v2` §3.9 item 5, §8.7): the foreword's *Trenn brodhat hy hos lern hald, … saed trenn*; the Cry (Rhyna's ink to the wedge after *sost*, Halvard's for *Talda! Talda! Talda!*); the guild's word (one ink to each wedge); and **[Jack]** the four lines of *Two at the Gate*, which are the guild's antiphony, not Halyna's, but which Seren's own foreword promises in two inks when "they speak a single line between them". The leaf-hand strings above mark the wedges; the renderer's `ink2` option switches ink there.
