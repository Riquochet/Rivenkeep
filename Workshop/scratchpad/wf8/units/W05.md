### 1.1 The title

*The Wreck That Went Home.* (The lexicon's title, O1089: "the wreck that returned home".) *vymm* is *mymm*, "return, come back", softened after *re*.

### 1.2 The headnote

> **Hosen tum Mesk Myst: kethet hy scethel Hyll Dhumol, sa re felm ul o rarrol pawast; re hyl o hy ol hald grestet, eth re vymm o dem et tolm sost dem sennyl lo ull; doss o ul lern Vess Vollol.**

as · remember · Mystwood: held (known) · from · one-hull · Tide · S·Remembering, REL · PST · sink · in · its · S·coming · second; PST · S·turn-back · it · from · our · wall · wounded, and · PST · S·return · it · to · the · stone · very · to · dying · at · that; is · it · in(+N) · face · Night · Naming.

*As the Mystwood remembers: known from a hull of the Tide of Remembering, sunk on its second coming, having turned from our wall wounded and come back to the same stone to die there; it faces the Night of the Naming.*

- The opening, *Hosen tum Mesk Myst: kethet hy …*, and the close, *doss o ul lern …*, are IV.4's headnote formula exactly: *kethet*, "held", is the canon's verb for knowing wood, and "it faces X" is *doss o ul lern X*, the next leaf's title softened as the possessor of *ul lern*, "in the face of". *Vess Vollol* (O2738, "the Night of the Naming") begins with *v*, which the softening leaves as it is.
- **"A hull of the Tide" is *scethel Hyll Dhumol***: *scethel*, "one hull of a fleet", is the canon's own singulative of *scethan* (orrowen_v2 §3.2), so "a hull of the Tide of Remembering" is one member of that fleet, with *Hyll Dhumol* (the lexicon's name for the Tide) as its possessor.
- *felm*, "drown, sink", for "sunk"; *ul o rarrol pawast*, "in its second coming" (*darrol* softened after the possessive *o*; the ordinal after its noun, as *et tolm hosast*). The possessive after the nasalising *ul* is left unmutated, as the analyzer reads it and as IV.4's *ul o rask* stands.
- ***re hyl***, "it turned back", is *kyl* softened after *re*: the canon's "curl, turn; hook, turn back", the verb of the turned stone (*tolm kylet*), and the very shape of the Turned Stern's stroke, "a stroke that hooks back on itself".
- **"The same stone" is *et tolm sost***, "the very stone": *sost* "self, very" (*et dask sost*, "the very end"), as IV.4 has *hy et hebbet sost*, "from the gift itself".
- **"To die there" is *dem sennyl lo ull***: the verbal noun carries English's "to" (lexicon §5.3), after *dem* "to, toward, for". *senn* is the canon's "die: go out, as a fire in peace"; its verbal noun takes the slender harmony, *sennyl*. The Orrowen pairs *dem et tolm sost* and *dem sennyl*, "to the very stone, to its dying".

> **Re heth Halvard o, eth re yal o yarlath umo; nath re femm Rhyna o orl ull, hy vodh re yal stinel dhant lunnat ul ey garlremullath; veth re heth ey o hosen re heth o o.**

PST · S·hold · Halvard · it, and · PST · S·be · his · S·hands · upon-it; NEG · PST · touch · Rhyna · it · day · that, from · what · PST · S·be · salt-cuts · open · in · her · palms; but · PST · S·hold · she · it · as · PST · S·hold · he · it.

*Known by Halvard, whose hands were on it; Rhyna had the salt-cuts open in her palms that day and did not touch it, and knew it as he knew it.*

Word for word: *Halvard held it, and his hands were upon it; Rhyna did not touch it that day, for the salt-cuts were open in her palms; but she held it as he held it.*

- **"Known by Halvard" is active**, *Re heth Halvard o*, "Halvard held it", as IV.4's *Re heth Rhyna o* and I.1's *Re hadh Crennel Kael*: Orrowen has no agent after a participle.
- *o yarlath umo*, "his hands upon it": *garlath* softened after the possessive *o*; *umo* is *um* "upon" with its 3sg ending (§3.6).
- ***nath re femm***, "did not touch", is the negative past the lexicon now gives (O2861, *nath re*). IV.4 wrote Halvard's "never" with *nafedh*; here the English is "that day", not "never", so the negative past is the true form.
- **The "and" becomes "for".** English lays the two clauses side by side (she had the cuts open, and did not touch it); Orrowen says why, *hy vodh*, "from what", its "because". *stinel dhant*, "salt-cuts", is the lexicon's (O2376). *ul ey garlremullath*, "in her palms" (*garlremull*, "hand-hollow", broad, so *-ath*): the analyzer does not yet follow a mutated trigger into the next word, so the dual *ul ba yarlremull*, "in two palms", is reported ill-formed there; the plural with *ey* is used instead.
- *veth re heth ey o hosen re heth o o*, "but she held it as he held it", mirrors IV.4's *re heth o o hosen re heth ey o* with the two turned about.

> **Re yal trenn et sceth sost cadhat pynt; re nydhe Halyna et haral ul o rask dhom vess, eth nath re luska sona lom o rytyl veth amm ull.**

PST · S·be · tale · the · hull · itself · laid · quick; PST · weigh-3DU · Halyna · the · law · in · its · S·end · seven · night, and · NEG · PST · allow-3DU · they-two · at-me · its · writing · but · that-when.

*The hull's own tale was quickly told; the law at its end they turned over for seven nights before they would let me write it.*

Word for word: *The tale of the hull itself was laid quickly; Halyna weighed the law at its end seven nights, and let me write it only then.*

- **"Told" is *cadhat*, "laid"**: a tale is laid as a course is laid (*cadh trenn*), and *trenn* is "a tale laid in one night". *trenn et sceth sost*, "the tale of the hull itself", is a construct, so its head takes no article.
- **"Turned over" is *nydh***, the canon's "count; measure, reckon; weigh": the pair weigh the law, as Kael, *Nydherd*, counts a column. *haral* (reserve, "an order; a command; a law") is the law. *dhom vess*, "seven night": a numeral before a singular noun (§3.1), with no preposition for how long, as I.1's *Re orrar bress*, "we walked a month".
- **They are two, and take the dual** (*nydhe*, *luska*), as the formal register requires; and because a dual verb always has its subject (§3.4), the second clause names them with the dual pronoun, *luska sona*. English "they" is Halyna, so the first clause names the pair.
- **"Before they would let me" is *nath … veth amm ull***, "not … but then": §3.8's "only" of a clause (*nath re yorrar veth amm …*, "who fought only when …"), so "they let me write it only then". *lusk lom* + a verbal noun is I.1's "let me" (*Nath re lusk o lom kylyl wrod hyo*); the object pronoun of the verbal noun is its possessor, *o rytyl*, "its writing", as IV.4's *o wrodhol*.

> **Re wrodha Halyna o, hos eth ullen, ul ba luth; re hadh Seren o.**

PST · S·tell-3DU · Halyna · it, one · and · other, in · N·two · ink; PST · S·lay · Seren · it.

*Told by Halyna, by turns, in two inks; set down by Seren.*

- IV.4's formula, word for word. Halyna take the dual and carry their sealing lintel in the leaf-hand; Seren writes her own name with none.

> **Amm re yal et mesk cresket, ell re omm et brodhol, doss o rellorat eth nahlennet.**

when · PST · S·be · the · wood · broken, or · PST · fail · the · telling, is · it · marked · and · unmended.

*Where the wood was broken, or the telling failed, it is marked, and not mended.*

- IV.4's formula, word for word. V.3 has no such gap either; the promise stands on every wood leaf.

## 3 · WHAT THE TRANSLATION DID, AND WHY

**The grain.**

1. **Every sentence is carved, once** (§10.11). One mark to a content word; a referent already cut is pointed at (`@f`) or run to, never cut again in its knowing. **Files are the pronouns**: *we* is file 12, *you* file 0, *our kin* 11, *the roots* 5, *home and the groves* 8; twelve runners end on a file referent.
2. **The heart stays plain** (§3, A9). Rings 1–2 carry no pocket, braid, bind or break, and ring 1 no fringe: "more than a word" is ×3, "a caution" the plain EYE (whose carving is cut whole in ring 7), and ring 2's "where a stone flashes, a gun is", which §18 gives as a cut-pocket, is the wall's own `FLASH ⇒ GUN`. The first pocket is in ring 3, the one braid in ring 5.
3. **Three pockets, three kinds of carrying.** Ring 3 **cites** an older carving by its root (`STERN½@1`, the young wood's *Home when wounded*); ring 7 **cuts whole** a new one (`{ EYE ⇒ BOND+HOLD }`, the heart's *Look before you trust*); ring 10 **says** what the groves learn (a said-pocket with runners among its items). The leaf is about how a knowing travels, and the three pockets are its three ways: remembered, carved, learned.
4. **The dread is the band, not a sign.** Ring 6's "which is only this: that one place in the water had become a place where wood dies" is §9.2's own definition of the DREAD band; the knowing that goes out is one water-place, under the DREAD band, with wood dying on it, `{only}`. There is no fear in it, as the grain has none.
4a. **A knowing that goes out of us is sent, not gone.** Rings 4–6 tell how what the hull knew went out of it, into the roots, to its kin, to every hull. An act is cut on its doer's file (§10.3), and a plain `GO` on ours would say that *we* went; so each is our causative, `>GO` ("we send"), *to* the knowing and *in* to where it went. Only the hull's own journeys (rings 1, 3, 7, 11) are `GO`.
5. **The same wound, the same stone, the same sea.** Two rays (v1's "the same one") join the low wound of ring 2, "where we were struck" of ring 5 and "where it had found us before" of ring 8; a third joins the sea that came in at that wound (ring 2) to the sea still coming in (ring 8), which is "with the sea in us already". The stone keeps its file (0) through every ring, so "the same stone" needs no ray; where a ring's first thing on file 0 would be something else (ring 8's flash), the stone is cut first.
6. **Truth or nothing.** "The guns let us go, or did not see us go. We do not know which" is two questioned marks (A6), let go (`!HOLD?`) and not see (`!EYE?`), not a blind: the wood holds both and does not say which. The likeness of ring 5 is hollow (shown, not meant), and the iron's count in ring 8 is hollow ("as though").
7. **No break, no bind.** V.3 has no thwarting (§7.1 lists none for it) and no two acts bound into one; none was forced.

**Mended from the proof rings** (`coverage/tests/V-3_proof.gn2`; the ones kept are named in §2): the two likeness persons moved from the even files 4 and 6 to the placeless files 1 and 3, with the city on the even file 2 between them; the grey no longer stands on the kin's file by a band alone (a band is not a thing-sign, §11.2), and "back into the grey" is the MIST band over our own going with a runner home; `!NEW+HOLD` is now `NEW+!HOLD`, the mirror on the head as every negated ligature of IV.4; "slowly, as sap goes" keeps the proof's `→as ~SAP+GO`; the heart's carving and the lessons are written in.

**Orrowen.**

- **No new word was needed.** Every word of the frame is already in the lexicon (among the tier-3 and reserve words it uses: *kaelir* wreck, *scethel* one hull of a fleet, *felm* sink, *darrol* a coming, *grestet* wounded, *femm* touch, *stinel dhant* salt-cuts, *garlremull* palm, *haral* law, *nydh* weigh, *lusk* let, *rytyl* writing, *orl ull* that day, *nath re* did not, *Vess Vollol* and *Kaelir sa re vymm dem varn*, the two titles). `wf8/additions/W05.tsv` therefore holds no rows.

- **Two analyzer limits steered the wording**, both flagged for the tool's owner: a mutated trigger (*ba*, nasalised *pa*) is not followed into the next word, so the grammatical *ul ba yarlremull* "in two palms" reports ill-formed and *ul ey garlremullath* is written; and a possessive after *ul* is left unmutated, as IV.4 already follows (*ul o rarrol*, *ul o rask*).

**Findings for the spec and the validator** (none blocks anything; they are also in `wf8/additions/W05_grain.md`).

- **A9 misses one V.3 heart item**: ring 2's "where a stone flashes, a gun is" (§18's cut-pocket) falls in band I and needs the plain cut used here.
- **The register's `!~` ("silent and not empty") reads backwards in the validator** ("a seeming no stone"). This leaf uses `!SPENT` instead.
- **A pocket placed across files whose cells a later knowing fills** is rejected by the packer (K06) rather than lifted to the next year; the carving of ring 7 was therefore set on files 1–2, not across our own file.
- **Rays in celled rings.** `ray 12: r5/1–r8/1` names a year but not a cell; ring 8's year holds four of our marks. The renderer should run a ray to the mark of the same sign kind (here the WOUND), and the notation may want a cell (`r8/1.2`). The same holds for `ray 4: r2/1–r8/1` (to the TIDE).

**What the back-translation mended** (`wf8/backtrans/W05.md`; the leaf was read back blind from its native lines, without Seren's `# file` tags, as IV.4's was).
