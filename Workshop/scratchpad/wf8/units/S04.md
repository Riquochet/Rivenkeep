## II.3 · THE LEAF

### Title

> ## *Lodhan eth Tolmath Vardh*

**II.3 · Of the Bonded and the Theoliths**

*The Bonded and the stones of God.* (The lexicon's title, O1225. *Tolmath Vardh* is a construct, so it never takes the article.)

### Seren's headnote

> Re hadha Halyna lo et tevath lo et odh, ul surr hosast et tynur, hos eth ullen, trenn eth trenn, hosen brodha sona o lo dhevath gor fedh. Hosel ul sona wrodhol sy re luska sona lom rellorol cedh re wrodh gor dhrenn, hy vodh vallant et tevath seskyl. Re wrodha sona o rask umom, eth re rytym o hosen re vyske sona o.

*Laid by Halyna for the children at the hearth in the first year of the siege, by turns, a paragraph each, as they always tell it to children. It is the one telling of theirs in which they let me mark whose paragraph is whose, for the children like to know. The last of it is about me, and I have written it as they said it.*

Word for word: *Halyna laid [it] for the children at the hearth, in the first year of the siege, the one and the other, paragraph and paragraph, as they two always tell it to children. Only in this telling of theirs did they two let me the marking of who told each paragraph, for the children love knowing. They two told its end about me, and I wrote it as they two said it.*

- ***Re hadha Halyna***, "Halyna laid", is the headnote's active shape (pilot I.1), with the dual: Halyna are two (§3.9).
- ***hos eth ullen, trenn eth trenn***: "by turns" is pilot IV.4's; "a paragraph each" is *trenn eth trenn*, "course and course", built as I.1's *hos eth hos* (an addition). A paragraph is a course.
- ***hosen brodha sona o***: a broad dual verb sounds like the plural imperative (*brodha*, "tell!"), and "a dual verb always has a subject" (§3.4), so Seren writes *sona*, "they two", after it. She does this after every broad dual in the leaf.
- "whose paragraph is whose" is ***cedh re wrodh gor dhrenn***, "who told each paragraph": an embedded question, as I.1's *hunna cedh re dhess*. *Gor* softens *trenn*.
- "The last of it is about me" is ***Re wrodha sona o rask umom***, "they told its end about me": the bite on *rask* (S·*dask*) marks *o* as "its".

### Halyna's telling, to the children

*Two inks, by turns; the Book marks each paragraph with its teller's name, and so does Seren here.*

> **Rhyna.** Darra virr, gor dhev, eth reska ul vodh doss veskyl los. Doss et trenn sy lo dhevath, eth doss lunn rernyl lo et hemman, amm dossant velv.

**Rhyna.** Come closer, all of you, and sit where you can see. This is a tale for the young, and the old may listen if they are quiet.

Word for word: *Come near, every child, and sit where seeing is at you. This tale is the children's, and the leave of listening is at the old, if they are quiet.*

- ***gor dhev***, "every child" (*gor* softens *tev*), for "all of you".
- ***Doss et trenn sy lo dhevath***, "this tale is at the children": the "at" idiom of having (§3.7). The tale belongs to them.
- ***doss lunn rernyl lo et hemman***, "the leave (freedom) of listening is at the old": the lexicon's *doss lunn lo*, "may".

> **Halvard.** New Stonwrytan gor Orrowan, veth ew Orrowan gor Stonwrytan. El galat hosast ull; ketha o, eth nath luska o. Re vellor nahos fedh ul et Stonwrytan lo dhevyl, hosen ul lest. Re voll et Stonwrytan tevath ul so dhevowath, hos hy theld sy eth hos hy theld ull, ul et Orrow: mest hy lorr Stonwrytan, el venn; veth merr hy lesteleth sugornath, eth merr hy hellurath vaskornath; eth hos fedh, myskent et ryteth surr, hy lest vernul ul Gethow Stellath. Ew stathul strom et vollol, eth ul ryteth surr et Stonwrytan re nahebb odha ell gedh o nafedh. Hy vodh re heth et Stonwrytan vennoth Orrow hosen re heth haldath Orrow, eth re ammad o ul lesteth Vardh sa re yald; eth tev vollat demont, tev vollat dem bana.

**Halvard.** Not all Shorelanders were Stonewrights, yet all Stonewrights were Shorelanders. That is the first thing, and you must hold on to it. The guild was never a house you were born into. Children were called to it in their cradles, from any household in the land: most from Stonewright blood, it is true, but some from fishers' huts and some from merchants' halls, and once, the annals say, from a gaoler's house in Carnhold. It was a great honour to be called, and no mother or father in all the annals of the guild ever refused it. For the Stonewrights kept the faith of the Shorelands as they kept its walls, and taught it in the temples they raised, and a child called to them was called to both.

Word for word: *Not Stonewrights were all the Shore-folk, but Shore-folk were all the Stonewrights. That is the first thing; hold it, and do not let it go. No one ever came into the guild by birth, as into a house. The guild called children in their cradles, one from this household and one from that, in the land of the Shore: many from the blood of the guild, it is true; but some from the huts of fishers, and some from the halls of merchants; and once, say the annals, from the house of a gaoler in Carnhold. The calling was a great honour, and in the annals of the guild no mother or father refused it, ever. For the guild kept the faith of the Shore as it kept the walls of the Shore, and taught it in the houses of God that it raised; and a child called to them, a child called to both.*

- **The chiasmus is kept**: *New Stonwrytan gor Orrowan, veth ew Orrowan gor Stonwrytan*, the copula's past negative against its past, the predicate first each time.
- ***ketha o, eth nath luska o***, "hold it, and do not let it go": the imperative, where the English has "you must hold on to it". It is the shape of the phrasebook's *Keth et tolm. Keth o …*, Halyna's own words to the child Seren.
- "a house you were born into" is recast: ***Re vellor nahos fedh ul et Stonwrytan lo dhevyl, hosen ul lest***, "no one ever came into the guild by birth, as into a house". A copula with a relative clause on its predicate would leave *et Stonwrytan* stranded at the end of the clause.
- "any household in the land" is ***hos hy theld sy eth hos hy theld ull***, "one from this household and one from that": *gor* after a preposition would have to be softened or bedded (*hy yor*, *ul nor*), which the grammar does not settle (pilot I.1 §3).
- ***el venn***, "it is true", is Halvard's aside, three syllables where the English has four words.
- ***Gethow Stellath***: Carnhold (*Kethow Stellath*) bedded after *ul*. The broad *-ath* is the canon's own slip, which the analyzer notes and does not fault.
- ***re nahebb odha ell gedh o nafedh***: "no mother or father ever refused it" as a plain past with *nafedh*, "never" (pilot IV.4).
- **The last clause is verbless**, as a proverb is: *tev vollat demont, tev vollat dem bana*, "a child called to them, a child called to both", in the shape of *Grest um hos, grest um vana*. *Pana* is bedded after *dem* (*bana*).

> **Rhyna.** Re orrant veskerdeth et Stonwrytan ul molt et tevowath, eth re veskent et tevath. Re heth et Stonwrytan, eth keth tul: re lodh Mardh gor yanna amm nath re hossa sona tul; eth nath re naelurant et veskerdeth, veth re veskent galat sa re yal rytet tevar. Amm re vesk veskerd pa voll olen, re yala pa dhev cadhat ul hos tevow; eth re yalda pa theld so, hos eth ullen, ul hos lest, eth hy ull lo et ullen. Voll et Stonwrytan et lodhol ull: Lodh Helv; eth et pa dhev sa lodh o: Lodhan Helv, ell hosel et Lodhan; eth es vollan so hosen ull. Ew hosen ull lo Halvard eth Rhyna, sa nath re aedhe hos vess ul ba lest, ul sona hossol tunn. Eth rellora sy, hy vodh es maverys o hy ull: doss et lodh naelurat ul et tevow, veth gald et hoskol hosel o tunn.

**Rhyna.** The seers of the guild went among the cradles and read the children. The Stonewrights held, and hold still, that God had joined each pair before either drew breath, and that the seers did not choose, but only read what was already written. When a seer had read two names together, the two children were laid in one cradle, and both families raised them, turn and turn about, in one house and then the other. The guild calls that joining the Aetherbond, and the two it joins the Aetherbonded, or simply the Bonded; and so shall we. So it was with Halvard and Rhyna, who have never in their lives slept a night under different roofs. And mark this, for you will need it later: the bond is ordained in the cradle, but it is made whole only at the sealing.

Word for word: *The seers of the guild went among the cradles, and read the children. The guild held, and holds still: God joined every pair when they two had not yet breathed; and the seers did not choose, but read the thing that was written already. When a seer read two names together, the two children were laid in one cradle; and the two families raised them, the one and the other, in one house, and then at the other. The guild names that joining: the Aetherbond; and the two children that it joins: the Aetherbonded, or simply the Bonded; and we two will name them so. It was like that with Halvard and Rhyna, who did not sleep one night in two houses in their whole life. And mark this, for you will need it after: the bond is ordained in the cradle, but the sealing alone makes it whole.*

- "held … that": Orrowen has no "that" before a clause (pilot I.1), so the guild's belief follows a colon.
- "before either drew breath" is ***amm nath re hossa sona tul***, "when they two had not yet breathed": *nath … tul* is the canon's "not yet".
- **"Choose" and "ordain" are one verb, *naelur*.** God chooses; the seers *nath re naelurant*, did not choose. Rhyna's "ordained" a few lines on is *naelurat*, "chosen".
- ***gor yanna***, "every pair": *ganna* (the gate, the two posts) softened after *gor*.
- "raised them" is ***re yalda***, *gald*: the verb that raises walls raises children too.
- ***Lodh Helv*** (canon) and ***Lodhan Helv***, "those of the mortar of the sky", for the Book's "the Aetherbonded" beside the short ***et Lodhan***. The canon lists both English words under *et Lodhan*; the full name is an addition, built as *Lodh Helv*.
- ***eth es vollan so hosen ull***, "and we two will name them so": Halyna's "we" is dual throughout.
- "under different roofs" is ***ul ba lest***, "in two houses", built as IV.4's *ul ba luth*, "in two inks": *pa* bedded after *ul*.
- "made whole only at the sealing" is ***gald et hoskol hosel o tunn***, "the sealing alone makes it whole": *hosel* after a noun is "alone, only"; after a verb it would read "alone" of the verb (§3.8). *O tunn* is object and complement, so *tunn* is not softened.

> **Halvard.** Amm re dhevent et tevath lodhat dem gethyl wresk, re hebbent so dem Dolmath Vardh. Re yalant Tolmath Vardh lodhat, pa eth pa, prassath strom ul et Stonwrytan; re harm et Stonwrytan so hy so yaldatath fedh lyss, eth re ammadant. Re ammadant et tevath: stinyl, eth cadhol, eth rhynyl, eth ammelyl, eth kethyl nydhyl et tolmath. Eth re ammadant so et ryt. Re vollant Tolmath Vardh o: Ryt Dhunnoth; eth nath re vyskent galat ullen umo lo dhevath; eth nath gadhan galat ullen umo. Es hyrrilys o, amm doss o los. Ew Idrenna olna Dholmath Vardh, sa rossa ul reskyl lo et gebb vess sy, eth es ferre amm brodhan venn.

**Halvard.** When the bonded children were old enough to hold a chisel, they were given to the Theoliths. The Theoliths were bonded pairs themselves, great masters of the guild, called away from their works for a season to teach. They taught the children to cut and to lay and to set, and to pray, and to keep the count of the stones. And they taught them the vow. The Theoliths named the vow the Covenant of Completion, and they would say no more of it to children, and neither will we. You will understand it when it is yours to understand. Our own Theoliths were Idrenna, who are sitting by the door tonight, and will nod when we tell it right.

Word for word: *When the bonded children grew to the holding of a chisel, they gave them to the Theoliths. The Theoliths were bonded, two and two, great masters in the guild; the guild called them from their works for a short season, and they taught. They taught the children: cutting, and laying, and setting, and praying, and keeping the count of the stones. And they taught them the vow. The Theoliths named it: the Covenant of Completion; and they said no other thing about it to children; and we two lay no other thing upon it. You will understand it when it is yours. Idrenna were our Theoliths, who are sitting at the door tonight, and will bow when we two tell it true.*

- "old enough to hold a chisel" is ***dem gethyl wresk***, "to the holding of a chisel": the object of a verbal noun is its softened possessor (pilot I.1, G3), and *dem* beds *kethyl*.
- ***Dolmath Vardh, Dholmath Vardh***: *Tolmath Vardh* bedded after *dem*, softened after *olna*. It is a construct and never takes the article, so a preposition falls on *tolmath* itself.
- "bonded pairs themselves" is ***lodhat, pa eth pa***, "bonded, two and two" (an addition, as *hos eth hos*). *Tolmath Vardh sost* would say "the stones of God himself".
- "for a season" is ***fedh lyss***, "a small time" (an addition): *hos fedh* would be heard as "once".
- "to set" is ***rhynyl***, the verb in Rhyna's name, "she who sets fast" (§4.6). Halvard says it.
- ***Ryt Dhunnoth*** is canon, and its words stay withheld: Halvard names it and stops.
- "and neither will we" is ***eth nath gadhan galat ullen umo***, "and we two lay no other thing upon it". *Nath mysk-* would be heard as *nath* + *bysk*, "tie", and *nath* + *brodh* would begin *mr-* (pilot I.1 §3), so Halyna *lay* it, the tongue's own verb for telling.
- "when it is yours to understand" is ***amm doss o los***, "when it is at you": the "at" idiom of having.
- ***sa rossa ul reskyl***, "who (two) are sitting": the relative softens the state verb, *doss* > *ross* (grammar note 2 below). ***es ferre***, "will bow, nod" (*ferr*), in the dual.

> **Rhyna.** Amm tevent tevath lodhat dem susk, dossant hoskat; eth mysk et hoskol ul garm galat sa re vysk et tevow hosast, eth nayalat ullen. Voll et Stonwrytan o: et hoskol; eth hy orl ull doss et lodh tunn. Lo et hoskol hebb et Stonwrytan hos voll lonta, galdat hy vana, hosen doss ganna galdat hy pa yann eth hy dholm cadhat umonta; eth lo voll ull vollant so gor fedh hy ull, veth dossant vollath et tevow kethet. Hosen ull re yala Halvard eth Rhyna hoskat, eth vollat Halyna; eth lo voll ull seskys olna. Hy ull el humm Hyna Halvard, eth el verr Halvard Rhyna; eth hy dhevow ew hos ganna sona, eth hy hoskol hosel humm eth verr.

**Rhyna.** When bonded children come of age they are wed, and the wedding says aloud only what the cradle said first. The guild calls it the sealing, and from that day the bond is whole. At the sealing the guild gives the two of them one name, made of both, as a gate is made of two posts and the stone laid across them; and by that name they are called ever after, though the names of the cradle are kept. In that way Halvard and Rhyna were sealed, and named Halyna, and by that name you know us. So Halvard is Rhyna's husband and Rhyna is Halvard's wife, and they were one pair long before they were either.

Word for word: *When bonded children grow up, they are sealed; and the sealing says aloud the thing that the cradle said first, and nothing else. The guild names it: the sealing; and from that day the bond is whole. At the sealing the guild gives one name to the two of them, made from both, as a gate is made from two posts and from a stone laid upon the two; and by that name they call them always after, though the names of the cradle are kept. So Halvard and Rhyna were sealed, and named Halyna; and by that name you know us two. So the husband of Rhyna is Halvard, and the wife of Halvard is Rhyna; and from the cradle they were one pair, and from the sealing alone husband and wife.*

- "come of age" is ***tev dem susk***, "grow to the height" (an addition, from *dem susk*, "up").
- "only what the cradle said first" is ***galat sa re vysk et tevow hosast, eth nayalat ullen***, "the thing the cradle said first, and nothing else".
- **The gate explains itself.** *Ganna* is an old dual, "the two posts" (§3.2), so ***hosen doss ganna galdat hy pa yann eth hy dholm cadhat umonta***, "as a gate is made from two posts and a stone laid upon the two", is the word taken apart: *pa* softens *gann* to *yann*, *hy* softens *tolm* to *dholm*, *umonta* is "upon them two". It is the rule of the pair-name (§3.10).
- ***humm Hyna Halvard … verr Halvard Rhyna***: the copula's predicate first, and the two constructs laid crosswise. *Hyna* is *Rhyna* softened as a possessor (*rh* > *h*), as her name is softened inside *Halyna*.
- "they were one pair long before they were either" is ***hy dhevow ew hos ganna sona, eth hy hoskol hosel humm eth verr***, "from the cradle they were one pair, and only from the sealing husband and wife": the Orrowen counts from the cradle, where the English says "long before".

> **Halvard.** Re hoss et Lodhan hosen hos, eth re yald hosen hos, eth re ammel hosen hos; eth re yald o nawrodh. Re veskys Halyna um et hald ul gethyl. Re veskys o: nath garm hos lo et ullen. Nath mest galdat yanna hosen sy pa fedh, veth delvoth, hosen re nydhent prassath hemm o; eth ew galdat yanna hosen sy gor hald strom eth gor lest Vardh sa dhenth ul et noth cumm. Voll et Stonwrytan uld yanna: Hemm; eth essa yanna: Hemma; eth el hos trenn pa voll, hy vodh re ston et Hemma hosen tunn. Hy ull el Hemm Halvard o, eth el Hemma Rhyna ey; eth nel hemm hy et ullen o ell ey. Nath re hadh ey tolm pawast fedh.

**Halvard.** The Bonded lived as one, and worked as one, and worshipped as one, and they did it without words. You have seen Halyna on the wall in the holding. You have seen that neither calls to the other. The work of such a pair is not doubled but multiplied twelvefold, as the old masters counted it, and every great wall and soaring temple of the ten havens was the work of such pairs. The guild calls the man of a pair Elder and the woman Elderess, and the two names are one rank, for the Elderess stood equal in all things. So he is Elder Halvard and she is Elderess Rhyna, and neither is the elder of the other. There was never a stone she laid second.

Word for word: *The Bonded lived as one, and worked as one, and prayed as one; and did it mute. You have seen Halyna on the wall in the holding. You have seen it: one does not call to the other. The work of such a pair does not grow two times, but twelvefold, as the old masters counted it; and the work of such a pair was every great wall and every house of God that soars, in the ten havens. The guild names the man of a pair: Elder; and the woman of a pair: Elderess; and the two names are one course, for the Elderess stood equal, wholly. So he is Elder Halvard, and she is Elderess Rhyna; and neither he nor she is older than the other. She never laid a stone second.*

- ***Re hoss et Lodhan hosen hos***: *et Lodhan* is a collective and takes the singular verb (pilot I.1, G2), so "the Bonded lived as one" is said in the grammar as well as the words.
- "without words" is ***nawrodh***, "mute": the word of the mute stones.
- ***Nath mest … pa fedh, veth delvoth***, "does not grow two times, but twelvefold": *delvoth* is canon, and *mest* is "multiply".
- "one rank" is ***hos trenn***, "one course": a rank of the guild is a course of the wall.
- "equal in all things" is ***hosen tunn***, "equal, wholly": *ul gor yalat* would need *gor* bedded after *ul*.
- "neither is the elder of the other": *hemm* is "old" and "Elder" at once, so the English pun is the tongue's own plain sense.
- ***Nath re hadh ey tolm pawast fedh***, "she never laid a stone second": *ey* never mutates, which is why "her hand" below is simply *ey garl*.

> **Rhyna.** Galat sa ledh hos, ledh ullen o. Amm stin Rhyna ey garl ul et taldow withlern, ledh Halvard o um et hald wridlern, eth sesk o vodh garl. Galat sa haever hos, re haever ullen o, tevar. Pa sa el hos: el gor yalat lodh ull. Re yal myskyl umo lo vrassath hemm, eth pawast es hunnos o ul et trenneth sy. Grest um hos, grest um vana.

**Rhyna.** What the one feels, the other feels. If Rhyna cuts her hand in the south tower, Halvard feels it on the north wall, and knows which hand. What the one thinks, the other has thought already. Two made one: that is the whole of the bond. The old masters had a saying for it, and you will hear it again before these tales are done. Harm to one is harm to both.

Word for word: *The thing that one feels, the other feels it. When Rhyna cuts her hand in the tower of the south, Halvard feels it on the wall of the north, and knows what hand. The thing that one thinks, the other thought it, already. Two that are one: that is the whole of the bond. A saying about it was at the old masters, and a second time you will hear it in these tales. Harm to one is harm to both.*

- **Fronted objects picked up by *o*** (pilot I.1, G6): *Galat sa ledh hos, ledh ullen o*. The relative softens its verb (*sa haever*, from *kaever*).
- ***withlern, wridlern***: *bithlern* "the south", *bridlern* "the north", softened as possessors (*b* > *w*, as canon *nawrodhat*).
- ***re haever ullen o, tevar***: the comma keeps *tevar* "already" from being read as *o*'s possession (grammar note 5).
- "Two made one" is ***Pa sa el hos***, "two that are one": *pa* before a participle would soften it into a noun ("two works").
- "The old masters had a saying for it" is the "at" idiom, ***re yal myskyl umo lo vrassath hemm*** (*prassath* softened after *lo*).
- ***Grest um hos, grest um vana.*** is canon (§11.7), verbless, and split at its wedge for two voices.

> **Halvard.** Doss et Lodhan hy ullen merr fedheth, eth deld nafedh. Nath re yal lodh cresket fedh. Nath noss o ul ryteth surr, nath hos fedh, ul desketh et Stonwrytan. Treskol, nath re lusk Mardh o.

**Halvard.** The Bonded are seldom apart, and never far. No bond was ever broken. It is not in the annals, not once, in all the generations of the guild. Separation, God did not permit.

Word for word: *The Bonded are apart few times, and far never. A bond was not ever broken. It is not in the annals, not once, in the generations of the guild. Separation, God did not let it.*

- ***merr fedheth***, "seldom", and ***hy ullen***, "apart", are the lexicon's.
- ***Treskol, nath re lusk Mardh o.*** The object is fronted and taken up by *o*, as the English puts "Separation" first. *Treskol* is *tresk*, the word of *Covv Treskat* and of *Kethow Dhresk*: the one riving God did not permit.

> **Rhyna.** Eth hy ull senn et Lodhan olen. Nath gadhan los vess sy galat gunn hy ull; eth cadhan sy venn, hy vodh nath gadh o lomm umona. Amm doss hos hy et Lodhan hurrat, lo ommol ell lo suress ell lo et wadh, nath hoss ullen sell hy ull. Nath noss grestet um et hos sa hoss, eth nath noss cathal umo. Cresk et molt, hy vodh sesk o; eth sol et derdsast, hy vodh doss o yaldat tunn; eth senne sona ul sollan. Amm senn et garl ullen, ho nath senn et garl venn? Myskent uldath: thell orl, eth nath re hoss hos hosel fedh sell hy ull; veth mysk et Stonwrytan: ew clemeth; eth sesk o, hy vodh keth et Stonwrytan et nydhyl. Re hossant mest hy et Lodhan ul hos clemel so hoss dasken, bost lusul hy ullen; eth re sesk gor hos orrol ullen, eth re fenn.

**Rhyna.** And so the Bonded die together. That is the deepest thing we will tell you tonight, and we tell it plainly, for it is not a sad thing to us. When one of the Bonded is killed, by a fall or a fever or the sea, the other does not live long after. There is no wound on the one who is left, and no sickness. The heart breaks, for it knows; and the body stills, for its work is done; and they die in peace. When the left hand dies, does not the right one die too? Eight days, men say, is the longest that ever one lived on alone; but the Stonewrights say it was hours, and they should know, for they keep the count. Many have drawn their last breath in the same moment, a mile apart, and each knew the other was going, and went too.

Word for word: *And so the Bonded die together. We two lay for you tonight no thing deeper than that; and we two lay this plumb, for it lays no grief upon us two. When one of the Bonded is killed, by a fall or by a fever or by the sea, the other does not live long after that. There is no wound upon the one who lives on, and no sickness upon him. The heart breaks, for it knows; and the body goes still, for its work is whole; and they two die in peace. When the left hand dies, does not the right hand die? Men say: eight days, and no one alone ever lived longer than that; but the Stonewrights say: it was hours; and they know, for the Stonewrights keep the count. Many of the Bonded breathed their last breath in one moment, a mile apart; and each knew the going of the other, and followed.*

- "we tell it plainly" is ***cadhan sy venn***, "we two lay this plumb": Kael's words in I.1 (*es gadhom sy venn*).
- "it is not a sad thing to us" is ***nath gadh o lomm umona***, "it lays no grief upon us two": a feeling is laid on a person (§3.7), and it is the same verb.
- "the one who is left" is ***et hos sa hoss***, "the one who lives on" (*hoss* is "breathe, live, outlive"); *luskat* "left" would read "released".
- "its work is done" is ***doss o yaldat tunn***, "its work is whole": *tunn* is the word of the Covenant of Completion, *Ryt Dhunnoth*.
- **Jack's line**, *When the left hand dies, does not the right one die too?*, is ***Amm senn et garl ullen, ho nath senn et garl venn?***: the left is "the other hand", the right "the true hand" (both additions, from the lexicon's own *ullen* and *venn*). The negative question, *ho nath*, is an addition (grammar note 3); the English "too" is carried by the question itself.
- "they should know" is ***eth sesk o***, "and it knows": the guild is one, and takes the singular.
- "each knew the other was going, and went too" is ***re sesk gor hos orrol ullen, eth re fenn***, "each knew the going of the other, and followed": *fenn*, the word Halvard uses next of the one who does *not* follow.

> **Halvard.** Amm sy et galat dasken, eth hy ull dem lodull. Fedheth senn hos hy dhevow tevel, amm nath nossa sona hoskat tul; eth hy ull nath fenn ullen, veth hoss o, hy vodh nath re yal et lodh tunn tul. Voll et Stonwrytan tev hosen sy: nalodhat. El merr o; veth hy et Ommol nel merr o hosen re yal o, hy vodh re hurr et orrol sell tevath. Lo dhernil grike ganna lodhat tev nalodhat lo sona odh, hosen sullast et odh; eth doss o lonta. Keth et nalodhat hos voll dem sennyl, hy vodh nath noss hoskol sa hebb ullen lo; eth ul molt et Stonwrytan el lommol hos voll. Re vyskent prassath hemm: nath lymm Mardh et hos, veth amm lusk galdat lo et ullen.

**Halvard.** Now the last thing, and then to bed. Sometimes one of a cradle dies young, before ever the two are sealed; and then the other does not follow, but lives, for the bond was not yet whole. The guild calls such a child unbonded. It is rare, and since the Fall it is less rare, for the long road killed children. By custom an unbonded child is taken into the hearth of a bonded pair as its third, and is theirs. The unbonded keep one name all their lives, for there is no sealing to give them another; and among the guild one name is a mourning. The old masters said that God does not take the one without leaving the other a work to do.

Word for word: *Now the last thing, and then to bed. Sometimes one of a cradle dies young, when they two are not yet sealed; and then the other does not follow, but lives, for the bond was not yet whole. The guild names such a child: unbonded. It is rare; but since the Fall it is not as rare as it was, for the long road killed children. By rite a bonded pair takes an unbonded child at their hearth, as the third of the hearth; and it is theirs. The unbonded one keeps one name until death, for there is no sealing that gives him another; and among the guild one name is a mourning. The old masters said: God does not carry off the one, but when he leaves a work at the other.*

- ***nath nossa sona hoskat tul***, "they two are not yet sealed": *doss* bedded after *nath*, in the dual, with its subject.
- ***et Ommol***, "the Fall" ("the falling", an addition).
- "less rare" is ***nel merr o hosen re yal o***, "it is not as rare as it was": the lexicon compares with *hy* "than", but has no "less".
- ***Lo dhernil***, "by custom" (*ternil* softened after *lo*). "Into the hearth" is ***lo sona odh***, "at their hearth": after *ul* or *dem*, *odh* would be bedded to *modh*.
- "and is theirs" is ***doss o lonta***, "it is at them two".
- **The old masters' saying** is an "only" of a clause, ***nath … veth*** (pilot I.1, G8): "God does not carry off the one but when he leaves a work to the other". "Take" is *lymm*, "carry": *grik* bedded after *nath* would begin *nr-*.

> **Rhyna.** El olna sullast Seren, sa ross ul rytyl sy. Re yala ey eth Tarnel cadhat ul hos tevow: Tarnel, hass sugorn ul Gemm Hyll, sa re voll et Stonwrytan. Re senn o ul hiskenneth um et orrol sell, hy et brid, ul et vess; eth re yern ey lo o sest. Hy ull re hoss ey, eth el hos ey; eth amm re orr ey dem susk et brunn, eth nath re yal o loy, re riken ey lo olna odh.

**Rhyna.** Seren, who is writing this down, is our third. She was laid in one cradle with Tarnel, a fisher's son of Tidesmeet whom the guild called. He died in the passes on the long road, of the cold, in the night, and she woke beside him. So she lived, and she is one; and when she had come up the mountain without him, we took her to our hearth.

Word for word: *Our third is Seren, who is writing this. She and Tarnel were laid in one cradle: Tarnel, a fisher's son in Tidesmeet, whom the guild called. He died in the passes on the long road, from the cold, in the night; and she woke at his shoulder. So she lived, and she is one; and when she went up the mountain, and he was not with her, we two took her in at our hearth.*

- ***sa ross ul rytyl sy***, "who is writing this": the relative softens *doss*. IV.3 says it of Seren in the same words.
- ***Re yala ey eth Tarnel***: "she and Tarnel" as the subject of a dual, which keeps *Tarnel* from being softened after *lo*.
- ***ul Gemm Hyll***: Tidesmeet (*Cemm Hyll*) bedded after *ul*. It is homophonous with *gemm* "four", as the canon's *vess* "night" is with softened *pess* "asked"; the capital and the context carry it. *Hy* or *lo* would give *Hemm Hyll*, which clashes with *hemm* "old".
- ***el hos ey***, "she is one": *hos*, the number. Her name is "a single sorrow that overcomes".
- "without him" is ***eth nath re yal o loy***, "and he was not with her": the lexicon's *nath … lo*.

> **Halvard.** Nath noss kethyl et mesk loy hosen doss lona. Nath geth hos prener kethyl; kethe ganna o ul sona lodh, hosen kethe pa yarl tolm sa nath noss o yaldol lo hos garl. Hy ull ryt ey galat sa wrodhan, eth ryt ey venn hyona. El ey mammol ull, eth nel lyss o. Veth hunna galat sa hebb o loy. Nath senn ey hy sennyl ullen. Doss orrol loy, hosel, ul vodh nath noss orrol lona; eth doss seryl et flennath sy loy amm sennen. Re heth et Lodhan et odh eth et flenn gor fedh; eth re yal lodhat gor ryterd et Stonwrytan demoy. Amm re orr ganna, re orr sona flennath dem nelter ul sona sestow, eth re wrodh nahos hyo pawast; hy vodh el luth hosel flennath sa wrodh nahos. Doss maver hossol sell hy o wrodhardath um et flennath sy. Hy ull el Seren sa ryt o.

**Halvard.** She cannot know the wood as we do. A knowing is too great for one mind to hold; a pair holds it between them, as two hands hold a stone that one hand cannot lift. So she writes down what we tell, and she writes it truer than we could. That is her loss, and it is not small. But hear what it gives her. She will not die of another's death. She can go where we cannot, alone, and carry this Book on after us. The Bonded have always been the keepers of the hearth and of the leaf, and every scribe of the guild before her was bonded. When a pair went, their book went into the vault behind them, and no one told from it again; for a book that no one tells is only ink. This Book must outlive its tellers. That is why it is Seren who writes it.

Word for word: *The holding of the wood is not at her as it is at us two. One mind does not hold a knowing; a pair holds it in their mortar, as two hands hold a stone whose raising is not at one hand. So she writes the thing that we two tell, and she writes truer than we two. That is her loss, and it is not small. But hear what it gives her. She does not die of the death of another. The going is at her, alone, where the going is not at us two; and the carrying on of this Book is at her when we two die. The Bonded kept the hearth and the leaf always; and every scribe of the guild was bonded, until her. When a pair went, their Book went to the vault behind them, and no one told from it again; for a Book that no one tells is ink alone. A living longer than its tellers is a need upon this Book. So it is Seren who writes it.*

- "She cannot know the wood" is the canon's own shape (Seren's line, §11.4): ***Nath noss kethyl et mesk loy***. To know the wood is to hold it.
- "a pair holds it between them" is ***kethe ganna o ul sona lodh***, "in their mortar": *ul lodh* is "between" (what lies between stones), and *lodh* is also the bond.
- ***pa yarl***, "two hands" (canon), take the dual verb *kethe*; "cannot lift" is ***nath noss o yaldol lo hos garl***, "its raising is not at one hand".
- "carry this Book on after us" is ***seryl et flennath sy … amm sennen***, "the carrying-on of this Book … when we two die". *Ser*, "go on, carry forward", is the word the guild hears in her name (§4.6); its verbal noun *seryl* is an addition.
- "every scribe of the guild before her" is ***… demoy***, "until her".
- ***el luth hosel flennath sa wrodh nahos***, "a Book that no one tells is ink alone": the copula's predicate (*luth hosel*) first.
- "This Book must outlive its tellers" is pilot IV.4's "must", ***Doss maver [verbal noun] um …***, with the comparison of §3.6: ***hossol sell hy o wrodhardath***, "a living longer than its tellers" (*sell hy*, "longer than", or "long after", as in *nath hoss ullen sell hy ull*; either way the Book outlives them). *(Back-translation fix.)* The first draft had *hossol o wrodhardath*, meant as "the outliving of its tellers"; but *hoss* is first "breathe, live", so its possessor reads as its subject, and a blind reader heard "this Book needs the life (the breath) of its tellers", the opposite of the English.
- ***Hy ull el Seren sa ryt o***: the cleft of §3.1, "it is Seren who writes it".

### The close, said together

> *Eth re vyske et dasken sy olen, hosen daska sona o gor fedh:*

*And this last they said together, as they always end it:*

Word for word: *And they two said this last together, as they two always end it:*

- Seren's stage line, in her one ink. "End" is *dask* as a verb (the lexicon has *daskol*, *daskat*).

> Re lodh tevyl sona; nath re yal sona dhreskol lo sennyl.

Birth united them; death could not separate them.

Word for word: *Birth bonded them two; their separating was not at death.*

- "united" is ***re lodh***: the verb of the bond itself, *lodh*, "mortar; join".
- "could not separate" is the "at" idiom in the past, ***nath re yal … lo sennyl*** (§3.7): "their separating was not at death", death could not do it. *Treskol* is softened as *sona*'s possession (*dhreskol*).
- They say it together, so Seren's two inks lie on it together; how the builder draws that is the builder's (the Book: "this last they said together").

> Cadhan o hosen re yal cadhat lona.

This we lay as it was laid for us.

Word for word: *We two lay it as it was laid at us two.*

- **The hearth formula in the dual**, because Halyna say it: see note 1 below. The lexicon's O0234 is the plural, *Cadhar o hosen re yal cadhat lor*.

> *Eth re vysk et odh:* **Tumar.**

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

- The lexicon's formula (O0564), exactly as pilot I.1 has it: *Tumar* is "we remember", and yes.

## IV.3 · THE LEAF

### Title

> ## *Gebb et Pard*

**IV.3 · The Warden's Door**

*The door of the warden.* (The lexicon's title, O0697. Pilot IV.4's headnote names this leaf in the same words, softened as a possessor: *Yebb et Pard*.)

### Seren's headnote

> Et tessyl sa re wrodh Brenn Reldvar, et uld sa re rik et tolm, hosen re hadha ganna lodhat et Stonwrytan ul Davow Hemm o, hy o wrodow, ul hemmyth; eth re hoska sona o ul vestul et rytow, lo dholmath nawrodh. Re veske Halyna o ul garm lo et odh, ul Hyll Dhumol, hos eth ullen, trenn eth trenn; eth re hadha sona ryt surr ul o sestow. El brodath Wrenn et brodath ul lern ryt surr, eth nath re hlennym so. Doss flenn et mesk ul o lern.

*The confession of Brenn of the outpost, the man who picked up the stone, as a bonded pair of the guild at Eldhythe set it down from his mouth in his old age and sealed it in the archive chest with the mute stones. Halyna read it aloud at the hearth in the Tide of Remembering, by turns, a paragraph each, and laid the closing annal after it. The words before the annal are his, and I have not mended them. The wood's leaf faces it.*

Word for word: *The confession that Brenn of the Outpost told, the man who picked up the stone, as a bonded pair of the guild at Eldhythe laid it, from his mouth, in old age; and they two sealed it in the chest of the archive, with the mute stones. Halyna read it aloud at the hearth, in the Tide of Remembering, the one and the other, paragraph and paragraph; and they two laid an annal behind it. The words before the annal are the words of Brenn, and I did not mend them. The leaf of the wood is in its face.*

- **The confession is named, not possessed.** *Tessyl Brenn* would soften his name as a possessor (*Tessyl Wrenn*); Seren names him plainly the first time, ***Et tessyl sa re wrodh Brenn Reldvar***, "the confession that Brenn of the Outpost told". *Tessyl*, "an answering", is the lexicon's "confession: an answering for a deed".
- ***Brenn Reldvar***, "Brenn of the Outpost": the epithet construct, *deldvar* "an outpost" softened (*d* > *r*), as *Corlen Dhavow* (an addition). Names are names; the epithet is a place.
- ***et uld sa re rik et tolm***: pilot IV.4's *sa re rik Brenn hy et tuss*.
- ***ul Davow Hemm***: Eldhythe, *Tavow Hemm*, bedded after *ul*.
- "laid the closing annal after it" is ***re hadha sona ryt surr ul o sestow***, "they two laid an annal behind it". *Ryt surr* is a construct ("the inscription of the year") and takes no article; *ryt surr dasken* would read "the annal of the last year".
- ***nath re hlennym so***, "I did not mend them": *clenn* softened, the lexicon's *hl-* (as IV.4's *nahlennet*).
- ***Doss flenn et mesk ul o lern***, "the wood's leaf faces it": pilot IV.4's *doss o ul lern Yebb et Pard*, turned round, in the one form every stone headnote gives this line (III.1, IV.1, IV.5, V.2, V.5). *(Merge fix, `wf8/merge_report.md`: the first draft had* Doss flenn et mesk ul lern flenn sy*, "… in the face of this leaf".)*

### Halyna's frame, and Brenn's words

*Two inks, by turns, a paragraph each, unmarked, through the frame, Brenn's words and the annal. From the second paragraph to the words "So far the words of Brenn" the "I" is Brenn's, and the Orrowen is in his singular.*

> Kethen et tolm, veth nath noss et brodath sy lona. El brodath Wrenn et brodath sy. Re var Brenn ul et deldvar hyvenn sost ul rall; eth pa vurr surr hy ull re rarr o dem lest Vardh ul Davow Hemm, eth re vess o hunnol lo et Stonwrytan. Vesken so hosen re yalant rytet.

We hold the stone, but these words are not ours. They are the words of Brenn, who served at the outermost outpost in his youth, and came to the temple at Eldhythe forty years after and asked the Stonewrights to hear him. We read them as they were written.

Word for word: *We two hold the stone, but these words are not at us two. These words are the words of Brenn. Brenn served at the outermost outpost in youth; and forty years after, he came to the house of God in Eldhythe, and asked a hearing of the Stonewrights. We two read them as they were written.*

- ***Kethen et tolm***, "we two hold the stone": the Invocation's *Keth et tolm* in Halyna's dual.
- "these words are not ours" is ***nath noss et brodath sy lona***, "not at us two".
- ***brodath Wrenn***, "the words of Brenn": here the name is a possessor and is softened (*b* > *w*), as the canon softens *Mardh* in *lest Vardh*.
- "asked the Stonewrights to hear him" is ***re vess o hunnol lo et Stonwrytan***, "asked a hearing of the guild"; *re vess* (*pess*) sounds as *vess* "night", the canon's own homophony.

> Re yalom ul et deldvar lo rask et gaed, ul vodh hask et hal ul et wadh, dem et myst. Re yal rostnoth surr lom. Re yalar delv, eth et pard.

I was at the outpost at the end of the land, where the rock runs out into the sea toward the grey. I was nineteen. There were twelve of us and the warden.

Word for word: *I was at the outpost at the end of the land, where the rock runs into the sea, toward the grey. Nineteen years were at me. We were twelve, and the warden.*

- "I was nineteen" is the "at" idiom, ***Re yal rostnoth surr lom*** (an addition, *doss … surr lo*).
- ***lo rask et gaed***, "at the end of the land" (*dask* softened after *lo*).

> Ew deldvar rirn o, veth nath re vesk o rirn. Re hadh et pard et mesk domm ul et deldvar tunn: ul et gannath eth et hoskath, ul haldath et hellur, ul et genth eth et clenth eth stomorn et lodull. Ew rerrel lyrness uld dyll ull; eth ew hass uld dyll o, eth new mest hemm hyom o.

It was a poor post, but it did not look poor. The warden had set the black wood everywhere: in the doorposts and the lintels, in the walls of his hall, in his table and his chair and the frame of his bed. It was the trappings of a rich man's vanity, and he was a rich man's son, and not much older than I was.

Word for word: *It was a poor outpost, but it did not look poor. The warden had laid the black wood in the whole outpost: in the posts and the lintels, in the walls of the hall, in the table and the chair and the frame of the bed. That was the trappings of the pride of a rich man; and he was the son of a rich man, and not much older than I.*

- "everywhere" is ***ul et deldvar tunn***, "in the whole outpost": the lexicon's *ul gor reskow* would need *gor* bedded after *ul*.
- "his hall … his bed" are left to the context: *o* before *h* and *l* shows no bite (§3.5), and the list keeps one shape, *ul et …*, rather than *ul o yenth, ul o hlenth*.
- "vanity" is ***lyrness***, pride.
- ***new mest hemm hyom o***, "he was not much older than me": *hy* "than", conjugated.

> Re heth o et deldvar lo voll o yedh ul Redul Serdul, eth new lo o voll sost. Re luskant o dem gless, hy vodh re yal o navall ul hinnarath hy o volt; eth lo hless nath re omm o veth umor. Re sesk o stemir nafedh. Re sesk o hydull nafedh.

He held the post by his father's name on the Trade Council, not by his own. They had sent him to the edge because his temper had made him unwelcome in the cities, and at the edge there was no one for it to wound but us. He had never known battle. He had never known patience.

Word for word: *He held the outpost by the name of his father in the Trade Council, and it was not by his own name. They sent him to the edge, because he was unwelcome in the cities, from his temper; and at the edge it fell on none but us. He knew battle never. He knew patience never.*

- ***lo voll o yedh***, "by the name of his father": the bite on *yedh* (S·*gedh*) marks *o* as "his".
- "his temper" is ***o volt***, "his heart" (*molt*).
- "there was no one for it to wound but us" is ***nath re omm o veth umor***, "it fell on none but us": the "only" of a clause (G8). The lexicon has no verb "to wound", and *grest* bedded would begin *nr-*.
- ***Re sesk o stemir nafedh. Re sesk o hydull nafedh.*** The two sentences stand alike, as in the English. *Hydull*, patience, comes back at the end of the burning.

> Re vellor et besk ul ryst, ul sulter lyss myst, hy et myst. Nath re yal helvhoss, veth re vellor et sulter. Re dhav o um ol dhysal, eth re orr o dem susk dem nebb et pard. Ew susk eth stacorn o, covvat ul mystroval comullat, sa re varr amm nath re varr o. Nath re veskym hesp umo.

The stranger came in the morning, in a small grey boat, out of the grey. There was no wind, and the boat came anyway. He beached it on our shingle and walked up to the warden's door. He was tall and pale, clad in woven mist-cloth that moved when he did not. I saw no weapon on him.

Word for word: *The stranger came in the morning, in a small grey boat, out of the grey. There was no wind, but the boat came in. He came ashore on our shingle, and walked up to the door of the warden. He was tall and pale, clad in woven mist-cloth that moved when he did not move. I saw no weapon on him.*

- ***sulter lyss myst***: the grey boat of IV.5's rhyme (*Sulter Myst*).
- "and the boat came anyway" is ***veth re vellor et sulter***, "but the boat came in".
- ***re dhav o***: *tav*, "come ashore, land a boat", softened.
- ***dem nebb et pard***: *gebb* bedded after *dem*; IV.4 has it softened, *Yebb et Pard*.

> Re wrodh o hosast ul o wrodhen sost, brodeleth gand sa re scaevurant hosen helvhoss nerdess lo dhrenn yendeth hemm. Re yal ludal umor, hy vodh ew bont o; eth nath re sesker: hosen sy brodhant so, eth nayalat ullen. Hy ull re vysk o sull brod orrowen. Re vysk o vothen, hosen uld ul gadhol yalat sa hresk nayess.

He spoke first in his own tongue, sharp syllables that rolled like storm-wind through ancient branches. It frightened us, for it was loud, and we did not know that it was only how they speak. Then he said three words of ours. He said them carefully, like a man setting down something that might break.

Word for word: *He spoke first in his own tongue, sharp syllables that rolled like storm-wind through the old branches. Fear was upon us, for it was loud; and we did not know: thus they speak, and nothing else. Then he said three words of the Shore. He said them carefully, like a man in the laying-down of a thing that may break.*

- ***ul o wrodhen sost***: *ul o* unbedded, as pilot IV.4's *ul o rask*; the bite on *wrodhen* marks "his".
- "It frightened us" is the feeling laid upon us, ***Re yal ludal umor***.
- "that it was only how they speak" is quoted, not reported: ***hosen sy brodhant so, eth nayalat ullen***, "thus they speak, and nothing else".
- "three words of ours" is ***sull brod orrowen***, "three words of the Shore", as pilot IV.4's *brodath orrowen*.
- ***ul gadhol yalat sa hresk nayess***: the progressive (*ul* + verbal noun), its object softened as possessor, and the relative softening *cresk*.

> Hess… Helv… Sennyl…

"Stop… Sky… Dying…"

- **Canon, unchanged** (`orrowen_v2` §11.8): the Guest's three words are Orrowen already, the dry cut spoken. Seren writes them as they were said.

> Ew brodath venn so, brodhat venn. Seskym o amm sy. Veth el hunnatath hosel brodath sa nath noss trenn lont, eth nath re hunnar veth hunnatath.

They were the right words, spoken truly. I know it now. But words without sentences are only sounds, and we heard only sounds.

Word for word: *They were true words, told truly. I know it now. But words that have no sentence are only sounds; and we heard nothing but sounds.*

- "words without sentences" is ***brodath sa nath noss trenn lont***, "words at which there is no sentence": the lexicon's *nath … lo* for "without". *Trenn* is the sentence, the line and the course.
- "we heard only sounds" is ***nath re hunnar veth hunnatath***, "we heard nothing but sounds".

> Hy ull re lumm o um vysul yebb et pard, eth re hadh o ul o lern tolm dyss domm, strom hosen pa yarl. Re hadh o o hosen es gadhos blenth ul lern arra.

Then he knelt on the step of the warden's door and set down before him a strange dark stone, the size of two fists. He set it down the way you would set down bread before a guest.

Word for word: *Then he knelt on the threshold of the warden's door, and laid before him a strange dark stone, as great as two fists. He laid it as you would lay bread before a guest.*

- "the step of the warden's door" is ***vysul yebb et pard***, "the threshold": the lexicon's *lusul* is a pace, not a doorstep.
- ***re hadh o ul o lern tolm dyss domm***: the place before the stone, so that *o* is not read as the stone's owner (grammar note 5).
- "the size of two fists" is ***strom hosen pa yarl***, "as great as two hands": a construct would soften *pa* to *va*, "your".
- "you would set down" is ***es gadhos***, the future particle in a past frame (pilot IV.4's *es gadh*).

> Re visk et pard o lo sest. Re orr o ul et tuss lo et hald. Eth re dhyvullant uldath et pard. Re dhyvullom. Nath re dhyvullom fedh hy orl ull.

The warden kicked it aside. It went into the dust by the wall. And his men laughed. I laughed. I have not laughed at anything since.

Word for word: *The warden kicked it aside. It went into the dust by the wall. And the warden's men laughed. I laughed. I have not ever laughed since that day.*

- ***Re visk***: *misk*, "kick" (and "the foot"), softened.
- ***ul et tuss***: *dem tuss* would bed to *dem duss*, "to hard".
- "his men" is ***uldath et pard***, "the warden's men"; *o uldath* would show no bite.
- "at anything since" is ***fedh hy orl ull***, "ever from that day".

> Re dhald et besk. Re hadh o o yarl venn um sestow wiskyann et pard, dagorn, hosen re yald odha lom amm re yal suress umom; eth re wamm o so wisketh olen dem femmyl so wisklerneth, ul velvyth. Re yal stykil hosen sy um et pard: re lusk o o. Kaeverym: amm re hess o lo ull, es morr ol hossol ullen. Hy ull re rik et besk preller lyss ul o yarl ullen, eth re femm o o um sestyarl venn et pard.

The stranger rose. He put his right hand on the back of the warden's neck, gently, the way my mother did when I had a fever, and drew their heads together until their foreheads touched, in silence. The warden was so astonished that he let him. I think if it had stopped there, all our lives would have gone otherwise. Then the stranger took a little blade in his left hand and touched it to the warden's right arm.

Word for word: *The stranger rose. He laid his right hand on the back of the warden's neck, gently, as a mother did for me when a fever was upon me; and drew their heads together until the touching of their foreheads, in silence. Such wonder was upon the warden: he let him. I think: if it had stopped there, our life would go otherwise. Then the stranger took a small blade in his left hand, and touched it to the right arm of the warden.*

- "his right hand" is ***o yarl venn***, "his true hand" (*garl venn*, an addition); "his left hand" is ***o yarl ullen***, "his other hand".
- "the way my mother did" is ***hosen re yald odha lom***, "as a mother did for me": *en* beds a vowel, and *en modha* would stand on the page like an English word.
- ***so wisketh … so wisklerneth***: *bisk* "head" and *bisklern* "forehead" softened after *so* "their".
- "The warden was so astonished that he let him" is ***Re yal stykil hosen sy um et pard: re lusk o o***, "such wonder was upon the warden: he let him".
- "all our lives would have gone otherwise" is ***es morr ol hossol ullen***: *orr* bedded after *es* (*morr*, as I.1's *ul morrol*). The future in a past frame is the tongue's "would".

> Re orr et pard dem sestow ul ludal. Nath re yal grestet lo fedh. Hy vodh re dhresk o hy et besk, re orr gunn et ryt sa re yal kaeveret venthen; eth re rarr et lorr, eth re scyndal o. Re scyndal o lor: Grika o! Re riker o. Re scyndal o dem drunn hurrol. Re lynirent uldath et pard.

The warden drew back in terror. He had never had a wound. Because he tore himself away, the cut that was meant to be shallow went deep, and the blood came, and he screamed. He screamed at us, "Seize him." We seized him. He screamed for the killing blow. His men obeyed.

Word for word: *The warden went back in terror. A wound had not ever been at him. Because he tore himself from the stranger, the cut that was meant shallow went deep; and the blood came, and he screamed. He screamed at us: Seize him! We seized him. He screamed for the killing blow. The warden's men obeyed.*

- "He had never had a wound" is the "at" idiom with *fedh*: ***Nath re yal grestet lo fedh***.
- **The cut is *ryt***: the word for a cut vow, an inscription, a letter cut in stone. The Guest's rite was a writing on the body.
- ***re dhresk o***: *tresk*, "tear", the word of the Torn Cloak.
- ***Grika o! Re riker o.*** The cry is quoted, as the crowd's cries are in I.1, and the answer says the verb back: Orrowen's yes.
- ***dem drunn hurrol***, "for the killing blow" (an addition): *dem* leaves *dr-* as it is (§2.5).

> Re lynirer.

We obeyed.

- One word, as in the English: *lynir*, "obey, heed an order", 1pl.

> Nath re yorr et besk. Kaeverym amm sy: re yal ryt ullen sa re haever o, um o sestyarl sost. Re yald o o nafedh.

The stranger did not fight us. I think now there was another cut he meant to make, on his own arm. He never made it.

Word for word: *The stranger did not fight. I think now: there was another cut that he meant, on his own arm. He never made it.*

- "did not fight us" is ***Nath re yorr et besk***, "the stranger did not fight": *gorr* takes no object in the lexicon.
- ***Re yald o o nafedh***, "he never made it": pilot IV.4's *Re femm Halvard o nafedh*.

> Hy ull, lo haral et pard, re hadhar et derdsast ul o sulter, eth re hadhar et sulter ul vorr, hy vodh re vaver o nayalat vesket. Nath re vorr mesk et sulter ull hosen mesk. Re hunner et vorr susk hy vem venn, hosen amm re yal et mesk sost ul scyndalol. Re hastar o dem neld lo yannath, eth re lymm et hyll o dem neld, eth re hunnar o um et nynth hy amm re vammar hydull veskyl.

Afterward the warden had us lay the body in its boat and set the boat alight, so there would be nothing to show. The wood of that boat did not burn like wood. The fire leapt higher than any natural flame, as though the wood itself was screaming. We shoved it off with poles, and the tide took it out, and we could hear it on the water after we could no longer bear to look.

Word for word: *Then, at the warden's order, we laid the body in its boat, and we laid the boat in fire, for he wanted nothing seen. The wood of that boat did not burn like wood. The fire leapt higher than a natural flame, as if the wood itself were screaming. We shoved it away with poles, and the tide carried it away, and we heard it on the water after we had lost the bearing of looking.*

- "had us lay" is ***lo haral et pard***, "at the warden's order"; "so there would be nothing to show" is ***hy vodh re vaver o nayalat vesket***, "for he wanted nothing seen" (*vesket*, the participle of *vesk* "see", as *navesket* "unread" in the annal). *(Back-translation fix.)* The first draft had *nayalat lestiret*; but *lestir* is "show the way, guide", not "show", and a blind reader heard "he wanted nothing left that could show the way".
- ***hosen amm***, "as if" (pilot I.1).
- "after we could no longer bear to look" is ***hy amm re vammar hydull veskyl***, "since we had lost the bearing of looking": the tongue has no "no longer" (G10), and *hydull* is the patience the warden never knew.

> Re harm et pard lo saevul, eth re ryt et saevul dem et Redul orl ull sost. Re yalom lo sest somm re yal o rytet, eth doss o vyskyl lom hy volt:

The warden had the clerk write to the Council that same day. I stood by while it was written, and I can say it by heart:

Word for word: *The warden called to the clerk, and the clerk wrote to the Council that very day. I was beside him while it was written, and the saying of it is at me by heart:*

- "had the clerk write" is ***Re harm et pard lo saevul, eth re ryt et saevul***: the warden calls, the clerk writes.
- "that same day" is ***orl ull sost***, as I.1's *orl ull*; after *ul* it would be *morl*.
- "I can say it by heart" is the "at" idiom, ***doss o vyskyl lom hy volt***, "the saying of it is at me from the heart" (*hy volt*, an addition).

> Besk nasesket. Dask bynden. Hesset. Sceth vorrat eth luskat.

*Unknown intruder. Hostile intent. Neutralized. Vessel burned and released.*

Word for word: *Stranger unknown. End hostile. Stopped. Hull burned and released.*

- **The warden's report**, in the clerk's register (the rolls, the haven hand, `orrowen_v2` §9): four verbless namings, as the dry cut sets a line down, noun then quality. It is an addition as a whole.
- ***Hesset***, "stopped", for "Neutralized": the participle of *hess*, the Guest's own first word. What he asked for is what they wrote of him.
- Seren writes it in the living spelling; the haven spellings of the rolls are not specified word by word, and none is guessed.

> Nath noss brod um et hebbet. Nath noss brod um et lummol.

No word of the gift. No word of the kneeling.

Word for word: *There is no word about the gift. There is no word about the kneeling.*

- ***Nath noss …***, the canon's shape in Seren's line, twice.

> Re hovv et pard covveleth sell dem sennyl, lo dhrenn systeth bith ul Davow Hemm; eth seskym hy vodh.

The warden wore long sleeves all the rest of his life, through the hot summers of Eldhythe, and I know why.

Word for word: *The warden wore long sleeves until death, through the hot summers in Eldhythe; and I know why.*

- "all the rest of his life" is ***dem sennyl***, "until death".
- ***lo dhrenn systeth bith ul Davow Hemm***, "through the hot summers in Eldhythe": with *bith* between them, *Tavow Hemm* would not read as the possessor of *systeth*, so it takes *ul*.

> Re rikym et tolm hy et tuss lo et hald ul ryst fennet, amm re vesk nahos. Nath seskym hy vodh re yaldom o. Re hethym o pa vurr surr. Hebbym o amm sy lo et brodath sy, eth pessym haerilyl lo Vardh, hy vodh nath noss hos lom dem bessyl veth Mardh.

I picked the stone out of the dust by the wall the next morning, when no one was looking. I do not know why I did it. I have kept it forty years. I give it up now with these words, and I ask God to forgive me, for there is no one left to ask but God.

Word for word: *I picked the stone from the dust by the wall on the next morning, when no one looked. I do not know why I did it. I have kept it forty years. I give it now with these words, and I ask forgiveness of God, for there is no one at me to ask but God.*

- ***Re rikym et tolm hy et tuss***: pilot IV.4's *sa re rik Brenn hy et tuss*, in Brenn's own mouth.
- ***lo Vardh … veth Mardh***: God softened after *lo*, and plain after *veth*, which mutates nothing.
- "no one left to ask but God" is ***nath noss hos lom dem bessyl veth Mardh***, "there is not one at me to ask but God" (*dem* + the verbal noun, bedded: *pessyl* > *bessyl*).

### The annal

> Dem sy, brodath Wrenn. Amm sy hunna ryt surr.

So far the words of Brenn. Now hear the annal.

Word for word: *Until this, the words of Brenn. Now hear the annal.*

- ***Dem sy***, "until this", for "so far". *Hunna* is the plural imperative, to the hearth.

> Nath noss voll et pard kethet. Re stin Stonwrytan Dhavow Hemm o hy et scaevureleth, eth es noss o ul ol flennath nafedh. Um o sestyarl venn re yal et rellor hosel sa re yald ternil et arra sa re lumm um rerdsast orrowen: saed yalat peness, treskat ul molt.

The warden's name is not kept. The Stonewrights of Eldhythe cut it from the rolls, and it will be in no book of ours. On his right arm was the only mark the Kneeling Guest's rite ever made upon a Shoreland body: the half of a holy thing, cut off in the middle.

Word for word: *The name of the warden is not kept. The Stonewrights of Eldhythe cut it from the rolls, and it will be in our books never. On his right arm was the only mark that the rite of the guest who knelt made on a Shoreland body: the half of a holy thing, riven in the middle.*

- ***Stonwrytan Dhavow Hemm***, "the Stonewrights of Eldhythe": a construct, *Tavow Hemm* softened as the possessor, no article on *Stonwrytan*.
- "the rolls" is ***scaevureleth***, the lexicon's *scaevurel*, "a roll (a scroll)".
- "in no book of ours" is ***es noss o ul ol flennath nafedh***, "it will be in our books never": the future and *nafedh*, where the tongue has no future negative.
- ***ternil et arra sa re lumm***, "the rite of the guest who knelt": the Kneeling Guest by the name unit W01 gives him (O-W01-01), with IV.4's *et arra*; a definite possessor keeps its article, as *hosk et ganna*. After *lumm*, *fedh* "ever" would cling to the kneeling, so "ever" is left to *hosel*, "only".
- "cut off in the middle" is ***treskat ul molt***, "riven in the middle": *treskat*, the word of *Covv Treskat* and of the Rivenmen, *Treskan*.
- ***um rerdsast orrowen***: *derdsast* "body" softened after *um*; *orrowen*, "of the Shore", the adjective the tongue's own name is made of.

> Re hoska et ganna sa re hunna o et tolm domm eth et tessyl ul vestul rytow et Stonwrytan, lo dholmath nawrodh; eth re yalant lo ull navesket. Amm re ommant et cummath eth re orr et Stonwrytan dem susk et brunn, re yal et vestul hebbet dem brassel tevel sost um et orrol, hy vodh nath re yal galat dyll ulo. Ew et prassel tevel sost Seren, sa ross ul rytyl sy. Re lymm ey o gor lusul, eth hy et hiskenneth re lymm ey o hosel.

The pair who heard him sealed the dark stone and the confession in the archive chest of the guild, with the mute stones, and there they lay unread. When the havens fell and the guild walked up the mountain, the chest was handed to the youngest apprentice on the road, because it held nothing of worth. The youngest apprentice was Seren, who is writing this down. She carried it every step, and after the passes she carried it alone.

Word for word: *The pair who heard him sealed the dark stone and the confession in the chest of the archive of the guild, with the mute stones; and they lay there unread. When the havens fell and the guild went up the mountain, the chest was given to the youngest prentice on the road, for there was no thing of worth in it. The youngest prentice was Seren, who is writing this. She carried it every step, and from the passes she carried it alone.*

- ***et ganna sa re hunna o***, "the pair who heard him": *ganna*, the gate, is the pair.
- ***dem brassel tevel sost***, "to the youngest prentice": *sost* for the most (lexicon §5.3), *prassel* bedded after *dem*.
- "nothing of worth" is ***nath re yal galat dyll ulo***, "there was no thing of worth in it".
- ***sa ross ul rytyl sy***: II.3's words for Seren, exactly.
- ***re lymm ey o hosel***: *hosel* after the verb, "alone", which is what the English means here.

> Nath noss haral hemm hy sy ul molt et luntath: uld sa lumm lo tho yebb, ul vimm tho hrisur. Ew lunt yebbeth ol.

There is no older law among any people than this: that one who kneels at your door is under your roof. We were a people of doors.

Word for word: *There is no law older than this among the peoples: a man who kneels at your door, under your roof. We were a people of doors.*

- **The oldest law is a verbless saying**, as the proverbs are: ***uld sa lumm lo tho yebb, ul vimm tho hrisur***. It is the gloss the lexicon gives *arraharal*, the guest-law, now in words (an addition). The singular *tho*: the law is said to each host.
- "among any people" is ***ul molt et luntath***, "among the peoples": *ul molt gor lunt* would soften *gor* as a possessor.
- ***Ew lunt yebbeth ol***, "a people of doors were we": the copula's predicate first, as Kael's *El nydherd yalatath en*.

> Cadhan o hosen re yal cadhat lona.

This we lay as it was laid for us.

Word for word: *We two lay it as it was laid at us two.*

- The formula in the dual, as at the end of II.3 (note 1).

## NOTES FOR JACK AND FOR THE NEXT TRANSLATORS

### 1. The one decision: the hearth formula in the dual [Jack]

The lexicon gives *This we lay as it was laid for us* in the plural, ***Cadhar o hosen re yal cadhat lor*** (O0234). Both leaves here close in Halyna's mouth, and `orrowen_v2` §3.9 item 1 is explicit: "Where the Book gives Halyna a plural verb, the Orrowen original is dual. … The guild never did [let it slip]." Pilot I.1 already inflected the formula for its teller (*Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom*, for Kael). So this unit closes both leaves with ***Cadhan o hosen re yal cadhat lona***, "we two lay it as it was laid for us two", and adds it to the lexicon beside O0234 (S04-09). In II.3 the dual is also the sense: the tale was laid for the two of them by Idrenna. **For consistency across units:** every leaf Halyna tell should close with the dual; the plural stays for a leaf the whole hearth, or the True Men, lay together. If Jack would rather have one invariant formula, it is a two-word change in each leaf (*Cadhan … lona* > *Cadhar … lor*).

### 2. Grammar this unit had to settle (proposals, each reversible)

| # | Point | What the leaves do | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A dual verb keeps its subject** | a broad 3du verb with no noun subject takes *sona*: *hosen brodha sona o*, *daska sona*, *senne sona*, *nossa sona* | §3.4: "a dual verb always has a subject"; *brodha*, *daska* alone read as plural imperatives |
| 2 | **The relative softens the state verb** | *sa ross ul rytyl sy* (Seren, "who is writing this"), *sa rossa ul reskyl* (Idrenna) | *sa* softens (§3.3), and *d* > *r*; the canon has only the past relative (*sa re vess*), and the analyzer asks for *ross* |
| 3 | **The negative question** | ***ho nath*** + N: *ho nath senn et garl venn?* | built as *nath re* (pilot I.1, G1): *ho*'s softening falls on *nath*, which cannot change, and *nath* beds the verb. An addition (S04-13) |
| 4 | **Names mutate like any noun** | *brodath Wrenn* (S·Brenn), *humm Hyna* (S·Rhyna), *ul Davow Hemm* / *Stonwrytan Dhavow Hemm*, *ul Gethow Stellath*, *ul Gemm Hyll*, *dem Dolmath Vardh*, *olna Dholmath Vardh*, *lo Vardh* | the canon's own *lest Vardh*, *so Hrenn*, and IV.4's *Yebb et Pard*. Where a first mention should show the name bare, the sentence is built so no trigger falls on it (*Et tessyl sa re wrodh Brenn*; *Re yala ey eth Tarnel*) |
| 5 | **The pronoun *o* before a content word** | *o* as object or subject is kept off an unmutated content word: a comma (*re haever ullen o, tevar*), a particle between (*re hadh o ul o lern tolm*), or the word order changed | an unmutated word after *o* reads as a missing possessive bite, to the analyzer and to a reader (*o venn* was glossed "its breast", S·*menn*); §3.5, sharpened |
| 6 | **A possessive after a bedding preposition** | *ul o wrodhen*, *ul o sulter*, *ul ol flennath*, *lo o sest*, *lo olna odh*: the pronoun is not bedded | pilot IV.4's *ul o rask*; the analyzer agrees |

### 3. Collisions the leaves step around

- ***gor* after a preposition** (*hy gor, ul gor, lo gor, um gor*): would be *yor* or *nor*, which the grammar does not settle (pilot I.1 §3). Recast each time: *hos hy theld sy eth hos hy theld ull*, *ul et deldvar tunn*, *hosen tunn*, *ul molt et luntath*.
- **A bedded vowel after *ul*, *dem*, *es*, *en*:** *ul Morrow, ul mullen, dem modh, en modha* (the last would look like English "mother"). Avoided, except where it is the plain grammar and harmless (*es morr*, as I.1's *ul morrol*).
- ***dem dask*** > *dem nask*, "eat" (lexicon §8.2); ***dem tuss*** > *dem duss*, "hard"; ***ul orl*** > *morl*: each said another way.
- ***nath mysk*** (read as *nath* + *bysk*), ***nath / es* + *brodh*** (*mr-*), ***nath grik*** (*nr-*), ***dem grest*** (*nr-*): *cadh*, *lymm* and *omm* do the work instead.
- ***hos fedh*** is "once": "for a season" is *fedh lyss*.
- ***lo ullen*** is glossed by the analyzer as S·*rhullen* "of rivers": the leaves write *lo et ullen*, *hy et ullen* for "the other".
- ***gannath*** is both "pairs" and "posts" (*Gannath Myst*): the leaves say *ganna* in the singular, or *et Lodhan*.
- ***Gemm Hyll*** (N·Cemm Hyll) sounds as *gemm* "four": kept, as the canon keeps *vess*/*vess*; the alternatives (*Hemm Hyll*) clash with *hemm* "old".

### 4. Where the Orrowen is built differently from the English

For Jack's eye; none changes the sense.

| English | Orrowen | Why |

### 5. The hands

- **Ink.** Everything above is what the leaf-hand writes, whole: the mortar words, the endings, every bite. *Halyna* and *Idrenna* carry the sealing lintel wherever they stand; *Seren*, *Tarnel* and *Brenn* have none (only a pair-name has one; for Seren, "one name is a mourning").
