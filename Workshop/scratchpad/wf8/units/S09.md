## 1 · THE LEAF

### Title

> ## *Vesksirr ul Meskdhrenn*

**V.7 · The Mirror in the Grain**

The lexicon's title (O2734): *vesksirr* is "a mirror" ("see-glass"), and *meskdhrenn* is "the grain" ("the wood's course").

### Seren's headnote

> *Re hadh Seren Pa Luth o, lo sest Halyna, ul sull trenn, gor dhrenn ul et vess sa re yal et rytet kethet. Brodh Seren o, eth brodha Halyna ulo hosen re wrodha um et dhysal, hos eth ullen.*

*Laid by Seren Two-Inks, with Halyna, in three parts, each on the night its carving was known. Seren tells it, and Halyna speak in it as they spoke on the shingle, a line each.*

Word for word: *Laid it Seren Two-Inks, at the shoulder of Halyna, in three courses, each course in the night that the carving was held. Seren tells it, and Halyna (two) speak in it as they (two) spoke upon the shingle, the one and then the other.*

- **Active, as the pilots' headnotes are** (*Re hadh Crennel Kael …*; *re hadh Seren o*). *Cadhat lo X* would mean "laid **for** X", so "with Halyna" is *lo sest Halyna*, "at Halyna's shoulder": the lexicon's *lo sest*, "beside".
- **The parts are *trenn*, courses.** A *trenn* is "a tale laid in one night", and each part of V.7 was laid on the night its carving was known. So the three parts are three courses, *ul sull trenn, gor dhrenn* (*gor* softens *t* to *dh*), and the part headings are *Trenn Hosast*, *Trenn Pawast*, *Trenn Sullast*. The first draft had *saed*, "half; a part", and the analyzer glossed it "half".
- **"Known" is *kethet*, "held"**, the canon's verb for knowing wood, as in the IV.4 headnote.
- **Halyna take the dual** (*brodha*, *re wrodha*), as the formal register requires (§3.9 item 1).
- **"A line each" is *hos eth ullen***, IV.4's set phrase for the pair's antiphony, "the one and then the other" (O2858).

### Part I

> **Trenn Hosast: Sull Orrol, Hos Clem**

**Part I · Three Roads, One Hour**

*The first course: three roads, one hour.* After the numeral, *orrol* ("a going; a road") stays singular (§3.1). The heading is added as a name row (O-S09-07).

> Re yal o ul Hyll Lodhat. Re yal et crynir hy vovennerd scethan sa re rarr lo sull orrol ul hos clem. Re hadh Rhyna garl umo um et dhysal, eth re yal Halvard um et hald ul susk, lo et lodh, eth trenn cadhat saed. Re yalom lo ey sest, eth et greller lom. Re veskym ey ul stacornol; eth amm re veskym dem susk, re stacorn o um et hald ul hos hoss, eth et birnenn ul o yarl tul, hy vodh galat sa heth hos, keth ullen o. Re rarr o dem vimm, demor, eth re wrodha o. Nath re hether fedh rytet sell hyo, eth new haral hos sceth o. Ew haral et scethan tunn o, ul hos fedh, eth et lodhath sa re haskant ul lodh sceth eth sceth.

It was in the Joined Tide. The plank came from the Standard-Bearer of a fleet that had come on three roads at one hour. Rhyna laid her hand on it on the shingle, and Halvard was on the wall above us at the mortar, with a course half laid. I was beside her with the lamp. I saw her go pale; and when I looked up, he had gone pale on the wall in the same breath, with the trowel still in his hand, for what the one knows the other knows. He came down to us, and they told it. It was longer than any carving we had known, and it was not one hull's order. It was the order of the whole fleet at once, and the ties that ran between hull and hull.

Word for word: *It was in the Joined Tide. The plank was from the Standard-Bearer of a fleet that came by three roads in one hour. Rhyna laid hand upon it on the shingle, and Halvard was on the wall above, at the mortar, and a course laid half. I was at her shoulder, and the lamp at me. I saw her in paling; and when I looked up, he went pale on the wall in one breath, and the trowel in his hand still, for what the one holds, the other holds it. He came down, to us, and they two told it. We never held a carving longer than it, and it was not the order of one hull. It was the order of the whole fleet, at one time, and the bonds that ran between hull and hull.*

- *Hyll Lodhat* (the Joined Tide) is the lexicon's form. *movennerd*, "Standard-Bearer", is softened after *hy* to *vovennerd*.
- **"In the same breath" is *ul hos hoss*, "in one breath"** (added, O-S09-03). It stands beside *ul hos clem*, "in one hour", in the same paragraph.
- **"With the lamp" uses the "at" idiom** (§3.7): *eth et greller lom*, "and the lamp at me". It is a clause with no verb, laid beside the other with *eth*, as in pilot I.1's *eth so wresketh ul so simmeth*.
- **"I saw her go pale" is the progressive** of §3.4: *Re veskym ey ul stacornol*, "I saw her in paling". *stacorn* is both "pale" and "go pale".
- **"What the one knows the other knows" is *galat sa heth hos, keth ullen o*, "what one holds, the other holds it"**, the Book's saying of the bonded pair in the one form the foreword and V.1 give it (S01, S07): *keth*, the Bonded's own verb (*Kethen et tolm*). The object is put first and taken up by *o* (pilot I.1 G6). *(Merge fix, `wf8/merge_report.md`: the first draft had* sesk*, to know a fact, on the reasoning that the bond shares a mind and this is not a knowing of wood; the merge keeps one rendering of the recurring English, and records the reasoning for Jack.)*
- **"Longer than any carving we had known" is *Nath re hether fedh rytet sell hyo***, "we never held a carving longer than it":
  - *hy* is "than" (§3.6);
  - the negative past is *nath re* (pilot I.1 G1);
  - *hy gor* is avoided, because the grammar does not settle whether *gor* mutates after a trigger (pilot I.1 §3).
- **"The ties" are *et lodhath*, "the bonds"**, and "between" is the lexicon's *ul lodh*, "in the mortar of". The ties between hulls are thus told in mortar-words, and Seren lets the chime stand. *bysk*, "tie", is a verb only, and *re vysk* is also "said".
- **"Above us" is *ul susk*, "above".** The "us" is plain from the scene.

2. The east goes, and the north goes, with the bond between them.
3. The south waits, until ring 4, in the dread.
4. The reach of the south goes to the east; then the south goes.

In the bark: *Aelthae* (the Joined Tide).

- **E4-01 exactly as grain_v2 §17 gives it**, with fix B17 (the band-rule before III). mystaeri_spec §5.6 maps it, ring by ring, to this paragraph:
  - three war-piths are the three roads, and one shared ring is the one hour;

> Re hadh Rhyna et tolm hosast ulo, eth re hadh Halvard et hosk umo. Hy ull re yala velv fedh sell. Amm ull re vessym vodh re yal grest umonta, veth kaeverym re seskym o. Re vysk hos, eth re dhunn ullen:

Rhyna began it, and Halvard gave it its end. For a long while after, neither of them spoke. Then I asked them what was wrong, though I think I knew. One of them said, and the other finished:

Word for word: *Rhyna laid the first stone in it, and Halvard laid the capstone upon it. Then they two were silent a long time. Then I asked what harm was upon them two, but I think I knew it. One said, and the other finished:*

- **"Began" is the pilots' *cadh et tolm hosast*, "lay the first stone"** (O2859), exactly.
- **"Gave it its end" is its counterpart, *cadh et hosk um*, "lay the capstone upon"** (added, O-S09-01). A telling is laid as a course: it is begun with a first stone and ended with a capstone. The pair's Cry has the same shape: Rhyna begins, and Halvard ends.
- **"Neither of them spoke" is carried by the dual**: *re yala velv*, "they two were silent".
- **"What was wrong" uses the "upon" idiom for a feeling** (§3.7): *vodh re yal grest umonta*, "what harm was upon them two".
- **The antiphony frame is *Re vysk hos, eth re dhunn ullen***, "one said, and the other finished". *tunn*, "complete, finish", is softened after *re*.
  - It is kept word for word all three times it occurs, and is added as a set phrase (O-S09-02).
  - Each of the pair speaks alone in it, so its verbs are singular.

> Hosen sy galdar hald.

"It is how we work a wall."

Word for word: *Like this we build a wall.*

- "Work a wall" is *gald*, "build". "How" is *hosen sy*, "like this" (the lexicon's "such").

> Tovv et hos dem stinel et ullen, eth nath noss maver wrodhol umo.

"The one waits for the other's stroke, and does not need to be told."

Word for word: *The one waits until the stroke of the other, and there is no need of telling upon him.*

- **"Does not need to be told" uses the "upon" idiom** *doss maver um Y*, "need is upon Y", as the IV.4 headnote does, here negated as *nath noss*. The object of the verbal noun is softened as its possessor (*maver wrodhol*).
- *stinel* is "a stroke; a single cut".

> Eth re heth et rytet voll, et voll sa hebb et mesk lo et hyll tunn ull. Re wrodha Halyna et voll: *Aelthae*. Re rytym o. Re seskym et saed hosast. El *ael* o: et lodh. Eth re rytym o hos fedh ul et flennath sy, ul et *Ael'thar*, ternil et arra lo yebb et pard.

The carving held a name as well, the name the wood gives to that whole Tide. Halyna told the name: *Aelthae*. I wrote it down. I knew the first part of it. It is *ael*, the joining, and I had written it before in this Book, in *Ael'thar*, the Guest's rite at the warden's door.

Word for word: *And the carving held a name, the name that the wood gives to that whole tide. Halyna told the name:* Aelthae. *I wrote it. I knew the first part. It is* ael*: the bond. And I wrote it once in this Book, in the* Ael'thar*, the rite of the Guest at the door of the warden.*

- **"As well" is the sentence-initial *Eth***, "and, also".
- ***Aelthae*, *ael* and *Ael'thar* are the lexicon's loans**, spelled by sound. Seren glosses *ael* as *et lodh*, "the bond", the Shore's own word for what joins (the Aetherbond is *Lodh Helv*).
- ***ul et Ael'thar* keeps the lexicon's article** ("the Ael'thar"), so the loan's first vowel never meets the nasal of *ul*.
- **"The warden's door" is IV.3's title, *Gebb et Pard***, softened after *lo* to *lo yebb et pard*, as in IV.4's *Yebb et Pard*.
- **"Before" is *hos fedh*, "once".** *tevar* means "early", not "previously" (pilot I.1 §4).

> Re rytym et voll, eth nath rytym vodh re haeverym.

I have written the name, and I will not write what I thought.

Word for word: *I wrote the name, and I do not write what I thought.*

- **"Will not" is the plain present with *nath***, which carries both the habit and the refusal, as Kael's *nath gadhom o los* does in I.1.
- *re haeverym* is *kaever* softened after *re*.

> Cadhar o hosen re yal cadhat lor.

This we lay as it was laid for us.

- **The lexicon's formula exactly** (O0234): the plural of pilot I.1's *Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom*. Seren and Halyna lay it together.

> *Eth re vysk et odh:* **Tumar.**

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

- **The lexicon's formula exactly** (O0564). *Tumar* is the hearth's yes. The leaf-hand ends this course with the coping.

### Part II

> **Trenn Pawast: Vodh re ammadar lont**

**Part II · What We Taught Them**

*The second course: what we taught to them.* *vodh* is the headless relative, as in pilot I.1 (*Amm sy myskym los vodh ew et Crenn*). The heading ends in *lont*, "to them", not *so*, because *so* after a verb reads as "their". Added as a name row (O-S09-08).

> Ul Hyll sa re rystull nycul re dhald crynir hy sceth sa re rarr umor hosen re rarr et mesk tevel. Re farr o ul scethan sa re hask um et hald ul et orrol hemm, gor sceth hosel eth bont eth venn, eth nahos ul gethyl reskow lo sest ullen, hosen nath re vesker hy et grem hosast. Re dhyvull et hald, eth re wrodh gor wramm umo, hosen re wrodh gor wramm amm ull. Re vymment et scetheth hos hoss ul lern ol vorr; eth hy et myst ul sestow re rarrant et ganurardath, eth re orrant dem et brammath sa re wrodhant, gor hos, eth nath re orrant hyont. Re rike Halyna et crynir, eth re hethe o.

In the Tide that Learned Deceit a plank came up from a hull that had come at us as the young wood came. It had sailed in a fleet that ran at the wall in the old way, each hull alone and loud and straight, and not one keeping station with another, as we had not seen since the first winter. The wall laughed, and every gun on it spoke, as every gun had spoken then. The hulls turned one breath short of our fire; and out of the grey behind them the hunters came, and went to every gun that had spoken, and would not leave it. Halyna took the plank, and knew it.

Word for word: *In the Tide that Learned Deceit a plank rose from a hull that had come upon us as the young wood came. It sailed in a fleet that ran upon the wall in the old way, every hull alone and loud and true, and no one in keeping a station at the shoulder of another, as we had not seen since the first winter. The wall laughed, and every gun upon it spoke, as every gun spoke then. The hulls turned back one breath before our fire; and from the grey behind came the hunters, and went to the guns that had spoken, every one, and did not go from them. Halyna (two) took the plank, and held it.*

- **The names are the lexicon's:** *Hyll sa re rystull nycul* (the Tide that Learned Deceit) and *mesk tevel* (the young wood).
- **"Came up" is *re dhald*, "rose"** (*tald*, softened), as the plank rises from the wreck.
- **"Come at us" is *re rarr umor*, "came upon us"**, and Part III says the same of the Standard-Bearer.
- **"Each hull alone and loud and straight" is a clause with no verb**: *gor sceth hosel eth bont eth venn*. *venn* ("true, plumb") is a straight course.
- **"Not one keeping station with another"** is *nahos ul gethyl reskow lo sest ullen*, "no one in holding a station at another's shoulder". *keth* is nasalised after *ul*.
- **"Every gun" is *gor wramm*, and "every mast" is *gor vivenn*.** *gor* softens (§3.3): *b* becomes *w* and *m* becomes *v*, as the lexicon's own *gor yalat*, "everything", already shows on *galat*. The analyzer requires it.
- **"Every gun spoke" keeps the Book's verb, *brodh*.** The guns' speaking and silence is what Part II turns on, and the tongue's own *bramm*, "fire", would lose it.
- **"Turned" is *mymm*, "go back"** (*re vymment*). *kyl*, "change", would read "the hulls changed".
- **"Went to every gun that had spoken" is *dem et brammath sa re wrodhant, gor hos***, "to the guns that had spoken, every one". *dem gor* would nasalise *gor*, which the grammar does not settle (pilot I.1 §3), so *gor* follows the noun as *gor hos*, "each one". The same device recurs in Halyna's second line.
- **"Would not leave it" is *nath re orrant hyont*:** the negative past with *orr hy*, "go from".
- **Halyna take the dual:** *re rike* (from *grik*, whose *g* drops before a consonant, as IV.4's *rik*) and *re hethe*.

  - "First and far" is `DEEP+STRIKE@1`. DEEP is *eir*: deep, long, far.
- **The fork on file 6 is not told in this leaf.** `!FLASH<LONE½|KNOT>` is the carving's "If none flash, go ashore as the green wood went": it is in the wood, and the leaf does not tell it.
- **"Against us" is *ul ol lern*, "in our face".** After a preposition, *ol* can only be the possessive.

> Hosast, nath re hyrrilym o. Re vyskym: Nath noss nyculol lo et mesk. El et galat hosast sa re ammad hebbet et arra lor fedh um so rytet: galat sa heth Mesk Myst, el venn o gor fedh. Amm ull re hyrrilym, eth re rytym o somm re yal syllorn lom tul. Nath nycul et mesk. Re yal o haralat dem gadhol nycul ul ol brener, eth re lynir o.

Then I understood, and I wrote it down before my nerve could fail. The wood does not lie. It was told to make us believe a lie, and it obeyed.

Word for word: *At first, I did not understand it. I said: Lying is not at the wood. It is the first thing that the gift of the Guest ever taught us about their carving: what Mystwood holds, it is true always. Then I understood, and I wrote it while nerve was still at me. - **"I said that …" is quoted**, since Orrowen has no "that" before a clause (pilot I.1): *Re vyskym: Nath noss nyculol lo et mesk*, "lying is not at the wood". This is the "at" idiom for *cannot* (§3.7).
- **"What is carved in their wood is always true" becomes *galat sa heth Mesk Myst, el venn o gor fedh***, "what Mystwood holds, it is true always".
  - *ul Mesk Myst* would read as *ul* plus the bedded *besk*, and the analyzer glosses it so.
  - So Mystwood is made the one that holds, using the canon's own verb for a knowing kept in wood ("the grain holds it still").
- **"Before my nerve could fail" is *somm re yal syllorn lom tul***, "while nerve was still at me". *en syllorn* would not show its possessive (§3.5).
- **"It was told to make us believe a lie" is *Re yal o haralat dem gadhol nycul ul ol brener***, "it was bidden to lay a lie in our mind". To tell a tale is to lay a course; to plant a lie is to lay one (added as *cadh nycul*, O-S09-05). After *dem*, *cadhol* is bedded to *gadhol*, and *prener* to *brener* after *ol*.

> Re reske Halyna fedh sell um et dhysal, eth sona yarlath um et meskdhrenn tul. Hy ull re vysk hos, eth re dhunn ullen:

Word for word: *Halyna (two) sat a long time on the shingle, and their two's hands on the grain still. Then one said, and the other finished:*

- **"Their hands" is *sona yarlath***, with the dual possessive of a pair (§3.5).
- The frame is O-S09-02, with *Hy ull*, "then".

> Re rystullant o hyor.

> Re ammadar o lont lo et brammath velv, gor hos.

- **"Would not say it at first" is *hosast nath re vyske***, with the dual verb. *mysk* follows *re*, which softens it, so it cannot be heard as the bedded *bysk*.

## 3 · WHAT THE TRANSLATION DID, AND WHY

**The voices.**
- **Seren tells the whole leaf, humbly and exactly.** She is sure of what she saw, not of what she thought: *veth kaeverym re seskym o*, "but I think I knew it", and *nath rytym vodh re haeverym*, "I do not write what I thought".
- **Halyna speak a line each.** The frame is identical all three times, *Re vysk hos, eth re dhunn ullen*, and their lines are short and in the present, as a pair speaks. Kael and Voss do not speak in V.7, and no verse occurs.

**Grammar the leaf had to settle** (each point is reversible).

| # | Point | What the leaf does | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | ***gor* before a letter that softens** | *gor wramm* (every gun), *gor vivenn* (every mast), *gor haelir* (every wreck), *gor dhrenn* (each course) | §3.3 lists *gor* among the softening triggers, and the lexicon's *gor yalat*, "everything", already softens. The analyzer requires the mutation |
| S2 | ***gor* after a trigger** | put after the noun as *gor hos*, "each one": *dem et brammath …, gor hos* | the grammar does not settle *dem gor* or *lo gor* (pilot I.1 §3) |
| S3 | **a feeling in the dual** | *grest umonta*, "harm upon them two" | Halyna take the dual everywhere, so the conjugated preposition does too (§3.6: *umonta*) |
| S4 | **a clause's object before a verbal noun** | *Doss so hreskyl lor*, "their breaking is at us" (we can break them) | IV.4's rule (the pronoun object of a verbal noun is its possessor); here the softening shows it |
| S5 | **a stranded noun-preposition** | *galat sa re yal o rytet ul lern*, "the thing it was carved against" | G5's resumptive has no conjugated form for *ul lern*, so the gap is left |
| S6 | **"let us"** | the future of resolve, *es naldar* | the tongue has no first-person imperative |

**Collisions the leaf steps around.**
- ***ul Mesk Myst*** is read as *ul* plus the bedded *besk*. The leaf recasts it: Mystwood is the one that holds.
- ***dem bamm*** gives *mamm*, "lose". So "to draw a hull in" becomes *dem mellorol sceth*.
- ***dask*, "purpose", after a preposition** gives *nask* ("eat") or *rask*. So "on purpose" is *hosen re yal kaeveret*.
- ***amm nath*** is "unless", so "before the first shot" is *somm re yal et brammath velv tul*.
- ***ol brammath*** would give *mr-*. The article stands between, as IV.4 does it.
- ***kyl*** "turn" reads "change", so a hull turning back is *mymm*.
- ***bysk*** "tie" is a verb only, and is also heard in *re vysk*. The ties are *lodhath*, "bonds".
- ***o lernil*, *en syllorn*, *so rytet* after a verb:** the possessive would not show (§3.5), so each is recast. *so rytet* is kept only after *um*, where it cannot be a subject.

  - V7-III is new: it cuts the Last Tide's home clause with *then* runners, `HOME` for the place loved, and the standard as the carving the bearer bears.
