## THE LEAF

### Title

> ## *Sestow Flennath · Flennath Hethyleth*

**APPENDIX · THE BOOK OF KNOWINGS**

*The back of the Book · the Book of Knowings* (both are the lexicon's: *sestow flennath* O2288, *Flennath Hethyleth* O0625, *kethyleth* "knowings" softened as the possessor; the analyzer glosses it *heth* "spring", the canon's own homophony, lexicon §8.2).

### Seren's headnote

> *Re hadh Seren Pa Luth et ryteteth ul sestow et flennath sy, hos eth hos, hosen re hethe Halyna so hy et kaelirvesk; eth derret lo sy, ul vimm so veskrivullath. El saed et flennath sa ross lo et scethan sost. Re ryt et mesk gor wrod ulo, eth nath re ryter hos.*

*Set down by Seren Two-Inks at the back of this Book, one carving at a time as Halyna knew them from the wreck-wood, and gathered here under their roots. It is the fleet's own half of the Book. Every word in it was cut by the wood, and none by us.*

Word for word: *Seren Two-Inks laid the carvings in the back of this Book, one and one, as Halyna held them from the wreck-wood; and gathered in here, under their roots. It is the half of the Book that is at the fleet itself. The wood cut every word in it, and we did not cut one.*

- **Active, as the pilots' headnotes are**: *Re hadh Seren Pa Luth*, "Seren Two-Inks laid", as I.1's *Re hadh Crennel Kael* and IV.4's *re hadh Seren o*. Seren lays the carvings on the leaf as a mason lays a course.
- ***hos eth hos***, "one by one" (O2862, pilot I.1), for "one carving at a time".
- ***re hethe Halyna***: to know the wood is to hold it (§3.7), and Halyna take the dual (§3.9).
- ***derret***, "reaped, gathered in": a harvest word. The first choice, *cemmet*, is "met"; and *eth re yal so cemmet*, "and they were gathered", sets *so* before a *c*: a subject pronoun softens nothing (§3.5), but the analyzer takes *so* for "their" and asks for *hemmet* (pilot I.1, G9). So the clause is a bare participle, as the English is.
- ***sa ross***: the relative softens the state verb *doss* as it softens any verb (S1 below).
- ***eth nath re ryter hos***, "and we did not cut one": *none by us* is the verb said again, Orrowen's yes-and-no rule.

### Before the table

> *Gor rytet sa re hebb et mesk lor fedh, el hos stinel o dholm hosast; eth el o veskrivull et stinel hosast. Doss noth meskrivull. Re rystullom lerneth et meskrivullath ul veskyl, somm nath re veskym galat ullen tul; eth gor sa sesk et meskrivull ul veskyl, doss seskyl lonn et rytet lo, sell tevar hy wrodhol et ullen lo Halyna. Nath re ryt et mesk tevel veth et meskrivull. Re ryt gor hyll ul o sestow et meskrivull sost, eth re hadh umo, hosen cadh mesk scaevur; hy ull, el et ryteteth hemm sost et ryteteth tevel sost, sa re dhevent hemm. Ul et hylleth desir, re yal seskyl veskrivull rytet lom ul veskyl hy et hald, ul scaevurol haelir; veth nafedh mest hy et meskrivull, hy vodh et ullen, nath hebb et mesk o veth lo et Lodhan, eth doss maver o hethyl umont, eth nath vesk hos o.*

Every carving the wood has ever given us begins with one stroke, and the first stroke is its root. There are ten roots. I learned their shapes by eye before I could make out anything else, and one who knows the root by eye can tell the kin of a carving long before Halyna have told the rest of it. The young wood cut the root and nothing more. Each Tide after it cut the same root and added to it, as a tree adds a ring, so that the eldest carvings are the youngest ones grown old. In the late Tides I could tell the root of a carving by eye from the wall, in the time it takes a wreck to roll over; but never more than the root, for the rest the wood gives only to the Bonded, and it must be known, not read.

Word for word: *Every carving that the wood ever gave us, one stroke is its first stone; and its root is the first stroke. There are ten roots. I learned the shapes of the roots in seeing, while I had not yet seen another thing; and whoever knows the root in seeing, knowing the kin of the carving is at him, long before the telling of the rest by Halyna. The young wood did not cut but the root. Every Tide after it cut the very root, and laid on it, as a tree lays a ring; from that, the eldest carvings are the youngest carvings, which grew old. In the late Tides, knowing the root of a carving was at me in seeing from the wall, in the rolling of a wreck; but never more than the root, for the rest, the wood does not give it but to the Bonded, and the need of its holding is upon them, and no one reads it.*

- ***el hos stinel o dholm hosast***, "one stroke is its first stone": to begin is to lay the first stone (*cadh et tolm hosast*, O2859, pilot IV.4), and a carving begins as a wall does. *Mosk* "begin" is the Creed trap (lexicon §8.2), and stays out.
- ***ul veskyl***, "in seeing", is "by eye". After *lo* the word would also read as *mesk* "tree" softened, and the analyzer glosses it so; after *ul* neither *m* nor *v* changes, and the reading is one.
- **"Before I could make out anything else"**: Orrowen has no "before" that opens a clause, so Seren says *somm nath re veskym galat ullen tul*, "while I had not yet seen another thing" (*nath … tul* is "not yet", §3.8). *Amm nath* would read "unless". **"Long before"** is ***sell tevar hy***, "long earlier than" (an addition), with *hy* "than" (lexicon §5.3).
- ***Nath re ryt … veth et meskrivull***, "did not cut but the root": "only" of a clause (pilot I.1, G8).
- ***gor hyll ul o sestow***, "every Tide in its back": "each Tide after it". ***et meskrivull sost***, "the very root", is "the same root".
- ***hosen cadh mesk scaevur***: *cadh* "lay; add, lay on" is the verb for adding a ring, as for a course; *scaevur* is "a wheel; a ring".
- ***sa re dhevent hemm***, "which grew old": *tev* is "a child" and "grow, sprout" (lexicon §4.4); the analyzer glosses the noun, but *-ent* is a verb's ending.
- ***ul scaevurol haelir***, "in the rolling of a wreck", for "in the time it takes a wreck to roll over": the verbal noun, its doer the softened possessor (pilot I.1, G3). *(Back-translation fix.)* It was *somm scaevur kaelir*, "while a wreck rolls"; the blind reader heard *scaevur* as the noun "a ring" (which it is two clauses before, *hosen cadh mesk scaevur*), and the analyzer glosses it so, and read "while [it was] a ring of wreck". The *-ol* ending and the softened *haelir* leave one reading.
- ***doss maver o hethyl umont***, "the need of its holding is upon them": the lexicon's *must* (O0488), with the pronoun object of the verbal noun as its possessor, as IV.4's *maver o wrodhol*. ***eth nath vesk hos o***, "and no one reads it", for "not read": *hos* after a negative is "anyone".

> *Hebb et mesk mest hy wisk rytet. Hebb o et saedeth: cedh re dhovv um hedh, vodh re hunn gor sceth, amm vodh re haever et molt kylyl. Re rytym so ul so reskowath ul et trenneth. Lo sy, nath gadhom veth et bisketh, hy et mesk tevel sost dem et mesk hemm sost.*

The wood gives more than a carving's head. It gives the parts: who waited on whom, what each hull was told, when the heart meant to turn. Those I have written in their places in the tales. Here I set down only the heads, from the youngest wood to the eldest.

Word for word: *The wood gives more than the head of a carving. It gives the parts: who waited upon whom, what each hull heard, when the heart meant turning. I wrote them in their places in the tales. Here, I do not lay but the heads, from the youngest wood to the eldest wood.*

- ***cedh re dhovv um hedh***, "who waited upon whom": *tovv um* "wait on" (an addition), *cedh* softened after *um*. The same phrase answers the Judgement of the Grain That Rots below (*tovva um ommol et clennyl*).
- ***vodh re hunn gor sceth***, "what each hull heard", for "what each hull was told": the tongue says the passive with its doer where it can, and a trigger before *gor* is avoided (pilot I.1 §3).
- ***re haever***: *kaever* "think; mean, intend", softened after *re*.
- ***re rytym***, "I wrote", by the rule; the canon's broad *Rytom* belongs to the oath formula (§3.9), and is not Seren's.
- ***et mesk hemm sost***, "the eldest wood", is the lexicon's (O1378); *et mesk tevel sost* is built to match it.

### The table

| *Et meskrivull, hosen vollom o* | *Hosen vodh vesk o ul meskdhrenn* | *Et ryteteth, hy et tevel sost dem et hemm sost* | *Cedh sa re yal o lo, ul et dask* |
|---|---|---|---|
| The root, as I name it | What it looks like in the grain | The carvings, youngest to eldest | Whose it was at the last |

Word for word: *The root, as I name it · How it looks in the grain · The carvings, from the youngest to the eldest · Who it was at him, in the end.*

- *vesk* is "see; look; seem (be seen)". *Cedh sa re yal o lo*, "who it was at him", is "whose it was": the having idiom (§3.7) with its preposition left behind in the relative (pilot I.1, G5).
- **The Tides**, after each carving, are the lexicon's names: *Hyll Pynt* (Hasty), *Hyll Pawast* (Second), *Hyll Dhumol* (Remembering), *Hyll Lodhat* (Joined), *Hyll sa re rystull nycul* (the Tide that Learned Deceit); a Judgement is *Tesset*.
- **Seren's names for the roots are names**, and take no article (§3.2): *Fodh*, *Drod*, *Hoss* and *Bysket* are the plain words, capitalised; the other six are phrases (additions). **The Thrones' epithets** are Orrowen by §3.10, and written as the Book writes *Kael Nydherd* and *Harl Orrow sa Vorr*, with no comma between the name and its epithet: the construct (*Molt Hyll*, *Lorr Helv*, *Lymmerd Yendeth*, *Meskdhrenn Dholm*, *Molt Veskrivull*), the relative (*Meskdhrenn sa Lilv*), the noun and its adjective (*Grem Sell*), apposition (*Gunn ul vimm*; the verbal noun *Vorrol* and the participle *Lennet*). Unit S05 (III.1) reached the same six epithets independently (*Lorr Helv*, *Lymmerd Yendeth*, *Meskdhrenn sa lilv*, *Grem Sell*, *Vorrol*, *Lennet*); this unit takes S05's *Vorrol* for the Burning, so the wall names each Throne one way. The names themselves are loans, spelled by sound, and keep no meaning (Jack: names are names).
- **The wood's orders are the plural imperative** (*-a*): the wood speaks to a host of hulls. (The Captain's calls, §11.5, are singular because he speaks to one True Man.)
- **In the Book each row is one line**: the carvings run youngest to eldest, joined by arrows. Here each carving is set out under its root, with its round.

*Give them the middle; take the sides; close the hand.*

Word for word: *Give them the heart; take the two shoulders; close the hand.*

### 2. Collisions the unit steps around

- ***bysk* bedded is *mysk***: *ul bysket* would read *ul mysket* "in said". The Knot's gloss says "where there is a knot" (*ul vodh doss bysket*).
- ***mesk* softened is *vesk***: *um mesk* is *um vesk*, "upon seeing"; E2-06 keeps the article (*um et mesk*). *lo veskyl* "by sight" is also "at tree-ing"; *ul veskyl* is used.
- ***pa* after a trigger is *va***: *ul lodh pa stinel* would read "between your …"; the Breath says *stinel eth stinel*.
- **A pronoun subject before a mutable letter** (G9, from the tools' side): *re yal so cemmet*, *re yal o kethet* read as possessives; both clauses were recast (a bare participle; the active).
- **A trigger before *gor*** (pilot I.1 §3): "what each hull was told" is *vodh re hunn gor sceth*; "down every road" fronts *Gor orrol* and takes it up with *umo*.
- ***et Hosk* is the Title**: the Bar Before is *Hosk ul Lern*, without the article.
- ***cemm* softened is *hemm* "old"**: "gathered" is *derret* "gathered in, reaped".
- ***brodh* bedded is *mrodh*** (§2.5 forbids *mr-*): no *brodh* after *ul*, *dem*, *es*, *nath* anywhere in the unit; speaking stones are *wrodh* (softened), never bedded.
- ***lern* is "the face" before it is "a side"** (back-translation of S11): the flanks of a host are *et pa sest*, "the two shoulders" (E5-01); *pa lern* is left to mean two faces.
- **A word that is noun and verb at once** (lexicon §4.4) is heard as the noun where the same paragraph has used it as one, and the analyzer glosses it so: *scaevur* is "a ring" in *hosen cadh mesk scaevur*, so "while a wreck rolls" is said with the verbal noun, *ul scaevurol haelir* (back-translation of S11).
