### 1.1 The title

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

*The Thrones That Walk the Sea.* (The lexicon's title, O2093: "the great seats that go upon the sea".)

### 1.2 The headnote

> **Hosen tum Mesk Myst: kethet hy voltath et Crenneth Myst, sa vollant et hesperdeth Reskowath Strom, hos ul hos cumm, ul sestow ommol et reskow strom, amm hosast re yal mellorol et molt lonta.**

as · remember · Mystwood: held (known) · from(+S) · S·hearts · the · Mystarchs, · REL(+S) · name-3PL · the · soldiers · Thrones, · one · in(+N) · one · haven, · after · falling · the · throne, · when · first · PST(+S) · S·be.PST · reaching · the · heart · at-3DU.

*As the Mystwood remembers: known from the hearts of the Mystarchs, whom the soldiers call the Thrones, each in its own haven, as soon after its fall as its heart could be reached.*

Word for word: *As the Mystwood remembers: known from the hearts of the Mystarchs, whom the soldiers name the Great Seats, one in one haven, after the falling of the throne, when first the reaching of the heart was at the two of them.*

- **The opening is the wood leaves' formula**, as IV.4 lays it (*Hosen tum Mesk Myst: kethet hy …*), with the Thrones' hearts where the gift stood.
- *voltath* is *moltath* "hearts" softened after *hy* (m > v). Its possessor *et Crenneth Myst* (O0296) carries the article, so nothing mutates and the head takes none (the construct, §3.2).
- **"whom the soldiers call the Thrones"** is the relative with its gap for the object, *sa vollant et hesperdeth Reskowath Strom*: *voll* is "call by name" (canon), and the name follows. *Reskowath Strom*, "the Great Seats", is the plural of the lexicon's *reskow strom* "a throne" (O2092), as the title has it, capitalised as the soldiers' name (added, W02-01).
- **"each in its own haven" is *hos ul hos cumm***, "one in one haven" (W02-04). *o humm sost*, "its own haven", would put *cumm* into the softening, where it is heard as the reserve *humm* "husband" (lexicon §8.2, finding 2); *ul gor gumm*, "in every haven", would bed *gor* to *nor*.
- **"as soon after its fall as its heart could be reached"**: "after the fall of the throne, when first the reaching of the heart was at the two of them". It is the "at" idiom of ability in the past (§3.7), with Halyna's dual *lonta* (at-3DU), for it was they who reached it. *hosast* "first" stands before the particle, as pilot I.1's *Hosast re fennym*.

> **Vollant et velleth hemm so, ketherdeth strom et scethan strom tunn, lerneth dasken susk et Gannath Myst hemm sost: gannath hossen dasken et hald, tevet amm sy ul reskowath strom et gorrol, sa orrant, gor dhavowhinnar Orrow hos reskow strom.**

name-3PL · the · songs · old · them, · commanders · great · the · armada · whole, · shapes · final · high · the · Mystholders · eldest: · posts · living · last · the · wall, · grown · now · in(+N) · thrones · the · war, · REL(+S) · go-3PL, · every(+S) · S·harbour-city · Shore · one · throne.

*The old songs name them the sovereign commanders of the entire armada, the final exalted expressions of the eldest Mystholders: the last living pillars of the barrier, now grown into moving thrones of war, one for each Shoreland harbour-city.*

Word for word: *The old songs name them the great commanders of the whole armada, the last high shapes of the eldest Mystholders: the last living posts of the wall, grown now into thrones of the war, that walk; every harbour-city of the Shore, one throne.*

- **The old songs' titles are the Book's set phrases**, added to the lexicon as such (W02-07 to -10). "The sovereign commanders" is *ketherdeth strom*: the lexicon's *Ketherd* is "the Commander" (the Rite's name for the Captain) and "a commander"; lower-case and plural it is any commanders, as the songs meant. *strom* is "great; sovereign".
- **"Expressions" is *lerneth*, "shapes, faces"** (reserve *lern*): the Thrones as the last shapes the eldest Mystholders took. *brodhatath*, "things put into words", is the English word's other sense, and the wrong one.
- *Gannath Myst hemm sost*, "the eldest Mystholders", builds *hemm sost* as the lexicon's *mesk hemm sost*, "the eldest wood" (O1378). "The barrier" is *hald*, whose senses are "a wall; a barrier".
- **"now grown into moving thrones of war"** is *tevet amm sy ul reskowath strom et gorrol, sa orrant*: "grown now into thrones of the war, that walk", with the title's own verb for their going. The construct *reskowath strom yorrol* would be grammatical, but the analyzer reads a softened possessor only straight after its head, and *strom* stands between; the article keeps *gorrol* whole and the reading plain.
- **"one for each Shoreland harbour-city"** is pilot I.1's distributive *gor X hos Y* ("every True Man one troop"): *gor dhavowhinnar Orrow hos reskow strom*, "every harbour-city of the Shore, one throne". *tavowhinnar* is the shared lexicon's own "harbour-city" (O2494, the compound *tavow* + *hinnar*), softened after *gor*. *(Merge fix, `wf8/merge_report.md`: the first draft had the construct* hinnar Dhavow*, addition W02-02, a second form for a sense the lexicon already carried; a definite construct also reads "the city of the harbour", one city.)*
- *so*, "them", is followed by a pause (the wedge). The analyzer takes *so* before an unmutated consonant as the possessive "their" and asks for the softening; the pause keeps it the object pronoun (§3.5).

> **Gor fedh, re vellor hos hy Halyna ul molt et mesketh, hos eth ullen, eth re dhovv ullen lo hless, lo sest et hesperdeth, sa nath re orrant virr, hy vodh ew ludalen et Reskowath Strom, sennet sost; eth re heth et hos hyo o hosen re heth et hos ulo.**

every(+S) · time, · PST(+S) · S·come-in · one · from(+S) · Halyna · among(+N) · the · timbers, · one · and · other, · and · PST(+S) · S·wait · other · at(+S) · S·edge, · beside · the · soldiers, · REL(+S) · NEG · PST · go-3PL · near, · for · was · fearful · the · Thrones, · dead · even; · and · PST(+S) · S·hold · the · one · out-of-it · it · as · PST(+S) · S·hold · the · one · in-it.

*Each time, one of Halyna went in among the timbers, turn and turn about, and the other stayed at the edge with the soldiers, who would go no nearer, for the Thrones were fearful even dead; and the one outside knew it as the one within.*

Word for word: *Every time, one of Halyna went in among the timbers, the one and the other, and the other waited at the edge, beside the soldiers, who did not go near, for fearful were the Thrones, even dead; and the one outside held it as the one within held it.*

- "Each time" is *gor fedh*, "every time" (the lexicon's phrase). "One of Halyna" is *hos hy Halyna*, "one out of Halyna"; **"turn and turn about" is the IV.4 pilot's *hos eth ullen*** (O2858), and "the other" *ullen*.
- "Went in" is *mellor* "come in, enter" softened after *re*, as pilot I.1's *re vellor et trenn*. **"Among the timbers" is *ul molt et mesketh*, "in the heart of the timbers"**: the lexicon's "among" is "in the heart of", and it was a heart they went in to.
- "At the edge" is *lo hless*, *cless* "a raised edge" softened after *lo* (the onset *hl-*, as in *nahlennet*). "With the soldiers" is *lo sest et hesperdeth*, "at the shoulder of the soldiers" (the lexicon's "beside"): *lo et hesperdeth* alone would read "at the soldiers", the having idiom.
- "Who would go no nearer" is *sa nath re orrant virr*, "who did not go near", with the negative past of pilot I.1 (*nath re*, O2861).
- "For the Thrones were fearful even dead" is the copula's past with the quality first (as IV.4's *Ew duss sost brodhol*), and *sost* "even" after *sennet* "dead".
- **"The one outside knew it as the one within"** uses *keth* "hold", the canon's verb for knowing wood, with the conjugated prepositions *hyo* "out of it" and *ulo* "in it" (§3.6).

> **Doss molt reskow strom kethet ul hos hoss.**

is · heart · throne · held · in(+N) · one · breath.

*A Throne's heart is known in a breath.*

- *Doss … kethet*, "is held", is the state verb with the participle, as IV.4's *doss o rellorat*. "In a breath" is *ul hos hoss*, "in one breath" (W02-05).

> **Darr voll et reskow strom dasken, eth duss sost, hy vodh orr et voll ul et brodath dasken sost ul gethyl.**

comes · name · the · throne · last, · and · hard · very, · for · goes · the · name · into(+N) · the · words · last · very · in(+N) · N·knowing.

*Its name comes last and hardest, for a name is the last thing in a knowing that will go into words.*

Word for word: *The throne's name comes last, and hardest, for the name goes into the words the very last in a knowing.*

- "Its name" is *voll et reskow strom*, "the name of the throne": *o voll* would show no bite (*v* does not soften), so nothing would mark the possessive (§3.5).
- "Comes last and hardest": *darr* "come" in the present, the bare qualities after the verb as adverbs (lexicon §5.3), and *duss sost* "hardest" as IV.4 has it.
- **"For a name is the last thing in a knowing that will go into words"** is said as the tongue says it: the name goes into the words the very last. *ul et brodath* keeps *brodath* out of the bedding (*ul mrodath* would begin *mr-*, which §2.5 does not allow; IV.4 steps round it the same way). *ul gethyl* is *kethyl* "a knowing" bedded after *ul*.

> **Dossant cadhat ul drenn et cummath, eth nath nossant ul drenn et ommolath, ul lern Flennath et Cumm.**

be-3PL · laid · in(+N) · N·course · the · havens, · and · NEG(+N) · N·be-3PL · in(+N) · N·course · the · fallings, · facing · the Book of the Havens.

*They are set in the order of the havens and not of their falling, facing the Book of the Havens.*

Word for word: *They are laid in the course of the havens, and are not in the course of the fallings, facing the Book of the Havens.*

- "Set" is *cadh* "lay", the tongue's verb for setting down a telling. "In the order of" is *ul drenn*, "in the course of" (*trenn* bedded), as pilot I.1's *ul drenn vodh*.
- "And not of their falling" is *eth nath nossant ul drenn et ommolath*: *so hommol*, "their falling", could not show the softening on its vowel, so nothing would mark the possessive (§3.5).
- **"Facing the Book of the Havens" is *ul lern Flennath et Cumm***: the lexicon's title for Book Three (O0619) as the possessor of *ul lern*, exactly as IV.4's *ul lern Yebb et Pard* (*f* does not soften).

> **Re wrodha Halyna o, hos eth ullen, reskow strom eth reskow strom, ul ba luth; re hadh Seren o.**

PST(+S) · S·tell-3DU · Halyna · it, · one · and · other, · throne · and · throne, · in(+N) · N·two · ink; · PST(+S) · S·lay · Seren · it.

*Told by Halyna, a Throne each, in two inks; set down by Seren.*

Word for word: *Halyna told it, the one and the other, throne by throne, in two inks; Seren laid it down.*

- **The formula is IV.4's** (*Re wrodha Halyna o, hos eth ullen, ul ba luth; re hadh Seren o.*), word for word, with "a Throne each" laid beside "by turns": *reskow strom eth reskow strom*, "throne and throne", as *hos eth hos* is "one by one" (W02-03). Halyna take the dual (*wrodha*) and carry the sealing lintel; *Seren* has none.

> **Amm re yal et mesk cresket, ell re omm et brodhol, doss o rellorat eth nahlennet; eth hy flenn sy dem lern rytym et Reskowath Strom ul et vollath sa re hebbent lont sost.**

when · PST(+S) · S·be.PST · the · wood · broken, · or · PST(+S) · fail · the · telling, · is · it · marked · and · unmended; · and · from(+S) · leaf · this · forward · write-1SG · the · Thrones · in(+N) · the · names · REL(+S) · PST(+S) · give-3PL · at-3PL · self.

*Where the wood was broken, or the telling failed, it is marked, and not mended; and from this leaf on I write the Thrones by the names they gave themselves.*

Word for word: *Where the wood was broken, or the telling failed, it is marked and unmended; and from this leaf forward I write the Thrones in the names that they gave to themselves.*

- **The formula is IV.4's**, unchanged, to the semicolon. Then Seren's promise: "from this leaf on" is *hy flenn sy dem lern*, "from this leaf forward" (W02-06).
- **"I write" is *rytym***, *ryt* "cut letters, write" with the regular 1sg (slender *-ym*). The canon's *Rytom* is the oath's "I swear", a harmony slip the canon keeps (orrowen_v2 §3.9); Seren is not swearing.
- "The names they gave themselves": the giving is "at them" (*lont*, §3.6, as *Hebb et tolm lom* "give me the stone"), with *sost* "self".

*We came late. It was not we who burned your havens; that was the young wood, green and hasty, one sign to a hull.*

- **Reads:** (the root: the Throne's own line.) We come late, to your havens. The young wood burns your havens; we do not burn them. The young wood carves once, in its hulls.

- "It was not we … that was the young wood": two runners to one mark, ours mirrored.

- **The paragraph's last sentence was missing from the proof heart**, and is carved here (r3·4, r3·5).

### 2.3 Movement II (band II: ring 7, each Throne's own entry)

## 3 · WHAT THE TRANSLATION DID, AND WHY

**The grain.**

1. **Ten rounds, not one** (§11.4). III.2 is known "from the hearts of the Mystarchs … each in its own haven", so each Throne's heart is its own round, with its own root (the Throne's line), its own entry and its own fall. Band I is the opening knowings every heart holds, **the same GN in all ten** but for the root; the Book draws it truthfully in each round, glossable from the first Throne on.
2. **The heart stays plain** (§3, A9). Ring 1 has no fringe ("came late" is `DEEP+GO`, glossed as a compound), and band I no pocket: "you named us for beasts" is a NAME runner to `BEAST×3`, and the fear-names wait for band II's said-pockets. Band I does take the fringe outside ring 1 (`{most}`, `{last}`, `{still}`, `{all}`, `{slow}`, `{again}`), as §3 allows.
3. **The proof heart completed and corrected** (`wf7/coverage/tests/III-2_thaesaen.gn2`). It omitted the paragraph "They drew us up out of the earth and grew us into thrones … with the whole grown fleet in our keeping" (now r3·4–r3·5); it left the children of the long-lived in the grey (now in the gap, beside the groves); it cut "We came late" and "the sky to come again" with no going to the one come to (§9.2); "heavy" stood on a file of its own ("the weight"), now on the judgement's; and ring 8 cut the groves as thin, where the Book says the sky over them is.
4. **Names in the bark; Judgements cited.** No Throne's name is cut in its ring: the headnote says "a name is the last thing in a knowing that will go into words", and the grain agrees, cutting it last, in the seal's cup (A10). Each Judgement is its line's Deceit or Throne carving (E5-01, E5-02, T-01 to T-08), cited by its root half size in a cut-pocket (B14), so the Book draws the Judgement's own round beside ring 7.
5. **Truth or nothing.** Would-haves are hollow (Leavaren's kin that would have grown; Ralensaen's pillar that would walk home), and no heart cuts what it could not hold: the ten falls, and Neivaere's "what you saw in the grey" (the blind of place, `→ ∅`).
6. **Files are the pronouns.** "Came to us", "the sky held up", "only the sea in it", "came down … to you" and "they did not know us" end on files (`@12`, `@11`, `@4`, `@0`, `@12.r7/2`); "the spans were our kin" is said by standing the spans on the kin's file; "yourselves" is a runner to its own file (A4).
7. **Bands lead their knowings.** Wherever a band had to lie on one mark's year and not another's, the band's own mark leads its knowing, or the band lies alone on an empty file and a runner ends in it (the sky, the sea, the white, the night). Two places keep a band over more than the one mark they need, because a ring of four cells packs a file's marks into one year: Esthaer's black dust (the whole burning is black) and Senneir's dark (all our dead kin's holding is in the dark). Both are true of the telling.

**Findings for the spec and the validator** (none blocks anything; `wf8/additions/W02_grain.md` carries them for the builder):

- **`pack_round` does not see marks when it places a pocket.** It keeps the marks' cursors under `(pith, file)` and the pockets' under the bare file, so a pocket across a file that already holds marks in its ring lands in year 1 and raises K06 against those marks (the first draft of this unit, P3 on 12–13, did). §4.3 says a pocket takes a year "above the highest cursor among them". Every pocket here stands on files empty in its ring, as the pilots' do.
- **A half-size line root outside a pocket reads as a cited carving.** `SQUARE½` is the Mute Square cited, so the register's shard, `SQUARE½{only}[STILL]`, cannot stand in a ring. Proposed: `BREAK+SQUARE{only}` in the STILL band (§5.2).
- **`HAND+CLOSE` has two senses in the corpus**: E4-03's "the kept hand" (the validator's gloss) and E5-01's "close the hand", which Thaesaen's "We closed it" repeats. A15 might record the second.
- **`GREAT` reads "great hull"** where the register makes it "heavy" (ring 6), and **`>FALL` reads "fell"** where the register makes it "drive" (Vaelress). This unit used `>BENEATH`, "put beneath", for the second.
- **Two MIST bands in one year** (ring 2, year 1: files 12–12 and 11–11) are legal and print as two; a printer might join adjacent bands of one kind (11–12).
- **"Heavy", "close the hand", "what (the wood does not hold)", "put beneath" and "no tricks"** are register readings a blind decoder needs: listed in `W02_grain.md` for A15. The blind back-translation (`wf8/backtrans/W02.md`) added four more: **"wait for" what has not come** (the awaited thing hollow, `KNOT →for ~X`), **"hard to look upon"** (FLASH), **"hasty"** (NEW) and **"wronged"** (STRIKE).
- **Fixed after the blind back-translation** (`wf8/backtrans/W02.md`), four marks and two runners: band I's awaited sky is hollow (`11: ~GO{again}`) and its carvers many (`CARVE+US×3{last}`), in all ten hearts; Ralensaen's trees stand first on their file (`10: TREE×3`, and `g3` now runs to the canopy, `10.2.r7·3`); Naelthar's sails hold the sky (`SAIL×3+HOLD{last} →` the sky, where `→in` had put the sails in it).

**Orrowen.**

- **Every formula of IV.4's frame is kept word for word**: the opening *Hosen tum Mesk Myst: kethet hy …*, the telling's colophon *Re wrodha Halyna o, hos eth ullen, … ul ba luth; re hadh Seren o.*, *Amm re yal et mesk cresket, ell re omm et brodhol, doss o rellorat eth nahlennet*, and the closing *Eth re dhess nahos.* III.2's English adds "a Throne each" and Seren's promise about the names, and those are laid beside the formulas, not into them.
- **The lexicon's titles are used as they stand**: the leaf's own (*Reskowath strom sa orrant um wadh*, O2093), Book Three's (*Flennath et Cumm*), the Mystarchs and Mystholders (*Crenneth Myst*, *Gannath Myst*).
- **Collisions stepped round**: *o humm sost* (heard as "husband"); *ul gor gumm* (*gor* bedded to *nor*) and *hy gor* (softened to *yor*), which the grammar does not settle (pilot I.1); *ul mrodath* (an onset §2.5 forbids); *so* before an unmutated consonant, which reads as "their"; *o voll* and *so hommol*, whose possessive could not show; *Rytom*, the oath's "I swear", where Seren only writes.
- **Grammar the frame had to settle** (each reversible): a double object after *voll* "name" (*vollant … so, ketherdeth strom …*, "they name them commanders"); a construct whose head carries adjectives (*lerneth dasken susk et Gannath Myst hemm sost*; *gannath hossen dasken et hald*); the plural of *doss* with a participle for a passive (*Dossant cadhat*, *nath nossant*).
- **No root was coined.** Every word is in the lexicon; the ten rows added are the Book's set phrases and a construct, as pilot I.1 added its own (§5.1).
