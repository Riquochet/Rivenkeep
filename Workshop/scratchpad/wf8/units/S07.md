## 2 · V.1 · THE FIRST GLYPH

### Title

> ## *Rellor Hosast*

**V.1 · The First Glyph**

*The first sign.* (The lexicon's title, O2055.)

### Seren's headnote

> *Re hadha Halyna lo rask wress hosast et tynur, um yaldat et vess sa re yal et kiser hosast cresket; re hadhom o, eth ew en sa re heth et greller. Amm brodha Halyna, brodha sona hos eth ullen, trenn eth trenn; re rytym ul ba luth, eth nath re rellorom hos hy ullen.*

*Laid by Halyna at the end of the first month of the siege, of what was done on the night the first assault was broken; set down by me, who held the lamp. When Halyna tell, they tell by turns, a paragraph each; I have used two inks, and not marked whose is whose.*

Word for word: *Halyna (two) laid [it] at the end of the first month of the siege, concerning the deed of the night that the first assault was broken; I laid it, and it was I who held the lamp. When Halyna tell, they two tell one and the other, course and course; I wrote in two inks, and did not mark the one from the other.*

- **The opening is I.1's** (*Re hadh Crennel Kael, …*), with the dual: *Re hadha Halyna*.
- ***lo rask wress hosast et tynur***, "at the end of the first month of the siege": *ul* would bed *dask* "end" into *nask* "eat" (lexicon §8.2), so *lo* (+S); *bress* "month" is the possessor, softened (*wress*).
- ***um yaldat et vess …***, "concerning the deed of the night …": *um* "upon, about"; *galdat* "a deed, a thing done", softened after *um*.
- ***ew en sa re heth et greller***, "it was I who held the lamp": the cleft (§3.1), as IV.4's *ew o sa re hadh et tolm hosast*.
- ***hos eth ullen*** is IV.4's "by turns" (O2858); ***trenn eth trenn***, "course and course", is "a paragraph each", as unit S04 has it (S04-07; S02 has *trenn um dhrenn* for the same Book phrase: one of the two should win at the merge). ***ul ba luth***, "in two inks", is IV.4's. The subject pronoun *sona* in *brodha sona* keeps the second *brodha* from reading as an imperative plural.
- ***nath re rellorom hos hy ullen***, "I did not mark the one from the other": the pair's own *hos … ullen* again, and the negative past (G1).

### Halyna's telling

**¶1** *(first ink)*

> Kethen et tolm. Keth Seren et greller. Hosen ull re hadhan et tolm hosast; eth hosen ull kethen o tul.

We hold the stone. Seren holds the lamp. That is how this began, and how it has gone on.

Word for word: *We two hold the stone. Seren holds the lamp. Like that we two laid the first stone; and like that we two hold it still.*

- **"Began" is *cadh et tolm hosast***, "lay the first stone" (O2859, IV.4): the reserve *mosk* is the Creed trap. "How it has gone on" is said with the leaf's own verb: they *hold it still*.

**¶2** *(second ink)*

> Amm re yal et kiser hosast cresket, re lymm et hyll et kaelirvesk ul vimm et hald: crynireth myst eth crestalath, eth saed wreller sell hosen ganna, ul scaevurol ul et venth. Re vaverent et hesperdeth vorrol et kaelirvesk, eth re lusk et Crenn so, eth re vorrant et saed strom. Veth re rarr Hale Haskard dem susk hy et hylldhrenn, eth re vysk o: Doss rellorath ul et mesk. Eth re orra Halyna dem et dhysal, amm re yal et hyll kylet, eth Seren ul sestow, ul lymmyl et greller. Re harala Halyna lo et uldath: Bamma hos crynir dem susk hy et spant, eth cadha o um et tolmath! Re yal rytet ulo. Re orr o lo dhrenn et meskdhrenn, bynt eth venthen: hos rellor hosel.

When the first assault was broken, the tide brought the wreck-wood in under the wall: grey planks and ribs, and a length of keel as long as a gate, rolling in the shallows. The soldiers wanted to burn it, and the Captain let them burn most of it. But Hale the runner came up from the tide-line saying there were marks on the wood, and Halyna went down to the shingle when the tide had turned, with Seren behind them carrying the lamp. They had the men haul one plank up out of the surf and lay it on the stones. There was carving on it. It ran with the grain, fine and shallow, one figure and no more.

Word for word: *When the first assault was broken, the tide brought the wreck-wood under the wall: grey planks and ribs, and a part of a keel long as a gate, a-rolling in the shallows. The soldiers wanted the burning of the wreck-wood, and the Captain let them, and they burned the greater part. But Hale the Runner came up from the tide-line, and said: There are marks in the wood. And Halyna went to the shingle when the tide was turned, and Seren behind, carrying the lamp. Halyna bade the men: Draw one plank up out of the surf, and lay it on the stones! There was a carving in it. It went along the course of the grain, fine and shallow: one sign alone.*

- **Reported speech is quoted**, as I.1's cries are: Hale's *Doss rellorath ul et mesk*, and the pair's order to the men (*Bamma … cadha …*, the imperative plural). "They had the men haul" is that order.
- ***vorrol et kaelirvesk***, "the burning of the wreck-wood": the object of a verbal noun is its possessor (G3), said as a noun, because *o vorrol* would not show its bite (G9).
- ***bamm*** "draw, pull" for "haul": the reserve *cald* "haul" is avoided (its softening is *hald* "wall", lexicon §8.2).
- ***Hale Haskard***, as unit S02 gives "Hale the runner" (S02-03), in the apposition of *Kael Nydherd*.
- ***lo dhrenn et meskdhrenn***, "along the course of the grain": the grain is *meskdhrenn*, "the wood's course", so the carving runs "by the course of the wood's course". ***hos rellor hosel***, "one sign alone", is "one figure and no more".
- ***Re yal rytet ulo***, "there was a carving *in* it": with *um* ("upon") the line would have read the idiom *doss X um Y*, "Y feels X" (§3.7).

**¶3** *(first ink)*

> Re yal sull lonn rytyl Vystaeri lor amm ull, eth re hyrriler nayalat hyont: tolmath nawrodh ul vestul rytow; et tolm domm sa re sol lo so sest, esket ul dessyl uld hemm; eth amm sy, et kaelirvesk. Nath re wrodhant et tolmath, hy vodh lymm tolm nayalat. Re yalant haralath stemir rytet ul et mesk hossen, sesker amm sy; ul et vess ull nath re sesker galat veth rellorath ulo. Veth re luskant gostardath surrath et derryl brod ul et cummath: Hebb mesk domm kaever ul narl uld fedheth, amm sol o yarl lo ull sell loskat. Eth re yal nayalat ullen lor dem raevulol. Hy ull re femme Halyna o.

Of the Mystaeri's writing we had three kinds by then, and could make nothing of any of them: the mute stones in the archive chest, the dark stone that lay beside them wrapped in an old man's confession, and now the wreck-wood. The stones would not speak, for stone carries nothing. Battle orders were carved into the living wood, we know now; that night we knew only that there were marks. But the axemen of the harvest years had left a saying in the havens, that the black wood would sometimes put a thought into a man's hand if he rested it there long enough; and there was nothing else to try. So they touched it.

Word for word: *Three kinds of the Mystaeri's writing were at us then, and we understood nothing of them: the mute stones in the archive chest; the black stone that lay at their shoulder, covered in an old man's confession; and now, the wreck-wood. The stones did not speak, for stone carries nothing. Orders of battle were cut in the living wood, we know now; that night we knew nothing but marks in it. But the axemen of the years of the harvest had left a word in the havens: The black wood gives a thought into a man's hand at times, when his hand rests there long enough. And there was nothing other at us for trying. So Halyna touched it.*

- **This "we" is the wall's**, not the pair's: the plural (*lor, hyrriler, sesker*). Where the pair act, the verb is dual again (*Re femme Halyna o*).
- ***rytyl Vystaeri***, "the writing of the Mystaeri": the hybrid exonym softened as possessor (§3.2), as the analyzer reads it.
- ***lymm tolm nayalat***, "stone carries nothing": the same words as IV.1's *Veth lymm tolm nayalat* (unit S06).
- ***brod***, "a word", is "a saying", as the guild's saying is "the guild's word" (§11.11); the saying is quoted, in the habitual. ***ul narl uld***: *garl* "hand" bedded after *ul* (g > n, §3.3).
- ***dessyl***: *tessyl* "an answering; a confession (an answering for a deed)" bedded after *ul*.

**¶4** *(second ink)*

> Re hadh Rhyna pa yarlremull ryss um et meskdhrenn, eth re hadh Halvard pa yarl umont. Nath re haevere fedh raevulol hosel, hy vodh nath re seske tul: el loskat hos ganna yarl. Nath re wrodha sona. Re rarr et hyll um et dhysal, eth re orr. Re vorr et greller ryssyth nind dem vimm.

Rhyna laid her palms flat on the grain, and Halvard laid his hands over hers. It never came into either of their heads to try it alone, for they did not yet know that one pair of hands was enough. They did not speak. The tide came and went on the shingle. The lamp burned down a finger's width.

Word for word: *Rhyna laid two palms flat upon the grain, and Halvard laid two hands upon them. They two did not ever think of trying alone, for they did not yet know: one pair of hands is enough. They two did not speak. The tide came upon the shingle, and went. The lamp burned down a finger's breadth.*

- ***pa yarlremull***, ***pa yarl***: duals, "two palms", "two hands". The pair's four hands are counted, not owned: *ey* "her" does not mutate, so *ey garlremullath* could not show "her" (G9).
- ***hos ganna yarl***, "one gate of hands": a pair is a gate (§5, *ganna*).
- ***nath re seske tul***, "they did not yet know": *nath … tul* is "not yet" (§3.8).

**¶5** *(first ink)*

> Hy ull re dhald et kethyl ul et pa yarlremull, ell lern et kethyl. Mysk ey: re vorr o, nel hosen vorr vorr, veth hosen vorr dusstyvil brid ul et grem; eth re yal lern et rellor sost ulo: hos stinel ul gylyl dem lern, hosen fodh sa es gresk. Re heth Halvard o hosen re heth ey o, hy vodh el ull et lodh; eth ew Halvard sa re wrodh. Re vysk o: Orrow.

Then the knowing came up into her palms, or the shape of one. She says it burned, not as fire burns but as cold iron burns in winter, and that the sign's own shape was in it: one stroke curling forward, like a wave about to break. Halvard knew it as she knew it, for that is the bond, and it was Halvard who spoke. He said, "Shore."

Word for word: *Then the knowing rose into the two palms, or the shape of the knowing. She says: It burned, not as fire burns, but as cold iron burns in the winter; and the shape of the sign itself was in it: one stroke curling forward, like a wave that will break. Halvard held it as she held it, for that is the bond; and it was Halvard that spoke. He said: Shore.*

- ***ul et pa yarlremull***: the article stands before *pa* so that the dual's softening shows (after *ul*, the nasalised *ba* is not read as a trigger).
- ***lern et kethyl***, "the shape of the knowing": a softened *kethyl* in the construct would be *hethyl*, which reads as the reserve *heth* "spring" (lexicon §8.2), so the possessor keeps its article.
- ***nel hosen vorr vorr***, "not as fire burns": the verb, then its subject, as in the vow's *somm el tolm tolm*.
- ***ul et grem***: the article keeps *grem* from bedding into *nrem*, an onset §2.5 does not allow.
- ***Re heth Halvard o hosen re heth ey o*** is IV.4's line ("he knew it as she knew it"), with his name. ***eth ew Halvard sa re wrodh***: the cleft.
- ***Orrow***: "the Shore", the Wave's one word.

**¶6** *(second ink)*

> Nath re yal myskyl lo hosen vodh re heth o o. Re vysk o hy ull: ew hosen amm re hadh hos et brod ul o wrodow, eth re orr dem neld. Re vysk Rhyna: El venn o. Re hethe pana o ul neld et brod; veth nath re orr et ullen ul et brodath, hy vodh ew ull kaever lurr, eth nath re yal veth kaever et orrow lonta. Ew ull gor yalat sa re yal o wrodhol lonta, veth re hethe et crynir dem sennyl et greller.

He could not say how he knew it. He said afterward that it was as though someone had put the word in his mouth and gone away. Rhyna said he was right. They knew more than the word, both of them; but the rest would not go into words, for it was a forest's thought, and they had only the shore's. That was all of it they could tell, though they held the plank until the lamp went out.

Word for word: *Saying how he held it was not at him. He said afterward: It was as if one laid the word in his mouth, and went away. Rhyna said: It is true. Both held it beyond the word; but the rest did not go into the words, for that was a forest's thought, and there was nothing but the shore's thought at them two. That was all whose telling was at them two, though they held the plank until the going-out of the lamp.*

- **"Could not" is the "at" idiom** in the past, *nath re yal* VN *lo* (§3.7), as I.1's *nath re yal nydhyl lom*.
- ***hosen amm***, "as if" (O2863). ***ul o wrodow***, "in his mouth": *brodow* softened after *o*, so the possessive shows.
- ***ul neld et brod***, "beyond the word", for "more than the word". ***ul et brodath***: the article keeps *brodath* from *mr-* (IV.4).
- ***nath re yal veth kaever et orrow lonta***: "only" of a clause is *nath … veth* (G8). *Back-translation fix:* the draft had *kaever Orrow*, and a vowel shows no softening, so the possessor could not be told from apposition: straight after Halvard's *Orrow* it was read "the thought *Shore*" (the one word they had), not "the shore's thought". With the article, *kaever et orrow* can only be the construct (§3.2, *hosk et ganna*), set against *kaever lurr*, "a forest's thought".
- ***gor yalat sa re yal o wrodhol lonta***, "all whose telling was at them": the relative with its resumptive possessive (G5).
- ***dem sennyl et greller***, "until the going-out of the lamp": *senn* is to go out as a fire goes out in peace. *dem amm* "until when" is avoided (it beds to *dem mamm*, I.1).

**¶7** *(first ink)*

> Re ryt Seren o. Re rik ey hos flenn tunn hy et flennath sy demo, eth re ryt ey hos brod ul molt et flenn, Orrow, eth nayalat ullen; eth re ryt ey et rellor um et flenn ul lern. Mysk ey: re yal o lyss sost lo ull.

Seren wrote it down. She took a whole leaf of this Book for it, and wrote one word in the middle of the page, *shore*, and nothing else, and drew the sign on the facing leaf. She says it looked very small there.

Word for word: *Seren wrote it. She took one whole leaf from this Book for it, and wrote one word in the heart of the leaf, Shore, and nothing else; and she drew the sign upon the leaf in front. She says: It was very small there.*

- ***ryt*** is "cut letters, write; draw (a sign)" at once: Seren writes the word and draws the sign with one verb. ***ul molt et flenn***, "in the heart of the leaf", is "in the middle of the page". ***et flenn ul lern***, "the leaf in front", is I.1's facing leaf.

**¶8** *(second ink)*

> Hy ull re yal tolm hosast et Kethyl cadhat, um et dhysal ul vimm et hald sy. Ul et clemeth velv ul lodh et kethyleth, re rystulla Halyna brodhen dagorn Vesk Myst, brodhen et bynd, sa nath noss vesket veth kethet; eth re wrodha o, hos rellor gor fedh. Galdar o hosen ull tul, gor fedh amm cresk kiser: et kaelir bammat dem susk, hos garl Halyna um et meskdhrenn, eth et greller lo Seren; hy vodh re rystulla, hy et vess ull: galat sa heth hos, keth ullen o. Nath hebb et mesk et kethyl tunn lo vrener hosel. Re hadhant hesperdeth so yarlath umo, eth nath re ledhent veth crynireth thenth. Re raevul et Lodhan ullen hy et Stonwrytan o hy ull, eth re heth merr, veth nath re yal o hethyl sell loskat lo, dem o wrodhol. Re hadh Seren hos garl umo, hos fedh, hosel, eth re ledh ey galat sa re rarr uloy, strom hy hethyl; eth re lusk ey.

So the Knowing began, on the shingle below this wall, and in the quiet hours between the holdings Halyna set to learning the enemy's gentle Mystwood tongue, which is not read but known, and to telling it, one sign at a time. It is done so still after every assault: the wreck hauled up, one of Halyna's hands on the grain, and Seren with the lamp; for they have learned since that night that what one knows, the other knows. The wood gives its knowing whole to no single mind. Soldiers have laid their hands on it and felt only wet planks. Other bonded pairs of the guild have tried it since, and known a little, and could not hold it long enough to tell it. Seren laid her hand on it once, alone, and felt something come into her too great to hold, and let go.

Word for word: *So the first stone of the Knowing was laid, upon the shingle below this wall. In the quiet hours between the holdings, Halyna learned the gentle tongue of the Mystwood, the tongue of the foe, which is not read but held; and they two told it, one sign every time. We do it like that still, every time an assault breaks: the wreck drawn up, one hand of Halyna upon the grain, and the lamp at Seren; for they two have learned since that night: the thing that one holds, the other holds it. The wood does not give the knowing whole to a single mind. Soldiers have laid their hands upon it, and felt nothing but wet planks. The other Bonded of the guild have tried it since, and held a little, but its holding, long enough, was not at them, for its telling. Seren laid one hand upon it, once, alone, and felt a thing that came into her, greater than holding; and she let go.*

- ***ul lodh et kethyleth***, "in the mortar of the holdings": *ul lodh* is "between" (what lies between stones), and ***kethyleth*** is the wall's holdings and the wood's knowings at once (*keth*: hold, keep, know). The English pun survives whole.
- ***brodhen dagorn Vesk Myst, brodhen et bynd***, "the gentle tongue of the Mystwood, the tongue of the foe": *Mesk Myst* softened as possessor; two possessors cannot hang on one noun, so the tongue is named twice. ***sa nath noss vesket veth kethet***, "which is not read, but held" (G8).
- ***Galdar o hosen ull tul***, "we do it like that still", for "It is done so still": after *o* a mutable letter is read as a possessive (G9), so *doss o galdat* would say "its building is". ***gor fedh amm cresk kiser***, "every time an assault breaks".
- ***galat sa heth hos, keth ullen o***, "what one holds, the other holds it": the fronted object taken up by *o* (G6).
- ***hos garl***, "one hand": Seren is unbonded and "has one hand" (orrowen_v2 §11.4). ***strom hy hethyl***, "greater than holding": *hy* is "than" (§3.6).
- "Other bonded pairs of the guild" is ***et Lodhan ullen hy et Stonwrytan***, "the other Bonded of the guild": a collective, with the 3sg verb (G2). (*gannath*, the plural of *ganna* "pair", is already "posts; the black pillars".)

## 3 · V.2 · THE NIGHT OF THE NAMING

### Title

> ## *Vess Vollol*

**V.2 · The Night of the Naming**

*The night of the naming.* (The lexicon's title, O2738: *vollol* is *voll* "a name, to name" with the verbal noun.)

### Seren's headnote

> *Re hadh Seren Pa Luth virr lo rask Hyll Pawast, ul et vess sa re hethe Halyna crynireth et mesk tevel ul dunnoth, lo rask. Solant et crynireth sa wrodh ey umont ul et nelter vimm sost tul; orr hos dem vimm amm maver, eth cadh hos o yarl umont. Doss flenn et mesk ul o lern.*

Word for word: *Seren Two-Inks laid [it] near the end of the Second Tide, on the night that Halyna held the planks of the young wood in wholeness, at the end. - ***Hyll Pawast*** and ***mesk tevel***, "the Second Tide" and "the young wood", are the lexicon's (O1048, O1381).

- **"Anyone may"** is said as a habit, "one goes down when he wishes": *doss lunn lo gor hos* would put *gor* after a softening trigger, which the grammar leaves unsettled (I.1 §3).
- ***Doss flenn et mesk ul o lern***, "the wood's leaf faces it", as units S06 and S08 give the line (S08-07).

### Seren's telling

- **"Not all of it" is the verb said back** (*Nath re hether gor yalat*): Orrowen has no "no" (§3.4).

**¶16**

All that first winter we had said that they picked the wrong fortress. Kael said it first and we all said it after him, and laughed. They had not picked anything. They had one word apiece and no other, and they had been sent.

Word for word: *Through that first winter we said: They chose the wrong keep! Kael said it first, and we said it after Kael, every one, and we laughed. They did not choose a thing. One word was at them, apiece, and nothing other; and they had been sent.*

- ***lo dhrenn et grem***, "through the winter", as unit S05 (S05-22). The wall's jest is quoted.
- ***naelur***, "choose", both times; ***navenn***, "false, mistaken, wrong". ***luskat***, "loosed; one sent": *re yalant luskat*, "they had been sent".

**¶17**

> Amm re dhald et orl, re hadhom et pa hrynir sest lo sest um et pressir, et hosast sost eth et hos hy et dask deld; eth re hemm et meskdhrenn cresket, hosen cemme pa saed et mesk treskat. Nath re vessym lonta vodh kaever o, eth nath re wrodha o lom.

When it was light I laid the two planks side by side on the floor, the first of all and the one from the far end, and the broken grain met, as the halves of a split log meet. I have not asked them what it means, and they have not told me.

Word for word: *When the day rose, I laid the two planks shoulder at shoulder upon the floor, the very first and the one from the far end; and the broken grain met, as the two halves of the riven log meet. I did not ask them two what it means, and they two did not tell it me.*

- ***sest lo sest***, "shoulder at shoulder", is "side by side", as unit S11 gives it (O-S11-16).
- ***re hemm***, "met" (*cemm* softened), sounds as *hemm* "old": the canon's own homophony (lexicon §8.2), settled by *re*.
- ***pa saed et mesk treskat***, "the two halves of the riven log": *treskat* keeps the canon's broad ending. The article keeps *mesk* from being read as the bedded *besk* "stranger".
- **The yes-and-no rule**: *nath re vessym … eth nath re wrodha o lom*, the verb said back.

**¶18**

> Hy ull re reske Halyna um vressir et nelter, ul molt et crynireth, eth re reskym lonta; eth lo dhrenn fedh sell, re wrodh nahos. Hy ull re vyskym galat sa re rytym lo sy, eth re luska o, eth re ston o.

Then Halyna sat down on the floor of the vault among the planks, and I sat down with them, and for a long while no one spoke. Then I said what I have written here, and they let it stand.

Word for word: *Then Halyna sat upon the floor of the vault, amid the planks, and I sat with them two; and through a long time no one spoke. Then I said the thing that I wrote here, and they two let it be, and it stood.*

- ***re wrodh nahos***, "no one spoke", is built as the wood leaves' close, *Eth re dhess nahos*.
- **"They let it stand"** is ***re luska o, eth re ston o***, "they let it be, and it stood": *ston* is the wall's standing and the oath's last word.

**¶19**

> Re haeverer: El gennarath so. Ew et tevel sost so, luskat hosast, hy vodh tev et tevel pynt sost. Nath noss dath sost et mesk ul ol sestow. Doss o ul devyl tul.

We had thought them fools. They were the youngest, sent first because the young grow quickest. The worst of the wood is not behind us. It is still growing.

Word for word: *We thought: They are fools. They were the youngest, sent first, for the young grow quickest. The worst of the wood is not behind us. It is in the Growing still.*

- ***tev … tevel***: "grow" and "young" are one root (*tev* "a child; grow"), so the reason is heard in the words.
- ***Doss o ul devyl tul***, "it is in growing still": the progressive (§3.4); *tevyl*, "a growing", is also V.6's title word (*Of the Growing*), so the last line of the leaf points at the leaf that will tell it.
- ***ul ol sestow***, "in our back": behind us.

### The close

> Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

> *Eth re vysk et odh:* **Tumar.**

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

(Both are the pilots' formulas exactly: O0238, and O0564 with *Tumar*.)

## 6 · NOTES FOR JACK AND FOR THE MERGE

1. **"Only a mark" as *hosel kael*, "only a notch"** (V.1, the pair's last line). The tongue's *rellor* is sign and mark at once, so the contrast needs another word; *kael* is the mark that counts and says nothing, and it is Kael's name. **[Jack]** if the chime is too much: *hosel ryt*, "only a cut". After the back-translation the clause is *rellor sa nath noss trenn lo, el hosel kael o* (either word slots into *el hosel … o*).
2. **The soldiers' seven names** are unit S11's renderings of Seren's heads, reused so that the wall says them one way (*Dem et orrow* … *Dem varn, amm grestet*).
3. **"A paragraph each"**: this unit follows S04 (*trenn eth trenn*); S02 has *trenn um dhrenn*. One should win.
4. **"Sentence"** is *trenn*, as in IV.4. The lexicon row for *trenn* (O2579) already lists it, "a sentence (a course of words)", among its eleven senses; the blind back-translation still read *nath re yal trenn* as "there was no tale" (the row's earlier sense), which the context allows, so no change was made.
5. **Collisions stepped around** (all by the rules already written): *ul dask* > *nask* "eat" (so *lo rask*); *cald* "haul" > *hald* "wall" (so *bamm*); *ul grem* > *nrem* and *ul brodath* > *mr-* (the article); *ol bramm* > *mr-* (so *hy ol hald*); *dem amm* > *dem mamm* (so *dem sennyl*); *gor* after a trigger (recast each time); *hethyl* in a construct heard as *heth* "spring" (the article); *ul mesk* heard as bedded *besk* "stranger" (the article); *doss o galdat* and *re yal o cadhat* heard as possessives (recast active); *ul ba* + a softened word, which the analyzer does not read as *pa*'s softening (*ul et pa …*, a small analyzer gap worth closing).
6. **The mystaeri spec's own note on E1-01** (`wf6/mystaeri_spec.md` §5.5: its round is split along its axis, "the two planks of V.2") is what ¶4 and ¶17 tell; V.1's chip and V.2's first round are the same sign, drawn by Seren and carved by the wood.
7. **Still owed**, as for all tier-3 text: the Welsh, Irish and Tolkien dictionary pass on the reserve roots this unit uses (81 roots): *bamm*, *bisk*, *breller*, *bress*, *brid*, *bynd*, *bynt*, *cless*, *crestal*, *crynir*, *dagorn*, *dath*, *deld*, *demull*, *derr*, *desir*, *dhysal*, *dhyvull*, *drig*, *drod*, *duss*, *dyll*, *dynd*, *esk*, *fedh*, *felm*, *femm*, *fenn*, *fodh*, *gennar*, *gost*, *greller*, *grik*, *haral*, *havul*, *hellur*, *hinnar*, *hydull*, *hyrril*, *kaelir*, *kaever*, *kiser*, *ledh*, *lern*, *lestir*, *lonn*, *losk*, *lymm*, *mamm*, *maver*, *mellor*, *merr*, *mysk*, *naelur*, *nelter*, *nind*, *prener*, *pressir*, *pynt*, *raevul*, *rellor*, *resk*, *ryss*, *rystull*, *saed*, *scaevur*, *sest*, *spant*, *stemir*, *stin*, *styvil*, *susk*, *ternil*, *thenth*, *tynur*, *velv*, *venth*, *vestul*, *vimm*, *virr*, *wesk*.
