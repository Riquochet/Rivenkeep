## 1 · SEREN'S FRAME, IN ORROWEN (THE LEAF-HAND, HER ONE INK)

### 1.1 The title

> **Farrol et Aelvaren**

| Farrol | et | Aelvaren |
|---|---|---|
| voyage | the | Aelvaren |

*The Voyage of the Aelvaren.* (The lexicon's title, O0571: *farrol* "a voyage; sailing" is *farr* + *-Ol*; *Aelvaren* is the Mystaeri name, written by sound. Names are names.)

### 1.2 The headnote

> **Hosen tum Mesk Myst: kethet hy volt vovennerd Hyll Dhumol, hy vodh myske Halyna: keth gor sceth tevet hy amm ull o ul saed, eth ew baeg sost o ul et molt sy; doss o ul lern Sulter sa vorr.**

as · remember · Mystwood: held (known) · from · S·heart · S·Standard-Bearer · Tide · S·Remembering, from · what · say-3DU · Halyna: holds · every(+S) · hull · grown · from · when · that · it · in(+N) · part, and · was · bright · most · it · in · the · heart · this; is · it · in(+N) · face · Burning-Boat.

*As the Mystwood remembers: known from the heart of a Standard-Bearer of the Tide of Remembering, for Halyna say every hull grown since holds this in some measure, and this heart held it clearest; it faces the Burning Boat.*

- *Hosen tum Mesk Myst: kethet hy …* and *doss o ul lern …* are IV.4's formulas exactly.
- ***hy volt vovennerd Hyll Dhumol*** is a chain of constructs, each possessor softened: *molt* "heart" after *hy*; *movennerd* "a Standard-Bearer" ("the one of the banner", O1432) as its possessor; *Hyll Dhumol*, the Tide of Remembering, as IV.4 writes it. IV.2's headnote (unit W03) builds its "heart of a Standard-Bearer of the Second Tide" the same way, *hy volt vovennerd Hyll Pawast*.
- **"Halyna say" is the dual**, *myske* (3DU), as the formal register requires (§3.9, item 1). What they say is given as said, after the colon: Orrowen has no "that" before a clause (pilot I.1).
- *gor sceth tevet hy amm ull*, "every hull grown from that time": *tevet* is the participle of *tev* "grow, sprout"; *hy amm ull*, "from then". "In some measure" is ***ul saed***, "in part".
- **"This heart held it clearest" is recast with the copula**: *ew baeg sost o ul et molt sy*, "it was clearest in this heart" (*baeg* "bright; clear", *sost* for the most). The direct *re heth et molt sy o baeg sost* would set the object pronoun *o* before *b*, and *o* before a letter that softens reads as "its" (§3.5): the analyzer asks for *waeg*.
- *Sulter sa vorr* is IV.5's title (O2425); *s* does not soften, so the facing formula carries it unchanged.

> **Re rik Halvard o ul et nelter, somm re yal Rhyna um et hald, eth re heth ey o hosen re heth o o; myskent et hesperdeth lo ey sest: re hast ey pa yarl ul et nunth helvnynth, eth re nahebb ey myskyl hy vodh.**

PST · S·pick-up · Halvard · it · in(+N) · the · vault, while · PST · S·be · Rhyna · upon · the · wall, and · PST · S·hold · she · it · as · PST · S·hold · he · it; say-3PL · the · soldiers · at · her · shoulder: PST · plunge · she · two(+S) · S·hand · in(+N) · the · butt · rain-water, and · PST · refuse · she · saying · why.

*Halvard took it up in the vault while Rhyna was on the wall, and she knew it as he knew it; the soldiers beside her say she plunged both hands into the rain-butt, and would not say why.*

- *re rik* is *grik* "pick up" softened after *re*, the *g* dropping before a consonant, as IV.4's *sa re rik Brenn*. *ul et nelter*, "in the vault" (the reserve *nelter*, "an arched room below"), as W03 writes Halvard's vault in IV.2.
- *somm* "while" is canon. *um et hald*, "on the wall", as Kael's *Re yalom um et ganna*.
- ***eth re heth ey o hosen re heth o o*** is IV.4's line with the pair turned: there he knew it as she knew it (*re heth o o hosen re heth ey o*); here she knew it as he did. *keth* "hold" is the canon's verb for knowing wood.
- *lo ey sest*, "at her shoulder", is *lo sest* "beside" with the possessive between, as I.1's *lo ol sest*.
- **"Both hands" is the dual itself**, *pa yarl*, orrowen_v2 §3.2's own example ("two hands"): the dual already says "both". *hast* "push; plunge; shove" (reserve); *nunth helvnynth*, "a rain-butt" (O1824).
- **"Would not say why" is *re nahebb ey myskyl hy vodh***, "she refused the saying of why" (*nahebb* "refuse", 'un-give'). *mysk* stands after no nasalising word anywhere in the frame, so it is never heard as *bysk* (the pilots' trap).

> **Re yal et brodhol pynt, hy vodh el lomm et hos galat ul et mesk sa ross brodath loskat umo lo Orrow.**

PST · S·be · the · telling · quick, from · what · is · grief · the · one · thing · in(+N) · the · wood · REL · S·be · words · enough · upon-it · at(+S) · Shore.

*It was quickly told, for grief is the one thing in the wood for which the shore has words enough.*

- **"It was quickly told" is "the telling was quick"**, the state verb with the verbal noun, as the formula's own *ell re omm et brodhol*. The passive *re yal o brodhat* would set *o* before *b* again (read "its telling").
- **"Grief is the one thing …" is the copula's cleft** (*El et tolm sa heth*): *el lomm et hos galat ul et mesk*, "grief is the one thing in the wood".
- **"For which the shore has words enough" is the "at" idiom** (*Doss X lo Y*, §3.7) in a relative: *sa ross brodath loskat umo lo Orrow*, "that words enough are about it at the Shore". *um* is also "about, concerning" (what a thing is told upon), and the conjugated *umo* takes the relative's gap, as pilot I.1's *sa re rytent … umo* (G5). The relative particle softens the state verb, ***doss* > *ross*** (§3.3, *d* > *r*): the first present relative of *doss* in the pilots' Orrowen; the analyzer reads it so, and it is flagged in §3.
- *Orrow*, "the Shore", is the canon's own name (O1880): the shore that has the words is the Shore's people and its tongue.

> **Re wrodha Halyna o, hos eth ullen, ul ba luth; re hadh Seren o.**

PST · S·tell-3DU · Halyna · it, one · and · other, in · N·two · ink; PST · S·lay · Seren · it.

*Told by Halyna, by turns, in two inks; set down by Seren.*

- IV.4's formula exactly (O2858 *hos eth ullen*). *Halyna* carries its sealing lintel in the leaf-hand; *Seren* has none ("one name is a mourning").

> **Amm re yal et mesk cresket, ell re omm et brodhol, doss o rellorat eth nahlennet.**

when · PST · S·be · the · wood · broken, or · PST · fail · the · telling, is · it · marked · and · unmended.

*Where the wood was broken, or the telling failed, it is marked, and not mended.*

- IV.4's formula exactly. IV.6 has no such gap, but the headnote's promise stands on every wood leaf.

*This is held in the grain. And not in this grain only. The hull that brings it to you was not there; but it knows, for it grew out of what we were.*

- "It grew out of what we were" is a *then* runner from us to its growing, `BOND+BEARER ⇒ GROW`: "from" is the runner's source end (§6.5). "For" is `→bc`.
- "To you" cannot end in the heart ring, where file 0 is the root's (A4), so the runner points into ring 2, `@0.r2/2`, as the fix B16 did.

## 3 · WHAT THE TRANSLATION DID, AND WHY

### 3.1 From the prototype to the whole leaf

The grain2 prototype (grain_v2 §12) was a first carving to prove the layout. W04 keeps its root, bands, files, its break (`e1 ⊳ f1`), its braid (`abab!`) and its pocket, and brings it to the pilot's standard:

1. **The §21 fixes (B16)**: the kind-arc for every hull in the heart; no terminal in the root's cells (`a2 → @0.r2/2`); runners to a referent cut in a full year run to the mark; the cut-pocket within six files.
2. **Every content word carved.** Uncut in the prototype, and cut here: *grew out of* (r1); *light, your shore, home* (r2); *boards, a child of the long-lived, goes* (r3); *shoved off, away from home* (r4); *sailed ourselves, charred, the way home* (r5); *on that road, passed near, ran* (r6: now marks and runners, not a re-cut *us*); *where no keel has gone* as *never*, *all that way* (r7); *black, pale, standing down to the water* (r8); *the eight things the keel held*, as the Book lists them (r9); *the host, the blade, they did not know* (r10); *their dead, root to root, the far edge of the world* (r11).
3. **One referent, one file, never re-cut** (§10.11): the prototype's extra `BOND+BEARER` marks, cut only to give a runner a source, are gone; *us* is file 12 and `@12`. The general law of ring 3 stands on its own files (the long-lived's, and wood's), not on the Guest's.
4. **Truth or nothing, in the purpose**: the rite made (r2) is hollow, never made; the carrying home is whole, because it happened.
5. **Bands written first in their knowings** (the IV.4 finding), and the negated mark first where a memory ray leaves it.

### 3.2 Findings for the spec and the validator (none blocks anything)

1. **A pocket in knowing form is always packed into its ring's first year** (`pack_round` keys a pocket's cursors by file, a mark's by `(pith, file)`, so neither sees the other). A pocket on a file that holds a mark in its ring fails K06, and a pocket with empty files lands in year 1 even when its governing mark is in year 2. IV.6's P1 moved to files 3–8, and prints in r9/1 while its *that* runner leaves r9/2 (legal: inside a ring a runner may run inward). Proposed fix in `wf8/additions/W04_grain.md`.
2. **Two *to* runners from one act** (object and goal): "carried the Guest … to your shore" (r2), "carry him … home" (r2), "sailed ourselves home" (r5). The grain's *to* is both the done-to and the goal (*na*), so the reading takes them by context; where a place could be cut, it is (the SHORE of r2, the HOME of r5), and "out into the open sea" uses `→in @4`. Worth a line in §6.1: two *to* runners from one mark are read object first (the thing), goal second (the place or bearing).
3. **Two bands on one file in one year** (r8: DARK and STILL on our file, "black and small … and were still"). With four cells the knowings share a year, so the bands cannot be told apart in time. Legal, and the plates will show both; if Jack wants them apart, a band could be allowed to name its cells.
4. **The pocket and its governing mark** (ring 9): the Book should draw IV.4's round beside ring 9's plate, as §8 does for a whole round cited, since what the keel held is the IV.4 story.
5. The validator's plain reading glosses `ROOT×3` as "the root-men (roots)" and `!MOUTH?` as "does not speak?": the register's *roots* and *ask* are meant. A15 rows for this leaf are in `wf8/additions/W04_grain.md`.
6. **From the blind back-translation** (`wf8/backtrans/W04.md`): a *to* runner from a thing that does nothing reads as motion ("goes to"), so a place's extent ("standing down to the water") needs its act cut, in ligature with the thing that stands (`SILVERBARK×3+RISE`); and `!HOLD →` an act that carries the blind needs the reading "did not know why", which §6.1 alone does not give. Both are fixed here (ring 8 cuts `SILVERBARK×3+RISE`; A15 rows added).

### 3.3 Orrowen

- **No new word.** Every word of the frame is in the lexicon (145 words); W04's Orrowen additions file is empty but for its header.
- **Two recasts to keep a pronoun from reading as a possessive** (§3.5): "this heart held it clearest" as *ew baeg sost o ul et molt sy*; "it was quickly told" as *re yal et brodhol pynt*.
- **"Both hands" is the dual** *pa yarl*; "would not say" is *nahebb*, refuse.
- **For Jack: *sa ross***, the relative of the state verb in the present, is *sa* + S *doss* by the letter of §3.3 (*d* > *r*). It is regular, but it is the first time the pilots write it, and *ross* looks like a word of its own. The alternative is to avoid the relative of *doss* (*lomm, et hos galat ul et mesk; doss brodath loskat umo lo Orrow*, "grief, the one thing in the wood: there are words enough for it at the Shore").
