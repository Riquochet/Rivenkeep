## THE LEAF

### Title

> ## *Hylleth sa re nydh et Hald*

**V.5 · The Tides, as the Wall Counted Them**

*The Tides that the Wall counted.* (The lexicon's title, O1054: *Hylleth* tide-PL, *sa re nydh*, "that counted", *et Hald*, "the Wall".)

### Seren's headnote

> *Re hadh Kael Nydherd ul vran trenn, hos trenn lo rask hos hyll; hy vodh nath re hadh o hyll veth amm re yal o tunn. Doss vollath et hylleth lo et hald sost. El flenn et tolm um et tevyl; eth doss flenn et mesk ul o lern.*

*Laid by Kael the Counter in six parts, each at the end of its Tide, for he would not tell a Tide until it was over. The names of the Tides are the wall's own. It is the stone leaf of the growing, and the wood's leaf faces it.*

Word for word: *Laid Kael the Counter in six courses, one course at the end of one Tide; for he did not lay a Tide but when it was whole. The names of the Tides are at the wall itself. It is the leaf of the stone upon the growing; and the leaf of the wood is in its face.*

- **Active, as in I.1's headnote** (*Re hadh Crennel Kael, …*): "laid" is *cadh*, and to tell is to lay.
- ***ul vran trenn*, "in six courses".** A *trenn* is "a tale laid in one night", and each of Kael's parts was laid on its own night, at its Tide's end. *saed* "part" would also serve; S05 uses it for Halyna's two parts.
- ***hos trenn lo rask hos hyll***, "one course at the end of one Tide": the counter's distributive, as I.1's *hos eth hos*. *lo rask gor hyll* would set a softening trigger before *gor*, which the grammar does not settle (pilot I.1 §3).
- ***nath re hadh o hyll veth amm re yal o tunn***, "he did not lay a Tide but when it was whole". "Only when" is *nath … veth* (G8), and a Tide is laid when it is whole (*tunn*), like a course of stones.
- ***Doss … lo et hald sost***: the "at" idiom of having (§3.7). The wall itself has the names, so they are "the wall's own" (*sost*, "self; own").
- ***El flenn et tolm um et X; doss flenn et mesk ul o lern*** is S05's (III.1) headnote formula in the same words; it was found independently. It is built on IV.4's *doss o ul lern Yebb et Pard* ("it faces the Warden's Door") and IV.4's *ul o rask*. *um* is "upon, about" (what a thing is told upon). Additions S08-05 to S08-07.

### Kael's telling

**I · Hyll Pynt**

**I · The Hasty Tide**

> Nydhym. El nydherd en. Doss et nydhyl hosast lo sy.

I count. That is what I am for. Here is the first count.

Word for word: *I count. A counter am I. The first count is here.*

- ***El nydherd en*** is I.1's *El nydherd yalatath en* ("a counter of things am I"), cut short. Orrowen has no "what I am for", so Kael says what he is, and his use is in the word: *nydherd*, "one who counts".
- ***nydhyl*** is "a count, a reckoning", and also "a sounding (a reckoning of the water)" (O1837). The third count uses that sense.

> Thell wemm, eth re heth nahos reskow lo sest ullen. Ew ull et ryst hosast, eth re yal gor ryst et hyll ull hosen o. Re vellor gor sceth hosel, eth re runn o et hald hosast sa re femm o; eth amm re ston et hald, re runn o et hald sost pawast, eth pawast, dem felmyl. Re vollant et hesperdeth hemm so hy et Ommol: gorrscetheth, vorrscetheth, lymmscetheth, lodhscetheth, gunnsultereth. El vollath navenn so, eth garlar so tul. Re yal nahos um et pressireth. Re orrant et uldath veskrivull hy et lymmscetheth hosen syndalol vidhet, eth re sennent um et dhysal; el meskeleth so lo et hald, hosen amm es lyss voll so. Gor fedh, lo sorenn, re yal vorr et scethan ul lyssyl; eth re rystullar myskyl: *Doss vorr et scethan vyssorat*; eth re hossar. Re dhyvullar. Haeril ol, Vardh: re dhyvullar.

Eight sails, and not one keeping station with another. That was the first morning, and every morning of that Tide was like it. Each ship came alone and struck the first wall it touched, and when the wall held it struck the same wall again, and again, until it sank. The old soldiers had names for them from the Fall: warships, fire-ships, transports, mortar-ships, torpedo-boats. They are wrong names, and we use them still. No one stood on the decks. The root-men came off the transports like spilled kindling and died on the shingle; the wall calls them treelings, as if a name could make them small. By noon their fire always slackened, and we learned to say *the fleet has spent its fire*, and breathe. We laughed. God forgive us, we laughed.

Word for word: *Eight sails, and none held a station at the shoulder of another. That was the first morning, and every morning of that Tide was like it. Every hull came in alone, and it hammered the first wall that it touched; and when the wall stood, it hammered the wall itself a second time, and a second time, until sinking. The old soldiers named them from the Fall: fight-ships, fire-ships, bring-ships, mortar-ships, deep-boats. False names are they, and we handle them still. No one was upon the decks. The men of the root went from the bring-ships like poured kindling, and went out upon the shingle; treelings are they to the wall, as if a name would make them small. Every time, at noon, the fire of the fleet was in lessening; and we learned saying: 'The fire of the fleet is spent'; and we breathed. We laughed. Forgive us, God: we laughed.*

- ***Thell wemm***: the numeral before a singular (§3.1). The count opens verbless, as Kael counts.
- ***re runn o***, "it hammered": this is *drunn*, the mallet's heavy blow, softened after *re* (*dr-* > *r-*, as in I.1's *Garvel Runnowath*). A hull strikes a wall as a maul strikes stone.
- ***pawast, eth pawast***, "a second time, and a second time", is the tongue's "again, and again".
- ***dem felmyl***, "until sinking". *dem* before *amm* would bed it into *mamm*, a reserve word (I.1 §3), so a clause after "until" is said with the verbal noun.
- **The five wrong names** are the lexicon's compounds (O0778, O2787, O1316, O1231, O0818), with the slender plural *-eth*. They are wrong in Orrowen too: nobody fights on a "fight-ship", and a "bring-ship" brings nothing but root-men.
- ***et Ommol*, "the Fall"**, is *omm* "fall" + *-Ol*, the event named as *Vess Vollol* is. This is addition S08-04, and it is S05's word for "Of Their Fall" too (*So Ommol*).
- ***uldath veskrivull*, "the men of the root"** (*meskrivull* "root" softened as possessor, as in *Molt Veskrivull*), against ***meskeleth*, "treelings"**. The lexicon gave *meskel* for both. This paragraph needs them apart, because the wall renames the one as the other (addition S08-03). *-el* is the diminutive, so the wall's name really does make them small, which is Kael's point.
- ***el meskeleth so lo et hald***, "treelings are they to the wall". This is the copula with the predicate first. *voll et hald so meskeleth* ("the wall names them…") is heard as the construct "the name of the wall", which puts the article on a construct's head, the Last Carver's error; the analyzer reads it so.
- ***re sennent um et dhysal***: *senn* is to die as a fire goes out. Kindling spilled on the shingle goes out.
- ***hosen amm es lyss voll so***, "as if a name would make them small": *lyss* "small" is also the verb "reduce".
- ***re yal vorr et scethan ul lyssyl***, "the fleet's fire was in lessening": the progressive (§3.4) of *lyss* "small; reduce" (*lyssyl* O1337), which is what "slackened" is. *(Back-translation fix.)* The first draft had *re yal vorr et scethan luskat*, "the fleet's fire was loosed", meaning "slackened" (*lusk* lists "slacken" last). A blind reader heard "the fleet loosed its fire at noon", that is, "the fleet fired", because in this leaf *lusk* is always "loose, send" (*re luskant broclinth*, *Es luskar o dem varn*, the saying *sa luskith lont*), and "fire loosed" is a volley in any tongue. The active *re lusk vorr et scethan* was already out ("fire loosed the fleet").
- ***Doss vorr et scethan vyssorat*** is the saying as the wall says it: "the fire of the fleet is spent" (addition S08-12). "And breathe" is its own clause, *eth re hossar*, "and we breathed".
- ***Haeril ol, Vardh***, "Forgive us, God". The prayer is verb-first. God called by name softens (§3.3, the vocative): *Mardh* > *Vardh*, the form of *lest Vardh*.

> Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

(The hearth formula as the lexicon gives it: *I lay it as it was laid at me.*)

> *Eth re vysk et odh:* **Tumar.**

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

*Tumar* is canon and unchanged: "we remember", and Orrowen's yes. The frame is the lexicon's formula. The two lines close each of the six counts, exactly so.

**II · Hyll Pawast**

**II · The Second Tide**

> Et nydhyl pawast, eth duss hy et hosast.

The second count, and a harder one.

Word for word: *The second count, and hard from the first* (*hy* "from" is "than", §3.6: harder than the first).

> Re vellorant pa eth pa amm sy. Et hos ul lern, re rik o et pedh; eth et hos ul sestow, re rik o et trun. Eth lo wress sullast et hyll ull, re harm begorn um et taldow rerddhalden lom dem susk, dem veskyl galat sa nath nadhumom. Re rik sceth pedh lo et pell bridlernen; eth bost lusul hyo, dem et bithlern, re stacorn wemm sceth ullen, myst eth lurrel ul stacornol dem sulv hosen lern uld, eth re hyl o. Nath re vesk o. Re heth o. Ew ull et wemm hosast sa re dhess.

They came in pairs now. The front one took the shot and the back one took the ground. And in the third month of that Tide a lookout on the east tower called me up to see a thing I will not forget. A hull was struck off the north point, and a mile south of it another hull's sail went pale, grey-green blanching to white like a man's face, and it turned. It had not seen. It had felt. That was the first answering sail.

Word for word: *They came in two and two now. The one in front, it caught the shot; and the one behind, it caught the ground. And at the third month of that Tide a watcher on the eastern tower called to me, up, to see a thing that I do not forget. A hull caught a shot at the northern point; and four hundred paces from it, toward the south, the sail of another hull blanched, grey and green in blanching to snow, like the face of a man; and it turned. It did not see. It held. That was the first sail that answered.*

- ***pa eth pa***, "two and two", is built as I.1's *hos eth hos* (addition S08-08).
- ***Et hos ul lern, re rik o et pedh***: the topic comes first and *o* takes it up (G6). Said verb-first, *re rik et hos ul lern et pedh* would be heard "the one in the face of the shot caught…", because *ul lern* is a preposition that takes a possessor.
- ***et trun***: on the wall, *trun* is "the water a battery watches" (§11.5). The back hull takes the battery's own ground.
- ***lo wress sullast***, "at the third month":
  - *ul* would nasalise *bress* to *mress*, an onset the tongue forbids (§2.5).
  - An article (*ul et bress …*) would put *et* on a construct's head.
  - So the time is "at": *lo* softens *bress* to *wress*.
- ***lom dem susk, dem veskyl***, "to me, up, to seeing": purpose is *dem* with the verbal noun. *lo veskyl* would be heard as the softened *meskyl*, and the analyzer reads it so.
- ***galat sa nath nadhumom***, "a thing that I do not forget". The negative present is also the habitual, as I.1's *nath gadhom o los*.
- **The place-words are the lexicon's**: *bost lusul* "a mile" (four hundred paces, O0117), *bridlernen*, *rerddhalden* "northern, eastern", and *bithlern* "the south".
- ***lo et pell bridlernen***: *pell* after any softening or nasalising word becomes *vell* ("song") or *bell* ("gold"), so the point takes the article and an adjective.
- ***re stacorn wemm sceth ullen***: *stacorn* "go pale" has no object, so *sceth ullen* can only be the possessor, "another hull's sail".
- ***Re heth o*, "It held"**: *keth* is the canon's verb for knowing as the wood knows (§3.7: "to know the wood is to hold it"). The Shore has no other word for knowing by touch, and Kael's "It had felt" uses it.
- ***sa re dhess***, "that answered": S·*tess*, the formula's own *re dhess* (*Eth re dhess nahos*).

> Ul hyll ull re vellorant et biskscetheth, eth re lunnant so wisketh, eth re vellor et fymmet dhenthen um et hald lo et helvhoss. Mysk et Stonwrytan: naelur et helvhoss et kethow, eth orr et fymmet ul vodh dossant et brammath lor velv.

That Tide the crown-hulls came, and opened their crowns, and the winged seed came over the wall on the wind. The Stonewrights say the wind chooses the keep, and the seed goes where our guns are quiet.

Word for word: *In that Tide the crown-hulls came in, and they opened their heads, and the winged seed came in over the wall by the wind. The Stonewrights say: the wind chooses the keep, and the seed goes where the guns at us are quiet.*

- ***so wisketh***, "their crowns": *bisk*, the head of a hull, softened after *so*, so the possessive shows (§3.5). *lunn* "an open space; freedom" is also "open" (lexicon §4.4).
- ***Mysk et Stonwrytan:***: what is said is given as it was said, because there is no "that" before a clause (I.1). The guild is a collective and takes the 3sg (G2).
- ***et brammath lor***, "the guns at us". "Our guns" with *ol* would nasalise *brammath* to *mr-*, which the tongue forbids, so the "at" idiom says whose they are. *dossant* agrees with its plural subject (G2).

> Re lymm gor scethan movennerd hy et hosast, veth amm sy re dhovv et ullen umo. Amm re felmer o, re orr et movenn dem mullen, hos pawast sa re ston ul reskow et hosast; eth hy ull re rik et scethan molt et hos ull. Hy ull mysker lo et vorr: *Naelur et molt sa luskith lont.*

Each fleet had carried a Standard-Bearer from the first, but now the rest waited on it. Sink it and the standard passed to another, a lieutenant standing in, and the fleet took on that one's temper after. So we say at the fire: *Choose which heart you leave them.*

Word for word: *Every fleet carried a Standard-Bearer from the first, but now the rest waited upon it. When we sank it, the standard went to another, a second who stood in the place of the first; and after that the fleet took the heart of that one. So we say at the fire: 'Choose the heart that you leave them.'*

- ***Amm re felmer o***, "when we sank it", for "Sink it and…". The English imperative is a condition, and Orrowen says a condition with *amm* ("when; if").
- ***dem mullen***: *ullen* nasalised after *dem* (a broad vowel takes *m-*, §3.3).
- ***pawast*** is "second" and "a lieutenant", the one who is second. The phrase is "a second who stood in the place of the first". *ul reskow* alone is the lexicon's "somewhere", so the place is named.
- ***eth hy ull re rik et scethan molt et hos ull***, "and after that the fleet took the heart of that one": to take on a temper is to take a heart. *grik* "catch, take" is softened after *re* (*g* before *r* drops, §2.5), the verb of *re rik o et pedh* above. *molt* is "heart" and "temper" at once, so the saying's *heart* is the fleet's temper. *hy ull* stands first: after *grik* it would be heard "took … from that". *(Back-translation fix.)* The first draft had *re hovv et scethan molt et hos ull hy ull*, *covv* "wear" for "take on". A blind reader took *covv* in its first sense, "cloak", and heard "the fleet cloaked the heart of that one": the fleet hid its Standard-Bearer, which turns the saying into a guessing game.
- **The saying** (addition S08-13) is in the singular imperative: it is said at the fire to one gunner, as the Captain's calls are.

> Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

> *Eth re vysk et odh:* **Tumar.**

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

**III · Hyll Dhumol**

**III · The Tide of Remembering**

> Et nydhyl sullast.

The third count.

> Lo wress pawast et hyll sy, hos vess, re varr et Crenn et bramm bithlernen, sa re hurr mest sost, eth re hadh o o pa vurr lusul lo dhrenn et hald. Lo ryst re vellorant. Nel dem et hald. Dem et reskow ul vodh re yal et bramm. Hos sceth hosast, hosel eth niss, ul nydhyl nynth; eth et ullen ul neld, dem veskyl galat sa re wesk o. Re vellorant dem veskyl: ho ross et bramm lo ull tul?

In the second month of this Tide, one night, the Captain had the south gun moved, the one that had done the most killing, and set it forty yards along the wall. In the morning they came. Not to the wall. To the place where the gun had been. One hull first, alone and slow, sounding the water, and the rest standing off to see what it found. They came to see whether the gun was still there.

Word for word: *At the second month of this Tide, one night, the Captain shifted the southern gun, that had killed very much, and he laid it two twenties of paces along the wall. At morning they came. Not to the wall. To the place where the gun was. One hull first, alone and slow, in counting water; and the rest in the far, to see the thing that it found. They came to see: is the gun there still?*

- ***re varr***: *marr* is the Captain's first call (*Marr tho dhrun*, "shift your ground"), said here of the gun itself. "Had … moved" needs no causative, because the Captain shifts his own guns. *bithlernen*, "southern", is addition S08-01.
- ***pa vurr lusul***, "two twenties of paces": the Shore counts in twenties, and a yard is a pace (*lusul*).
- ***re hadh o o***, "he laid it": the subject and object pronouns side by side, as in IV.4's *re heth o o*.
- ***Nel dem et hald***: the copula's "not" with no verb. Kael's two fragments stay fragments.
- ***ul nydhyl nynth***, "in counting water": to sound the water is to count it (*nydhyl*, "a count; a sounding"). The hull counts, as Kael does.
- ***ho ross …?***: "whether" is the yes-or-no question itself, given as the hulls' own. *ho* softens *doss* to *ross* (*d* > *r*, §3.3), as *ho dhumos?* softens *tum* (addition S08-15).

> Re haeverer: el kynteth so, sa rystullant lo hulter. Nel so. Veskent hosast, eth hy ull oskant. Rystullith veskyl ferryl et scetheth: sceth sa ferr um Orrow, el venn o; eth sceth sa ferr dem neld, doss o ul gaeveryl um varn. Hy ull re varrar et brammath lo vess, eth re vellorant lo ryst dem veskyl; eth ew gorrol velv hy ull, eth re vallom o lyss hy et gorrol bont.

We had thought them beasts that learn by the stick. They are not. They look before they trust. You learn to read the lean of them: a hull that leans to the shore means it, and a hull that leans away is thinking of home. After that we moved our guns by night, and they came looking in the morning, and it was a quieter war, and I liked it less.

Word for word: *We thought: beasts are they, that learn by the staff. They are not. They look first, and then they trust. You learn to read the leaning of the hulls: a hull that leans upon the Shore, it is in earnest; and a hull that leans away, it is in thinking upon home. After that we shifted the guns by night, and they came at morning to look; and it was a war quiet after that, and I loved it less than the loud war.*

- ***el kynteth so … Nel so.***: the copula said, then said back with its "not". This is Orrowen's no (§3.4).
- ***Veskent hosast, eth hy ull oskant***, "they look first, and then they trust". *osk* is to rest one's weight on a thing.
- ***el venn o***, "it is true", for "means it" (addition S08-11). *venn* is the plumb wall. *kaever* "mean, intend" is the same word as the next clause's "thinking", and Kael sets the two hulls against each other.
- ***um Orrow***, "upon the Shore": *dem* ("to") would nasalise *Orrow* to *Morrow* (§3.3).
- ***doss o ul gaeveryl um varn***: the progressive (§3.4), *kaever* nasalised after *ul*.
- **The comparison.** "A quieter war, and I liked it less" gives nothing to compare with, and Orrowen compares with *hy* ("from, than"), which needs its other term.
  - *velv hy ull* is heard both "quiet from then on" and "quieter than that". Either reading carries the sense.
  - ***re vallom o lyss hy et gorrol bont***, "I loved it less than the loud war": the term is the war of the first Tides, which came *bont*, "loud" (V's *bont eth hosel eth venn*). *(Back-translation fix.)* The first draft ended *re vallom o lyss*, meant as "I loved it little". A blind reader heard "I loved it a little", the opposite of "I liked it less": a bare *lyss* after a verb cannot tell "little" from "a little", and *lyss hy ull* would be heard "little, after that".
  - See note 2, S-G2 **[Jack]**.

> Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

> *Eth re vysk et odh:* **Tumar.**

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

**IV · Hyll Lodhat**

**IV · The Joined Tide**

> Et nydhyl gemmest.

The fourth count.

> Re yalant et drunnatath ul drunnol et vess tunn, hy sceth dem sceth ul myst. Lo ryst re vellorant um sull orrol olen, bridlern eth rerddhald eth brodow et rhull, hosen amm re rig hos garl so. Sull orrol, hos clem.

The drums went all the night before, knocking from hull to hull out in the grey. In the morning they came on three roads at once, north and east and the river mouth, as if one hand had flung them. Three roads, one hour.

Word for word: *The drums were in knocking the whole night, from hull to hull in the grey. At morning they came upon three roads together, the north and the east and the mouth of the river, as if one hand had flung them. Three roads, one hour.*

- ***et drunnatath ul drunnol***: the drums (*drunnat*, "a struck thing") are "in knocking". The progressive keeps *drunn* whole (a nasalised *d* before *r* stays *d*, §2.5). After *re* it would be *runn*. To a Shoreland ear the Mystaeri knock (§2.3), and so do their drums.
- **"The night before" is *et vess tunn*, "the whole night"**. The next sentence's morning says which night it was.
- ***re rig***: *drig*, "fling", softened (*dr-* > *r-*).
- ***Sull orrol, hos clem***: verbless, as Kael counts. *orrol* is "a going; a road".

> Hy ull re veskym et galat dyss sost ul et gorrol. Lo harm et Crenn, re felm bramman Lanner so vovennerd, hos clem ul sestow et dhess. Nath re omm et ullen. Ul et clem sa re ryt et molt sennet lont, re hylent, eth re vellorant um et orrol rerddhalden, ul nynth pondul; re yal nayalat lo ull dem drunnol. Re vellorant desirel, eth nel olen: re stacorn hos wemm, eth hy ull et fennet, eth hy ull et fennet, hos hoss ul lodh, hosen amm re vaver gor sceth weskyl et clem hosel. Veth re hethent o. Re hethent clem vrener sennet.

Then I saw the strangest thing of the war. At the Captain's call, Lanner's battery sank their Standard-Bearer an hour after dawn. The rest did not falter. At the hour the dead heart had carved for them, they turned and came in on the east road, into empty water; there was nothing there to strike. They came a little late, and not together: one sail went pale, and then the next, and then the next, a breath apart, as if each hull had to find the hour for itself. But they kept it. They kept a dead mind's hour.

Word for word: *Then I saw the strangest thing in the war. At the call of the Captain, the battery of Lanner sank their Standard-Bearer, one hour at the back of the dawn. The rest did not fall. In the hour that the dead heart cut for them, they turned, and came in upon the eastern road, into empty water; nothing was there for striking. They came in latish, and not together: one sail blanched, and then the next, and then the next, a breath in the mortar between, as if every hull wanted the finding of the hour alone. But they held it. They held the hour of a dead mind.*

- ***et galat dyss sost***, "the strangest thing". *sost* marks the most (lexicon §5.3), and "in the war" avoids a construct.
- ***Lo harm et Crenn***: *carm* "a call", softened after *lo*.
- ***so vovennerd***, "their Standard-Bearer": *movennerd* softened after *so*, so the mutation marks the possessive. *felm* ("drown; sink") does not soften after *re* (*f*).
- ***hos clem ul sestow et dhess***: *ul sestow* is "behind, after".
- ***Ul et clem***, "in the hour": *lo* would soften *clem* to *hlem*, a cluster the grammar has no repair for (lexicon §8 item 3). The article keeps it whole.
- ***re ryt***: the dead heart *cut* the hour. This is the verb of oaths and inscriptions, and the fleet's morrow in VI is cut the same way (*Rytent ryst*).
- ***desirel***, "latish", is *desir* + the "-ish" *-el* of *dommel* and *sulvel* (addition S08-02).
- ***eth nel olen***, "and not together": the copula's "not".
- ***hos hoss ul lodh***, "a breath in the mortar between" (addition S08-09). A mason measures a gap by what lies in it.
- ***re vaver gor sceth weskyl et clem hosel***, "every hull wanted the finding of the hour, alone". "Had to" is *maver*, "need, want", as in I.1's *Nath re vaver o neskyl*. The "upon" idiom (*re yal maver um gor sceth*) would set *um* before *gor*.
- ***Re hethent***: *keth*, hold, keep. They keep the hour as they know things, by holding.
- ***clem vrener sennet***: *prener* "mind" softened as the possessor.

> Eth nath re vellorant merr ul drenn hos fedh: et scetheth sa re felmer so vovennerd sost ul so vimm. Re yalant dend, et scetheth, hosen dhakereth, et rytet mammat eth nayalat lont veth et lomm, dem mymmrest.

And some did not come on in order at all: the hulls whose own bearer we had sunk beneath them. They went wild, the orphaned hulls, the carving lost and only the grief left, out for revenge.

Word for word: *And some did not come in, in course, one time: the hulls that we sank their very Standard-Bearer beneath them. They were wild, the hulls, like orphans, the carving lost and nothing at them but the grief, toward revenge.*

- ***ul drenn … hos fedh***, "in course … once". The order of a line is a course (addition S08-10, from I.1's *ul drenn vodh*), and "at all" is "not once".
- ***sa re felmer so vovennerd sost ul so vimm***: a relative with its pronoun left behind (G5). *ul so vimm*, "in their low", is "beneath them", as *ul vimm* is "under".
- ***hosen dhakereth***, "like orphans": *dhaker* ("one bereft: an orphan, a widow") is a noun, and the lexicon has no "orphaned".
- ***nayalat lont veth et lomm***, "nothing at them but the grief": I.1's *lo nayalat veth osk* shape for "only".
- ***dem mymmrest***, "toward revenge": *mymmrest* is "return-harm".

> Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

> *Eth re vysk et odh:* **Tumar.**

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

**V · Hyll sa re rystull nycul**

**V · The Tide that Learned Deceit**

> Et nydhyl leskest, eth nath vallom o.

The fifth count, and I do not like it.

Word for word: *The fifth count, and I do not love it.* (*vall* is "love; like".)

> Re vellorant lor hosen re vellor et mesk tevel, bont eth hosel eth venn; eth re dhyvullar hosen re dhyvullar ul et grem hosast, eth re wrodh gor wramm; eth re vellorant et ganurardath hy et myst, dem et brammath sa re wrodhant, gor hos. Re luskant broclinth um orrol clenn gor orl, scetheth sa new scetheth, eth re rellorant et orrolath sa re dhesser lont; eth um orrol ul vodh nath re yern hos bramm lor, re luskant et uldath veskrivull.

They came at us as the young wood came, loud and alone and straight, and we laughed as we had laughed the first winter, and every gun spoke; and out of the grey the hunters came for every gun that had spoken. They sent straw down a new road every day, hulls that were not hulls, and marked which roads we answered; and down a road where no gun of ours had woken, they sent the root-men.

Word for word: *They came at us as the young wood came, loud and alone and straight; and we laughed as we laughed in the first winter, and every gun spoke; and the hunters came out of the grey, to the guns that spoke, every one. They loosed straw upon a new road every day, hulls that were not hulls, and they marked the roads that we answered at; and upon a road where not one gun at us woke, they loosed the men of the root.*

- ***hosel*** after a verb reads "alone" (G8), which is what the young wood was. *mesk tevel* is the wall's name for the Hasty Tide's hulls (lexicon).
- ***ul et grem hosast***, "in the first winter": *lo grem* would soften to *yrem* and then lose the *y* before *r* (§2.5). The article keeps the winter whole.
- ***re wrodh gor wramm***, "every gun spoke":
  - *bramm* softens after *gor* (§3.3).
  - *re wrodh* is *brodh* "speak", softened as in *nawrodhat*.
- ***dem et brammath …, gor hos***, "to the guns …, every one". *dem gor* would set a nasal before *gor*, which the grammar does not settle (I.1 §3). *gor hos* is the lexicon's "each one".
- ***scetheth sa new scetheth***, "hulls that were not hulls": three words, the copula's past "not" inside a relative.
- ***re rellorant***: *rellor*, "a sign, a mark", is also the verb.
- ***sa re dhesser lont***, "that we answered at": a relative with its preposition left behind (G5).
- ***nath re yern***: *gern* "wake", softened (G1, the negative past). ***bramm lor***, "a gun at us", means ours (see II).

> Veth vesk virr. Doss trovul ul so nycul gor fedh. Nath luskant et dhenneth kaelir, eth nath ferrent amm omm pedh lo so sest. Doss hollarol venn driget, eth keth hollarol navenn o dhrenneth. Haemer sceth havulat vimm. Eth nath re stacornant wemmeth et mesk tevel hos fedh; gendsceth sa stacorn wemm umo, nel gendsceth o. Gor nycul sa re nyculant lor ul hyll sy: re yal seskyl lor, amm re vesker. Nath re veskent merr hyor, eth dossant ul vimm et stelleth rerddhalden. Ew veskyl, ul hyll ull, et galat berd.

But look close. Their lie always shows its seam. The phantoms leave no wreck, and they do not flinch when a shell falls by them. A true rout scatters, and a feigned one keeps its spacing. A laden ship rides low. And the young wood's sails never once went pale; a green hull whose sail blanches is no green hull. Every lie they told us this Tide, we could have known, if we looked. Some of us did not look, and they are under the east cairns. The brave thing, that Tide, was to look.

Word for word: *But look near. There is a seam in their lie every time. The phantoms do not leave a wreck, and they do not flinch when a shot falls at their shoulder. A true rout is scattered, and a false rout keeps its courses. A laden ship floats low. And the sails of the young wood did not blanch one time; a green hull that a sail blanches upon it, it is not a green hull. Every lie that they lied at us in this Tide: knowing was at us, if we looked. Some of us did not look, and they are under the eastern cairns. Looking was, in that Tide, the brave thing.*

- ***Veth vesk virr***: the imperative to one hearer, the hearth's "you".
- ***Doss trovul ul so nycul***, "there is a seam in their lie". The tongue's *lestir* "show" means "show the way", so the seam is said to be there, as a mason says a false wall has its joints.
- ***Doss hollarol venn driget***, "a true rout is scattered": *drig* ("throw, fling, scatter") takes an object, and the participle makes the rout the thing scattered.
- ***keth hollarol navenn o dhrenneth***, "a false rout keeps its courses" (addition S08-14): it keeps its spacing as a wall keeps its courses.
  - *o* softens *trenneth* to *dhrenneth*, so the possessive shows (§3.5).
  - The lexicon's *lodh* "spacing" would not show it.
- ***Haemer sceth havulat vimm***: *haemer* is "float, ride (on the water)", and the adverb is the bare quality after the subject.
- ***gendsceth sa stacorn wemm umo***, "a green hull that a sail blanches upon it": a relative with its preposition left behind (G5). *gendsceth* is the wall's "green hull" (O0725). *nel gendsceth o* uses the copula's "not".
- ***re yal seskyl lor***, "knowing was at us": "we could have known", the "at" idiom in the past (§3.7). *sesk* is knowing a fact. The fronted lie is the thing known.
- ***merr hyor***, "some of us", as I.1's *nahos hyor*. *rerddhalden* is "eastern".
- ***Ew veskyl … et galat berd***: the copula puts the predicate first: "Looking was … the brave thing".

> Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

> *Eth re vysk et odh:* **Tumar.**

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

**VI · Hyll Dasken**

**VI · The Last Tide**

> Et nydhyl dasken.

The last count.

> Rytent ryst amm sy, hosen ryter. Re vellor gor scethan eth niltenn ulo, eth re yal kylyleth lo et niltenn: amm doss sy, doss ull. Eth lo rask et niltenn re yal kylyl dem varn. Amm re omm et movennerd, eth et pawast hy ull, re orrant dem varn. New driget. Re orrant.

They carve a morrow now, as we do. Each fleet came with a plan in it, and the plan had turnings, if this then that. And at the end of the plan there was a turning for home. When the bearer fell, and the second after it, they went home. Not scattered. Went.

Word for word: *They cut a morrow now, as we cut. Every fleet came in, and a plan in it; and there were turnings at the plan: if this is, that is. And at the end of the plan there was a turning to home. When the Standard-Bearer fell, and the second after that, they went home. Not scattered. They went.*

- ***Rytent ryst***: *ryt* is to cut letters, carve, swear, and *ryst* is "morning" and "the morrow". The morrow is cut as a vow is cut. *hosen ryter*, "as we cut", repeats the verb, as Orrowen answers.
- ***eth niltenn ulo***: a clause with no verb laid beside the other with *eth*, as I.1's *eth so wresketh ul so simmeth*.
- ***re yal kylyleth lo et niltenn***: "had" is the "at" idiom; the existential stays 3sg (G2).
- ***amm doss sy, doss ull***, "if this is, that is": *amm* is "when" and "if".
- ***New driget. Re orrant.***: the copula's past "not" against a verb, as in I.1's *New hosen ull. Ew hosen ull.* Kael's "Went" gets its subject from the ending.

> Hy ull amm sy hyrrilym galat sa re wrodha Halyna lom ul Hyll Lodhat: *Nath noss carmol et scethan dem varn, hy vodh lynir et mesk et mesk.* Re dhessym lonta amm ull: *Es luskar o dem varn.* Eth nath re seskym galat sa re haeverym. Re sennent et carmath sa re yal o harmol dem varn lont. Nath noss o harmol dem varn. Veth doss o luskol dem varn. Keth et mesk ludal.

So now I understand what Halyna told me in the Joined Tide: that the fleet cannot be recalled, for the wood obeys the wood. I answered them then that we would send it home, and I did not know what I meant. The voices that could call it home are dead. It cannot be called home. But it can be sent home. The wood knows fear.

Word for word: *So now I understand the thing that Halyna told me in the Joined Tide: 'There is no calling of the fleet home, for the wood obeys the wood.' I answered the two of them then: 'We will send it home.' And I did not know the thing that I meant. The voices have died that the calling of it home was at them. There is no calling it home. But there is sending it home. The wood holds fear.*

- ***re wrodha Halyna***: Halyna are two and take the dual (3du *-a*, §3.9), as in IV.4's headnote. ***lonta***, "at the two of them", is the 3du preposition (§3.6). Kael's English "them" is the pair.
- **What Halyna said and what Kael answered are quoted**, because there is no "that" before a clause.
- ***lynir et mesk et mesk***: the subject and the object are the same word. The article on both shows it is not a construct, since a construct's head takes no article (§3.2).
- ***Nath noss o harmol …. Veth doss o luskol …***, "cannot be called … can be sent". This is the "at" idiom with no one at whom (§3.7): "there is no calling it home; there is sending it home".
  - The pronoun object of a verbal noun is its possessor, set before it (IV.4's *o wrodhol*).
  - *o harmol* shows its softening. *o luskol* cannot (*l*), and is read by its twin.
- ***Re sennent et carmath sa re yal o harmol dem varn lont***, "the voices have died that could call it home": a relative with *lont* left behind (G5). *carmath* "voices" and *carmol* "calling" are one word.
- ***Keth et mesk ludal***, "the wood holds fear": *keth* is how the wood knows (§3.7).

> El ull nydhyl et hylleth. Re nydhym et wemmeth myst gor orl et gorrol, et scethan dhenn eth nahos um o vressireth; eth seskym et nydhet, eth nath gadhom o lo et odh sy.

That is the count of the Tides. I have counted the grey sails for the whole of the war, the ghost fleet with no one on its decks, and I know their number, and I will not say it at this hearth.

Word for word: *That is the count of the Tides. I counted the grey sails every day of the war, the ghost fleet, and no one upon its decks; and I know the number, and I do not lay it at this hearth.*

- ***gor orl et gorrol***, "every day of the war", is I.1's *gor orl et hollarol sell*. *et wemmeth myst* and *et scethan dhenn* are the wall's own names (lexicon).
- ***o vressireth***, "its decks": *pressir* softened after *o*.
- ***et nydhet***, "the number". "Their number", *so nydhet*, would not show the possessive before *n* (§3.5).
- ***nath gadhom o***, "I do not lay it": I.1's *nath gadhom o los*. Kael's rule and Kael's refusal are the same words. *mysk* "say" would be heard as the nasalised *bysk* after *nath* (I.1 §3).

> Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

> *Eth re vysk et odh:* **Tumar.**

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

### 5. The hands

- **Ink (this leaf).** Everything above is what the leaf-hand writes: the mortar words, the endings, and every mutation as a bite under its base letter (*re runn* is the letter *d* with the softening bite; *so vovennerd* is *m* with it; *dem mullen* is the vowel with the nasal bite). The token strings are not given here. As the I.1 pilot notes, `to_ink.py` does not yet read the tier-3 lexicon. The analyzer's parses in `wf8/tmp/S08/report.txt` (base letter, mutation, suffix) hold everything a token string needs.
- **Chisel.** Nothing in V.5 is cut in stone. The six Tide names would be cut dry from their lexicon rows if the wall ever cut them, and the sayings (S08-12, S08-13) carry a dry cut in their rows.
