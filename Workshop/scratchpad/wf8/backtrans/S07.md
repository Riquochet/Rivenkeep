## 1 · Method, and how far the test was blind

1. **The blind file.** `wf8/blind/S07.txt` holds only the unit's native lines, with their unit line numbers. There are 43 `>` blockquote lines of romanised Orrowen and the 9 fenced GN v2 blocks. To choose them, a script printed the unit's structure (line type and length, never the text). The English lines, the word-for-word lines, the notes and the headings were never shown.
2. **Contamination, and how it was handled.** The unit had English inside two blockquotes, which the extraction then showed:
   - line 210, the facing leaf's English caption (*The facing leaf holds the sign …*), a translation of the Orrowen line 208;
   - the tags *(the one ink)* and *(the other ink)* after lines 178 and 180.

   All three were stripped from `blind/S07.txt` before the reading. The Orrowen of line 208 had been seen with its English, so it is scored below but marked **not blind**. The tags carry nothing that had to be read back. This is also what fixes L1 and L2 correct.
3. **The reading.** The blind text was read with only these sources:
   - `orrowen_v2.md` §0 and §3 (the grammar and the stone grammar), §5.10 to §5.12 (small words, numbers, affixes);
   - the lexicon: the shared `wf7/lexicon_orrowen.tsv` plus `wf8/additions/S07.tsv`, merged privately into `wf8/tmp/S07bt/lexicon_orrowen.tsv` (2,882 rows), and `lexicon_orrowen.md` §4 and §5;
   - a private copy of `orr_analyze.py` (check and `--gloss`) and a row look-up (`wf8/tmp/S07bt/lk.py`);
   - for the grain chips, `grain_v2.md` §17 and §21 (A10: kinds of round; `*`: the root), `grain_coverage.md`'s table of the 47 carvings, and `grain_validate.py` in its plain and `--literal` modes.

   The translator's workspace `wf8/tmp/S07/` (its `gloss.txt`, `draft.tsv` and template hold the English) was not opened until the comparison.
4. **Frozen before the look.** The draft was saved as `wf8/tmp/S07bt/blind_draft_S07.md` at 02:58:10, sha1 `5dd8b66a6edd02d61ec9e6f4142668d5c0f3586a`. Only after that was `units/S07.md` read in full and compared. The draft is summarised in §2.
5. **Limitation.** Once the English had been seen, no second blind reading was possible. The post-fix readings in §6 are the analyzer's glosses of the fixed clauses, read by rule. A fresh blind reader should re-read the four fixed places (§7).

## 4 · What was not fixed, and why

These are the "close" paragraphs. None is an error: in each, the reader took another of a word's listed senses, or the difference is a recast the unit already documents. Three are worth a line for the merge:

- **#11, *eth re heth merr*** ("and [they] knew a little"). The subject is left out, so VSO order reads *merr* as the subject: "and a few held". The difference is one of quantity (some of the pairs knew, against all of them knowing a little), and the clause that follows says the same either way. The sentence's 3sg agreement (*re raevul et Lodhan ullen …*, *… lo*) is right by pilot I.1's G2, a collective with the 3sg. If a later pass wants the object unmistakable, *eth re heth o merr* ("and it held a few") does it. It was not changed here.
- **#13, *trenn*.** The reader took "tale", where the Book has "sentence". The row lists both, and the context (a hearth of tales) allows either. N1 corrects the unit's note, which had said the sense was missing.
- **#26, *et hosast ul vimm*** ("the first below"). Read in a vault, *ul vimm* sounds like a place. The Book means "first in the list below". This is one nuance, and the order itself was read exactly.

### 5.5 Spec: three clarifying lines, proposed (not applied: `orrowen_v2.md` and `lexicon_orrowen.md` are shared and in use)

1. **§3.2, after the construct table (F1):** *"When the possessor begins with a vowel or with a letter the softening leaves unchanged, a construct looks exactly like apposition (et brod tolm, "the word stone"). Where the sense could go either way, give a definite possessor its article (kaever et orrow, "the shore's thought"), or recast."* (This is the construct's twin of §3.5's line on possessive pronouns.)
2. **§3.4, under the copula (F2):** *"The copula's subject follows the predicate. If both are bare nouns they read as one construct (el hosel kael rellor, "it is only a sign's notch"). Give the subject its article, or front it and take it up with o, as in I.1's Ew hosel covv hesperd o."*
3. **§3.1, or lexicon_orrowen.md §5.2 (F3):** *"A bare quality after a noun is that noun's adjective. "Wholly", "at last" and the like cannot follow an object as bare words; say them as phrases (ul dunnoth "wholly", lo rask "at last", gor fedh "always")."*
