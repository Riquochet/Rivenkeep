### 2.2 The wood's telling (the grain)

Seal: *This is held in the grain.*

- **r1.** [root: HOME] This is our home. We were old, and then your forebears raised their first stone (we were old before your fathers built). We name ourselves the Naelear; we lived in the trees, and the trees in us, in the breath.
- **r2.** You, the shore-men, called it by three names: the grey land (the Mystlands); and again, the wall. We lived in the breath.
- **r3.** All our land lay (flat) beneath the trees as they breathed, to its farthest edges. The trees breathed out the grey as sleepers breathe. The breath lay among their trunks, and fed them, and fed us. We tended it as you tend a hearth.
- **r4.** The open sun went into the grey, and then a soft light was on our land: the between-light, neither day nor dusk. We grew for that light. Never did we have saplings in the dark. In the open sun we die slowly, as a leaf of the forest floor withers in the desert. At the last we watched our saplings die of the sun.
- **r5.** We guided the wood's growing; we pruned it and sang to it; we led its roots to the water, and it grew. The trees breathed out the grey, and it was our breath. We tended the wood and the wood held us, each only with the other: one life. And the wood knew its own mind.
- **r6.** The wood needed the breath, the breath needed the wood, and we needed both. Where the breath is not, the wood rots. Without the trees the breath does not come again. Without the trees and the breath we die slowly, first the young, then the elders.
- **r7.** We live twice as long as a shore-man, and half again. We have been old in our land through more of your lives than you have names.
- **r8.** The woods were many. The twisted boughs we bent in our rites. We sang to the pale saplings, and they spoke to us; at dusk they sang together. Under the silver elders the council sat, because there every tongue is a tongue of truth.
- **r9.** At the farthest edge of the grey, where it falls to your sea, the long ranks of black pillars rise and hold the breath to the earth, as a mast holds up a sail. You call them "mist-holders"; we call them "mist-hearts", the anchors of our sky. You found only black pillars.
- **r10.** Our tongue of truth is spoken with wood. We speak in the thunder-tongue, as the storm speaks in the high boughs, and every deep of the forest speaks it in its own voice. A carver carves in the living wood. Whoever comes to it does not hear the carving, but holds it. Whoever lays a hand on the carving then knows the carver's mind, through all his speech (whatever tongue he spoke), so that no word could be twisted and no promise misheard.
- **r11.**
  - We say [the Aelthar in thunder, like thunder in the boughs] (uncertain: perhaps "a broken Aelthar is as hollow speech").
  - We cut gently the whole of the Aelthar; the saying and the cutting are bound, each only with the other, into one word (remembered, always).
  - Once, [we/it] broke (uncertain).
  - All that is given in the wood is sacred, binding, and cannot be mistaken.
  - We did not know there was another road to belief.
- **r12.** From the edge we watched your shore. We watched you build your havens, and the small lamps in them at dusk. We watched your boats go a little way into the grey and turn back, your faces white; we did not know why.
- **r13.** We thought it our fault (we did not put down our shame). We did not go to your shore. We tended the breath more, and it grew thicker; we hid in it as deep as we could, and were glad. Your iron came to us where we hid, and you never knew at all that it wounded those of the grey, because we were hidden. From your first haven on, we listened to you. You never heard us, not once. We made sure of it.
- **r14.** Home, in the grey:
  - the flat wood;
  - the soft light;
  - the pale saplings singing at dusk;
  - the silver elders;
  - the pillars at the edge of the world, holding up the grey.

  A few of us are young again, perhaps (?); and so we hold our home: we remember it, always.

Seal: *…and the grain holds it still.*
