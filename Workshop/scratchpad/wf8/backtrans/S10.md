## 3 · Paragraph by paragraph: scores and causes

**Scale.**
- **exact**: the Book's sense comes back whole.
- **close**: the sense comes back, but a nuance is lost. This is either a recast the unit declares in its notes, or a reading the lexicon offers first.
- **drift**: the meaning shifts.
- **wrong**: a different meaning.

**Causes.**
- **declared**: the unit's notes say it builds the line that way.
- **polysemy**: the lexicon lists the right sense, but not first.
- **reader**: my slip; the spec, the lexicon or a house formula settles it.
- **translation**: the Orrowen allows a reading that nothing in the line rules out.
- **spec**: the specs disagree.

## 4 · The fixes

Each fix is in `wf8/units/S10.md`, with its word-for-word line and a note that names this report. None needs an addition: every word is in the shared lexicon, and each line parses clean on the shared lexicon alone as well as on the merge.

### 4.1 L301: "the hearth sang in the wood's way"

- **Before:** *Hy ull, ul et fedh hosast, re vell et odh **ul morrol et mesk**. …*
- **After:** *Hy ull, ul et fedh hosast, re vell et odh **hosen et mesk**. …*, "the hearth sang like the wood".
- **Why.** *ul* + a verbal noun is the progressive, and the leaf itself uses *ul morrol* for "going" thirty lines earlier. *hosen* "as, like" (O0991) says the manner and nothing else.
- **Read again:** "Then, for the first time, the hearth sang like the wood." **exact.**

### 4.2 L426: "That is what an unbonded life is for"

- **Before:** *El **dask** hossol nalodhat ull, re wrodha sona lom hos fedh, …*
- **After:** *El **kaeveret** hossol nalodhat ull, …*, "That is the meaning (what is meant) of an unbonded life".
- **Why.** *kaeveret* (O1097, from *kaever* "think; mean, intend") keeps the construct and the copula order of the first version, and it cannot be read "the end". It is also the word of the Carver's stone, "that would carry a meaning" (*sa es lymm kaeveret*, L471), so the Epilogue now ties Seren's purpose to the stone's. The pun on *dask* is lost; §6 item 5 lists it for Jack.
- **Read again:** "That is the meaning of an unbonded life, they told me once." **close**: "the meaning" is not quite "what it is for", but it is the purpose, not the finish.

### 4.3 L482: "No one came with it"

- **Before:** ***Nath re rarr hos lo**, eth nayess el podul ull.*
- **After:** ***Re rarr o hosel**, eth nayess el podul ull.*, "It came alone, and perhaps that is wise".
- **Why.** *hosel* after the verb is "alone" (§3.8). *o* before *h* shows no bite, so it is the subject, as G9 says. The second sentence, *Nath re nethiler arra tul*, stays: *nethil* lists "earn, pay for" and *arra* is bare. The unit's note now says so.
- **Read again:** "It came alone, and perhaps that is wise. We have not yet paid for a guest." **close** ("paid for a guest" for "earned a guest").

### 4.4 L576: "Set down for whoever has learned the grain"

- **Before:** *Nath re heth hos lo odh sy o. **Cadhat lo hos** sa re rystull et meskdhrenn.*
- **After:** *Nath re heth hos lo odh sy o. **Cadhat dem hos** sa re rystull et meskdhrenn.*, "Laid for (until) one who has learned the grain".
- **Why.** *dem* is "until, as far as; for, for the sake of" (O0390). The Epilogue already uses it this way: *rytet dem hos sa vesk hosel*, "cut for one who reads alone", which I read as "for" blind. *dem* is never the doer. The hearth formula keeps its *cadhat lom* / *cadhat lona*, where the sense rules out "by me". This line is not the formula, so the house formula is untouched.
- **Read again:** "No one at this hearth has held it. Laid for one who has learned the grain." **exact.**
