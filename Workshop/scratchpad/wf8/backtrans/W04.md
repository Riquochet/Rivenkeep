### 2.2 The wood's telling (the grain)

- **Seal.** This is held in the grain.
- **r1.** It is held in every hull of our kind. The hull that brings it to you (the shore) did not see it; it was not there (A15-W04). It holds it because it grew from the Aelvaren.
- **r2.** We were the Aelvaren, the Bearer of the Binding, and proud of the name. We were an old, small hull, grey, and light (A15-W04). We carried the Guest to the shore, through the thinner grey. We waited in the water below the door, to carry him home with the Aelthar made whole (hollow: as it would have been, and was not).
- **r3.** The Guest came back to our wood, not living. The shore-men sent him, dead, in our wood. His blood went into our wood. What one of us holds goes out, at the last, with his blood, into the wood that drinks it; so we hold all of it.
- **r4.** The shore set the old wood (us) alight; we raged in grief. We burned more than they had kindled us. We cried out, because wood screams. They sent us out. The current took us out into the open sea (A15-W04), and we did not go home, because it took us.
- **r5.** The current took us; burning, we turned for home, and our turning broke the current's taking. [We turned] because the open sea did not hold (let go of) our keel. Our keel knew the road, and the road goes home. We sailed ourselves home over the water. Charred wood, dying wood, still knows the road ("even charred, even dying": A15-W04).
- **r6.** At the edge of the grey, shore-men's hulls came at us twice, on our road. Then they turned aside and ran, and why, the wood does not hold.
- **r7.** In the thinnest grey we found the hidden old road on the water, the road held in the roots, where no shore-man's keel has ever gone. We burned wholly, all along that road. The fire went out of us slowly, and the grey closed in, by turns, until at the last the grey closed over. Then there was only our keel left, and a few of our ribs, and the Guest's bones in them.
- **r8.** From that road, then: half a hull, in the dark (blackened), we rose up onto the white sand of home. **The council (the silverbarks) came down to the water.** We went under and lay down among them, in stillness.
- **r9.** The elders came to us and held us, knowing us because they had sent us. With their hands bound together they laid them on our keel, and they held the whole of what it held, an older carving cited whole: the gift; a shore-man's foot, and the dust; a shore-man's neck taken gently; brow leaning to brow; the blade that slipped; the blow, and the fall; the burning; the sending out on the water. Wood smooths nothing over (hides nothing at all).
- **r10.** The elders looked at what the shore had said: the shore had answered the holiest Aelthar with a killing (truly a killing) and with fire. The elders held it for a killing, and it was one. The blade went; from that, the host's arm drew back; why, the wood does not hold. **The elders did not know of that drawing back.** They did not know that it was the shore-man's not-knowing (ignorance: A15-W04). They did not ask. They saw only war.
- **r11.** The elders laid our keel down among the roots of the council, with the Guest's bones still in it, as the long-lived lay down their dead. Then the council's roots drank it. Then, from the council, we went, through the roots of every grove, to every grove and to the farthest edge of the world. Every sapling, grown for all the hulls to come, drank it with the rain. Every hull of our kind goes to your walls carrying our grief.
- **Seal.** …and the grain holds it still.
