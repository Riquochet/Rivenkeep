### 2.2 The Second Tide's fragments (the excerpt; not blind, §1)

The iron came. All the long ranks (a memory ray: remembered). We all fell; the edge came open, in the white. We carved, on the stone. It did not speak (answer).

### 2.3 The wood's telling (the grain)

- **r1.** [The root: the axe.] We are the long ranks, at the edge of the grey. We were tall, dark of bark. We held **the edge**, in the earth, as roots hold earth. We did not think of it, as one of you does not think of **drawing breath**. We lived as pillars in the grey, to hold and only to hold, and we were glad.
- **r2.** From within the grey we watched the shore. Every hull that came near, we marked. **You only looked, at the edge.** Every brush of your palms was a cut in us; every passing, a wound. We keep every mark.
- **r3.** In the high summer of your havens the iron came from the right hand, in many hulls with many hands, and the shore-men came up through the water with it, to our bark. What one pillar felt, all the long ranks felt, through the roots, to the farthest edge. We remember it always.
- **r4.** One of us fell, and then another. The shore-men came back to us summer after summer, until the men who first brought the iron had grown old; and they sent their children.
- **r5.** As all of us fell, **the edge came open**, white, as light comes through a rent in a sail: then hard light, then a great hard light. The young of the long-lived turned their faces away from the light, and wept, **and we do not know why**.
- **r6.** Our stumps put up straight black shoots, as stumps do. In a few summers they stood up, young pillars, among our fallen. By turns: the iron came again for the young pillars; they grew more; the iron came again.
- **r7.** **We carved (remembered always) because we could not hold [it back].** You never laid a hand on the wood to know it; you only felled it, and carried it away behind. We cut on the stone our plea, *Stop*, because you never laid a hand on us, and because we thought (stone is what you love; you build your houses and walls with it) that the shore-folk would heed most a carving cut in the thing they love.
- **r8.** We carved it in our own words. We took a word of yours, from across the water, and carved it again, as near your speaking of it as could be. We laid the stones down among the pillars still standing. We believed that every carving speaks, because ours do: a child's belief. The stone has no mind; it did not answer [our carving]. You took the stones, with the timber, **over the water**, and where you carried them the wood does not hold.
- **r9.** At the last the stumps put up nothing. The grey grew thinner all along the edge. We held our sap and waited. The iron came more slowly, and then stopped; then we grew again.
- **r10.** We, never a pillar, speak to you. We are the last shoot of a stump cut low, where the iron first went in: a thin green thing, with the whole of the axe in our rings. The iron passed over us, because we were too thin: thinner than a pole.
- **r11.** The council, the silver elders, looked for hearts to bear their carvings. They looked, and did not choose the truest sapling, nor the oldest wood. They chose us, for the axe (remembered always) in us.
- **r12.** You call our wood *Blackthorn* (a stump in the dark). We go where the losing is greatest: to the one wounded hull, as a hurt hand goes to its hurt. We do not stop; we cannot help it: it is in our rings.
- **r13.** When a gun strikes the hulls, the hulls go at the gun that struck and at the second gun, and will not leave. You know why: the iron in the edge of the grey, summer after summer; and then the white came open.
- **Seal.** This is held in the grain … and the grain holds it still.

## 7 · Findings and open items

1. **A1 in a year** (proposed in `W03_grain.md`): a band laid on the empty cells of a file that holds no thing-sign in that ring is the band as a thing, and `→ @f` goes to it; a thing-sign on the file makes the thing its referent instead. IV.2's grey needed it, and every leaf that names the grey, the sea or the white as a file's one will.

Other leaves with "did not know why" (A, V, W in §18's blind row) should be read against this: where the wood is the one who does not know (IV.4's "We do not know why"), the blind alone is right.
