## 1 · Method, and how the test was kept blind

1. **The blind file.** `wf8/blind/S03.txt` holds only the 35 lines of `units/S03.md` that begin with `>`: the romanised Orrowen blockquotes, each keyed by its line number. They were extracted by a script (`awk '/^>/'`), without the file being opened. Two things were left out: the English, which is never blockquoted in the house style, and the word-for-word lines and notes. The unit has no Grain Notation (both leaves are stone), so no GN line was needed. The file's sha1 is `828b9e50…`.
2. **The reading.** The blind text was read using these sources only:
   - `orrowen_v2.md`: §0–§3 (grammar), §5.10–§5.12 (small words, numbers, affixes) and §11.6 (the Stonwryt);
   - `lexicon_orrowen.md`;
   - the lexicon: the shared `lexicon_orrowen.tsv` plus `additions/S03.tsv`, merged privately into `wf8/tmp/S03bt/lexicon.tsv`;
   - a private copy of `orr_analyze.py`, pointed at that merged lexicon: `--file` (exit 0, 0 reported) and `--gloss`, whose output is in `wf8/tmp/S03bt/gloss.txt`;
   - two small lookup scripts, `lk.py` (by form) and `lm.py` (by meaning), in the same folder.
3. **Frozen before the look.** The back-translation was written to `wf8/blind/S03_draft.md` before the unit's English or the Book was opened. It was saved at 02:55:28, sha1 `66387470…`, and a copy is at `wf8/tmp/S03bt/draft_saved_copy.md`. Only after that were `units/S03.md` and the Book (`wf7/book/legends_v13.md`, lines 338–406) read and compared. The unit's English matches the Book word for word.
4. **One leak, declared.** While mapping the unit's structure before extraction, one `grep '^#'` printed the unit's H1. That line carries the two leaf titles in English. Both titles are also given verbatim by lexicon rows O2777 and O1118, so the reader would have met them anyway. The two title scores are still marked *discounted*. No other English was seen before the draft was frozen.
5. **Limitation.** Once the English had been seen, no second blind reading was possible. The "after" scores for the two fixed blocks come from the analyzer's gloss of the corrected lines (§6), read by the same rules. A fresh reader should re-read I.5 ¶8 and ¶9 (§7).

## 5 · Spec and tool gaps found (proposals only; the shared files were not edited)

1. **Apposition is not placed by §3.1.** §3.1 says modifiers and possessors follow their noun, but it says nothing about an appositive noun phrase ("a Mystarch, one of the Thrones"). F2 shows the need for a line such as this: *"An appositive stands directly after the noun it renames, before any prepositional phrase; set later, it is read as adverbial."* Proposed for `orrowen_v2.md` §3.1, to be added by whoever holds that file.
2. **The *ā*-stem plural can mask a harmony slip.** Old *ā*-stems drop their *-a* and go broad before a suffix (*essa* > *essath*, *hemma* > *hemmath*). So a wrongly broad plural of the matching slender stem (*hemm* > *\*hemmath*) is a valid word with another meaning, and `orr_analyze.py` cannot see the slip. **Proposed for the analyzer:** when a word parses only as an *ā*-stem noun plus *-Ath*, and the stem without *-a* is a noun of the other harmony class, add a note such as *"(also: a harmony slip for hemmeth?)"*. The other such pairs in the lexicon (*ganna/gann-*, *essa/ess-*, and so on) could be listed by script. Not done here, to keep the shared tool untouched while other units run.
3. **"Part … part" has no word of its own.** *saed* carries both "half" and "a part" (L317). This is a lexicon question for Jack, not an error in S03.
