## 4 · Causes and fixes

### C1 · r1: *you* unnamed in the heart ring (translation; close → exact)

- **Was:** `r1·3  2: AXE    3: SQUARE    2: >LIE`, with a1–a3 from `2.r1·3` and `2.2.r1·3`.
- **Now:** `r1·3  2: STONEFOLK×3    2: AXE    3: SQUARE    2: >LIE`, with a1 `2.2.r1·3 → 3.r1·3`, a2 `2.3.r1·3 → 3.r1·3` and a3 `2.3.r1·3 ⇒ 3.r1·4`.
- **Canonical:** `r1/1 … 2: STONEFOLK×3`, `r1/2 2: AXE`, `r1/3 2: >LIE`. Ring 1 keeps its 3 years, and the heart stays plain: no fringe, one cell. The file legend now reads "you, the shore-men (in the heart ring only …: STONEFOLK×3 names you there)".
- **The comment-free reading now:** "The shore-men fell with iron stone. The shore-men lay down stone; then stone's lying down. Stone does not live." That is *You cut stone and lay it, and it lies where you laid it, and it is dead.*
- **Mark count:** 317 → 318. The unit's shape paragraph is updated.

### C2 · r9: `!CARVE →` the law (translation; drift → close)

- **Was:** `r9·3  11: SILVERBARK×3{all}    11: !CARVE`, run i3 `→` the law. Read by rule it says "no council carved that law". That is a different claim (who made the law), not "which no council can carve against" (no council can overrule it).
- **Now:** `11: HEAR` (hear; heed; obey, its own gloss), with i3 unchanged.
- **The reading now:** "All the council hear (heed) the years' law", so every council obeys the law of the years and none carves against it. It is *close*, because the modal "can" goes; "every council heeds it" is the grain's way of saying no council overrides it.
- **Alternative considered:** a break (the law thwarting a council's carving) would keep "carve", but it needs a runner from the law and would add a device to say less plainly.
- **Process note:** the translator's own `plain.txt` printed the drift ("The council does not carve the years' law"), and the unit's **Reads** line smoothed it into the intent. **A Reads line should follow the validator, not the Book.**
