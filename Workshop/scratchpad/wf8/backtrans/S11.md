## 1 · Method, and how the test was kept blind

1. **The blind file.** `wf8/blind/S11.txt` (sha1 `741c4ef7…`) holds only native lines, with the unit's line numbers:

   - every GN block (47 rounds).
   
   To choose them, the unit was scanned by script: line starts, a count of English function words per blockquote (none had two or more), and a check that the code blocks hold no non-GN words. Only the `####` headings were seen, which carry the English root-names ("The Wave", "The Lone Stroke" …).
2. **The reading.** It used these sources and nothing else:

   The carvings' English in the specs (§17's example titles, and the young-wood orders that the validator prints for a root) was seen only as the tools print it.
3. **Frozen before the look.** The reading was saved at 02:55:25 to `wf8/backtrans/S11_work/blind_backtrans_S11.md`, then made read-only (sha1 `3edb94f9…`). Only then was `wf8/units/S11.md` read in full. The tool outputs of the blind pass are beside it: `check_before.txt`, `gloss_before.txt`, `literal_before.txt`.
4. **Limits.**
   - Once the English had been seen, no second blind reading was possible. The "after" scores are the analyzer's glosses of the two fixed lines (§6), read by the rules. A fresh reader should read those two lines (§8).
   - **Two kinds of native text were missed by the blockquote rule**, because the unit sets them outside blockquotes:

     They were checked afterwards by gloss only (§6.3). They are lexicon names and the pilots' shapes, and the gloss reads them right. They are **not blind-scored**.
   - **Housekeeping.** At the start, the private merge `wf8/tmp/S11/lexicon.tsv` and the translator's private `wf8/tmp/S11/orr_analyze.py` were overwritten with fresh copies.
     - The merge is the same file by construction: shared plus `S11.tsv`, now with `O-S11-20`.

## 5 · The fixes

### 5.1 Translation

The unit is written by its generator, `wf8/tmp/S11/gen_s11.py`. Before any change, the generator was run to a scratch path, and its output was byte-identical to the unit. The fixes were therefore made in the generator, and `wf8/units/S11.md` was regenerated from it, so a later run keeps them. In the unit, each fix is marked *(Back-translation fix.)* in its line's notes, with the misreading it cures.

| ¶ | Line | Before | After | Reads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 46 | *… hy et hald, **somm scaevur kaelir**; veth …* | *… hy et hald, **ul scaevurol haelir**; veth …* | "in the rolling of a wreck". The verbal noun *scaevurol* (O2219), with its doer as the softened possessor (*kaelir* > *haelir*; pilot I.1, G3). The *-ol* ending cannot be "a ring", and the softened possessor leaves one reading |
| 9 | 200 | *Hebba lont et molt; grika **et lerneth**; eska et garl.* | *Hebba lont et molt; grika **et pa sest**; eska et garl.* | "give them the heart; take the two shoulders; close the hand". *et pa sest* is the dual for a pair (§3.2), with the article, as *et pa varn* (S03). *sest* is this unit's own word for a side (*sest lo sest*, "side by side"). The body image (heart, shoulders, hand) makes the pincer read itself, and *esk* then reads "close" |

### 5.3 Gaps in the spec (proposals for the spec's owner, not applied)

`wf7/orrowen_v2.md` and `wf7/lexicon_orrowen.tsv` are shared by the translators now at work, so these are written here and in the unit's notes, not into the spec.

1. **§3.2, the dual.** A natural pair should be said in the dual even where English says "the sides" or "the flanks". Proposed line: "the two sides of a host are *et pa sest*, 'the two shoulders'". Also, the plural of *lern* is heard "faces", and *pa lern* is "two faces" (units S01 and S10 use it both ways). The lexicon's *lern* row could add "(of a host, use *pa sest*)".
2. **Lexicon §4.4, zero derivation.** Add a line: "a word that is noun and verb at once is heard as the noun after *somm*, *amm* or *hosen amm*, where the same paragraph has used it as a noun. Say the verb with a person ending, or use the verbal noun." (*scaevur* "a ring" and "roll", ¶3.)

### 5.4 Tool notes (for the tools' owners; the shared tools were not edited)

1. **`orr_analyze.py`, clause openers.** After *somm* and *amm* (a clause follows), a v,n word with a noun after it should prefer the verb reading, as it already does after *re*, *es*, *nath*, *ho* and *sa*. Its noun gloss "wheel ruin" is what the blind reader followed in ¶3. The translator's own report shows the same gloss.
2. **`orr_analyze.py`, the dual in *-a*.** *kaevera* is glossed "think-DU", a noun dual in *-a*. But §3.2 makes the *-a* duals fossils (*ganna*, *pana*); the living dual is *pa* + S. A productive noun + *-a* reading should rank below the plural imperative (¶56; the unit already notes it).
3. **`grain_validate.py`, COMPOUNDS.** `HAND+CLOSE` has only the noun reading, "the kept hand". In E5-01 (r6, r7) the carved line uses it as an act, "close the hand". A verb reading ("close the hand|closing the hand|") would have kept the ¶9 reader from "cover".

### 6.2 What was not fixed, and why

- **The 17 closes.** Each is either the lexicon's own polysemy (*saed* half/part, *ryst* morning/morrow, *sulv* snow/white, *nynth* spring/water, *lusk* loose/send, *drod* spark/flash) or a difference the unit builds in on purpose and says so (S2, S3, G9, *omm*, *esk*). Changing them would be style.
- **E2-01, "Through, not against" (¶7).** Both phrase rows give the Book's sense among their first senses, and the reader chose others. The head is two words and elliptic by nature, so the drift was the reader's, and the line stands.
- **E4-08, *Veska nalelv* (¶60).** *vesk* has "look; seem" in its row, and the reader's doubt was needless.
- **The grain.** The rounds are canon, and they match their sources exactly.
