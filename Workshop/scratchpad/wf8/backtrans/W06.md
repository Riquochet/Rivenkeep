## 2 · The blind reading (summary; the whole draft is `wf8/blind/W06_draft.md`)

**Seren's frame.**
- The Council beneath the Thin Sky.
- As the Mystwood remembers: known from the heart of a Standard-Bearer of the Bonded Tide, from the grain we call Silverbark, which grew from a cutting of the silver elders of their council-grove; for the grove was their council-hall, and the grove heard the vote.
- Rhyna laid her hand on it at the shingle, and took it away again very quickly; and Halvard came all the way up out of the vault, and his face was wet.
- The knowing was over in a moment.
- The telling lay upon Halyna every day of that tide, because a council has many minds, and the two of them had one pair of mouths.
- Halyna told it, the one and then the other, in two inks; Seren laid it down.
- Where the wood was broken, or the telling faltered, it is marked, and left unmended.
- And no one answered.
- The facing leaf, which Seren wrote:
  - This has no course of ours.
  - We were not there, and we knew nothing of the vote.
  - I have left this leaf empty, and I ask everyone who holds this Book after me: leave it so.

**The grain, ring by ring.**
1. The long-lived sat in council beneath the silverbarks. The silverbarks were our hall: no roof but the high boughs, no floor but the moss. They held every word the council spoke, as a silverbark holds all it hears. From them came a shoot, which is we; and we give it as it truly was, for we do not turn aside.
2. All along the edge the grey was thin. A few black pillars stood there with gaps between them, like the few teeth left in a jaw, and the grey went out through the gaps. After the soft between-light came the hard white light.
3. Every new summer the littlest young were thinner, and some never opened their eyes. The eldest were dying slowly, at half their long lives, their strength going out of them like water from a cracked jar.
4. The eldest counselled waiting, as the trees wait: "The iron comes seldom now. The pillars do not grow; they wait. Wait with the pillars. When the iron stops, they will grow again, and grow the grey again." They spoke so because they were struck, and they were dying of it; they knew they were dying, and still they counselled waiting.
5. The Aelvaren came home by the old hidden road. It had burned wholly; only its keel came back, with the Guest's bones in it. When it came, something broke.
6. The young had grown up while the grey was slowly dying. They watched their mothers and fathers die slowly in the open light, and could not help them. They saw the Guest come home in ash. They came into the grove and put a question to the council.
7. The eldest rose and asked: "Send again? Learn the shore-men's words. They do not know us; they do not know the wood; not once have we taught them. Go slowly." Their voices were few and faint, and they were dying.
8. The young said to the eldest: "We listened through three of their lives, and learned three words; and the shore-men did not hear them."
9. The grove heard the young and knew them half right. We could have learned more, for we live long. We listened to the shore across the grey between. We went once, alone, to the shore, with our hands open; the shore killed the Guest, and so we did not send again.
10. We did not ask whether the whole shore-people killed the Guest, or one house at the edge. We did not ask whether the shore-men along the shore and in the ten havens had ever heard our own name. We chose to grow more hulls, rather than learn their words.
11. War: for the short-minded, and for anger.
12. The council put the question, and then chose. The eldest still pleaded for waiting; but the council chose war, and the choosing broke their plea. A great anger and a grief bore on the choosing, by turns.
13. In the dark the council took the straightest of the young silverbarks at the grove's edge, laid its heart open, and cut in its heart the first carving of the war, the carving whose root is *Grow*. The young silverbark still stands; it has not lost the carving; it grows.
14. The iron stopped coming to the edge in the summers after. The grove (?) did not wait for the iron to stop.
15. One after another the eldest fell into the moss. The carving did not fall with them. It is in every heart on the water, beneath all their carvings, as the first ring lies beneath all the rings.

(The seal: This is held in the grain … and the grain holds it still.)

## 4 · The fifteen "close" blocks, and why none is an error

- **Mine (the reader's), with the Book's sense in the tools or the spec:** the headnote, the facing leaf's first and second sentences, and rings 9, 10, 13 and 15.
  - `--gloss` gives *Hyll Lodhat* "the Joined Tide" and *lo sy* "here".
  - §3.4 puts the copula's predicate first.
  - §3.3 makes one ring one hour.
  - The ray in ring 15 names the pleaders.
  - The `?` in ring 10 sits on `STONEFOLK×3`, not on the hearing.
- **Losses the unit already documents:** the facing leaf's "the rest of that Tide" (for the telling), and in the rings "we can give it no other way" (1), "even those" (8), "those same summers" (14) and B12's recut of ring 12.
- **Nuances the grain has no device for, and does not pretend to:** "can" and "cannot" (1, 7, 13), "else" (10), "since" (15), "by then" (2), and "for which" (4).
  - These are the grain's economy (§6.5, §10.11), not errors. Where the unit reached for a substitute (A6's hollow for "could have"), it read back correctly.
- **Ring 9's "the shore killed the Guest".** The unit cut `>DYING` on the shore's file deliberately. It says (its §2.3, ring 9) that the killing is the shore's and that ring 10 asks by whose hand. The Book says only "was killed", but its next paragraph asks whether a people or one house of that shore did it, so the reading is close, not a drift. No change.

## 5 · The fixes

### 5.1 Ring 11, the angry young (a translation error; the drift)

| | Knowing form (`§2.3`, `tmp/W06/V-4.gn2`) | Canonical (`§4`) |
|---|---|---|
| before | `r11·1  13: STRIKE×3    15: THIN+MIND    5: ANGER` | `r11/1 5: ANGER    13: STRIKE×3    15: THIN+MIND` |
| after | `r11·1  13: STRIKE×3    15: THIN+MIND    5: ANGER+SAPLING×3` | `r11/1 5: ANGER+SAPLING×3    13: STRIKE×3    15: THIN+MIND` |

- **The cause.** The unit's note said "one to `ANGER` on the young's own file: the angry young are these young". That is true only with the `# file` tag, which is Seren's apparatus and not the wood (A16's last paragraph). Read from the wood by §11.2, `ANGER` is a thing-sign standing first, so it names a new referent, and A16 does not list feelings among a person's belongings.
  - The untagged validator agrees: "5 [anger (heat)]: anger (heat)".
- **The fix** is one mark, modifier + head: `ANGER+SAPLING×3`, *rhes·leathaelen*, "the angry young".
  - It is formed as `THIN+MIND`, "the short-minded", which stands beside it on the same split runner.
  - Its head, SAPLING×3, is the young of rings 6–10, on their own file.
  - It is told from `ANGER+STONEFOLK`, "an enemy" (§21.3), by its head.
  - The compound and its gloss are added to `additions/W06_grain.md`. Without them the validator reads the ligature part by part.
- **After.** The untagged reading is "… for the short-minded and the angry young" (`tmp/W06bt/plain_after.txt`). With the tags it is "the young (the angry young)" (`tmp/W06/plain.txt`). The block scores **exact**.
- **Ring 12 is left as it is.** `GREAT+ANGER` and `GRIEF` now stand first on file 5, so the wood says "a great anger, and grief" without an owner. That is what the Book says: "the weight of anger and grief".

### 5.2 Ring 14, the grove (a translation error; close in my reading, wrong by the letter)

- **The cause.** Ring 13 cuts "at the edge of the grove" as `EDGE` on the grove's file, 8, where it stands first. By §11.2 the edge becomes file 8's referent, and A16 has no row for a place's edge (it has one for a built place's doors and stones). So ring 14's `8: !KNOT` says "the edge did not wait".
- **The fix.** Add `ray 8: r1/1–r14/1`, v1's ray, "the same one": the one that did not wait is the silver elders of ring 1. It is one hairline, with no mark cut again and no packing changed. It is added to the ring-14 block in §2.4, to §4 and to the source.
  - Ring 13's edge still reads as the grove's edge, because the young silverbark runs `→in` it on the grove's file.
  - Ring 15's `MOSS` names the moss, which the Book also has ("into the moss").
- **After.** A reader who follows §11.2 ("a ray says that marks in different rings are the same one") gets "the grove had not waited". The block stays **close**, because "those same" is still left to the ring order, as the unit intends. The validator prints the ray, but its referent label does not follow rays (§6).

## 6 · Findings for the spec and the tools (proposals; nothing shared was edited)

1. **A16 should count feelings as belongings** (`W06_grain.md` finding 9): `HEART`, `ANGER`, `SHAME`, `GLAD` and `GRIEF` on the file of the person already standing there, as the mind is. Without it, any leaf that cuts a feeling first on a person's file silently changes the file's referent. V.4 no longer relies on it.
2. **A16 should count a place's edge as a belonging** (finding 10): `EDGE` on the file of a place already standing there, as `DOOR` and `SQUARE` are a built place's. An `EDGE` that stands first on an empty bearing (ring 2's) would still name the edge of the wood. V.4 no longer relies on it.
