1. **The blind file.** `wf8/blind/S04.txt` holds only the 46 lines of `units/S04.md` that begin with `>` before its notes section, keyed by line number. A script extracted them (`awk 'NR<551 && /^>/'`). To learn which lines are native, two things were read first:

   - a count of bold labels. This showed that the `> **Rhyna.**` / `> **Halvard.**` labels open native lines.

   Nothing else in the unit was opened: not the English, the word-for-word lines, the notes, the headings or `wf8/tmp/S04/src_*.md`.
2. **The sources.** The blind text was read with these only:
   - `orrowen_v2.md`: §0, §3.1–§3.10 and §5 (the lexicon tables, the small words, the numbers, the affixes);
   - the private merged lexicon, `wf8/tmp/S04bt/lexicon.tsv` (shared `lexicon_orrowen.tsv` + `additions/S04.tsv`, 2,901 rows), searched by form and by sense;
   - a private copy of `orr_analyze.py`, run as `--file` (0 unknown, 0 ill-formed before the fixes) and as `--gloss` (with the set-phrase hints). The outputs are `wf8/tmp/S04bt/check_before.txt` and `gloss_before.txt`.
3. **Frozen before the look.** The whole reading was saved to `wf8/blind/S04_draft.md` at 2026-09-28 03:05 (sha1 `2b2227fb…`), before any English was opened. It carries its own `?` marks and its alternative readings. Only then was `units/S04.md` read in full. §3 quotes the frozen reading.
4. **Not blind, in part.** Seven blocks were not a fair test, because the lexicon quotes their English:
   - the two titles, from the lexicon's title rows O1225 and O0697;
   - L165's last sentence and L174's hand-question (S04-20, S04-18);
   - the close L238 (S04-19);
   - the warden's report L455 (S04-17);
   - the guest-law in L527 (S04-16).

   Several other rows name the Book phrase they were made for ("carry this Book on after us", "come of age", "for a season", "by heart"). The draft's header lists them. A future blind test should either expect this, or strip the English from the `meanings` of phrase rows before the reader sees them.
5. **After the look.** The after-scores (§4) are not blind. They are the analyzer's glosses of the two fixed lines, read by the rules. A fresh reader should re-read those two lines.

**Scoring.**
- **exact**: all of the meaning comes back, and only the wording differs.
- **close**: a nuance or a word-choice is lost, but no clause means something else.
- **drift**: a clause comes back meaning something else.
- **wrong**: a clause comes back meaning the opposite, or something unrelated.

A block is scored by its worst clause.

## 4 · The fixes

### 4.1 II.3, L213: "This Book must outlive its tellers" (wrong → close)

- **Before:** *Doss maver **hossol o wrodhardath** um et flennath sy.*
- **After:** *Doss maver **hossol sell hy o wrodhardath** um et flennath sy.*
- **Word for word, after:** "A living longer than its tellers is a need upon this Book."
- **Why it failed.** The unit followed pilot IV.4's rule: the object of a verbal noun is written as its possessor (*maver o wrodhol*, "the need of its telling"). That rule is safe for a transitive verb like *brodh*. *Hoss*, though, is first "breathe, live", and only then "outlive, live on". So *hossol o wrodhardath* reads as the tellers' own life, not as the outliving of them. The gloss gives "life … S·teller-PL", and the reader heard a Book that needs its tellers alive, which is the reverse of the English. A second cause added to it: the analyzer's phrase hints did not show *doss maver um*, "must", because the slot was three words long (§5.3, P3). "Must" therefore came back as "needs".
- **The fix** uses the comparison of §3.6: *sell hy X* is "longer than X", and after *hoss* it is also "long after X", as in the unit's own *nath hoss ullen sell hy ull*. Either reading says the Book outlives its tellers. The analyzer's gloss now reads "be wish life long from(+S) his/it(+S) S·teller-PL upon(+S) the Book this". By §3.6 (*hy* after a quality is "than"), that gives **"This Book must live longer than its tellers."** Close; the paragraph's other close points are unchanged.
- **Where it was changed:**
  - `units/S04.md`: the blockquote, the word-for-word line, and the note, which is marked *(Back-translation fix.)*;
  - `tmp/S04/ii3.tsv` P12j, `ii3_draft.txt`, `doc_blocks.txt`;
  - `tmp/S04/build_doc.py`.

### 4.2 IV.3, L435: "so there would be nothing to show" (drift → close)

- **Before:** *… hy vodh re vaver o nayalat **lestiret**.*
- **After:** *… hy vodh re vaver o nayalat **vesket**.*
- **Word for word, after:** "for he wanted nothing seen".
- **Why it failed.** *Lestiret* is the participle of *lestir* (O1200), "show the way, guide". The lexicon has no plain "show", and a guide is the wrong kind of showing. The blind reading, "he wanted nothing left that could show the way", is what the lexicon says.
- **The fix.** *Vesket* is the participle of *vesk* "see" (its row: "seen (the participle)"). It is the same word as the annal's *navesket*, "unread", so "nothing seen" means nothing left to show. **Note for readers:** the analyzer's gloss prints the row's first sense, "colour"; the participle is its second.
- **Where it was changed:**
  - `units/S04.md`: the blockquote, the word-for-word line ("nothing shown" > "nothing seen"), and the note, which is marked *(Back-translation fix.)*;
  - `tmp/S04/iv3.tsv` B14, `iv3_draft.txt`, `doc_blocks.txt`;
  - `tmp/S04/build_doc.py`.

**How the file was changed.** `units/S04.md` is generated by `tmp/S04/build_doc.py` from the drafts. A rebuild into scratch was first checked to be byte-identical to the unit as it stood. The two fixes were then made in the build's sources (above), and the unit was rebuilt. ## 5 · What was not fixed, and why

### 5.1 The two reader slips (drift, text unchanged)

- **L53**, *Re wrodha sona o rask umom*, "they told its end about me". The Orrowen is right by the lexicon, and the unit's note says so. The slip traces to a documentation gap in the spec (P1 below). With P1 in `orrowen_v2`, a reader would get "The last of it is about me".
- **L79**, *tev vollat demont, tev vollat dem bana*. The reader made *dem* possessive, which the grammar does not allow. Read by the rules, it is "a child called to them, a child called to both", which is the unit's own word-for-word. The English "both" refers to faith and walls, and the Orrowen leaves the referent to the context, as the English does.

### 5.2 The closes

None of the nine closes is an error. Each is one of three things:
- a word with a second lexicon sense the reader passed over (*hesp* "weapon", *hosen* "equal", *trenn* "sentence", *flennath* "book");
- a recast the unit documents in its §4 (the closing annal, two fists, the clerk);
- the canon *o* "he/it".

L138's *hosen tunn* could be made plainer as *hosen et Hemm, tunn*, "as the Elder, wholly". That is style, and it is left to the translator.

### 5.3 Proposals for the shared files (not applied)

| # | Where | Proposal | Found at |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | `orrowen_v2` §3.6 and §5.10 | Give *um* its second sense, as the lexicon row already does: "upon, on; **about, concerning** (what a thing is told upon): *brodh X um Y* 'tell X about Y'. To tell a thing *to* someone is *lo*." | L53 |
| P2 | `orrowen_v2` §3.2 (the construct), beside pilot IV.4's rule "the object of a verbal noun is its possessor" | "With a verb whose first sense takes no object (*hoss* breathe, live; *senn* die; *orr* go; *omm* fall), the possessor of the verbal noun reads as its **subject**: *hossol X* is 'X's life', never 'the outliving of X'. | P3 | `orr_analyze.py` `phrase_hints()` | A slot phrase that ends in a preposition (*doss maver um*, *doss … lo*, *nath noss … lo*) should match across a longer slot, up to the end of the clause, not just two words. *Doss maver hossol o wrodhardath um …* has a three-word slot, and after the fix it has five.
