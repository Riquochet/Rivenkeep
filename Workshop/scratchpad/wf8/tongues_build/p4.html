12.3 · The three tabs: The Book | Plain Words | Original

Jack’s note 3: “I like that there are 3 tabs for the book.” Every tale carries three tabs, and so do the foreword, the Invocation and the Book of Knowings (Legends map §8). The Book is the canonical text (The Legends); Plain Words is the modern translation (The Legends, in Plain Words); Original is the native text itself (The Legends, in Their Own Hands). The three files are built to be parallel: the same leaves under the same ids, in the same order, so the tab can swap one for another in place.

  • The native hand is drawn above The Book and Plain Words; the tabs change only the English beneath it. The grain has no language, so it is identical under every tab; only its glosses change: The Book uses the Book’s words (shore, thin wood, the dread), Plain Words plain ones (the coast, cheap ships, the edge of our guns’ range).
  • Original shows the native text for decoders. For a stone leaf: the leaf-hand, whole, facing the Book’s translation, and any inscription the leaf quotes in its own hand (the dry cut, the Hal, the coal) beside its leaf-hand transcription. For a wood leaf: the whole round, then Seren’s ring plates in order, each with its literal reading under it once the ladder allows.
  • A locked leaf shows its own hand under all three tabs, because there is nothing yet to translate. The tab bar still shows, so the player learns where the translation will appear.
  • The tabs are views, not edits. Switching never changes, removes or reorders a word, so the Book-only-adds law holds. The choice is remembered with the settings, not the save, and opens on The Book the first time. The Book wins where they differ.

12.4 · For the builder

  • No leaks. A locked stone surface’s accessible title is “A leaf in the course-hand, not yet read” (a Hal surface’s, “A Stonwryt in the first builders’ hand, not yet read”; a dry-cut stone’s, “A stone in the course-hand, not yet read”); a locked grain round’s is “A carving in the grain, not yet known”, and a single locked mark’s or name’s “A mark in the grain, not yet told” or “A name in the grain, not yet told”; the unmarked Stone’s is “Nothing is given here”. None carries the English, the Orrowen, or an id that names what it tells. A leaf-hand line set in the font carries the same accessible name, not its text. The drawings in this doc are the earned forms.
  • One ink per hand. Every drawing paints only in currentColor, under stone-ink or wood-ink; the page sets the ink, and the ids of every drawing are prefixed so any number can share a page.
  • The leaf-hand is a font. Garl Flenn (15 KB as woff2) sets any leaf from its true-encoding markup; the converter reads the tier-3 lexicon through the analyzer, so every bite and harmonic letter is right. A release build should merge the font’s overlapping contours and draw the sealing lintel as a contextual mark.
  • The dry cut is drawn by rule and read back by rule: every dry-cut drawing in this doc was read back to its token string before the page was written.
  • Weight. A whole-leaf grain round is budgeted at 350 KB, a ring plate at 60 KB; all eight wood leaves whole, with plates, come to about 3.5 MB. Load a leaf’s round and plates when the leaf opens.
  • Size is meaning. A round is drawn at the scale of its wood, so a Hasty sapling is small and a Throne’s heart fills the leaf. Rounds of nine rings or more need a tap-to-enlarge below about 900 px; a course-hand line in body text is legible at 1.55 em.
  • Reduced motion. Any “rings growing”, “runners growing” or “marks growing in” reveal honours prefers-reduced-motion and shows the result at once.
  • Truth or nothing. A stone leaf with no authored Orrowen shows its first paragraph in the hand and the rest as ruled empty courses, never faked. The validators run before export: the round trip for the course-hand and the dry cut, the grain validator for every round, the analyzer for every line of Orrowen.
  • The generators stay out of Docs/. The renderers, the sound-law engine, the analyzer, the validator, the font builder, the sign tables and the lexicon data live with the build tooling. This doc carries the rules and the data needed to redraw any of it.

13.2 · What we took in spirit, and what we did not

From Arrival (the heptapods’ writing, from Ted Chiang’s story and the film’s design) the grain takes three ideas: a writing of meaning with no sound behind it; a sentence taken in whole rather than read in order; and a form drawn in circles, where the writer must hold the whole before making the first mark. Our version of that last one is the canon’s: a knowing is known whole, as memory, and in Arrival the medium is the writing’s shape where ours is touch. The grain takes none of the look:

{{tab m_arrival}}

Version 2 moves the grain toward three looks v1 had banned outright (branching vines, interlocking, knots), so the boundaries are restated by their signatures, not by broad words. v2 is intricate without borrowing anyone’s look:

{{tab g_arrival}}

From Ogham the course-hand takes the spirit: an alphabet made to be cut, strokes set on a line, stone-honest and straight. It takes none of the system:

OghamThe course-hand
strokes on either side of a stem line, or through itletters stand on one side of a bed, never across it
letters are counts of one to five parallel scoresno letter is a count; a letter is an upright (where the sound is made) and laid stones (how)
vowels as notches on the linevowels are loose pinnings between the letters; the only marks below the bed are the mutation bites, single, never grouped
five-letter families named for their first letter; tree names; read up an edgea place-by-manner grid; no letter names; read along a course, line by line, as a wall is laid

From the reformed Egyptian of Jack’s note the dry cut takes the economy, and the idea behind it, logograms with phonetic complements, which Egyptian, Maya and Chinese writing all share. Nothing in it copies the characters of the Anthon transcript or any Egyptian sign: its signs are the First Tongue’s own marks, worn straight. The leaf-hand likewise takes only an idea, a running hand made from an inscriptional one, as Roman cursive was.

13.3 · The look-alike screen

Every new drawing of v2 (the 328 word-signs, their heads, the turned stone, the leaf-hand’s letters) was screened by machine against 1,463 characters of real scripts: Latin capitals and small letters, digits, Greek and Cyrillic capitals, the Runic block, Ogham, Tifinagh, Hebrew, the Kangxi radicals and 172 common CJK characters, katakana, hangul jamo, the Canadian syllabics, Cherokee, Old Italic, Old Turkic, Old Hungarian, Phoenician, Carian, Lycian, Gothic, and 52 symbols including the crosses, the swastikas, ⊥, ⊤, Π, ∀ and the planetary signs. Each candidate and each reference is drawn into a 64 px box, thinned to a one-pixel skeleton, and the two skeletons compared by their mean chamfer distance (0 is the same shape; a plain T scores 0.007 against Latin T). Below 0.030 against a recognisable script is a copy and must change; 0.030 to 0.045 is looked at by eye.

{{tab s_lookalike}}

Every sign after the first two rows scored between 0.030 and 0.045 against a recognisable character and was looked at by eye beside it. None is a copy: each match is an artefact of the skeleton at 64 px, and the sign reads, at any size, as a head under a crown. No sign scored below 0.030 except the Fore, a Latin cross by design (“The Latin cross is fine”), and the Door, kept whole by both peoples.

  • Dropped during design because the screen or the eye found a copy: a Stem-and-cope sign (丅, T), a Bed sign (⊥, 土, 士), a Still sign (二, 王, =), a Three sign (川, Ш), a Hand sign (Ψ), a Split sign (Y), a Sprout and a Tally sign (runic ᚠ, ᛓ), two tree signs (runic ᛀ, †), a sail sign (P, ᚹ), a mouth sign (K), a wall sign (A), a gate with its capstone (开), a fire sign (a goblet), and a first leaf-hand in which every letter became an arch (it read as Armenian). The leaf-hand’s g, which read as П when its shore stood upright under the slant, was given a stronger back-lean.
  • By law, W7: no branch leaves a word-sign’s stem at a slant as a rune’s branch does. Crowns are free stones, never dots, acutes or curls, so a crowned sign never reads as a letter with a Tolkien vowel mark.
  • The leaf-hand’s near calls, looked at by eye and kept: a and A (Г, 0.037), o and O (ד, 0.036), k (0.034 to a Kangxi radical), the gate (Π, 0.034, the Door). Each is a short stroke-pair every alphabet has somewhere. The hand has angular turns, a nib and hairline joins along the line: no loops, no bowls, no stem-and-bow, no headline.
  • What the screen cannot do. It has no fonts for the invented scripts (Tengwar, Cirth, Aurebesh, Dovahzul, D’ni, Kryptonian, Sheikah, the Hylian scripts); those were checked by eye against the charts of the v0.1 pass, and nothing new resembles them. Arrival’s logograms are ink circles; nothing in the stone is round.
  • The grain’s 47 new signs were checked by eye beside their v1 neighbours and against letters, digits, runes and interface icons; twelve first drawings were recut and one withdrawn (§9.9). The first marks are proto-writing, so some are the world’s commonest strokes; none is a rune, an Ogham letter, a Cirth letter, a Tengwa or an Arrival logogram (§2.6).

13.4 · What is ours

  • One ancestor under two opposite tongues. A First Tongue whose catch became one people’s knock and the other’s long vowels; whose particles froze into one tongue’s mutations and the other’s tense suffixes; whose one word for a standing thing became one people’s stone and the other’s tree. The stone kept the sound and the wood kept the meaning.
  • The round as a sentence of time, and now relations grown as roots: runners that follow the grain, whose ends say their role, whose every crossing is read, braids for “by turns”, binds for “each only with the other”, pockets for a knowing inside a knowing, and a heart kept plain by law.
  • Real wood as the vocabulary of the marks: the fire scar that is Burn, the check that is a wound, the knot the grain flows round, the frost ring, the ring shake, included bark, the graft, the wedging year-ring, the added rays, the resin pocket.
  • A masonry alphabet, and a masonry grammar. Letters as ashlars on a mortar line; words as stones; mutations as bites cut into the mortar; the sealing lintel over a pair-name; and now the small words as mortar, left out of a dry cut as a dry-stone wall leaves out its mortar, with not the one stone laid the wrong way.
  • A history written into the hands. The Hal is the same hand, mirrored and running the other way, still spelling the endings and the three sounds the living lost, so the forgetting is visible; the Last Carver’s five tells are the same rules, broken by a carver who learned them from a cloak.
  • Two tongues built from the canon’s own names and from each people’s metaphors: to lay a tale, to cut a vow, a pair as a gate, to know as to hold; a hull as a bough, a scout as a root, sky and breath as one word.

Outside the scripts: echoes in the canon itself

  • The Myst games. Myststone and Mystwood sit near two of that series’ Ages, and the Book’s Riven and Myst- already make the echo. Nothing in any hand adds to it: the D’ni letters and numerals were checked hardest, and none of the hands shares anything with them. No Orrowen word matches a published D’ni word except gor (D’ni “time”, Orrowen “every”), a form match only.
  • Avatar. “The memory that runs back through the roots” is close to that film’s forest that holds its ancestors’ memories. The grain carves memory as a ray cut into wood, and its runners are cuts inside one heart, never links between trees and never a glowing or living network.
  • Tree-carving traditions, named with respect so that no later sign drifts toward them: the Moriori rākau momori of Rēkohu, carved in living kōpi trees, and the Basque shepherds’ aspen carvings. The grain is cut across the end-grain of a heart, never on standing bark, and carves no human figures.
  • “It is known.” The canon line “what is carved in living wood is not heard. It is known” ends on a phrase a well-known television series made a catchphrase. It is ordinary English and canon, noted only because readers may smile at it.

13.8 · The audit still owed before anything ships

  • The dictionary pass (Welsh, Irish, Scottish Gaelic, Breton, Cornish, Manx; the Elvish lexicons) on every reserve word: the 194 that have word-signs, the 305 the tier-3 lexicon uses, and the 26 the grain’s new signs read as. Under note 4 it is a check for obvious matches only: the same word with the same meaning. mamm “lose”, new with the I.1 pilot, looks like Welsh and Irish mam and should be looked at first.
  • An exact-string web search for the new names: Tumaʔ, ryt broc, rellor wrod, brodath lodh, Garl Flenn, and the headline Orrowen words and haven originals.
  • A fresh originality pass on the v2 grain renders by a new eye, as v0.1’s was done, including a web image search for “tree ring script”, “carved wood interlace glyph” and “dendroglyph writing”.
  • A look by eye at the leaf-hand at reading size in the Legends page’s own theme; the screen was run on skeletons.
  • Fresh blind readers: for the re-cut grain drawings, for the Hal once it has its lexicon, for the seven fixed lines of I.1, and for IV.4’s cited round.
