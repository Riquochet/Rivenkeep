1The Conceit: The Book Is a Translation

Every word the player reads in the Book is English, and none of it was written in English. Seren Two-Inks wrote her leaves in Orrowen, the Shoreland tongue, in the leaf-hand, the running ink of the course-hand. What the Rivenmen cut in stone they cut dry, in word-signs, leaving the small words to the reader. The wood’s knowings were never written at all: they were carved as grain, a writing of meaning that has no sound, and Halyna told them aloud. Under both lies one older tongue, the First Tongue, that neither people remembers. What the player holds is the translation. A leaf the player has not earned stays in its own hand. Earning it translates it.

What Jack asked (2026-09-27, the first notes)

That the line “Untold. The Mystaeri writing should feel like the aliens’ writing in Arrival, and Shoreland like an ancient, Celtic-sounding tongue. Both should be real languages, written the same way throughout. He also asked for a tab that lets the player read either the Book or the Plain Words translation. Version 0.1.0 answered all of it.

1.1 · The first spread, as the player first sees it

The Book opens on I.1, The Torn Cloak. On the facing leaf the Book and the game both show the Stone out of the Grey as the wood gives it before the Epilogue: its own rings, with nothing cut in them. In the grain an unmarked round has no root and so no axis, and it reads, truthfully, nothing is given here. Under it is Seren’s line, written in her ink, in the leaf-hand. The English arrives later, beneath the hand, when the player earns it.

The same line stands under every blank wood leaf in the Book, beneath that leaf’s own round, fully cut and not yet glossed. So from the first minute the player can see both peoples’ writing on one spread, and cannot read either. That is the shape of the whole Book: something they can see and do not yet understand.

1.2 · Two peoples, two tongues, one ancestor

Shoreland (the Rivenmen)Mystaeri (the Naelear)
The spoken tongueOrrowen, “of the Shore” (§3)Seilrhass, “bough-thunder” (§7)
How it soundsstops and clusters, a rolled r, back vowels and a rounded y, stress on the first syllableno stops at all, no o or u, and one knock after the first syllable of every longer word: Ael’thar
How it worksverb first; two initial mutations; a dual number; conjugated prepositions; no verb “to have”. It is Celtic in how it works and in nothing it saysverb last; modifiers before the noun; postpositions; living and mute things are grammatically apart
Its writingthe course-hand (Garl Dhrenn), an alphabet of straight chisel strokes on a mortar line, in three hands: the dry cut for stone, word-signs with the small words left out (§5); the leaf-hand for ink, the whole tongue joined and quick (§6); and the Hal, the first builders’ (§4.8)the grain (Eilseth): the cross-section of a trunk, marks cut across its growth rings, heart to bark. It records meaning and has no sound (§8, §9)
How it is taken inread with the eyes: letters by sound, word-signs by the word they stand forknown by touch, whole, as a memory. Only the Bonded can hold it; the telling is the hard part
Its old formthe Hal, the first builders’ speech. The living can see every sign of it and cannot sound itthe Eldest speech, surviving in the oldest carved names (Esthaer, and the borrowed Mystaeri)
Its ancestorThe First Tongue, Tumaʔ, “what is held in common”: one people, before the grey, who cut 45 marks on whatever stood. The stone kept its sound; the wood kept its meaning. Neither people knows it (§2)
A further handthe Captain’s coal, the Title only; the Last Carver’s, the Epilogue onlythe soft letters (rhelranth), “fine and curling, as beautiful as frost on a window”: sound spelled on stone. A sketch, not yet drawn (§8.9)

One line holds the difference: stone writes what is said; wood writes what is meant. The course-hand can be learned by anyone with eyes, which is why the Last Carver could cut it. The grain can be seen by anyone and known only by the Bonded, which is why Seren can name its roots by eye and must leave the rest to Halyna. And one line holds the kinship: the stone kept the sound of the First Tongue, and the wood kept its meaning, so the Knowing is the nearest living survivor of a tongue no one alive can speak.

1.4 · The four rules the conceit keeps

Truth or nothing

Players decode. So every letter, word-sign and mark the player can see is a true encoding of a real text, drawn by rule. Nothing is ever decorative. Where no text has been written in the tongue yet, the leaf shows nothing, or ruled empty courses, and never invented letters. Empty wood is drawn as rings with no marks, which in the grain means exactly “nothing is cut here”.

The Book only adds

A translation is laid beneath its hand; it never replaces it (Legends law 7). The fragments of a wood leaf stay above its whole telling, and the tabs are views, never edits.

Every unlock is a milestone every player reaches

Translations ride campaign milestones and events the sim has already recorded, each event with a campaign fallback. No skill achievement ever holds back a leaf (Legends laws 1, 4 and 5).

Decoders are rewarded, never required

The course-hand is a real alphabet, and the dry cut’s word-signs are a real list. The Title is shown with its English from the first launch, so it works as a key. A player who studies it can read Seren’s line before earning it, and that is the reward. The grain’s root signs can be read by eye, as Seren reads them. Everything past the root must be known, and waits for Halyna. The kinship of the two tongues is never told; a decoder may find it.
