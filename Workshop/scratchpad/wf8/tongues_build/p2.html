7Seilrhass: The Thunder-Tongue

Seilrhass, “bough-thunder” (spoken Seil’rhass), is the spoken tongue of the Naelear. “We speak as the storm speaks in the high boughs, in cracks and breaks” (II.2). It is loud, knocking and dialectal, and it is the moment’s tongue: “the thunder is for the moment, the softness for always”. The softness is the grain, which is the same words with the knock taken out, and no sound at all. The tongue is built out of the names Jack already has. Every one of the coined Mystaeri names stays valid, with its meaning. And it descends, with Orrowen, from the First Tongue (§2): it is built on the ancestral stem, without the case ending, and every Seilrhass word falls out of the laws S1 to S8 with no mismatches. The two tongues share 38 words that look nothing alike (§2.5).

7.1 · What things are called

{{tab m_names}}

The Name Map’s renames are honoured throughout: saen “heartwood” (was orn), nei “silver” (was sil), Naelear, Rhenear. Jack kept “the Mystlands”; its Orrowen original is Mystow, “the place of the grey”.

7.2 · Sounds

Every Mystaeri name the Book prints uses only these sounds, and the list is closed: nothing may be added without a canon reason.

{{tab m_sounds}}
  • There are no oral stops: no p, t, k, b, d, g. There are no m, f, h (except inside rh and th), w or y, and no o or u. Those sounds belong to Shoreland.
  • Frequency. Common: n, l, r, th, s, e, a, ae. Middling: v, ei, ea. Rare: rh and bare i. z, x, q and the endings -ion, -iel, -dor never occur.
  • A syllable is (C)V(C). No onset clusters (rh and th are single sounds). A coda is l, n, r, s, th, ss, nn or nth; sth occurs in one worn root only, esth “fire”. No more than two consonants meet across syllables; where a compound would make three, a linking -e- goes in. Vowel-final roots take -n- before a suffix vowel: rei “go”, reine “goes”.

7.3 · The knock

The rule, from every spoken form in the Book: every word of two or more syllables breaks once, after its first syllable, even inside a root: Ra’lensaen, Ra’lenthae. One-syllable words and the small words never knock. A compound is one word and takes one knock (Nael’saen), and so is a suffixed verb (Ral’theine “remembers”).

What it is: a glottal catch, with a high falling pitch on the syllable before it and the rest of the word said level and soft. Speech comes out in beats of one hard syllable and a soft run, which is why the wall calls the ghost fleet’s knocking “the drums”. The carved soft reading drops the catch: Ael’thar is spoken, Aelthar is carved “whole and soft”.

Why shore ears hear stops. The Story Bible says the spoken tongue has hard stops, yet no word has one. After the catch the next consonant is released hard, and a Rivenman hears a stop that is not there:

{{tab m_knock}}

Dialects. “Each deep of the forest speaks it its own way.” Edge speech, nearest the grey’s edge, is the Guest’s, and the one described here. In Grove speech, the council’s, the knock is only a pitch-fall and th is voiced between vowels. In Deep speech the catch is velar and heard as a k: “Aelk-thar”. The dialects differ in a handful of words too, but never in the grain, which is why the canon calls the grain the one thing “bridging even the many dialects”.

7.4 · History in brief

Seilrhass had an older stage, the Eldest speech, which survives in the oldest carved names and in one borrowing. The exact laws, from the First Tongue through the Eldest speech to the living tongue, are in §2.3 (S1 to S8). In brief, four changes lead from the Eldest speech to the living tongue, and they account for every irregularity in the canon names.

  1. The old stops were lost. *t and *k after the first syllable became the knock; elsewhere *t became th and *k became rh. The knock is the ghost of a stop, which is why the drums sound like stone struck.
  2. Final vowels fell in words of two syllables or more (*thaele > thael “tree”). vaere keeps its -e because that -e is the long e of the First Tongue’s *-ai, which did not fall; so does Neivaere. (v0.1 said the vowel stayed after r; the ancestor replaces that rule.)
  3. The collective -aer was reshaped to -ear by analogy with the singular -ea “one of”. Naelear and Rhenear are living forms. Esthaer, the eldest Throne-name, was cut before the change and keeps -aer, and so does the Rivenmen’s early borrowing Mystaeri, which took the old ending and added a Shoreland plural.
  4. The old agent ending *-ane merged with the plural -en. It survives frozen in varen “bearer”. The living agent ending is -ea.
  5. The tense suffixes were once particles. -e, -es and -ar are the First Tongue’s *e “now”, *es “at another time” and *ar “toward”, fused to the verb after the final vowels had fallen. So the wood’s past -es is the stone’s future es: the stone looked forward with it, and the wood looked back.

7.5 · Word-building

Compounds are modifier + head, one word with one knock: Nael·saen “mist-heart”, Rhen·vael “stone-grain”, Seth·varen “carving-bearer”. The English glosses may run either way; the Seilrhass order never does. Names may be who-is compounds: Aelrhen, “bond-stone”, is one who binds himself to stone.

{{tab m_suff}}

Zero derivation. A root is a noun or a verb by its place: seth a carving, to carve; thae a tide, to grow; thael a tree, to rise, to stand; seil a bough, to bend as a bough bends; nael the mist, to veil. A tree is a standing, and a bough is a bending.

7.6 · Grammar: verb last

Order. Subject, then object, then verb, and nothing after the verb except the small words that close a clause. Time and place come before the object. Modifiers, possessors and relative clauses come before their noun, and postpositions after it. It is the mirror of verb-first Shoreland.

8The Grain (Eilseth)

This section is the grain as v0.1 built it, and all of it stands. Version 2 (§9) keeps the anchor at the heart and adds the intricate, intertwined outer rings Jack asked for; where it replaces a rule here, it says so, and this section marks the place. A carving is the cross-section of a trunk. The heart is the beginning and the bark is the end, because the knowing grows outward as a tree does. Meaning is carried by marks cut across the growth rings: one ring is one step in time, and the angle a mark stands at says who or where. It records meaning, not sound. It is taken in whole. It is grown, not written, and a later carving literally contains its earlier form. The Book’s own image for it is exact: “the eldest carvings are the youngest ones grown old.”

8.1 · What it is

  • A writing of meaning. A carving records what is meant, not what is said, which is why the canon calls it the writing “bridging even the many dialects” of the thunder-tongue: “whoever lays a hand on the carving knows what the carver meant, whatever tongue the carver spoke”.
  • Taken whole. The Bonded take a round in at once, as a memory. The reading order in §5.8 exists only for eyes and programs.
  • Grown, not written. Rings are growings (thae). The knife-cut is a V with two flat facets, one lit and one in shadow: visually the opposite of an ink stroke.
  • Every sign has a soft reading (eisranth), the Seilrhass word for its meaning spoken without the knock. The ten root signs also keep Seren’s English names, which she gives them by the look of the stroke (“the root, as I name it”).
  • Truth or nothing. Every round the player sees is a real encoding of a real knowing. No round ever carries decorative marks.

8.2 · How a knowing is carved, and how it is known

The carver cuts; the wood grows. An order is cut into a living heart, and the wood grows on around it. Each mark in a round is the cross-section of a ribbon grown along the trunk’s fibres, so every cross-cut shows the same round, and on a plank the carving “ran with the grain, fine and shallow” (V.1). Seren draws every knowing as its round, the end-face, because that shows all of it at once. The Book’s drawings are hers.

The knower touches, and it is given whole. There are no words in a round to turn. A spoken sentence can be reordered, misheard or punned on, and its small words can be lost across water, as the Guest lost Shoreland’s. A round has no word order, no homophones and no small words. Every relation is laid out in space: who is the file, when is the ring, “the same one” is a ray, “because” is a tie, “not” is a mirror. One drawing has exactly one reading, so “no word could be twisted and no promise misheard” is literally true of the grain.

Why only the Bonded. A round is “a forest’s thought”, too large for one shore-mind: Seren “felt something come into her too great to hold, and let go”. The Aetherbond’s shared mind can hold it, because “what the one knows, the other knows”. It needs no special mechanism. It is the same act as the knowing of a wall.

The telling is the hard part. Halyna “set each word down like a marking-stone where the knowing lies”. In grain terms, every English word they choose is a gloss laid on one mark. That is exactly what the Book shows: the round, with Seren’s words set beside the marks they belong to. The telling grows more faithful because more classes of mark can be glossed (§5.6), not because the wood changes.

Deceit without lies. A carving of the Tide that Learned Deceit cuts its decoys hollow. Read whole, the round says plainly: show this; do not mean it. It is the enemy’s eye on the water that is fooled, never the knower of the wood. “The wood does not lie. It was told to make us believe a lie, and it obeyed.” The lie is a hollow cut, and the hollowness is itself carved.

8.3 · The round

{{tab m_anat}}

All rings of a round share one shape around the pith, with a little seeded wobble, so they never cross; a knot bends the ring lines near it outward, so the grain visibly flows round it. Every free parameter is seeded from the round’s id (mulberry32 over an FNV-1a hash), so the same round is the same drawing for every reader, which is the Book’s law of determinism. Adding or removing a mark never moves a ring: the Stone’s unmarked stage and its marked stage share every ring exactly.

8.4 · The ring grammar: how a round is read

The axis. The root sign is cut from the pith outward in the heart ring, and its direction is the axis. Everything is read relative to it; the page may turn the round, and the reader finds the root first. The Book draws the axis near the top, turned by a seeded ±12° so no two rounds line up mechanically.

Files: angle is who and where. A round has 16 files, radii at 22.5° steps clockwise from the axis, numbered 0–15. Everything cut on one file within a band is about one referent: one hull-group, one road, one stone, one people. Even files are bearings, relative to the carving’s main road, facing the shore; odd files are placeless, for referents with no road. Real compass names belong to Halyna’s telling; the Knowing generator fills them from the board.

{{tab m_files}}

Rings: radius is when. One ring is one step. Marks in the same ring happen together, at one hour: “three roads, one hour” is three marks in one ring. Outer rings come after inner ones. A thin empty ring means a morrow passes. A mark stretched across rings holds through those steps: until.

The three bands: beginning, middle, end. Two band-rules, doubled ring lines, part the round into three. They are the Book’s two grain-rules made visible, and they mean different things in an order and in a telling:

{{tab m_bands}}

A sapling (Hasty wood, Burn, the green sliver) is too young for heartwood: it is all heart, one ring and no rules. Every wood leaf’s round has exactly as many rings as the leaf has paragraphs, and its band-rules fall where the leaf’s two grain-rules fall. In a joined round (several piths inside shared rings), the shared rings are measured about the round’s centre, and every pith’s root points along the one axis.

Line quality classifies. The line of a closed shape tells which world a thing belongs to. Straight-edged (the square and its kin) means of stone: the wall, the gun, the house behind the stone, the shore-men’s home, the door. Curved (lens, drop, loop) means of wood: bough, hull, seed, heart, home, one of us. The same person-figure with a square crown is one of stone, and with a lens crown one of us. Open strokes (acts) are not classified.

Weight carries nothing. The stroke width is fixed per round. A thicker cut never means louder or more urgent (that device belongs to Arrival).

Not is a mirror. Negation is the sign’s mirror image across its own file. So every sign must look different from its mirror: the chiral ones already do, and every other sign carries the entry-nick, a short cut entering beside its head from the clockwise side, where the knife went in. Mirrored, the nick is on the other side.

Grammar devices: ways of cutting

{{tab m_dev}}

How a tie’s ends are read. By the marks it touches, not by the file its end happens to fall on. Each end stops one knife-width (3 units) from its mark and nearer to it than to any other. A tie across rings leaves its source’s head and lands on its target’s foot. The thick end is the source, the fine end the target. A tie that ends on a file where nothing is cut in that ring ties to that file’s bearing, a place.

Composition rules

  1. One root per round, cut first, in ring 1, from the pith, along the axis. It owns files 15, 0 and 1 of ring 1.
  2. At most two signs on one file in one ring, as a ligature, modifier inward and head outward, exactly as a Seilrhass compound. Reading heart to bark is reading left to right in Seilrhass. v2 In a telling, at most one mark per cell (§9.7).
  3. At most eight marked files per ring, and every mark keeps at least 3 units from its ring lines and its neighbours. v2 The eight-file limit is withdrawn; the gate rule keeps the air instead.
  4. Conditions lie along the ring; acts and things cut across it. A mark cut inside a condition band takes that condition (“wait in the grey”).
  5. Rays join marks on one file; ties join marks on different files. Only the memory ray runs inward. v2 Ties become carved runners of role to or then (§9.4).
  6. The seal and the name stay in the bark. Nothing is ever cut beyond the bark’s outer contour.
  7. The canonical order: band I, II, III, then the bark; within a band ring by ring outward; within a ring file by file clockwise from 0; then bands, rays, memory rays and ties.

8.6 · How a carving grows across the Tides

The Law of the Rings, made literal. What a carving can hold is how old its wood is.

{{tab m_law}}

The growth rules

  • The same root. Every carving of a line cuts that line’s root in ring 1, on the axis. That is what Seren reads by eye.
  • Containment. A carving that grows from a parent reproduces the parent’s rings exactly as its own innermost rings. The parent’s bark becomes included bark, and the new Tide’s rings grow outside it. The inner rings are never re-cut, because the wood “cannot unlearn what is carved in it”. Where a child changes what its parent said, it does so with a later mark on the same file: the outer ring speaks last.
  • Old bark and rules. Band-rule 1 of any grown carving lies on the ring where the root’s sapling bark was, so it is drawn as both a double line and included bark.
  • Borrowing, dress, chains, reach-back. A primitive borrowed from another line enters as a graft, a V of included bark around the marks that carry it. Early forms worn as a disguise are cut hollow. A hinge that carves an older stratagem ends in that stratagem’s root cut half size. A carving that reaches back contains the far ancestor’s rings, not the middle one’s.
  • Joined piths. From the Joined Tide on, each formation of an order has its own pith, inside shared rings. The piths’ own rings never touch: separate hearts, then one trunk, as stems that grow together really look. It is the dark mirror of the Aetherbond.
  • Thrones. A Throne’s Judgement is an order in eldest wood, its true name in the bark. In battle its bark closes one pale ring per groan.
  • The war-pith. Grow until the shore is silent was cut on the last round pith of the war (§6.18). Every heart grown under that council since has a square pith, “beneath all the other carvings, as the first ring lies beneath all the rings”. The square is the Mute Square, the sign for stone: the war’s wood grew the mute thing into its own heart.

8.9 · A third hand: the soft letters (a sketch) Jack

The canon gives the Mystaeri a third writing: “letters no one could read, fine and curling, as beautiful as frost on a window”, cut on stone, where “here and there among the letters they had tried to cut our own words by their sound … the sounds were close, and the symbols wrong” (IV.1, IV.2, IV.5). These letters, rhelranth “frost-words”, spell sound. Stone carries no knowing, which is why “stone does not translate”: to anyone without Seilrhass they are mute. By the truth rule they may only be drawn if they are real, so this sketch makes them real. It is not drawn yet.

{{tab m_softletters}}
  • How it is written. Left to right along a gentle curve, as on the face of a standing stone; a word ends with a space. Each curl is at most 270°, single and never nested. The strokes are fine and even, and every stem is upright: never thick-and-thin, never a Z or a 2 for a skeleton (§13).
  • Shoreland by sound. The letters have no stops and no back vowels, so a Shoreland word takes the nearest sounds: p b m f w are written v, t d th, k g h rh, o a, u e, a Shoreland y i. “The sounds were close, and the symbols wrong.”
  • The mute stones’ warning (proposed): Nael enth li naelsaenen ni rhith. Le rhithveir ni eisse. Ve nael sath li eisse. At the edge of the grey, do not fell the pillars. They are not timber. We are behind the grey. The Guest’s three Shoreland words are spelled among the letters by sound, and the obvious plea in Orrowen, Hess. Doss et helv ul sennyl. “Stop. The sky is in dying.”, is built from them. A player who has learned Orrowen can then read the sound-spelled words inside the frost-letters; the rest of the stone stays mute.
  • They never translate by reading, because stone does not translate. Their meaning is added beside them from the wood: at IV.2 (“so we cut our plea on the mute thing”) or at IV.4; the three grey stones of Eldhythe’s harbour-house get theirs when Thaesaen falls.
