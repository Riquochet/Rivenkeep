10The Texts

Every sample written so far, each given the same way: the drawing in its own hand, the tongue as the Book spells it, word by word, the translation, and the exact letter string (for the course-hand) or the Grain Notation (for the grain). New in v0.2: each Shoreland text is also given in the leaf-hand, as Seren’s ink writes it, and cut dry, as a Stonewright would cut it, with the dry cut’s own reading (the mortar words the reader lays back in are in brackets); and the wood texts are drawn by the v2 renderer, beside their v1 drawings where they differ. Every drawing here is generated by rule from the text beneath it, and every text reads back from its drawing by rule.

How to read the pieces. Word by word puts each word over its gloss (the abbreviations are in §2.3). The hand is the letter string the course-hand writes: letters are separated by .; ^S and ^N are the bites; A and O are the harmonic letters; , is the wedge, | the perpend and #gate the end of an oath; = puts the sealing lintel over a word. GN is Grain Notation (§5.8): * the root, ! not, ×3 many, + a ligature, ^rj a span, ‖ a band-rule, ¦ included bark. The dry cut string (§5.2): @word is the word-sign read word, +A.th its complement letters, ! the turned stone, %h a capped numeral letter.

10.15 · The Hasty seven, and Burn

The Hasty Tide’s wood. Each is a green sapling round, one season old: one ring, no heartwood, a square war-pith, and one sign cut from the pith along the axis. A Hasty sign carries its whole young-wood order in its own anatomy. That is why Halyna could tell one word at first and the whole order at the Night of the Naming: they learned to hold the rest of the same one sign. 10.18 · The Stone out of the Grey (the leaf facing I.1)

The Epilogue. A stone that had been wood came in on the tide in a small grey boat: Myststone, “the one stone his people had that would carry a meaning”. It was cut with a chisel “in our own letters, so that anyone might read it”, and Seren read it with her eyes and did not lay her palms on it. So the grain in the Stone was never known by anyone at the hearth. The Last Carver cut letters for eyes and grain for hands, and only the letters were read. This leaf is the first the player sees and the last to be filled.

  • The five tells (§4.7): no bed; every stroke tapered at both ends; the Title’s nasal bite copied faithfully; no mark at the end, where the cutting stops; and, new in v2, he cut the mortar: every small word, in letters, as the coal Title had them, and one more. No Stonewright cuts a small word in stone. “The right thing, said truly, by one who did not yet know how we say it.”

The grain Jack

The Stone’s grain is the wood’s half of The Torn Cloak: what every hull saw on the last whole stone, and what went home through the roots to the groves, where “he knew it … as the wood knows a wall”. It holds all five things of the Title, as the wood names them; the letters stop after two, and the grain does not stop. Eldest wood, petrified, round pith; nine rings, 4 / 3 / 2 by movement, and the seal. The axis points right, so the top stays clear for the letters.
