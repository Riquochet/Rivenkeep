11Tier 3: The Whole Book in Its Own Tongues

Jack’s note 5: “I’d like tier 3 on how far to take the translation.” Tier 3 is the furthest: the whole Book in its own tongues. Every stone leaf is written whole in Orrowen, in the leaf-hand, as Seren wrote it; every wood leaf is carved whole in the grain, with Seren’s frame (her title, her headnote and her closing line) in Orrowen, because those are hers and not the wood’s. This section gives the vocabulary that makes it possible, a lexicon of {{n olex}} entries covering every word of the Book’s stone text, and two whole leaves done as pilots, one of each kind, each read back into English blind to test whether the tongue says what the Book says. The native text lives in the Original tab’s own document, The Legends, in Their Own Hands.

11.1 · What tier 3 means

  • A stone leaf is written in the leaf-hand, whole, because the Book is Seren’s ink (“This Book is ink”). Every mortar word, every ending, every mutation. An inscription quoted in a leaf (the Title, a Stonwryt, a lintel) is drawn in its own hand beside it: coal, cut letters, the Hal, or the dry cut.
  • A wood leaf is carved whole, one great round per leaf, every knowing cut once (§9.11), and Seren reads it in ring plates on the facing page.
  • The translator keeps three habits of the tongue: verb first; the dual for pairs (Halyna are always two, and take a dual verb); and the “at” and “upon” idioms for having, being able and feeling.
  • Nothing is coined. Every word rests on the First Tongue’s roots, and every grain sign on its marks. Every reserve word still owes the Welsh, Irish and Tolkien dictionary pass before it ships (§13.8).

The surfaces, as the Orrowen v2 spec sets them, with two changes from v0.1: Seren’s note is drawn in the leaf-hand, and the black chest’s lid carries the foremark.

11.2 · The lexicon: how it was made, and what it found

{{n olex}} entries, every one resting on the First Tongue’s roots (the inherited words, the suffixes and the 371 reserve roots), made into the rest by Orrowen’s own suffixes, its prefix na-, its compounds and its constructs. No new root was coined, and no root that only the wood kept was revived in the stone, so the ancestor’s 38 kin words stay exactly 38 (where the Book needs such a word, Orrowen builds it from its own material: eye is veskow, “the seeing-place”).

  • The Book is covered. All 1,202 English lemmas of the stone text, its 62 names and its 62 hyphenated words have an Orrowen rendering: 1,189 lemmas by an entry, and 13 (of, a, have, can, could, belong, ago, either, neither, without, whoever, whichever, wherever) by the grammar itself. By running word count, three-quarters of the stone text lands on canon words.
  • The canon survives whole. Every canon word, name and sense is in the lexicon with its canon gloss verbatim; the audit finds 0 missing and 0 changed.
  • {{n olex_logo}} entries carry a dry-cut word-sign: 328 are the word-signs themselves, about 1,030 are a word-sign with its complement letters, 197 are compounds cut as two signs, and the rest are phrases whose stones have signs. With the 328 signs, the turned stone and a few complement letters, a mason can cut more than half of the lexicon without spelling a stem. No new word-sign was needed.
  • The analyzer reads romanised Orrowen and the Hal, undoes both mutations and the endings, checks harmony and every mutation against the word before it, and reports unknown or ill-formed words with suggestions; it passes all 87 samples of the spec, finds all 11 of 11 crafted faults, and finds both of the Last Carver’s tells.

How the words are made

{{tab l_kinds}}

The spelling rules every pattern obeys: a harmonic suffix vowel takes the stem’s class; a stem-final c/k is respelled by the vowel after it; an old ā-stem drops its -a before a vowel suffix and goes broad; the canon’s three old broad stems keep their broad endings (treskat, flennath, tevath). Where a suffix’s sense is regular, the derivation is systematic (every verb root has its verbal noun, participle and agent); where it is not (a place, a folk, an un-doing, an “-ish”), only the words a speaker would use are made. A form that would collide with an existing word is not made.

The derivational patterns with examples {{tab l_patterns}}

The compound’s seam is the canon’s: the modifier, then the head softened (gorndholm, Halvard, sirrvell). Two seam rules are new, and both are proposals: a softened dr- gives r- (moltrunn, a heartbeat), and a seam that would make a letter pair the Book reads as one sound (t+h, d+h, r+h, a+e, o+w) is not allowed: myst + cless would spell mysthless, so “a fog-bank” is the construct cless vyst instead. Zero derivation adds a sense and never a form: tev “a child” is also “grow”, its root being *taw- “sprout”; lunn “freedom” is also “open”.

The canon has a small, sharp set of particles; the Book’s English needs many more small words. Orrowen makes them from nouns and its own particles, and every one is an entry.

Kael’s telling

{{inki1 2}}

{{romi1 2}}

Give me the stone. No, I will stand. I have sat enough this winter.

Orrowen has no “no”: a man offered a seat refuses it by saying the verb back, Nath reskym, “I do not sit”.

His oath

{{inki1 3}}

{{romi1 3}}

I swear on the lintel of the western stair, where the old wardens cut their names, that I will tell this plain, for I have no other way of telling. I was on the gate that day. I am a counter of things and not a singer, and what I did not count I will not tell you.

The oath is the canon’s Rytom um hosk…, and it closes with Ston., “It stands”, as every oath does: the one word of the leaf the English does not carry Jack. es gadhom sy venn, “I will lay this plumb”: to tell a tale is to lay a course, and a plain telling is a true course. The wardens cut their names and Kael cuts his oath: one verb.

The True Men’s verse

Ganna cresket, pithulath driget,
ul gemmyl lo orrow, wemmeth myst;
re yyst hosel um dholmath luskat,
eth re dhresk et covv um o sest.
Re ryt o lesk galat dem sennyl,
eth re vaver nahos hyor vranast.

The gate was broken, the lords were scattered,
the grey sails gathered along the shore;
one man climbed where the stones lay loosened
and tore the cloak his shoulders wore.
He wrote five things a man might die for,
and none of us has needed more.

First-syllable stress; the odd lines four beats, falling away on a two-syllable ending (DRI-get, LUS-kat, SEN-nyl); the even lines close on the same consonants, not the same vowels, myst · sest · vranast, a rhyme of the ear a mason would call a true joint. “None of us has needed more” is nahos hyor vranast, “none of us a sixth”: the True Men count Jack.

Grammar the leaf had to settle

{{tab p1_grammar}}

Collisions the leaf steps around

  • N + br- gives mr-, an onset the tongue does not allow, so no nasalised brodh anywhere: Kael’s tell after es and nath is cadh, “lay”, the tongue’s own idiom for telling.
  • mysk “say” after a nasalising word reads as nasalised bysk “tie”, so the leaf never puts mysk after es or nath. And re vysk is both “said” and “bound”: “bound” is given as the participle.
  • darr “come” softens to rarr, so mellor “come in” is used; the reserve mosk “begin” is the Creed trap, so “the crying began” is re dhald et carmol, “the crying rose”.

11.5 · I.1 read back blind

The test: could the Orrowen be read back into the Book’s English using only the specs, the lexicon and the tools? A reader was given only the Orrowen lines and the two notation lines of the leaf (the Title’s dry cut and Seren’s leaf-hand string), with no English, no word-for-word lines and no notes; wrote the back-translation; froze it (hashed and timestamped) before the pilot’s English was opened; and only then compared.

The result: 12 exact, 6 close, 4 drift, 2 wrong, of 24 paragraphs

All six drifts and wrongs traced to nine clauses. The two wrong paragraphs both held a statement of character: what the True Men are, and that there was no greater man than the Captain. Both failed on the same weakness, a small word with two readings (hosel alone/only; hy from/than) in a position where the wrong one is the natural one. The True Men’s creed, “who fought only when the cause was just”, came back as a last stand, “who fought alone when the end was sure”. Fixed: 8 clauses in 7 lines of the leaf; 3 lexicon rows; 4 lines of the spec made explicit (the unmarked possessive, hy as “than”, “only” as nath … veth, nath … tul as “not yet” only: all now in §3.3); 2 defects in the analyzer. After the fixes the analyzer reports 0 unknown and 0 ill-formed across the leaf, and still passes all 87 samples. The Title, in the leaf-hand and cut dry, and Seren’s line both came back exactly.

11.6 · The wood pilot: IV.4, The Gift Held an Hour, whole in the grain

Seren’s frame, in Orrowen

The title

{{inkp4 0}}

Hebbet sa re yal kethet hos clem

The Gift Held an Hour.

“The gift that was held one hour.”

The headnote

{{inkp4 1}}

Hosen tum Mesk Myst: kethet hy et hebbet sost, et tolm domm sa re rik Brenn hy et tuss, ul Hyll Dhumol; doss o ul lern Yebb et Pard.

As the Mystwood remembers: known from the gift itself, the dark stone that Brenn picked out of the dust, in the Tide of Remembering; it faces the Warden’s Door.

{{inkp4 2}}

Re heth Rhyna o hos clem lo et odh, eth re rarr o uloy niss, hosen nynth um dholm, hy vodh hebb et mesk hemm sost nayalat pynt.

Rhyna held it an hour at the hearth, and it came into her slowly, like water over stone, for the eldest wood gives nothing quickly.

{{inkp4 3}}

Re femm Halvard o nafedh.

Halvard never touched it.

{{inkp4 4}}

Re yal o lo et lodh um et parow, eth re heth o o hosen re heth ey o, eth ew o sa re hadh et tolm hosast ul et brodhol.

He was at the mortar across the ward, and he knew it as she knew it, and it was he who began the telling.

{{inkp4 5}}

Ew duss sost brodhol et trenn ul o rask, hy vodh re yal maver o wrodhol um Halyna hosen es gadh et arra o, ul et brodath orrowen sa re yal lo nafedh.

The sentence at its end was the hardest to tell, for it had to be told as the Guest would have said it, in words of ours he never had.

{{inkp4 6}}

Re wrodha Halyna o, hos eth ullen, ul ba luth; re hadh Seren o.

Told by Halyna, by turns, in two inks; set down by Seren.

{{inkp4 7}}

Amm re yal et mesk cresket, ell re omm et brodhol, doss o rellorat eth nahlennet.

Where the wood was broken, or the telling failed, it is marked, and not mended.

The close

{{inkp4 8}}

Eth re dhess nahos.

And no one answered.

  • “Began” is “laid the first stone”, cadh et tolm hosast: to tell a tale is to lay a course, so to begin a telling is to lay its first stone. The reserve mosk “begin” is the Creed trap, avoided.
  • “Known” is kethet, “held”, the canon’s own verb for knowing wood. Halyna are two, and take the dual (wrodha, 3du); “by turns” is hos eth ullen, “the one and the other”: the pair’s antiphony in three small words. Seren writes her own name in letters, with no lintel.
  • “It had to be told” is the “upon” idiom, doss maver um, “need is upon”, in the past; the pronoun object of the verbal noun is written as its possessor, o wrodhol, “its telling” Jack.
  • “As the Guest would have said it” is es gadh et arra o, “as the Guest would lay it”, with the tongue’s own verb for telling, as Kael’s es gadhom sy venn: after es, mysk “say” would be heard as bysk, and brodh would begin mr-, the onset I.1 avoids. “Words of ours” is et brodath orrowen, “the words of the Shore”, the article keeping brodath out of the nasal after ul. (Revised after the blind reading, which had es mrodh … ul ol mrodath.)

The wood’s telling, in the grain

One ring per paragraph, one or more knowings per sentence, files as the pronouns: we is file 12, you file 0, the Guest file 1, and “whoever held us” is simply file 7 with nothing cut on it. The root is GIFT, “We were given once”.

{{tab p4_files}}

Ring 1: the heart stays plain. No pocket, braid, bind or break, and no fringe. “Green to its edges” is a runner to the edges; “though you took us for it” is HOLD as the likeness, not a said-pocket; “time forgot us” is the ages not holding, run to the old wood itself.

{{gn p4r1}}

We are the eldest wood. We were a tree when the world was green to its edges. We fell, and the ages lay down on us as leaves lie down on leaves, until time forgot us and we grew dark and hard. We are not stone, though you took us for it.

Ring 9: the rite as he meant it, and the first bind carved in a whole leaf. Ring 9 is cut in the wood’s own voice, so the grain must not cut as done what was never done. What was made is cut whole (the shallow cut on the host’s arm was opened); what was never made is cut hollow, “shown, not meant”: letting go of the neck, the Guest’s own cut, and the pressing of the arms, whose two bind strands are hollow runners. The bind is the rite’s meaning, harm to one is harm to both, and its out-runner is the promise. Left and right are placement: the host on file 4 stands clockwise of the Guest on file 1, so the host’s right arm and the Guest’s left meet in the bind.

{{gn p4r9}}

With his left hand he would open a shallow cut on the host’s right arm, and let go the neck, and cut his own left arm, for he would ask no blood he did not give. Then the two would press their bleeding arms together, shoulder to shoulder: a promise of united front, that harm to one was harm to both.

Ring 12: the break. The ring’s centre is the arm’s runner crossing the blade’s shallow runner square and splintering it, lap l2 ⊳ l1: the grain’s own grammar says what the English says with “for”. “Half made” is {half} on the rite; “heard all of it” is HEAR{all}; “no one picked us up” is the shore’s file not taking.

{{gn p4r12}}

The blade that was meant to go a little way went deep, for the arm was moving. The host’s blood came, and he cried out, and his men came. The Guest did not lift his hand against them, and the cut he would have made on his own arm was never made. They struck him down on the stones of the door, and he died there with the rite half made. We lay in the dust and heard all of it, and no one picked us up.

What the translation did

  • Every sentence is carved, once. One mark to a content word; a referent already cut in a knowing is pointed at, never cut again. Thirteen runners end on a file referent.
  • The heart stays plain, and the fine work is outward: the first pocket is in ring 3, the first break in ring 12. Jack’s “anchor in the middle … details in the outer rings” holds in this leaf exactly.
  • Truth or nothing, in the conditional. IV.4 is full of would. The grain has one device for it, the hollow, and uses it strictly: what was done is cut whole, what was never done is cut hollow.
  • Two breaks, both the leaf’s own turning points: the moving arm thwarting the shallow cut (ring 12), and the foot’s finding thwarting our could-have-spoken (ring 13): “the one that could have spoken to him; and it was the one his foot found”.
  • Two rounds cited, not re-carved. Ring 8 cites the rite as he meant it by the Aelthar’s own round; ring 15 cites the gift’s sentence by its round, root STOP. Each is a cut-pocket holding the root half size, with the cited round drawn beside it.
  • The blind stands alone in ring 11: one mark, one empty cup, “We do not know why. Fear leaves no mark on wood.” The barest ring of the round is the one the whole leaf turns on.

The whole leaf, round and plates facing the Book’s English, is in the Original’s IV.4.

11.8 · What the pilots teach the next translators

  • The small words are where meaning slips. Both wrong paragraphs of I.1 turned on a small word with two readings; the IV.4 frame’s one drift was an idiom a glosser did not show as one. Write the clause so only one reading is natural, and let the analyzer show set phrases.
  • The stone and the wood fail differently. Orrowen fails on homophony and small words; the grain fails where a reading lives outside the spec. Both now have rules for what was found.
  • Truth or nothing reaches into the grammar: the grain’s hollow for what was never done, the stone’s Ston. for an oath the English does not close.
  • A fresh blind reader for every leaf, before it ships, with the notation and the Orrowen only, and the file legend stripped by a parser before anything is printed.
  • The lexicon is generated, and rows appended by hand are lost on a rebuild unless their recipes go into the builder’s sources: the tooling note every pilot carries.
