9The Grain, Version 2: Intricate and Intertwined

Jack’s note 7: “I like that Seilrhass has an anchor in the middle of the tree and the details are added in the outer rings. This keeps it separate from The Arrival. But the same flavor. I would like to see it more intricate and more intertwined.” Version 2 keeps the anchor exactly: the pith, the root sign cut from it along the axis, the rings as steps, the sixteen files as who-and-where, the three bands, the seal in the bark. Everything new is cut outside the heart ring, and it grows denser ring by ring, because each outer ring has more wood in it and older wood holds more. More intricate: a ring’s knowings stack in years, outer rings split each file into cells, and a fringe of finer marks, parts and pockets say what v1 could not. More intertwined: every relation between signs is a runner, a narrow living cut that grows through the wood from the sign that governs to the sign it governs, crossing the others over and under, and every crossing means something.

9.1 · What Jack asked, and what the prototypes showed

{{tab g_ask}}

Two layouts were built and drawn with the whole of IV.6 before the rules were written.

  • Full-circle sub-rings, one per knowing, failed. Each sentence became a thin ring right round the round, holding one to three marks. IV.6 became 68 concentric lines with specks on them: a vinyl record, sparse and mechanical, and its runners, confined to channels and gates, read as a circuit board.
  • Years packed by referent, cells in the outer rings, and runners grown through the wood is what v2 adopts. The same leaf is 29 years and 915 units in radius, against about 1,950 for the rejected layout. The marks cluster where the story is, and the runners flow along the grain between them. The heart stays plain.
  • A lattice router alone draws 45° jogs and parallel bundles, which again read as a circuit board. String-pulling in polar coordinates removed the look: a straight run there is a gentle drift across the grain, never a chord.

9.2 · What stays, and what changes

Unchanged from v1: what the grain is (meaning, not sound; taken whole; grown, not written; a soft reading for every sign; truth or nothing); the round (pith, heart ring, growth rings, band-rules, included bark, the bark and its seed); the axis and the sixteen files; the rings (outer after inner; an empty ring a morrow; a span until; three bands, three movements); the local frame, the entry-nick, the classifier (straight means of stone, curved means of wood) and the rule that weight carries nothing; the 70 signs, six bands and every v1 device, among them the ten roots, Burn, and BARB, the Bar Before, a Latin cross, kept; names in the bark; and the tie-end reading rule, which now applies to runner ends.

{{tab g_changes}}

Every Admiral carving and every v1 sample keeps its Grain Notation, and its meaning, exactly. Only the drawing changes: ties become carved runners, marks take v2’s breadth, the ring lines yield a little round every mark, and the meaningless year-hairlines of one-year rings are no longer drawn, because in v2 a year-line always means a year (§9.13).

9.3 · The Law of the Rings, made visible: intricacy grows outward and with age

The canon’s law is that “a hull of one season holds one sign; of forty summers, a phrase; of a shore-man’s life, a sentence, and a memory in it. The eldest pillars hold a judgement.” v1 made it literal in ring counts. v2 makes it literal in the knife as well. Each age of wood may carry only the devices it has grown into, and within any round the heart is plain and the fine work is outward. This is Jack’s “anchor in the middle … details added in the outer rings”, enforced by rule.

{{tab g_law}}
  • Ring 1, the heart ring, holds the root and, in a telling, the first knowings. It takes no fringe, braid, bind or pocket, and has one cell per slot.
  • Band I takes runners and the fringe, and at most two cells. It takes no pocket, braid, bind or break.
  • Beyond band-rule 1 everything is allowed, and cells grow with the girth.
  • Where a leaf’s opening words want a fine device, the heart cuts them plainly instead, and the fine devices stay outward: “though you took us for it” in IV.4’s heart is a runner to a likeness, not a pocket. The tier-3 pass found seven leaves whose openings want one, and every one resolves this way without moving a word Jack.

So the carving is quiet at the heart and busy in the sapwood, as a real tree’s figure is.

9.4 · Years and cells

{{tab g_levels}}
  • Years. A ring of a telling is cut in years of about 32 units (seeded). Each year holds one step of each referent’s story. Under every mark that is not in its ring’s first year, the grain shows a faint year-line: a partial ring, real wood’s wedging ring, a year that grew only where something grew in it. Years are never drawn as full circles (that was the rejected vinyl look). A ring of one year, every Admiral order and every v1 sample, keeps v1’s width.
  • The packing rule. Knowings are placed in the order the telling gives them, and each mark takes the next free cell of its own file: cell by cell clockwise across the slot, then the next year outward. So within a referent, later is outward; across referents in one ring the grain does not order, and where order across referents matters, a then runner says so. Adding or removing a runner never moves a mark.
  • Cells: more rays as the girth grows. Ring k has c = clamp(⌊(r·SLOT − 6) / 58⌋, 1, 4) cells per file’s slot. Every cell lies inside its slot, so a cell never changes the referent. Trees add rays as their circumference grows; the cells are that. On IV.6 the cells (1, 1, 1, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4 by ring) cut the leaf from 53 years to 29.
  • Breadth and room. A mark’s breadth shrinks with its cell, and the root may be up to 56 units broad in a heart ring of at least 72, so the root is always the largest mark. Every half-file and half-cell line keeps 3 units clear, and the top 5.5 units of every year are free: that is the runners’ room.
  • The grain’s flow. Round every mark the nearest ring lines bow away by up to 1.2 units, and where a runner crosses a ring line, the line dips: the wood yields round what is cut in it, as grain yields round a passing root. It is anatomy, and carries nothing.

9.5 · Finer marks: new elements, parts, the kind-arc and the fringe

{{tab g_elems}}

The part device. A sign cut with some of its elements whole and the rest hollow names that part of the thing. It is v1’s hollow (“shown, not meant”) turned to use: the rest of the thing is shown, so that the part is meant. It gives the body and the made thing their parts without new signs.

{{tab g_parts}}

The kind-arc, ‿, a fine ring-arc cut under the three feet of a ×3, means those of that kind: a whole people (Seilrhass -ear), where ×3 alone is a plural (-en). US×3‿ is Naelear; STONEFOLK×3‿ the shore-folk as a people; ROOT×3‿ ralear, forebears. It comes from the Bed, *loð-: those set in one bed.

The fringe, written SIGN{f,…}, is eleven tiny cuts at three stations round a sign: how many, which and how much, and how it went. It is the Tally (*kail-) grown round the sign: only is the Lone Mark small, still the Bed small, at last the End reduced to its point, gently the Cup, slowly the Curl drawn out. It is never Ogham: it sits beside or under one sign, never along a stem-line, never as a run of parallel strokes, and counts stop at four.

{{tab g_fringe}}

9.6 · Runners: the living branch-lines

A runner is a narrow living cut that springs from the head of the sign that governs, and grows through the wood to the sign, file or pocket it governs. It is the Course (*trenn-, “a running line”) grown from a Stem’s head: like the wood’s roots, it runs with the grain and crosses the rings where it must. It is cut, not drawn: a V-cut with two facets, 1.35 units half-wide at its node, where it swells into a small bud, tapering to 0.45 at its end. The thick end is the source; the taper gives the direction, and is not weight. A runner may be cut smoothed (a hidden relation) or hollow (a seeming one), as a mark may. Its end says its role, confirmed by where it lands:

{{tab g_roles}}
  • The file is the pronoun. A runner of role to or in whose target is written @f ends in the empty cell of file f: “to that file’s one”. The grain needs no sign for us in every knowing; it points at our file.
  • Split runners. A runner may fork once, into two or three shoots of the same role: “what one pillar felt, all the ranks felt”. A shoot never forks again.
  • The blind is what “Fear leaves no mark on wood” means. The grain has no sign for a shore-man’s fear. Where the host’s fear stood, the wood cuts only an empty cup, a because that lands on nothing, and Halyna glosses it: “We do not know why.” Jack
  • How a runner is grown. By a seeded, grain-following search on a polar lattice, which walls off every mark and never crosses a ring line inward (only memory runs inward), prefers to run along a ring and climb late, keeps runners apart so they cross when they must instead of running in bundles, and is then smoothed and given a slow meander. The meaning is in the endpoints, the terminal and the crossings, never in the path’s shape. A decoder only traces a route.
  • Composition. Prefer the file referent to a runner that crosses years to a repeated sign. A runner stays within its ring when it can, and across rings runs only outward. Two runners of the same role from one mark to one target are one runner. No runner passes through a pocket. At most one fork, three shoots and 300° of turning, and no loop.
{{tab g_rays}}

Where the small words went. Seilrhass joins its words with small words, and the grain never cut one. v1 had two relation-lines; v2’s runners, crossings and knots give the grain its whole grammar of relation, as the chisel’s economy asks of the stone, without a single small word. The economy of note 1 was always the grain’s.

9.7 · Crossings: pass, break, braid, bind

At every crossing of two runners, one is cut through (over) and the other is broken on both sides (under). The kind of crossing is read from the under-strand’s ends and from what the strands are doing. This is the Upon (*um-, “upon; seen from below, beneath”), the one first mark that was already about over and under; the wood kept it as BENEATH, and v2 makes it the grammar of every crossing.

{{tab g_pass}}

A break is declared in the notation (lap e1 ⊳ f1) and crosses square, exactly once. A pass is never declared; it happens wherever two routes cross, and it carries its fixed reading. Every crossing is at 15° or more. In the tellings: IV.6’s “we turned against the current” is our turning breaking the current’s taking; V.4’s “the vote was carried by the weight of anger and grief” is the young’s runner breaking the elders’ plea; IV.4’s “the host drew back … the blade … went deep” is the drawing-back breaking the rite’s shallow cut.

Braids. Two or three runners that share a stretch of one cord and plait along it hold one relation by turns, and the over-strand at each crossing is whose turn it is. A braid has exactly as many crossings as its pattern is long, and it is short, open at both ends, and runs along one ring.

{{tab g_braid}}

Binds: knots where meanings bind. Two runners meet head-on, and each hooks through the other: the two relations are bound into one, and each holds only with the other. “Harm to one is harm to both.” “One life, in two kinds.” A promise; a rite’s joining. A bind may send out one runner of role then or to, what the bound pair together does. Three strands chain as two locks, never a three-fold rotational figure. A bind is grammar and never a sign: BOND (ael) is the word, two cuts that grow into one, and AELTHAR is the rite’s name-sign. 9.8 · Pockets: a knowing inside a knowing

A pocket is a lens-shaped hollow in a year, whose lines part round it, holding a knowing that another knowing contains: words said, sung, asked or believed, or a carving cited whole. It is real anatomy, a resin pocket or a bark pocket between the rings, round which the grain splits and rejoins. It is the grain’s ring-split.

{{tab g_pockets}}

A pocket spans at most six files, takes its year’s whole mark band, and is 8 units deeper; the year’s lines bow away from it. It has one mouth, a small gap at the tip facing its governing runner, and exactly one that runner ends just inside it. It holds up to eight items, cut with the finer knife in its own reading order, and it may hold one pocket more. No runner passes through a pocket. When the cited knowing is itself a whole round (IV.4’s ring 15 cites the gift’s sentence), the pocket holds that round’s root at half size, and the Book draws the cited round beside it.

9.9 · The 47 new signs

A new sign was added only where a concept of the eight wood leaves needed a Seilrhass root. Everything else is a ligature, exactly as Seilrhass builds its compounds: timber is AXE+WOOD, the council SILVERBARK×3, to learn NEW+HOLD, to believe TRUE+HOLD (“hold true”), to hope MORROW+HOLD, a guest BOND+US, a host DOOR+STONEFOLK, a lie SQUARE+CARVE (“a cut that carries nothing”), war STRIKE×3. Parts come from the part device. Every new sign grows from the First Tongue’s marks by the wood’s laws (§2.6), and most belong to a family grown from a v1 sign, so a reader who knows the head of the family can guess its members: the MOUTH family (MOUTH, HEAR, SING, CRY, LAUGH, EAT); the EYE family (EYE, WEEP, FIND); the HAND family (HAND, TAKE, LOSE); the Cup family (HOLD, NAME, TEND, HOLY); the HEART family (HEART, ANGER, SHAME, GLAD); the Bed family (DEEP, EARTH, SAND, MOSS); the Star family (FLASH, SOFTLIGHT, MORNING, DUSK, SUMMER). Every soft reading is a Seilrhass word that already exists: 21 are in the lexicon, and 26 are reserve roots whose forms the sound laws computed. None is coined. In the tables, L is a lexicon word and R a reserve root; Chiral says whether the sign already differs from its mirror (one that does not carries the entry-nick).

9.10 · Composition rules v2

  1. One root per round, cut first, from the pith, along the axis, spanning the whole heart ring. In a telling, the root names what the telling is of: the Stone’s root is STONEFOLK, “it is of one of the stone-folk”, and the gift’s is STOP, the plea.
  2. One mark per cell, and a mark is one sign or a ligature of two, modifier inward, head outward. Ligature parts are cut at 0.7 breadth with the finer knife.
  3. Where things stand. An act is cut on the file of its doer; a thing on the file of the one it is, or belongs to. A runner goes from the act to what it governs. A belonging (a part, a person’s mind, word, name, breath, hand, eye, bones or blood) never introduces a new referent on its file; it belongs to the one already there.
  4. Conditions lie along the ring over files, in one year, and a mark inside a condition’s files and year takes the condition.
  5. Line quality classifies closed shapes only. Runners, fringe marks and terminals never classify.
  6. Runners follow their composition rules; a break must be declared; a pass is never declared.
  7. The root sets the axis. The seal and names stay in the bark. No runner enters the bark band.
  8. Properties of one mark combine: negation, hollow, smoothed, count, ordinal, question, half, span, part and the fringe (~!STRIKE, WOOD+!SMOOTH{all}, BEARER:keel{only}).
  9. Likeness. An as runner goes to its likeness; a likeness that is only an image, not present in the knowing, is cut hollow: “the trees breathed out the grey as sleepers breathe” is TREE×3+LIVE →as ~LIE.
  10. No small words are cut.
  11. Economy for tier 3. Every knowing of a wood leaf is carved, but only once: one mark per content word; a referent repeated in a knowing is pointed at, not re-cut; a clause shared by two knowings is carved in the first and reached from the second by a runner.
  12. Age gating and the canonical order bind every round.

9.11 · Carving a whole wood leaf

One great round per leaf. A wood leaf is one telling, known from one heart, so it is one round: its three movements are its three bands, parted by band-rules where the Book’s two RING markers stand; its paragraphs are its rings; inside each ring the paragraph’s knowings fill the years and cells, and runners knit them together; the formula “This is held in the grain” is the seal in the bark Jack.

{{tab g_layouts}}

From a leaf’s text to its round: split the leaf at its two RING markers into three bands, one ring per paragraph; give places and bearings the even files relative to the telling’s road (the shore ahead on 0, home and the grey behind on 8, the right hand on 4, the left on 12), and persons and things with no road the odd files; the teller takes its bearing’s file, or 12 by custom; for each sentence cut its content words on their referents’ files and join them with runners by role; pack; declare the breaks, braids and binds the knowings mean; validate. A router failure means the notation must change, never the rule. The sentence’s framing is Halyna’s, not the wood’s: “We do not know why” is the blind, and “for fear leaves no mark on wood” is its gloss.

9.14 · Notation, decoding, and the proof

Grain Notation v2 is the canonical linear form of a round, written in the order a round is read, and a strict superset of v1: every v1 text is a v2 text with the same meaning (a v1 tie is a v2 runner of role to or then), and one drawing has exactly one canonical notation, which draws that drawing up to seeded noise.

{{tab g_tokens}}
  • Decoding a v2 drawing adds three steps to v1’s: count years by the year-lines and the mark feet, and cells clockwise within a slot; trace each runner from its node, through its crossings (the continuous strand is over), to its terminal, and read its role from the terminal; read each pocket’s items in its own order. The drawing then gives back its notation.
  • The literal reading replaces each token with its gloss in the canonical order. It is a gloss, not the telling; Halyna’s English is the telling.
  • A validator is run before any export: signs and ligatures known, parts legal, age gating, one mark per cell, every runner’s terminal on its own target, breaks crossing exactly once, braids with exactly as many crossings as their pattern, pockets closed, the heart plain. It found and fixed the drafts’ own slips, and it is what the blind back-translation of IV.4 read from (§11.6).
