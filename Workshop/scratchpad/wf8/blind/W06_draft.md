## A. Seren's frame (Orrowen, the leaf-hand)

**[50] headnote.** As the Mystwood remembers: known from the heart of a Standard-Bearer of the Bonded Tide, from the grain we call Silverbark, which grew from a cutting of the silver elders of their council-grove; for the grove was their council-hall, and the grove heard the vote.

**[69]** Rhyna laid her hand on it at the shingle, and took it away again very quickly; and Halvard came all the way up out of the vault, and his face was wet.

## B. The wood's telling (the grain, V-4: long wood, war-pith, 15 rings, band-rules after rings 1 and 12, the seal in the bark)

Files as I read them: 0 the shore (bearing ahead); 2 the edge (a diagonal bearing); 3 the littlest young (r3), their mothers and fathers (r6), a young silverbark (r13); 5 the young (r6 on), then anger (r11-12) (?); 6 a likeness (hollow); 7 the eldest; 8 the silverbarks, the council-grove (home, behind), then the edge (r13) (?), the moss (r15); 9 the long-lived in council; 10 the old hidden road; 11 the sky, thin in the grey (r2 on), the grey between (r9); 12 we, the teller (a shoot of the silverbarks); 13 hard light (r2), the Aelvaren (r5), war (r11-12); 14 hulls (r10), every heart on the water (r15); 15 something that broke (r5), the short-minded (r11), many summers (r14).

This is held in the grain.

**Band I**

1. The root is a silverbark: this is the telling of a silverbark. The long-lived sat in council beneath the silverbarks. The silverbarks were our hall: no roof but the high boughs, no floor but the moss. The silverbarks held every word the council spoke, as a silverbark holds all it hears. From them came a shoot, which is we; and we give it as it truly was, for we do not turn aside.

**Band II**

2. All along the edge, the grey was thin. A few black pillars stood there with gaps between them, like the few teeth left in a jaw, and the grey went out through the gaps. After the soft between-light came the hard white light.
3. Every new summer the littlest young were thinner, and some never opened their eyes. The eldest of the long-lived were dying slowly, at half their long lives, their strength going out of them like water from a cracked jar.
4. The eldest spoke, and counselled waiting, as the trees wait: "The iron comes seldom now. The pillars do not grow; they wait. Wait with the pillars. When the iron stops, they will grow again, and grow the grey again." They spoke so because they were struck (wounded), and they were dying of it. They knew they were dying, and still they counselled waiting.
5. The Aelvaren came home, by the old hidden road. It had burned wholly; only its keel came back, and the Guest's bones in the keel. When it came, something broke.
6. The young had grown up while the grey was slowly dying. They watched their mothers and fathers die slowly in the open light, and could not help them. They saw the Guest come home, in ash. The young came into the grove and put a question to the council.
7. The eldest rose and asked: "Send again? Learn the shore-men's words. The shore-men do not know us; they do not know the wood; not once have we taught them. Go slowly." Their voices were few and faint, and they were dying.
8. The young said to the eldest: "We listened through three of their lives, and learned three words; and the shore-men did not hear them."
9. The grove heard the young and knew them half right. We could have learned more, for we live long. We listened to the shore across the grey between. We went once, alone, to the shore, with our hands open; the shore killed the Guest, and so we did not send again.
10. We did not ask whether the whole shore-people killed the Guest, or one house at the edge. We did not ask whether the shore-men, who live along the shore and in the ten havens, had ever heard our own name. We chose to grow more hulls, rather than learn their words.
11. War: for the short-minded, and for anger. (War is for the short-minded and for anger.) (?)
12. The council put the question, and then chose. The eldest still pleaded for waiting; but the council chose war, and the choosing broke their plea. A great anger and a grief bore on the choosing, by turns.

**Band III**

13. In the dark the council took the straightest of the young silverbarks, at the edge (of the grove), laid its heart open, and cut in its heart the first carving of the war: the carving whose root is *Grow* (the council's *Grow until the shore is silent*, cited by its root). The young silverbark still stands; it has not lost the carving; it grows.
14. The iron stopped coming to the edge in the summers after. The grove (?) did not wait for the iron to stop.
15. One after another, the eldest fell into the moss. The carving did not fall with them. It is in every heart on the water, beneath all their carvings, as the first ring (the first Tide) lies beneath all the rings.

…and the grain holds it still.

## C. Points I could not settle blind

- **[141]** *Nath noss ol drenn lo sy*: "this has no course of ours" (the "at" idiom, with *sy* as the haver), or "we have no course for this". Either way, the stone side has no telling to face this leaf.
- **[91]** *brodhol um Halyna*: the "upon" idiom is for a feeling; read as "the telling weighed on / occupied Halyna".
- **Ring 1, a7/a8**: GIFT →as TRUE, GIFT →bc !TURN on our file. "We give it true, because we do not turn aside" is the best I have.
- **Ring 4, d9**: the eldest's speaking →bc WOUND. The wood gives "because they were struck", not what struck them.
- **Ring 6, f7**: MOUTH? → @9 with no pocket, so the young's question is not given.
- **Ring 11**: STRIKE×3 →for {THIN+MIND, ANGER}. Nothing says *who* is angry. By §11.2, ANGER (a thing) standing first on file 5 names a new referent there, so the young's file becomes "anger", and in ring 12 "a great anger", with the grief as its own.
- **Ring 14, n3**: !KNOT on file 8. By §11.2, EDGE in ring 13 took file 8 ("the edge"), so the letter of the rule reads "the edge did not wait". I read it as the grove (file 8's ring-1 referent) because the edge cannot wait.
- **Ring 3, file 6**: the first mark in the ring is ~GO (an act), so by §11.2 file 6 is still ring 2's likeness (the teeth). The hollow and the runners still give "like water from a cracked jar".
