## I.5

**L32** *Vollol et Treskan* — The Naming of the Rivenmen (Treskan, "the folk of the cleft").

**L40 (headnote)** Laid by Corporal Voss, Voss Who Asked, on the eve of the going-down to the havens. He asked
the question in the first winter of the siege, and he waited every day of the war for his answer.

**L57** Give me the stone. I have owed this hearth an answer for a long time.

**L69** In the first winter I asked a question at this fire; few of you were here, and they heard it. I asked:
Why do we fight for stone and ice? I was cold, and I had buried a friend under a cairn on the eastern face,
because no spade would go into the ground; and I thought it a hard question. A mason said to me: It is not the
ice I fight for. That was a good answer, but it was his answer. This is my answer, and I have sworn it (? cut it)
every day of the war.

**L84** It was the week after the Rite of the Cornerstone. The Captain of the Torn Cloak held the staff one day,
and laid it down, as you know. In that week Halyna called the whole Keep to the courtyard, soldiers and guild and
the people of the column (? *lunt et trenn*) together, and they stood under the torn cloak, and one said and the
other finished:

**L95–97** The Commander (? "the Holder") fought. / The guild built.

**L107** That was the whole speech. It was enough. We looked at one another then, soldier and mason, and we saw
it: we were not two peoples sharing out a ruin (? or "that a ruin had split": the verb is 3du, so the two peoples
are its subject). We had become one, as two courses become one wall when the mortar sets. There was a people made
new in that courtyard, out of the war and the building both; and neither half alone could have stood.

**L121** Then we gave the Keep's name to the land. Rivenkeep, we said: the whole land, from the high ridge to the
farthest fallen harbour. Rivenkeep is the havens the grey sails hold. Rivenkeep is every road between them, and the
Keep's edge is the sea's edge. I say it this once, like this, and you will understand it ever after. When we say
Rivenkeep we mean this keep on the ridge, and we mean all our land; and that is not two names knotted into one.
It is a vow.

**L136** And we named ourselves Treskan, the people of the riven stone, wardens of the keep that did not fall.
When we speak of our fathers we call them Orrowan, because they were Orrowan; and ourselves we call: Us (? *vollar
ol sost: Ol*, literally "we name ourselves: we"). That is the custom of this hearth, and now you know where it
came from (lit. "where it stood up").

**L151** Then each one of us laid a hand on the foot of the pike where the banner flies, one by one, all through
that day until night; and we swore upon it: We will take back every stone. Not one stone that was not ours. But
every one that was.

**L164** The Elderesses did not love the new name, and they did not say it. You hear them still at the fire,
whispering: "Shore", when they think no ear is near. Once I asked an old woman why she did it; and she said: It is
a name to come home to, perhaps, when the last wall stands and the last Mystarch lies broken. I did not understand
her then. I think I understand now.

**L178** So: here is my answer, and you shall have the reckoning of it (? *doss o nydhyl los*, "its
count/list is at you"). Once, on the eastern wall, I said a thing that men still say back to me: We do not fight
because we are right. We fight because our children are behind these walls. It is true, and it is not the whole
of it. We do not fight for stone and ice. We fight because every stone out there is Rivenkeep, and Rivenkeep is
home; and we swore on the banner: We will bring it home. Go up the eastern tower on a bright morning and look
along the shore. You will see the nearest of our keeps, and the smoke of the next one beyond it; and beyond these
keeps, where the eye cannot reach, they stand one after another along the ten haven-lands, a crimson (blood)
pennant on each one. We built every one of them in this war, stone on stone, and the grey sails have attacked
every one of them, and every one still stands. But we do not wait in a keep until the end. We go out. Now a
Mystarch sits in each of the ten havens, on one of the great seats of the grey (? the phrase *hos hy reskowath
strom et myst* hangs in apposition; it may mean each haven is "one of the grey's thrones"), and he waits.

**L196** Tomorrow we go down to the havens.

**L202** This I lay as it was laid for me.

**L210** *And the hearth said:* **We remember.**

## II.1

**L236** *Kethow sa re yal nadhumat* — The Keep That Was Forgotten.

**L244 (headnote)** Laid by Halyna after the manner of the guild's old annals, in two inks, by turns, in the
first summer of the siege. It is the stone leaf of The Two Homes (? or "of the two homes"), and the facing leaf
belongs to the wood.

**L262** Hear the annal of the Keep, as the guild kept it, and as we did not.

**L273** In the age before the havens, when there was not yet a walled town on the eastern shores and no road
between the mountains and the sea, the first of the guild came up onto the high ridges. They found a place where
the ridge is split in two, as if a great sword had fallen on it; and in the cleft they built a keep of the ridge's
own grey stone. That was Rivenkeep, the first work of the first builders. They call Eldhythe the Eldest Haven, and
truly; but it is the eldest only among the havens, because the Keep was old when the first stone of Eldhythe had
not yet been quarried.

**L287** The quarry-wardens of the high ridge quarried its stone, and cut their names on the lintel of the western
stair, one beneath another, generation after generation, as long as the quarry was worked. Halvard Stone-Warden is
of their line. And Aldwena, a bonded pair of the first builders, laid the lintel of the inner gate with their own
four hands; and Rhyna of the Inner Gate is of their line. So, the gate you came in by tonight (? verbless: *Hy ull
et ganna sa…*, "from that, the gate that…"): his fathers quarried it, and her forebears laid it; and in it the two
lines became one, an age before the day Halvard and Rhyna were laid in one cradle.

**L302** Under that lintel, as under the foundation (? *rivull*: "foundation, the bottom course"; the canon puts the
Stonwryt under the capstone) of the great works of the guild, they cut the Stonwryt. It is a vow, sealed in the
bedrock, paid for with sweat and craft, a master's promise: The wall will stand. No man buys it. No one pays for it
but the hands that laid the wall; and it is cut where no one will see it again unless the wall falls.

**L317** In the later days, when the havens were built and had grown rich, the Orrowan named the coin after it. The
Stonwryt coin was half gold and half black stone, and no one struck it but the bonded master-pairs of the guild, in
workshops no stranger ever entered. So the markets of the havens and the temples stood on one footing: trust,
sealed in stone.

**L331** Then the people went down. The ridges were cold and the harbours warm, and trade came along the shores
and not over the mountains. The guild followed the people, because the people wanted temples and walls. Generation
after generation the Keep was let go, until it was more legend than memory: the keep that never fell, a name in the
songs, a place that never was, as the children of the havens believed.

**L344** No one kept it. No one of the guild climbed up to renew the pointing or to open the drains. The rain got
into the joints, and the frost came in behind the rain, and winter after winter the ice split whatever the rain had
loosened. It never fell. It was only let go.

**L358** When the havens fell one by one, the people who were still alive fled up along the mountain roads to the
one place that would hold, as the songs said. What they found was a shadow: broken gates, broken walls, a hall, and
its roof lying in the hearth. What no man could unbuild, the age had withered (lit. "dried"). Forgetting had done
what no foe could ever do (*gald* "build; do": literally "forgetting built a thing whose building was never at a
foe").

**L372** Halyna came in under the inner gate at dusk, with the column behind them, and found the lintel lying in the
gateway. It had fallen in the forgotten years, and no one heard it fall. It was whole. Halyna knelt, and turned it
over together, because it is a stone that needs two; and on the underside was the Stonwryt of the first builders,
as sharp as on the day it was cut, and beside it the tooth (marks) of the quarry-wardens' chisels.

**L386** The stone itself had kept its promise. It was we who did not keep ours.

**L397** Then Halyna quarried a new lintel for the gate, and when the Rite was complete they raised it and laid it
in its place, together. The old stone they brought into this hall, and laid it in the niche of the hearth-wall. It
is the stone you hold when you speak. Whoever holds it holds the vow of the first builders, and the shame of their
children.

**L411** This we (two) lay as it was laid for us.

**L421** *And the hearth said:* **We remember.**
