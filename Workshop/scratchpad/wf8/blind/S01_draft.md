## Of This Book

**[21]** *Flenn Seren*: Seren's Leaf (the foreword; the phrase row calls it "Of This Book").

**[29]** Written by Seren Two-Inks, prentice to Halyna and the third of their hearth, at the front of this Book, when half of the Book was already filled.

**[40]** This is the Book of the Riven Stone. It holds the tales that we lay at the Long Hearth in Rivenkeep, in the hall behind the inner gate, under the wall where the torn cloak flies. We lay them in the hours after an assault, when the guns grow cold and the mortar has not yet set, and there is nothing yet for the hands but to hold them to the fire [?] (lit. "nothing is yet at the hands but their holding at the fire").

**[53]** We did not make the rite. The first builders of the Keep made it, and we forgot it, and we found the rite again at the bottom of a chest in the deepest vault, as you will see. When their stone was laid and their tools were cleaned, the first builders sat at this hearth, and every night one of them laid a tale. We build as they built. The wall is rising again, and this Book is the same.

**[64]** Beside the hearth, in a niche of the hearth-wall, rests the old lintel of the inner gate. It fell in the forgotten years and lay in the gate until the day Halyna turned it over [?] (*kyl* "change, turn"). Halyna are the Elder and the Elderess of the guild in this Keep. They are of the Aetherbonded, whom the guild calls only "the Bonded"; and the two of them hold one name, Halyna; one by one, they are Halvard and Rhyna. Halvard's fathers quarried that stone, and Rhyna's forebears laid it, and the first builders' Stonwryt is still sharp on its underside. Whoever holds it tells. When the tale is complete, the teller lays it in its niche again, and we say: The tale is laid. For a tale that we do not lay but only tell is dead by morning, and a tale that we lay is a part (half) of the wall.

**[80]** *(the Hal, read with `--hal`)* THIS WALL WILL STAND. WE TWO LAID IT AND WE TWO ANSWERED IT, WHILE STONE IS STONE. IT STANDS. ⟨gate⟩ ⟨ALD | BEN under one lintel: the pair-mark of Aldwena⟩

**[86]** *(the same, in the living tongue)* This wall will stand. We two laid it, and we two answered it, while stone is stone. It stands.

**[90]** When our tale is laid, the hearth answers it, together: *Tumar*, We remember. The answer comes from the first words of the Captain's Title, and it is the one thing at this hearth that we say the same way every time.

**[99]** Not every tale in this Book is ours. Some are the wood's. They come from the wreck-wood that the tide brings under our wall, and from the black stone that rests in the chest I carried up the mountain, and from other wood. We do not read the wood, because there is no word in it. Halyna touch it, and the two of them hold its whole course, as we hold a memory: its beginning, its heart and its end, all at once. It is enough for one of them to touch it, because what one holds, the other holds. The holding is not the hard thing. The telling is. Words can turn, and a holding cannot; so they tell it in the fewest and truest words they can find, and they set every word like a marker-stone where the holding lies. The wood's tales are not told with the stone [?] (lit. "are not told at the stone"). When a telling of the wood stops, the hearth does not answer, because it does not know how. I have written that silence every time, as it was.

**[113]** I do not hold the wood myself. I am not one of the Bonded: I am unbonded, the guild's word for me [?]; and when I went up the mountain, Halyna brought me into their hearth, as the hearth's third. I laid my hand on the wood once, and let go, because something came into me greater than my holding. So I write what Halyna tell. Every carver of the guild before me was bonded. Why does an unbonded one write this Book? Halyna tell that in its own place, to the children.

**[124]** Halyna wanted the tales of the wood in this Book. "A tale told from one face of a wall," one of them said, and the other finished it: "half a tale." So, where the two faces have seen one thing, we lay the two tellings facing each other: our tale on the left hand, and the wood's tale on the right. Where Halyna could not yet tell the wood's leaf, I have left the leaf blank, and there is only the grain on it. Whoever opens this Book may look into the face of a thing we have not yet understood, and wait at our side.

**[137]** When Halyna tell, they carry the tale by turns, a line each, because each knows the tales as the other knows them, and neither waits on the other. I have written in two inks, one ink for each, and they do not want it marked which ink is the one's and which the other's. When they tell one line between them, one at its first stone and the other at its lintel, that is in the two inks as well.

**[149]** The Captain does not lay a tale. He sits at the end of the bench with his hood up, and hears the telling of his own deeds, and says nothing but: It was not like that. On the wall and in the orders, he is the Commander, because the Rite named him so. At this hearth he is still the Captain, as he wishes.

**[160]** When we speak of our fathers, we call them Orrowan, because they were Orrowan; and ourselves we call simply: us. The people behind the mist we call Mystaeri, the mist-folk. That is our tongue's word for them, and not their own.

**[170]** Where a carving was broken, or Halyna could not tell the holding, I have said so, and I have not mended it with guesses. Where the tales do not agree, I have kept both.

**[179]** This Book is ink. Halyna mean to cut it in the granite of this hall, when there is peace enough for the cutting. Until that day we keep it in the chest I brought, among the mute stones.

## The Invocation

**[195]** *Ammelyl*: The Invocation.

**[199]** Spoken every night at the Long Hearth, before the first tale, and laid here before the first tale of the Book.

**[211]** In memory of our home, our families, our God, our freedoms, our peace.

**[217]** Sit, if there is room, and stand if there is not. The guns are cold, and the mortar will not set before night [?] (*ul lern vess* "before night", or "against the night").

**[227]** Here is the hearth, and here is the stone that fell from the inner gate. Whoever holds it tells.

**[235]** We tell these tales because we forgot once. Our fathers forgot a keep, and it broke. Their fathers forgot a wrong, and it came back to us under grey sails. So we tell everything: the building and the breaking, and what we built as well as what was built before us.

**[246]** Some of these tales are ours: courses of true cutting, like the stonework of the wall. Some are the wood's, and their telling is hard, and they are slow. Where the two do not agree, we have kept both.

**[256]** Hold the stone. Lay the tale. When it is laid, the hearth will answer.

**[262]** And the hearth said: *Tumar* (We remember).

## I.2 The Cry of the Bonded

**[274]** *Carm et Lodhan*: The Cry of the Bonded.

**[282]** Laid by Seren Two-Inks, prentice to Halyna and writer (carver) of this Book, on the night after the first assault the Rivenmen withstood (held). She was a child of the guild on the day she tells of, and sat in the courtyard among the others.

**[294]** "Hold the stone," Halyna said to me, when I was small and afraid of telling at this hearth. "Hold it, and it will hold you." So I hold it.

**[304]** I was in the courtyard that day among the guild, because I had been called to the guild early, and I had no craft yet but carrying water. We sat in the old courtyard, which had grassed over, a few hundred of us [?] (*merr lesk murr*), masters and prentices, and the Bonded beside us. I had come through the passes at the side of a guild boy, and I came into that courtyard alone. We had come from ten havens. We had seen each of them die, and we had gone away from them one by one, with the chisels in our packs, and none of us had lifted a hand, because the creed forbade it. Build; do not destroy. Guard; do not attack. No one of the guild ever carried a sword. We had no arms but mortar and chisel, and they had not been enough.

**[321]** The faith that had stood by us [?] (*sa re ston ol*) through twelve generations was still upon us, but it sat on us like a wet cloak. I think every one of the elders in that courtyard was turning over one hard thing, and none said it aloud: walls that we build for peace must sometimes be guarded with blood. And if that was so, who gives the blood? And who lifts the hand?

**[332]** Then the Captain came down from the wall. I had seen the cloak go up, and heard the soldiers cry out, and I did not know what it was to us. He went into the middle of the courtyard. He was not tall. There was coal-black on the Captain's fingers. He looked at the elders, and at the broken gate behind, and he asked one question. This is the only speech of his that is said again at this hearth:

**[342]** "Your walls are falling. Will you build, or will you watch and die?"

**[351]** No one answered him. I remember the wind in the grass.

**[357]** Then Halyna rose. You know them by that name now. The guild gave it to them at their sealing, as it always gives one to a bonded gate, and we call them by it. The guild calls the Aetherbonded nothing but "the Bonded", and Halyna are the Bonded of the Keep. I did not know them then, except as the Elder and Elderess who sat apart and spoke to no one, because they had no need to. I will name them one by one, this once only, because the tale needs it. She was Elderess Rhyna, Rhyna of the Inner Gate: she had bonded forebears, who laid the lintel of that very gate in the age before the havens. He was Elder Halvard, Halvard Stone-Warden, of the quarry-wardens of the high ridge: Halvard's fathers quarried that very stone. They were husband and wife, as the Bonded are, and they had been one since the cradle. They looked up at the torn cloak, and they bowed to it in the one breath, because the Bonded do not wait on each other. Then the two chisels rose as one, and caught the last light.

**[373]** The cry came from both, and it was one cry. She began it:

**[383]** "The mortar cries from the ground. Rise, Stonewrights! Lay stone upon stone. Stand, every wall, until the very end [?] (or "make every wall stand to the very end"),"

**[387]** and he ended the cry, his chisel beside her chisel:

**[395]** "Rise! Rise! Rise!"

**[399]** Some tell it that she called and he answered, as if there were two calls and a silence between them. There was no silence. It was one call in two throats, and she was not first, except that a call must begin somewhere.

**[410]** Then the whole guild rose, and I among them, but I do not remember my rising. We took up the cry, and the soldiers on the wall took it from us, until they saw the very walls of the Keep tremble [?] (*dem et fedh sa* "until the time that"). An old stone does not shake. I felt it shaking under my feet.

**[420]** The terms were made that night, in the ruin of this hall, and Halyna made them. The guild does not carry arms: it never did, and it never will, and the Captain did not ask it to. But the guild will build between the volleys, and mend a breach while the guns are still roaring, with a speed and precision that only the Aetherbonded had, because a bonded gate builds as one body with four hands. One of them said the first half to the Captain, and the other the second half, and I cannot tell which was which. It has been the guild's word since then: Not the sword, but the stone. Not the killing, but the holding.

**[434]** So the siege-day was shared, and this hearth has names for its hours. There is the Laying, when the guild builds and mends. There is the Hauling, when the guns are hauled to the place the Captain wants them. There is the Holding, when the grey sails come in and there must be standing on the wall, and every battery fights under one of the Captain's True Men. Every gun hauled, a course unlaid, because the same hands are on the ropes; and the Captain knows it, and he moves a gun as a careful man spends silver. And if you see a Stonewright on the wall in the Holding, look again. You will see two.

**[447]** We sang on that first night by torchlight in the cleft of the inner gate while we laid, one voice to the comma and the other voice the rest, as Halyna had taught us:

**[458–461]**
*Stone upon stone, and the stone keeps faith;*
*hand upon hand, and the hand holds true* [?] (*keth et garl venn*: or "holds the right hand");
*two at the gate, when the grey sails gather,*
*two at the gate, and the gate stands whole.*

**[482]** This I lay as it was laid for me.

**[488]** And the hearth said: *Tumar* (We remember).
