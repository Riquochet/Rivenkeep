## II.3

**L45 Title.** *The Bonded and the Theoliths.*

**L53 Headnote.** Halyna laid this for the children of the hearth, in the first year of the siege, by turns, a paragraph each, as they always tell it to children. In this telling alone they let me mark who spoke each paragraph, because the children love to know. They told it to me to its end [lit. "told its end upon me"; ? "laid its purpose on me"], and I wrote it as they said it.

**L69 Rhyna.** Come near, every child, and sit where you can see. This tale is for the children, and the elders may listen, so long as they are quiet.

**L79 Halvard.** Not every Shorelander was a Stonewright, but every Stonewright was a Shorelander. That is the first thing; hold on to it, and do not let it go. No one ever came into the guild by birth, as one comes into a house. The guild named children in their cradles, one from this family and one from that, all over the Shore: many from Stonewright blood, that is true; but a few from fishers' huts, and a few from traders' halls; and once, the annals say, from the house of a gaoler in Carnhold. The naming was a great honour, and in the guild's annals no mother or father ever refused it. For the guild kept the faith of the Shore as it kept the walls of the Shore, and taught it in the temples it built; and a named child was theirs, a named child was both's [? verbless: "child named until-them, child named until-both": the child belonged to the guild and to the family both].

**L94 Rhyna.** The guild's seers went among the cradles and read the children. The guild held, and holds still: God bonded every pair before they had yet drawn breath; and the seers did not choose, but read what was already written. When a seer read two names together, the two children were laid in one cradle; and the two families raised them by turns, in one house and then in the other. The guild calls that bonding the Aetherbond (*Lodh Helv*); and the two children it bonds, the Aetherbonded (*Lodhan Helv*), or simply the Bonded; and so we will call them. So it was with Halvard and Rhyna, who never slept one night in two houses, in their whole life. And mark this, because you will need it later: the bond is chosen in the cradle, but the sealing alone makes it whole.

**L110 Halvard.** When the bonded children were old enough to hold a chisel, they were given to the Theoliths. The Theoliths were bonded, two by two, great masters of the guild; the guild called them from their works for a season, and they taught. They taught the children cutting, and laying, and setting, and prayer, and keeping the reckoning of the stones. And they taught them the vow. The Theoliths called it the Covenant of Completion (*Ryt Dhunnoth*); and they said nothing more about it to children; and we two say nothing more about it. You will understand it when it is yours. Idrenna were our Theoliths, who are sitting at the door tonight, and they will nod when we tell it right.

**L126 Rhyna.** When bonded children come of age, they are sealed; and the sealing says aloud what the cradle said first, and nothing else. The guild calls it the sealing (the laying of the lintel); and from that day the bond is whole. At the sealing the guild gives them one name, built from both, as a gate is built from two posts and from a stone laid upon them; and by that name they are called ever after, but the cradle-names are kept. So Halvard and Rhyna were sealed and named Halyna; and by that name you know us. So Halvard is Rhyna's husband, and Rhyna is Halvard's wife; and from the cradle they were one gate, and only from the sealing husband and wife.

**L138 Halvard.** The Bonded breathed as one, and built as one, and prayed as one; and they built without a word. You have seen Halyna on the wall at their watch. You saw it: the one does not call to the other. The work of such a pair is not twice as much, but twelvefold, as the old masters reckoned it; and the work of such pairs was every great wall and every temple that soars [? *dhenth* "fly, soar"] in the ten havens. The guild calls the man of a pair Elder (*Hemm*), and the woman of a pair Elderess (*Hemma*); and the two names are one course [one rank], because the Elderess has stood as whole [as the Elder]. So Halvard is an Elder, and Rhyna an Elderess; and neither he nor she is elder than the other. She has never laid the second stone.

**L152 Rhyna.** What the one feels, the other feels. When Rhyna cuts her hand in the south tower, Halvard feels it on the north wall, and knows which hand. What the one thinks, the other has already thought. Two that are one: that is all the bond is. The old masters had a saying about it, and you will hear it again in these tales. Harm to one is harm to both.

**L165 Halvard.** The Bonded are seldom apart, and never far apart. No bond has ever been broken. It is not in the annals, not once, in all the guild's generations. Separation God did not permit.

**L174 Rhyna.** And so the Bonded die together. We will not tell you anything deeper than that tonight; and we tell this plainly, because it lays no grief on us. When one of the Bonded is killed, by a fall or by fever or by the sea, the other does not breathe long after. The one who still breathes has no wound, and no sickness. The heart breaks, because it knows; and the body rests, because its work is built whole; and the two die in peace. When the left hand dies, does not the right hand die? Men say: eight days, and no one has ever lived alone longer than that; but the guild says: it was hours; and it knows, because the guild keeps the reckoning. Many of the Bonded have drawn their last breath in the same moment, a mile apart; and each knew the other's going, and followed.

**L188 Halvard.** Now the last thing, and then to bed. Sometimes one of a cradle dies young, when the two are not yet sealed; and then the other does not follow, but lives, because the bond was not yet whole. The guild calls such a child unbonded. They are few; but since the Fall they are not as few as they were, because the long march killed children. By rite, a bonded pair takes an unbonded child to their hearth, as the hearth's third; and the child is theirs. The unbonded keep one name until death, because there is no sealing to give them another; and among the guild one name is a mourning. The old masters said: God does not take the one except when He leaves work for the other.

**L201 Rhyna.** Our third is Seren, who is writing this. She and Tarnel were laid in one cradle: Tarnel, a fisher's son in Tidesmeet, whom the guild named. He died in the passes on the long march, of the cold, in the night; and she woke at his shoulder. After that she lived on, and she is one; and when she came up the mountain, and he was not with her, we took her to our hearth.

**L213 Halvard.** She cannot know the wood as we can. One mind does not hold the knowing; a pair holds it in their bond, as two hands hold a stone that one hand cannot raise. So she writes what we say, and writes it truer than we do. That is her loss, and it is not small. But hear what it gives her. She will not die of another's death. She can go, alone, where we cannot go; and she can carry this Book on when we die. The Bonded have always kept the hearth and the page; and every scribe of the guild has been bonded, until her. When a pair went, their Book went down into the vault after them [lit. "in their back"], and no one read from it again; for a Book that no one speaks is only ink. This Book needs the breath of its tellers [lit. "the need of the life of its tellers is upon this Book"; ? "needs living tellers"]. That is why it is Seren who writes it.

**L230.** *And they said this last part together, as they always end it:*

**L238.** Birth bonded them; death could not part them.

**L248.** We two lay it as it was laid for us.

**L256.** *And the hearth said: We remember.*

## IV.3

**L268 Title.** *The Warden's Door* (lit. "door of the warden").

**L276 Headnote.** The confession [*tessyl*, "an answer; an answering for a deed"] that Brenn of the Outpost spoke, the man who picked up the stone, as a bonded pair of the guild in Eldhythe set it down from his mouth, in his old age; and they sealed it in the chest of the archive, with the mute stones. Halyna read it aloud at the hearth, at the Tide of Remembering, by turns, a paragraph each; and they set the annal after it. Brenn's words come before the annal, and I have not altered them. The wood's leaf faces this leaf.

**L294 (Halyna).** We keep the stone, but these words are not ours. These words are Brenn's. Brenn kept watch at the outermost outpost in his youth; and forty years after, he came to the temple in Eldhythe and asked the guild for a hearing. We read them as they were written.

**L305 (Brenn).** I was at the outpost at the end of the land, where the rock runs into the sea, out to the Grey. I was nineteen. We were twelve, and the warden.

**L314.** It was a poor outpost, but it did not look poor. The warden had put black wood all through the outpost: in the posts and the lintels, in the walls of the hall, in the table and the chair and the frame of the bed. That was a rich man's vanity; and he was a rich man's son, and not much older than I.

**L325.** He held the outpost by his father's name in the Trade Council, and not by his own name. They sent him out to the edge because he was unwelcome in the towns for his heart [? "his temper"]; and at the edge it fell on no one but us. He had never known battle. He had never known patience.

**L336.** The stranger came in the morning, in a small grey boat, out of the Grey. There was no wind, but the boat came in. It beached on our shingle, and he walked up to the warden's door. He was tall and pale, clad in woven mist-cloth that moved when he did not. I saw no sword on him.

**L347.** He spoke first in his own tongue, sharp syllables that wheeled like a storm-wind through old branches. We were afraid, because it was loud; and we did not know that that is simply how they speak. Then he said three Orrowen words. He said them carefully, like a man setting down something that might break.

**L359.** Stop… Sky… Death…

**L365.** They were true words, truly spoken. I know it now. But words that carry no tale are only sounds, and we heard nothing but sounds.

**L374.** Then he knelt on the threshold of the warden's door, and laid before him a strange black stone, as big as two hands. He laid it as you would lay bread before a guest.

**L385.** The warden kicked it aside. It went into the dust by the wall. And the warden's men laughed. I laughed. I have never laughed since that day.

**L396.** The stranger rose. He laid his right hand on the back of the warden's neck, gently, as my mother did for me when I had a fever; and he drew their heads together until their foreheads touched, in silence. The warden was so astonished that he let him. I think: if he had stopped there, our lives would have gone otherwise. Then the stranger took a small knife in his left hand and touched it to the warden's right arm.

**L408.** The warden fell back in terror. He had never been hurt [? *lo fedh* read "at him, ever"; or "there was no wound at the time"]. Because he tore himself away from the stranger, the cut that was meant to be shallow went deep; and the blood came, and he screamed. He screamed at us: Seize him! We seized him. He screamed for the killing blow. The warden's men obeyed.

**L420.** We obeyed.

**L426.** The stranger did not fight. I think now: it was another cut he meant, on his own arm. He never made it.

**L435.** Then, at the warden's order, we laid the body in his boat and set the boat on fire, because he wanted nothing left that could show the way. The wood of that boat did not burn like wood. The fire leapt higher than true flame, as if the wood itself were screaming. We pushed it off with poles, and the tide carried it away, and we heard it on the water after we could bear to watch no longer [lit. "since we lost the patience of sight"; ? "after we lost sight of it"].

**L445.** The warden called for a clerk, and the clerk wrote to the Council that same day. I was at his shoulder while it was written, and I can say it by heart:

**L455.** Stranger unknown. Intent hostile. Stopped. Vessel burned and loosed.

**L465.** There is no word of the gift. There is no word of the kneeling.

**L473.** The warden wore long sleeves until his death, through the hot summers in Eldhythe; and I know why.

**L482.** I picked the stone up out of the dust by the wall the next morning, when no one was looking. I do not know why I did it. I kept it forty years. I give it now, with these words, and I ask God's forgiveness, because I have no one to plead to but God.

**L494.** Here end Brenn's words. Now hear the annal.

**L502 (annal).** The warden's name is not kept. The guild of Eldhythe cut it from the rolls, and it will never be in our Book. On his right arm was the only mark the rite of the Kneeling Guest ever made on a Shoreland body: half of a holy thing, torn through the middle.

**L515.** The pair who heard it sealed the black stone and the confession in the guild's archive chest, with the mute stones; and there they lay unread. When the havens fell and the guild went up the mountain, the chest was given to the youngest prentice on the march, because there was nothing of value in it. The youngest prentice was Seren, who is writing this. She carried it every step, and from the passes on she carried it alone.

**L527.** There is no older law than this among the peoples: a man who kneels at your door is under your roof. We were a people of doors.

**L537.** We two lay it as it was laid for us.

**L545.** *And the hearth said: We remember.*
