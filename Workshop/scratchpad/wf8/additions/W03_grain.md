**Corrections to the concept register** (`coverage/concepts.tsv`, and §18), found by carving the whole leaf:
1. **"bank", "as roots hold a bank"**: `→as ~ROOT+HOLD → ~EARTH` reads "seem to remember", because `ROOT+HOLD` is the compound *ralthein*, remember (§21.3, A14). Cut `HOLD →as ~ROOT`, `~ROOT → ~EARTH`: the likeness takes the verb from its source, as §10.9's `→as ~LIE` does.
2. **"the last shoot of a stump cut low"**: `NEW:stump+BENEATH` should be `BENEATH+NEW:stump` (above).
3. **"it came again; we put up more"** (§18 and B12): ring 6's braid is `aba`, not `abab`: the leaf gives three turns, the iron's, ours, the iron's ("The iron came back for the young ones. We put up more. It came again."), and a braid's pattern is one letter per crossing (§7.2).
4. **"tall and dark of bark"**: write `BARK` first in its knowing, so that the DARK band's year is the bark's (the IV.4 pilot's finding: a band's year is the year of its knowing's first mark).
5. **"grain" as the kind of a heart's wood** ("That is our grain, and you call it Blackthorn"): the register's `CARVE` for *grain* reads "you name our carving", which a decoder would take for a carving, not a kind of wood. IV.2 cuts `WOOD` on the teller's file, and NAME runs to it and to the said-pocket `{ NEW:stump[DARK] }` (Blackthorn). The register row for *grain* should say so for this sense; CARVE stays for "the shape of a knowing" (A15).
6. **A band lies over the whole of its file's year.** A band's year is its knowing's first mark's (the IV.4 finding), and every mark on its files in that year takes it, including marks of other knowings that the packing puts in the same year's other cells. Two cases in IV.2 had to be recut: the old men's white heads (`STONEFOLK:crown×3` under WHITE) would have whitened "summer after summer" in the same year, and a WATER band on the shore's file for "a word of yours across the water" would have put the shore-men's taking of the stones on the water too. The first became `DEEP+LIVE`; the second lays the band on the files **between** us and you (13–15), as §18 lays the MIST band "between us". **Proposed:** a validator note when a band covers a mark of another knowing. *(After the blind back-translation: the band over 13–15 covered the timber on file 14 in the same year, and the reading said "timber on the water"; it now lies on file 13 alone, an empty cell. The finding caught its own author, which is the case for the validator note.)*
7. **"mistake", "must not be mistaken"**: the register's `!~HOLD` ("no seeming knowing") is one drawing with `~!HOLD`, "a seeming not-holding" (A15: *would let go*), because the mirror of an outline is the same whichever prefix is written first. Cut `!TURN+HOLD` (A15 row above). The register's `~HOLD` for *mistake* itself stands.
8. **"did not know why they wept"** (§18, X): `WEEP →bc ∅` alone says that the wood does not know why. The Book says the young did not know. Add their own `!HOLD →in` the weeping (A15 row above).

**A proposed note for §6.4 (memory rays in a year of several cells).** `mem f: rK/Y → pith` names a file and a year, not a cell. Where the file holds more than one mark in that year, the ray leaves **the first cell's mark** (the first written in that knowing); an author writes the remembered mark first. IV.2 uses it four times (r3/2, r7/1, r10/1, r11/1); in r11/1 the axe stands alone on its file, in the other three it is the first cell. Without the rule two drawings (the ray from cell 1 or from cell 2) share one canonical GN.

**Findings of the blind back-translation** (`wf8/backtrans/W03.md`, 2026-09-28), for §21:

- **A band as a file's referent (A1, in a year).** A1 makes a band laid on an empty cell "the band as a thing", but a runner to `@f` goes to "that file's one" (§6.1), which §11.2 makes the first thing-sign standing on the file. IV.2 cut `EDGE` (the edge of the breath) first on the grey's file, so `→ @11` read "the edge", in "held the grey to the earth" and "you saw only grey". Fixed in the leaf by cutting the edge on file 14 and leaving file 11 to the band alone. **Proposed note for A1:** "In a year, a band laid on the empty cells of a file that holds no thing-sign in that ring is the band as a thing, and `→ @f` to that file goes to it; a thing-sign on the file makes the thing its referent instead, and the band only its condition."
- **Prefix order carries nothing.** `!~X` and `~!X` draw the same mark (a mirrored outline), so a negation cannot scope over a hollow, nor the reverse; both read "a seeming not-X". **Proposed** for A7: one canonical order for the prefixes, `~!` (as §3.7's `~!STRIKE` and A15's `~!HOLD` write it), and a validator note on `!~` that it is read as `~!`.
- **The blind is the wood's.** `→bc ∅` says the wood does not hold the cause; when the telling says that someone else did not know why, the grain cuts that one's `!HOLD` too (A15 row above).
- **Referents in the validator's reading without `# file` lines** (observed, not fixed: the validator is shared). Read comment-free, IV.2's teller (file 12) becomes "bark" from ring 3 (`12: BARK` stands first there), "no black pillar" from ring 10 (`12: !PILLAR`), "wood" in ring 12 and "the host (many hulls)" in ring 13; file 2 keeps "havens" into rings 7–8 (A16's rule for a CASTLE's stones), where the mute stones are meant. The blind reader kept "we" throughout, so none of this moved a score. **Proposed** for A16: a tree's `BARK` is a belonging (its skin); a mirrored thing-sign (`!PILLAR`, "not a pillar") is said of the referent and never introduces one.
