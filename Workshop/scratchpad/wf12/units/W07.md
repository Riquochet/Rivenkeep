## 1 · SEREN'S FRAME AND HALYNA'S READING, IN ORROWEN (THE LEAF-HAND)

### 1.1 The title

> **Tevyl**

V.6 · Of the Growing

- **Gloss:** growing (VN of *tev* "grow, sprout").
- The lexicon's title (O2530), as the wf8 unit has it: *tevyl* is *tev* "grow; a child" with the verbal noun *-Ol*, slender. A title's head takes no article (*Farrol et Aelvaren*, *Kaelir sa re vymm dem varn*). The Book sets the title as its heading, `### V.6 · Of the Growing`; the English above is that heading's words.
- **The word works twice.** *Tev* is the verb of growing and the word for a child, and *tevyl* is also "a birth": the leaf is the hulls' birth, the grown-not-built, and Seren names it with the word a hearth uses for its children.

### 1.2 The dateline

> **Et heth gemmest.**

*The fourth spring.*

- **Gloss:** the · spring · fourth.
- A naming, with no verb, as a date set at the head of a leaf is: the ordinal after its noun, as *et syst sullast* (IV.6) and *et grem sullast* (V.3). *Heth*, "spring, the lengthening" (O0962); *gemmest*, "fourth" (O0717, *gemm* "four" + the ordinal *-Ast*, slender).
- **The word works twice.** *Heth* is the spring, and it is also *keth* "hold; (of the wood) know" softened, as in *re heth*, "held": Seren's ear hears the season the heart was held in in the word for holding. She lets the headnote end its clause on it again (*dem heth*, "till spring").

### 1.3 The headnote

> **Re lymm Halvard et molt sy dem susk um et sest, eth amm re yal o umo tul, re rarr o ulonta pana, veth Rhyna lo et odh. Ew strom o hyonta, eth re sol o ul et nelter dem heth; re hadhom vell et molt ul vell, virr hosen es morr et luth.**

*Halvard bare this heart up on his shoulder, and ere he set it down it came to them both, though Rhyna sat at the hearth. Too great it was, and lay in the vault till spring; its song I set as verse, as near as ink will go.*

- **Gloss:** PST(+S) · bring · Halvard · the · heart · this · until(+N) · height · upon(+S) · the · shoulder, and · when · PST(+S) · S·be · it · upon-him · yet, PST(+S) · S·come · it · in-them-two · both, but · Rhyna · at(+S) · the · hearth. was · great · it · than-them-two, and · PST(+S) · rest · it · in(+N) · the · vault · until(+N) · spring; PST(+S) · S·lay-1SG · song · the · heart · in(+N) · song, near · as · FUT(+N) · N·go · the · ink.
- **Word for word:** *Halvard carried this heart up upon the shoulder, and when it was upon him yet, it came into the two of them, both, but Rhyna at the hearth. It was greater than the two of them, and it lay in the vault until spring; I laid the song of the heart in song, near, as the ink will go.*
- ***Re lymm Halvard et molt sy dem susk um et sest*** is the wf8 headnote's *re lymm Halvard o dem susk … um et sest*, with v3's object, "this heart" (*et molt sy*), and without the shingle, which v3 drops. *Lymm* is "bring; bear, carry", the verb of *lymmerd*, "a bearer": Halvard bore the heart as a bearer bears, and it is a bearer's heart. "On his shoulder" is *um et sest*, "on the shoulder": *um o sest* would set *o* before *s*, which does not soften, and read "upon it, shoulder" (§3.5); the article serves, as for a body's part.
- **"Ere he set it down" is "while it was yet upon him"**, *amm re yal o umo tul*, as the wf8 unit had it: Orrowen has no conjunction "before" (*ul lern* is "in front of", a place; *tevar* "early"), and the knowing came while he still carried it. *Umo*, "upon him", answers *um et sest*: upon the shoulder, upon him.
- ***Re rarr o ulonta pana***, "it came into them two, both", is the wf8 unit's, after IV.4's *re rarr o uloy* (the dual *ulonta*, and *pana* "both"). **"Though Rhyna sat at the hearth" is a clause with no verb**, *veth Rhyna lo et odh*, laid on with *veth* "but; though" (O2749), as V.3's headnote lays its own with *eth*: Seren's economy, four words for six.
- **"Too great it was" is "greater it was than they two"**, *ew strom o hyonta*: the copula with the predicate first (§3.4), and *hy* "than" (§3.6) in the dual, as the wf8 unit wrote it.
- ***Re sol o ul et nelter dem heth***: *sol* "rest, lie still", *nelter* "a vault", and *dem*, "until", which takes a noun (*dem sennyl*), so "till spring" is *dem heth* (*h* does not bed). The dateline's spring again, and the word again two words at once: till spring, till it was held.
- **"Its song I set as verse" is "I laid the heart's song in song"**, *re hadhom vell et molt ul vell*: *cadh* "lay" is the verb Seren sets a telling down with, here in her own first person, softened after *re*; *vell* is "a song; a rhyme; verse" (O2698), so the song is laid in song, one word for the thing and the form. "Its" is said with the noun, *vell et molt*, "the song of the heart" (a construct): *o vell* would set *o* before *v*, which does not soften, and read "it, song" (§3.5).
- **"As near as ink will go"** is *virr hosen es morr et luth*, "near, as the ink will go": the wf8 unit's *virr hosen lusk et luth* ("as the ink allows") with v3's verb, *orr* "go, walk" in the future (*es* + N, *o* > *mo-* before a broad vowel). *Luth* is the ink of her name, *Seren Pa Luth*.

**The grain.**

- **One ring to a paragraph, at most twenty-four** (§11.1–11.2, H05). v3's story has 25 paragraphs (5 / 14 / 6), one more than v1.3's 24; the Song is what its paragraph says the groves sang (*… and it is in our rings:*), so ring 23 holds both, as §11.3 first planned the Song. Every sentence is carved once (§10.11): one mark to a content word, a referent already cut pointed at (`@f`) or run to.
- **Kept from the wf8 round, where the meaning is the same** (`wf8/units/W07.md`, with its merge fix and its back-translation's mends): the root GROW and ring 1 whole; ring 2 (the memory ray, A9's `LIVE#1`, the long road); ring 3; the warriors' fine roots, their touch and the wood's hands, the winged seed (now ring 4); the crew (ring 5); the carving's *what and not whether*, its own grain and dread, *one mind, and many*; the grains' seven acts and the eighth (rings 13–14); *every council heeds the law*; the crudest, the council's want, the quickest growing, the angry hands, the edge of the sea, the children's children, the boats in the breath; *Burn* cited; the havens without walls, the chase into the mountains; the tide's pun; the carvers, the scattered souls, the last groves; *No voice among us can call us home*; the Song's seven pockets; the close.
