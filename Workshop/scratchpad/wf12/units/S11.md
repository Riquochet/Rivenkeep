## 1 · THE TITLE PAGE

> # *Trenneth Hethow Dhresk*

**THE LEGENDS OF RIVENKEEP**

Word for word: *The tales of Rivenkeep.* ("Legends of the holding-place of the cleft.")

- ***trenneth*** is the lexicon's own plural with a sense of its own (O2586): "the courses of a wall; the tales of a night". *trenn* is "a legend; an old tale; a tale laid in one night" and "a course of stones", so the Book's first word says at once *legends* and *courses*: the Book is a wall of tales, as the foreword says (*a tale that is laid is part of the wall*).
- ***Hethow Dhresk***: the canon *Kethow Dhresk*, Rivenkeep, softened as the possessor of a construct (*k* > *h*, §3.2), as the lexicon's *Ketherd Hethow Dhresk* (O2896) writes it; *Dhresk* after it settles the reading (alone, *hethow* could be heard as *heth* + *-ow*, lexicon §8.2). The head takes no article before a definite possessor. An addition (the title, O-S11-01).

> ## *Flennath Dholm Treskat*

*The Book of the Riven Stone*

- The lexicon's own name for the Book (O0618), "the Book of the Riven Stone"; *tolm* softened as the possessor, *treskat* "riven, torn", the word of the Torn Cloak and of Rivenkeep.

> *Et trenneth sa hadhar lo Odh Sell ul Gethow Dhresk, hosen re hadh Seren Pa Luth so.*

*The tales laid at the Long Hearth of Rivenkeep, as Seren Two-Inks set them down.*

Word for word: *The tales that we lay at the Long Hearth in Rivenkeep, as Seren Two-Inks laid them.*

- The first half is the foreword's own words (v1.3 unit S01: *Keth o et trenneth sa hadhar lo Odh Sell ul Gethow Dhresk*): the passive "laid" is the hearth's *we*, as Orrowen prefers a doer; *ul Gethow Dhresk*, "in Rivenkeep", and not the construct, for S01's reason.
- **"set them down" is *cadh* again**, *re hadh … so*: the hearth lays a tale, and Seren lays it a second time, on the leaf, as every headnote of hers says it (*Re hadh Seren Pa Luth*, "set down by Seren Two-Inks"). The English needs two verbs; the tongue has one, and the line turns on it.

**BOOK FIVE · THE BOOK OF THE TIDES**

> *Et gorrol: tesset sa re luskant et senneteth, eth sa re lymment et hosseneth, ul lern lunt sa re sesk nafedh: El et bynd ol.*

*The war: a doom set in motion by the dead, crewed by the living, against a people who had never known they were the foe.*

Word for word: *The war: a judgement that the dead loosed, and that the living bore, against a people that never knew: We are the foe.*

- ***tesset***, "a doom", is the Thrones' word: "a judgement (a verdict: 'a thing answered')" (O2513), the *Judgement* each Throne carved in the Book of Knowings. A doom is a judgement laid on a people (a new sense, O-S11-18). Book Five's war is, in the tongue, one Judgement before the Thrones carve theirs.
- "set in motion" is *lusk* "loose, let go, send off", the Captain's word for loosing guns (*Keth, ell lusk*): the dead loosed it, as V.4's wood says the war-criers *cut it loose*. "crewed" is *lymm* "bear, carry", the verb of the grain's bearer-hulls (*lymmerd*). The two relatives share their head, and their plural verbs (*luskant*, *lymment*) show *tesset* is what is loosed and borne.
- ***senneteth*** "the dead" and ***hosseneth*** "the living" are *sennet* "dead ('gone out')" and *hossen* "alive ('of breath')" made nouns by the plural (slender *-eth*); *hossen* is "of breath", and the Mystaeri's own name for themselves is *lunt et hoss*, "the people of the breath" (O2995): the living who crewed the doom are, in the tongue, the breathing.
- "who had never known they were the foe" is *sa re sesk nafedh: El et bynd ol*, "who never knew: We are the foe": what is known is set after a colon, as I.4's *nath re sesker: hosen sy brodhant so* (*sesk* is to know a fact), and here it is set in the knowers' own words, the thought they never had: the present *El* and the *ol* are theirs. *(Back-translation fix.)* It was *ew et bynd o*, "it was the foe" (*lunt* "a people" is singular, so *o*); but *o* is "he" and "it" both, and the blind reader could not tell whether the people was the foe, or the war, the line's first word. In the people's own *we* the foe can only be the people. And the people that never knew is the Shore, so the *we* is the hearth's too: the tellers of the Book say it of themselves, in the Argument of their own war. V.1's unit (S07), which gives the same head, now writes the same line.

### Seren's headnote

> *Cadhat ul sestow et flennath sy, rytet eth rytet, hosen re hethe {{Halyna}} gor hos hy et kaelirvesk eth hy et reskowath strom. El saed et flennath sa ross lo et scethan sost, hy vodh re ryt et mesk gor rytet ulo, eth nath re ryter hos.*

*Set down at the back of this Book, one carving at a time, as {{Halyna}} knew each from the wreck-wood and the Thrones. It is the fleet's own half of the Book, for the wood cut every carving in it, and we cut none.*

Word for word: *Laid at the back of this Book, carving by carving, as Halyna held each one from the wreck-wood and from the Thrones. It is the half of the Book that is at the fleet itself, for the wood cut every carving in it, and we did not cut one.*

- ***Cadhat***: "Set down" is a bare participle, as the English is (v3 names no one; v1.3's *Re hadh Seren Pa Luth* had her name). The participle of *cadh* stands first and unmutated.
- ***rytet eth rytet***, "carving by carving", for "one carving at a time": the distributive *X eth X* (O2862's *hos eth hos*, *trenn eth trenn*).
- ***re hethe {{Halyna}} gor hos***: to know the wood is to hold it (§3.7), the dual for the pair; *gor hos* "each one" (O0763). *et reskowath strom* "the Thrones" (the great seats), as the table's close has them.
- ***El saed et flennath sa ross lo et scethan sost*** is v1.3's, unchanged: the having idiom in a relative (*ross*, the softened *doss*, the v1.3 unit's S1).
- ***re ryt et mesk gor rytet ulo***, "the wood carved every carving in it": *ryt* "cut letters, carve" and *rytet* "a carving, a thing cut" are one word, so the line says what the wood did with the name of what it made. ***eth nath re ryter hos***: "we cut none" is the verb said again, with *nath*, as Orrowen says no.

### Before the table

> Gor rytet sa re hebb et mesk lor, ew hos stinel o dholm hosast; eth el o veskrivull et stinel hosast ull, hosen el rivull hald et trenn hosast. Doss noth meskrivull.

Every carving the wood gave us began with one stroke, and that first stroke is its root, as the first course is the root of a wall. Of roots there are ten.

Word for word: *Every carving that the wood gave us, one stroke was its first stone; and its root is that first stroke, as the first course is the foundation of a wall. There are ten roots.*

- **The word that works twice.** *meskrivull*, "a root", is "tree-foundation" (*mesk* + S·*rivull*, O1389), and *rivull* is "a foundation, the bottom course" (O2121). So v3's new simile, "as the first course is the root of a wall", is in the tongue the word for *root* taken apart: a carving's root is the wood's foundation course, and a wall's root is its *rivull*. The English says *root* twice; the Orrowen says *foundation* twice, once inside a word.
- ***ew hos stinel o dholm hosast*** is v1.3's "one stroke is its first stone" (to begin is to lay the first stone, *cadh et tolm hosast*, O2859), now in the past with the English (*began*). The carving is fronted and taken up by *o* (pilot I.1, G6). *el o veskrivull et stinel hosast ull*: the copula puts the predicate first, "its root [is] that first stroke".
- "Of roots there are ten" is *Doss noth meskrivull*, the existential, a numeral before a singular noun.

> Re rystullom lerneth et meskrivullath ul veskyl, lo et hylldhrenn eth ul et nelter, sell tevar hy veskyl galat ullen ul et meskdhrenn. Re vollom so, en sost, hy vodh lern navollat, omm o hy et garl hosen tolm thenth. Re vyskym gor voll sull fedh, eth hy ull re rytym o; eth re ston gor voll.

Their shapes I learned by eye, at the tide-line and in the vault, long ere I could make out anything else in the grain. The names are mine own, for a shape without a name slippeth out of the hand like a wet stone. Each I said three times ere I wrote it, and each held.

Word for word: *I learned the shapes of the roots in seeing, at the tide-line and in the vault, long before the seeing of another thing in the grain. I named them, I myself, for a shape unnamed, it falls out of the hand like a wet stone. I said every name three times, and then I wrote it; and every name stood.*

- "Their shapes": *so lerneth* after a verb would not show its possessive (*l* is unchanged, §3.5), so the roots are named, as v1.3 named them. ***ul veskyl***, "by eye" (v1.3's; *lo veskyl* is also "at tree-ing").
- ***lo et hylldhrenn***, "at the tide-line" (O1051), is "at the tide's course": the roots were learned on the line the tide lays, where the planks came in. ***ul et nelter***, "in the vault" (V.2's vault of planks).
- "long ere I could make out anything else in the grain" is ***sell tevar hy veskyl galat ullen ul et meskdhrenn***, "long before the seeing of another thing in the grain" (*sell tevar hy* X, O3005): the tongue has no "before" that opens a clause, and the verbal noun stands with no doer (*en veskyl* could not show its possessive).
- "The names are mine own" is ***Re vollom so, en sost***, "I named them, I myself" (v1.3's *en sost*).
- ***hy vodh lern navollat, omm o hy et garl hosen tolm thenth***: "a shape without a name" is *lern navollat*, "a shape unnamed" (*na-* + *vollat*); fronted and taken up by *o*. The tongue has no "slip"; a wet stone *falls* from a mason's hand (*omm*). ***lern* is "a face" before it is "a shape"**, so the saying is also "a face without a name falls out of the hand like a wet stone": Seren's proverb about the roots is one about memory too, and it is meant to be.
- "Each I said three times ere I wrote it" is said in order, ***Re vyskym gor voll sull fedh, eth hy ull re rytym o***, "I said every name three times, and then I wrote it" (*mysk* "say" softened after *re*; not *vesk* "see"). "each held" is ***re ston gor voll***, "every name stood": *ston* is "stand, hold firm" and the oath's last word (*Ston.*, "It stands"). A name said three times and then cut stands as a vow stands.

> Nath re ryt et mesk tevel veth et meskrivull. Re ryt gor hyll ul o sestow et meskrivull sost pawast, eth re hadh umo, hosen cadhant mesketh scaevur um scaevur. Hy ull, el et ryteteth hemm sost et ryteteth tevel sost, sa re dhevent hemm.

The young wood cut the root and naught more. Each Tide after cut the same root again, and added to it, as trees lay ring on ring. So the eldest carvings are but the youngest grown old.

Word for word: *The young wood did not cut but the root. Every Tide after it cut the very root again, and laid upon it, as trees lay ring upon ring. From that, the eldest carvings are the youngest carvings, which grew old.*

- v1.3's paragraph, with v3's three changes: *again* (*pawast*), "as trees lay ring on ring" (***mesketh scaevur um scaevur***, the piling *X um X* of *bystir um wystir* and *brod um wrod*; *cadh*, the mason's verb, for a ring as for a course), and "naught more" (*nath … veth*, "only" of a clause, G8).
- "are but" is carried by the copula's sameness: the eldest *are* the youngest. Two roots work twice in the last clause: *hemm* "old" (the eldest, and grown old), and *tev* "grow" inside *tevel* "young" (*sa re dhevent*, "which grew"): the young that grew, old.

> Hy et hald, ul et hylleth desir, re yal seskyl veskrivull lom ul scaevurol haelir. Re seskym nafedh mest hy et meskrivull, hy vodh et ullen, nath hebb et mesk o veth lo et Lodhan, eth doss maver o hethyl umont, eth nath vesk hos o.

From the wall, in the late Tides, I could tell a root in the time a wreck taketh to roll over. More than the root I knew never, for the rest the wood giveth only to the Bonded, and it must be known, not read.

Word for word: *From the wall, in the late Tides, the knowing of a root was at me in the rolling of a wreck. I knew never more than the root, for the rest, the wood does not give it but to the Bonded, and the need of its holding is upon them, and no one reads it.*

- v1.3's words, re-ordered as v3 orders them. "I could tell" is the "at" idiom (§3.7), its object the softened possessor of the verbal noun (*seskyl veskrivull*); "in the time a wreck taketh to roll over" is *ul scaevurol haelir*, v1.3's back-translation fix (the verbal noun, its doer softened).
- Three verbs of knowing, each in its place: *sesk*, Seren's knowing of a fact (a root by eye); *keth*, the Bonded's holding of the wood; *vesk*, reading. "it must be known, not read" is *doss maver o hethyl umont, eth nath vesk hos o*, the *must* idiom (O0488) and the verb said with *nath*.

> Lo sy nath noss veth et bisketh, hy et mesk tevel sost dem et mesk hemm sost. Et saedeth, cedh re dhovv um hedh eth amm vodh re haever et molt kylyl, re rytym so ul so reskowath. Lymment dhom rytet et mesk tevel vollath et hesperdeth: vollath dasorat lo yarlol, hosen hulter runn hemm.

Here are but the heads, from the youngest wood to the eldest. The parts, who waited on whom and when the heart meant to turn, I have written in their places. The seven of the young wood bear the soldiers' names, worn smooth with handling, like the haft of an old mallet.

Word for word: *Here there are not but the heads, from the youngest wood to the eldest wood. The parts, who waited upon whom and when the heart meant turning, I wrote them in their places. The seven carvings of the young wood bear the names of the soldiers: names polished by handling, like the staff of an old mallet.*

- "Here are but the heads" is ***Lo sy nath noss veth et bisketh***, the existential with *nath … veth* (*doss* beds to *noss*).
- "The parts …" is fronted and taken up by *so* (G6); its two clauses are v1.3's (*cedh re dhovv um hedh*, with *tovv um*, O2883; *re haever*, *kaever* softened). v3 dropped "what each hull was told", and so does the Orrowen.
- **The soldiers' names.** V.2 tells how the soldiers took the head of each young carving for its name; here they are ***vollath et hesperdeth***, set out again after a colon so that *dasorat* is the names' and not the soldiers'. ***dasorat*** is the mason's "polished, rubbed smooth; dressed (stone)", the word of the tenth root's name (*Stinel Dasorat*, the Smoothed Cut): the soldiers' mouths wore the names as the wood wore its last root. ***lo yarlol***, "by handling": *garlol* "using; handling" is the verbal noun of *garl* "a hand", softened after *lo* (*g* > *y*).
- "like the haft of an old mallet" is ***hosen hulter runn hemm***, "like the staff of an old mallet" (*hulter* "a staff, a rod"; *drunn* softened as the possessor, *dr-* > *r-*; an addition, *hulter drunn*, O-S11-15). *drunn* is the mason's mallet and also "a blow, a stroke": a haft worn smooth by strokes.

- ***rellorath kylet***, "curling signs", is S06's (IV.1's stones, *cut with curling letters*): a phrase of the lexicon now (O-S11-22), for O1131 gives *kylet* only as "changed", and S06's blind reader heard "strange signs", this unit's "turned signs". ***lo dhrenn et cless***, "along the edge" (*trenn* softened: "by the course of the edge"), is the stone's own rim, where *Plain Words* sets the letters (*the curling letters along its edge*). *(Back-translation fix.)* It was *rellorath kylet et cless*, "the curling signs of the edge"; but *cless* is "a bank" before it is "a raised edge", and *cless vyst* is a fog-bank, so the blind reader heard "the turned signs of the bank" and could not tell a stone's rim from the edge of the grey. Set on a path, *lo dhrenn*, the word is the stone's raised edge, as S06's *lo dhrenn et cless tunn* was read "along the whole edge". *Odh Sell* the canon's Long Hearth. *ul molt et rellorath* and not *ul molt so*, which before *sull* would read "their three words". The surface itself (the Mystaeri letters, and the three words among them by sound) is the builder's drawing and is not translated: "the rest of the letters is never read, for stone does not translate" (notes §5.6).

> *Hess… Helv… Sennyl…*

Seren writes them in her ink as she read them, by sound, from the stone: in the Orrowen they are not a translation but the words themselves, which the English could only gloss. *Sennyl* is *senn* "go out, die" with *-Ol*: the verb that put the Burning out (above).

Word for word: *At the feet of the black pillars they were cut, and taken away at one time with the timber, and sold. Three generations they hung above the fire of a harbour-house at Eldhythe. - *Merge (wf12):* "home on a spar" is now IV.3's *sa re vymment dem varn um vivenn* (S06; IV.5 has Daveth *re vymm … dem varn um vivenn*). The unit's play, given up for the one rendering: **one verb, two hangings.** *haemer* is "hang; float": the stones *re haemerent*, "hung", over the fire, and the axemen *sa re haemerent dem varn um stomornel*, "who floated home on a spar". The English has *hung* and *home on a spar*; the tongue has one verb in two sentences, and the stones that hang over the laughter float as the men did.

> Lo sy nath drunn hos mygur umont. Kaeverym: re veskym so. Myskym: kaeverym.

Here no pipe is knocked on them. I think I have read them. I say I think.

Word for word: *Here no one knocks a pipe upon them. I think: I have read them. I say: I think.*

- *nath drunn*: a bedded *d* before *r* stays *d* (§2.5); *hos* after a negative is "anyone".

And as Orrowen answers a question by saying its verb back, her last answer is a verb said back, *kaeverym*, "I think": not *Tumar*, which the hearth will say for her, but the nearest a careful woman comes to yes.

> Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

- The laying formula, canon (pilot I.1; Tier 3 §5.1), in Seren's singular.

> *Eth re vysk et odh:* **Tumar.**

### 3. Collisions the unit steps round

- ***ol brodath*** "our words" would bed *b* to *m*: *mr-* is no onset (§2.5). The shore's words are *et brodath orrowen*, as IV.1 has them.
- ***o gumm*** "his haven" softens to *o humm*, "her husband" (the v1.3 S04 note): "each for his own haven" is *cumm gor hos*, S05's.
- ***hy pa sest*** would be *hy va sest*, "from your side": "both sides" is *sest eth sest*.
- ***tolm myst*** is Myststone: the three stones are *tolm ryss myst*.
- ***so lerneth*** and ***en veskyl***: a possessive before a letter the mutation leaves alone does not show (§3.5); the roots are named again, and the verbal noun stands without a doer.
- ***et higileth so vygurath***: the analyzer reads a possessive pronoun after a definite noun as a construct with an article on its head; the ship-masters' line is S06's, without the possessive (*re runnant et higileth mygurath*).
- ***vysk*** (*mysk* "say" softened) and ***vesk*** "see, read" sit side by side in the Last Note; the vowels keep them apart (*re vyskym* "I said", *re veskym* "I read, I saw").
- ***ew et bynd o*** after a colon (Book Five's Argument): *o* is "he" and "it", and the line's first word is *Et gorrol*, the war; the blind reader left it open whether the people or the war was the foe. The known thing is said in the knowers' own words, *El et bynd ol*. *(Back-translation.)*
- ***cless*** is "a bank" before it is "a raised edge", and *cless vyst* is a fog-bank: *rellorath kylet et cless* was heard "the turned signs of the bank". A stone's edge is set on a path, *lo dhrenn et cless*, "along the edge". *(Back-translation.)*
- ***nalymm … lo sest*** X, "take away beside X", is heard "take away from beside X": "carried off with the timber" is *nalymmet ul hos fedh eth et mesk*, "taken away together with the timber". *(Back-translation.)*
- ***kylet*** alone is the lexicon's "changed" (O1131), and is read so: the Mystaeri's curling letters are the phrase *rellorath kylet* (O-S11-22). *(Back-translation.)*
