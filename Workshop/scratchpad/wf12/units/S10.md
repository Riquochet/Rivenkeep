## 1 · VI.3 · THE KNOWING OF THE GREEN WOOD

### 1.1 The title, the dateline and the headnote

**¶1** · `S10-VI3-01`

> ## *Kethyl et Mesk Lurrel*

**VI.3 · The Knowing of the Green Wood**

- The lexicon's title (O1125), as wf8 S10 had it: *kethyl*, "a holding; (of the wood) a knowing". To know the wood is to hold it.

**¶2** · `S10-VI3-02`

> *Et bremm vranast, amm re orr et sulter dem neld.*

*The sixth autumn, when the boat went out.*

Word for word: *The sixth autumn, when the boat went away.*

- *bremm* is "autumn, the gathering-in" (reserve); the ordinal follows its noun, as *et grem hosast*, "the first winter". "Went out" is *orr … dem neld*, "go away, far off" (the lexicon's *dem neld*): the boat went out and went far.

**¶3** · `S10-VI3-03`

> *Re hadh Seren Pa Luth, sa re heth et greller. Vess sy re yala {{Halyna}} lom lo et tolm, garl eth garl lo sest en narl; eth sell re hether o hosen ull, tevar hy wrodhol.*

*Laid by Seren Two-Inks, that held the lamp. Tonight {{Halyna}} stood with me at the stone, a hand each beside mine, and a long while we held it so ere any of us spake.*

Word for word: *Seren Two-Inks laid it, who held the lamp. Tonight Halyna were by me at the stone, a hand and a hand at the shoulder of my hand; and long we held it like that, before the speaking.*

- **The headnote is active**, *Re hadh* X (every stone headnote), with Seren her own subject: *Re hadh Seren Pa Luth, sa re heth et greller*. *keth* is "hold": she holds the lamp, the pair hold the stone and the wood, and later the fisher-girl holds the north lamp. One verb carries the leaf.
- ***garl eth garl***, "a hand each": the distributive X *eth* X (*hos eth hos*, O2862; *trenn eth trenn*, "a paragraph each"). The same two words come back when the pair put out a hand each to the sliver.
- "A long while we held it so ere any of us spake" is ***eth sell re hether o hosen ull, tevar hy wrodhol***, "and long we held it so, before the speaking": *sell* stands first, as in *Sell re vesk Tarnard umo* below, so the long while belongs to the holding. *Back-translation fix:* the draft had *re hether o hosen ull, sell tevar hy wrodhol* (the lexicon's *sell tevar hy* X, "long before"). The blind reader heard "we knew it so, long before the telling", a time long past and not a long silence. *dem brodhol* would bed *b* to *m* before *r* (§2.5); *re hether*, 1pl, because there are three hands on the stone.

### 1.2 Seren's telling (her ink; Halyna's split lines in their two inks)

**¶4** · `S10-VI3-04`

> Keth et tolm, Seren.

Hold the stone, Seren.

- Seren's own opening, bare, as on every leaf she lays herself (*Hold the stone, Seren.*): the canon's *Keth et tolm* with her name called (a vocative softens, and *s* does not change).

**¶5** · `S10-VI3-05`

> Lo dhrenn et orl ull re firr et reskow strom nothast ul vodh re omm o: domm o veskdhrenn, eth treskat lunnat, brunnel scethvesk susk hy dhavowlest. Ew {LAST_THRONE_AT_HAVEN}. Nath re orrant et hesperdeth virr, hy vodh re ludalant o, sennet sost.

All that day lay the tenth Throne where it fell, black of grain and split open, a hill of timber higher than a harbour-house. It was {LAST_THRONE_AT_HAVEN}. The soldiers would not go nigh it, for they feared it even dead.

Word for word: *Through the course of that day the tenth great seat lay where it fell: black its grain, and riven open, a hill of ship-timber higher than a harbour-house. It was {X}. The soldiers did not go near, for they feared it, dead even.*

- "All that day" is ***lo dhrenn et orl ull***, "by the course of that day", as the lexicon's *lo dhrenn et grem*, "all winter".
- "The tenth Throne" is *et reskow strom nothast*, the great seat of III.2's and VI.2's title words. ***treskat lunnat***, "riven, opened": *lunn* is both "open" and the *lunnath*, "freedoms", of the Title, so the felled Throne lies split open in the very word the Title frees.
- "A hill of timber" is ***brunnel scethvesk***, "a hill of ship-timber" (*brunnel*, "a small mountain"): plain *brunnel vesk* would be heard "a hill, see" (wf8 S10's trap with *saedel vesk*). "Higher than" is *susk hy*, and *hy* softens *tavowlest* to *dhavowlest*.
- **The token.** The v3 token gives the Throne's own name and its haven (the foreword: *where the wood speaketh, I have written its own names for things*), so the Orrowen gives the name as a loan and the place in Orrowen, with the subject pronoun after the name: *Ew* {LAST_THRONE_AT_HAVEN}*.* is *Ew Thaesaen o, ul davow Dhavow Hemm.* All ten are in §3, each run through the analyzer in this sentence.
- "For they feared it even dead" keeps wf8's *sennet sost*, "dead, even".

**¶6** · `S10-VI3-06`

> Re hethe {{Halyna}} rost molt hy et heth. Ew mesk sennet gor hos.

Nine hearts had {{Halyna}} known since the spring. Each was dead wood.

Word for word: *Halyna held nine hearts from the spring. Every one was dead wood.*

- **A word that works twice.** *re hethe* ("they two held, knew": *keth* softened) and *et heth* ("the spring") are one sound, the canon's own *keth*/*heth* homophony (lexicon §8.2). Seren lets the two stand side by side: the knowing and the season are said with one breath. After the article *heth* can only be the spring.
- A numeral stands before a singular noun (§3.1): *rost molt*, "nine heart".

**¶7** · `S10-VI3-07`

> Dem hedh re rarr et Crenn sost dem vimm, demo, eth re ston o sell virr, eth o wiskhovv dem susk. Hy ull re vesk o dem {{Halyna}}.

Toward evening came the Captain down to it himself, and stood long beside it with his hood up. Then he looked to {{Halyna}}.

Word for word: *Toward evening the Captain himself came down, to it, and he stood long near, and his hood up. Then he looked to Halyna.*

- ***eth o wiskhovv dem susk***, "and his hood up", is I.1's (pilot) and the foreword's (wf8 S01): the Captain's hood is the same three words on every leaf.
- "Stood" is ***ston***, the verb of a wall and of every oath's close: the Captain stands as the wall stands. ***Hy ull re vesk o dem Halyna*** is pilot I.1's *Re veskent et vennuldath dem so Hrenn*, "the True Men looked to their Captain", turned: now the Captain looks to Halyna.

**¶8** · `S10-VI3-08`

> Ul hedh re vellora sona, eth en ul sona sestow lo et greller. Ul susk re omm et nynth, semm eth semm. Ul meskdhrenn hemm sost re firr et molt, myst eth duss eth sol.

At dusk they went in, and I behind them with the lamp. Overhead the water dripped. In the oldest of the grain lay the heart, grey and hard and still.

Word for word: *At dusk they two came in, and I at their back with the lamp. Above, the water fell, tear by tear. In the eldest grain lay the heart, grey and hard and still.*

- "Overhead the water dripped" is ***Ul susk re omm et nynth, semm eth semm***, "above, the water fell, tear by tear": the lexicon has no "drip" and no "drop", and *semm* is "a tear; to weep". The distributive X *eth* X (as *garl eth garl*) makes it drop by drop. The wood weeps before Rhyna does, with the same word (*Re semm Rhyna*).
- *re vellora sona*: *mellor*, "come in, enter", 3du, with its subject (a broad 3du sounds as the imperative plural, §3.4). ***ul meskdhrenn hemm sost***, "in the eldest grain", as the lexicon's *mesk hemm sost*.

**¶9** · `S10-VI3-09`

> Lo sest et molt, ul dresk et meskvolt, re firr saedel sa new sell hy yarl.

Beside it, in a crack of the heartwood, lay a sliver no longer than a hand.

- wf8 S10's line, with *firr* "lay" for *yal* "was", as the English has *lay* twice: the heart lay, the sliver lay. *sa new sell hy yarl*, "that was not longer than a hand".

**¶10** · `S10-VI3-10`

> Ew lurrel o.

It was green.

- Three words for three: *Ew lurrel o*. *lurrel* is "forest-coloured" (*lurr* + *-el*): green as a living wood is green. wf8 had *Ew mesk lurrel o*, "it was green wood"; v3's line is the bare colour, and the Orrowen keeps it bare.

**¶11** · `S10-VI3-11`

> Re lymmym et greller dem vimm, demo, hy vodh nath re oskom et baegyth.

I brought the lamp down to it, for I trusted not the light.

Word for word: *I carried the lamp down, to it, for I did not trust the light.*

- *osk* is the canon's "trust", the word the Stonwryt coin is glossed by later in this leaf (*osk, hoskat ul dolm*). Seren did not trust her eyes before she trusted the coin. *nath re oskom*: *osk* is broad, so *-om*.

**¶12** · `S10-VI3-12`

> Vran grem re raevular et kaelireth dem haralath, eth et moltath dem vollath, eth et myst dem wemmeth. Nath re raevul hos hyor galat sa hoss. Veth re hoss sy ul nomm volt sennet, hosen hossant gendeleth ul dresk hald.

Six winters had we searched the wrecks for orders, and the hearts for names, and the grey for sails. Not one of us had looked for a thing that lived. Yet this had lived in the dark of a dead heart, as seedlings live in the crack of a wall.

Word for word: *Six winters we sought the wrecks for orders, and the hearts for names, and the grey for sails. Not one of us sought a thing that breathes. But this breathed in the dark of a dead heart, as seedlings breathe in a cleft of a wall.*

- The first sentence is wf8's (*raevul … dem*), with *Vran grem*, "six winters", set before it.
- **"A thing that lived" is *galat sa hoss*, "a thing that breathes".** *hoss* is "breath; breathe; live", and the wood's own people are *lunt et hoss*, "those of the breath". The sliver lives as the Naelear live, by breath; *hossant gendeleth*, the seedlings in the wall, breathe the same word.
- "Not one of us" is *hos hyor*, "one from us", after *nath re*.

**¶13** · `S10-VI3-13`

> Hy et grem hosast, re yal hosel garl um vesk loskat lonta. Veth re selle garl eth garl, eth re femme o ul hos clemel.

One hand only had {{Halyna}} needed on any wood since the first winter. All the same they put out a hand each, and touched it in the same moment.

Word for word: *From the first winter, a single hand upon a wood was enough for them two. But they two stretched out a hand and a hand, and touched it in one moment.*

- The first sentence is wf8's, said from the other side ("one hand was enough for them", *loskat lonta*), because *maver* "need" is first heard as "wish". ***garl eth garl*** is the headnote's "a hand each".

**¶14** · `S10-VI3-14`

> Re omm semm hy et mesketh ul et vem, eth re vedh o.

A drop fell from the timbers into the flame, and hissed.

Word for word: *A tear fell from the timbers into the flame, and it whispered.*

- "A drop" is *semm* again ("tear by tear" above), and "hissed" is ***medh***, "whisper, murmur", softened after *re* (*re vedh*): the lexicon has no hiss, and a drop on a flame is a small voice. The lamp's flame is *vem*.

**¶15** · `S10-VI3-15`

> Re hyl pa lern {{Halyna}} hosen hos.

Their two faces changed as one.

Word for word: *Halyna's two faces changed as one.*

- ***pa lern Halyna***, "the two faces of Halyna": one name, two faces, changed *hosen hos*, "as one". The dual *pa* + *lern* (*l* does not soften).

**¶16** · `S10-VI3-16`

> New haral o, ell grestet. Re wrodh Rhyna, hosen amm re hadh hos et brod ul ey brodow, eth re orr dem neld.

Not an order was it, nor a wound. Rhyna spake, as though one had put the word into her mouth and gone away.

Word for word: *It was not an order, or a wound. Rhyna spoke, as if one laid the word in her mouth, and went away.*

- "As though one had put the word into her mouth and gone away" is V.1's sentence (wf8 S07: *hosen amm re hadh hos et brod ul o wrodow, eth re orr dem neld*), turned to Rhyna: *ul ey brodow*, "in her mouth" (*ey* does not soften). "Put" is ***cadh***, "lay": the word was *laid* in her, as a tale is laid. *brodow* is "the word-place", the mouth.
- "Nor" is *ell*, "or", after the negative: *New haral o, ell grestet*.

> Varn.

"Home."

- ***Varn***, "home", the Title's first possessed thing (*ol varn*). One word, as green wood holds one sign (§3 of the grain: "a hull of one season holds one sign").

**¶18** · `S10-VI3-18`

> Re vysk Halvard: El venn o. Veth nath re hebb et mesk lurrel et brod hosel, eth nath re hess o ul vodh hesser. Re vyske sona o olen, ul hos hoss:

Halvard said that she was right. But the green wood gave not the word alone, and it stopped not where we stop. They said it together, in one breath:

Word for word: *Halvard said: It is true. But the green wood did not give the word alone, and it did not stop where we stop. They two said it together, in one breath:*

- "Halvard said that she was right" is V.1's *Re vysk Rhyna: El venn o* (wf8 S07), turned: in V.1 Rhyna said it of him. Orrowen has no "that" before a clause (pilot I.1).

- **The wood's line, as Halyna said it** (`<!-- W -->`): the sliver's round is below, the wood's own form. **¶20** · `S10-VI3-20`

> Re semm Rhyna, eth nath re semm Halvard, hy vodh re yal ey ul semmyl dem bana. Re hethym et greller dem dunnol Hyna.

The S04 reader read the same words in II.3 as "when he leaves a work for the other", and this reader took *lusk … lom* as "left to me" in VI3-37. **The reader's slip; the text stands** (one form with II.3).

**The mended lines, re-read** (the fixer's strict reading through the gloss):

- **VI3-03** *… eth sell re hether o hosen ull, tevar hy wrodhol.* "And long we held it so, before the speaking." A fronted *sell* is how the reader took *Sell re vesk Tarnard umo* ("Tarnard looked at it a long time"), and *tevar hy* is the reader's "before" (EP-09).
- **VI3-26** *Eth doss maver umona weskyl sulter demo.* "And we two must find a boat for it." The analyzer now names the idiom (*= doss maver um: must*).
- **VI3-29** *Gor hrynir et gorrol, re vysk o: Vorra o.* "Every plank of the war, he said: Burn it." *Vorra* is the plural imperative (the analyzer's IMP.PL, as the reader took *orra, veska* in EP-38). The fronted topic is taken up by *o*, as the reader took *Pa Varn vollar o* ("We call it The Two Homes"), and *gor* stands first, not after a preposition.
- **VI3-30** *Mesk sa re sesk et wadh, mesk sa sesk et orrol dem varn.* "Wood that knew the sea, wood that knows the road home." *sesk* is "know a fact; know (a thing that is so)", and this reader rendered it "knew" in EP-25 and EP-30.
- **VI3-38** *Piskur, el bysketeth hosel o, garl ul narl.* "A net, it is only knots, hand in hand." S04's blind reader read these words as "A net is only knots, hand in hand."
- **VI3-41** *Luska sona ul morrol lo,* "Let them two go with it": the dual for the two chisels, which the reader expected.
- **VI3-47** *Doss maver um et ullen: et lunt ullen.* "Need is upon the rest: the other people", that is, the rest needs the other. The idiom is glossed whole, as S04's *Doss maver um et flennath sy: …* is, and *ullen* is still said twice.
- **EP-02** *Galat sa re lusk et myst dem sestow.* "The thing that the grey sent (let go) back." *dem sestow* is the reader's own "back" (EP-29, "the tide went back"). With no verbal noun after *ul*, the lexicon's "let X do Y" has nothing to take.
