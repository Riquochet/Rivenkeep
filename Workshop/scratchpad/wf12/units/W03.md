### 1.3 The headnote

Word for word: in(+N) · night · reading · S·words · S·Brenn · PST · S·ask · child · what · PST · give · the · guest. Spring · this · PST · S·hold · Rhyna · it · one · hour, and · PST · S·come · it · into-her · slow, as · water · upon · S·stone; NEG · PST · touch · Halvard · it, across · the · ward · at · the · mortar, and · was · he · REL · PST · S·lay · the · stone · first · in · the · telling: *In the night of the reading of the words of Brenn, a child asked what the guest gave. - **"Brenn was read"**: the tongue has no passive, so it is a construct, *vess veskyl wrodath Wrenn*, "the night of the reading of the words of Brenn": a chain of possessors, each softened (*brodath* > *wrodath*, *Brenn* > *Wrenn*, as §3.2 softens a name, *lest Vardh*; *v* does not soften), and no head takes an article (§3.2). *brodath Wrenn* is IV.3's own name for what Halyna read at this hearth (its headnote, *Brodath Wrenn Reldvar*, and its close, *Dem sy, brodath Wrenn*, "So far the words of Brenn": unit S06), so the night is named for the reading of those words, and words are read, not seen: *vesk* is "see; read (letters, or a cradle)" (O2729). Fixed after the blind back-translation (BACK-TRANSLATION, W03-IV4-03): the first cut, *veskyl Brenn*, left the possessor unsoftened, and came back "the night of Brenn's seeing".
- **Seren's music.** *Ul vess veskyl wrodath Wrenn re vess tev*: *vess* the night and *vess* "asked" (softened *pess*, as in *Voss sa re vess*) are one sound, and between them the softened chain runs *v*, *v*, *w*, *w*, the night's own letter worn soft: on the night of the reading of his words, the night asks. *Heth sy re heth Rhyna o*: *heth* "spring" and *re heth* "held" (softened *keth*) are one sound and two words, so *This spring Rhyna held it* says it twice; the leaf-hand writes them apart (`h.e.th`, `k^S.e.th`), so the pun is the ear's and never the eye's. The third spring of the sentence is *nynth* ("a spring; water"): *hosen nynth um dholm*, the pilot's simile, kept.
- *nath re femm*, the negative past (O2861), for *touched it not* (v1.3's *never*, *nafedh*, went with its English). *um et parow lo et lodh*, "across the ward at the mortar", in the English's order: *um* is "upon; across, over" (O2661), and standing first it goes with Halvard, across the ward from Rhyna, not with the mortar (the first cut, *lo et lodh um et parow*, came back "the mortar upon the courtyard"). *lodh* is the mason's mortar, and the mortar of a sentence (*brodath lodh*, the small words), and the bond (*Lodhan*, the Bonded). The man at the mortar begins the telling of a stranger who had no mortar for his words (the reading, line 4).
- *ew o sa re hadh et tolm hosast ul et brodhol*, the pilot's cleft and the lexicon's idiom for *began* (O2859): he laid the first stone of the telling, of a gift that is not stone.
- v1.3's headnote formulas (*Hosen tum Mesk Myst …*, *Re wrodha Halyna o, hos eth ullen …*, *Amm re yal et mesk cresket …*) are not in the v3 Book (notes_v3 §1.5: the foreword says it once), so they are not here.

*(first ink)*

> *— Hemm. Domm, eth hemm. Tolm — nel. Nel tolm. Ew mesk.*

*— Old. Dark, and old. Stone — no. Not stone. A tree, once.*  

Word for word: *— Old. Dark, and old. Stone — is not. Is not stone. Was a tree.*

- **The inks alternate line by line, and neither is named** (notes_v3 §1.5); the unit marks them as the builder sets them, the first line in the first ink.

6. ***heth*, spring.** The Tier 3 report (§7 item 5) lists *heth* among the lexicon's findings for Jack that the wf8 units stepped round, because *re heth* is also "held" (softened *keth*); the wf12 datelines use it for the season (W04's note gives IV.4's as *Et heth sullast, ul Hyll Dhumol.*, the form this unit has). This leaf also steps onto the collision on purpose: *Heth sy re heth Rhyna o*, "This spring Rhyna held it", is Seren's turn, and the leaf-hand writes the two apart (`h.e.th`, `k^S.e.th`), so it is a pun for the ear only. If Jack would rather keep *heth* out, *Fedh sy* ("this season", O2930's *fedh*) serves, and the dateline's *Et heth sullast* becomes *Et fedh sullast* (losing *spring*).
7. ***veskyl wrodath Wrenn*.** The blind reader gave the first cut, *veskyl Brenn*, as "the night of Brenn's seeing", and caught its fault: a possessor is softened, names too (§3.2, *lest Vardh*), so *Brenn* there had to be *Wrenn*. The fix names what was read, *the words of Brenn* (*wrodath Wrenn*, IV.3's *brodath Wrenn* as a possessor), so *veskyl* can only be the reading of letters; it does not name the reader, as the English does not.
8. ***flenneth*, leaves.** W03-01 adds the tree's leaf to *flenn* as a sense of the existing entry (O0614), not a new word; the merge should fold it in. Its plural is the regular slender *flenneth*, one vowel from the Book's lexicalised *flennath*; the token tool writes every plural of *flenn* with the Book's full *a* and was mended by hand (§1.4).
9. ***O yell sost*, his best**, is *gell sost* (very good, as *hemm sost* is eldest) as a noun after the possessive; the gloss reads "his good self". If a reader takes it as "his own good", *O hebbet gell sost*, "his best gift", is the longer, plainer line.
10. ***Dannat*, hewn, for *Sawn*.** The tongue has no saw; it hews (*dann*, *dannwresk*). The grain's `AXE+WOOD` (timber) keeps the sawing on the wood's side.
