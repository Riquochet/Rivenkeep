## III.1 · OF THEIR RAISING (*SO YALDOL*)

*A stone leaf. Kael calls the roll and lays the leaf; each True Man answers for his own haven and swears; Seren reads from the rolls and frames the hall, in italic, as the Book has it. Everything is Seren's single ink: {{Halyna}} appear only in her headnote, and tell nothing.*

### Title

<!-- S05-III1-1 -->
> ## *So Yaldol*

**III.1 · Of Their Raising**

- The lexicon's *So Yaldol* (O2939), which the wf8 unit made for Part One of the old single tale: "their raising", *Yaldol* the softening of *galdol*. A title leaves out the English "Of", as the lexicon's titles do.

### Dateline

<!-- S05-III1-2 -->
> *Bremm hosast et tynur.*

*The first autumn of the siege.*

- A dateline is a naming, a noun with no verb (the D4 shape): *bremm hosast*, the ordinal after its noun (*et vess hosast*), with *et tynur* its possessor.

### Seren's headnote

<!-- S05-III1-3 -->
> *Re hadhant et vennuldath, hos ul hos cumm, amm re voll Kael Nydherd so; eth re ston gor uld dem dessyl, hosen ul vimm et movenn. Re weske {{Halyna}} et scaevureleth ul et vestul sa re lymmym. Galat sa re yal ulont eth ul dumol nahos, re veskym o ul garm.*

*Laid by the True Men, each for his own haven, as Kael the Counter called them, and every man stood to answer, as under the banner. {{Halyna}} had found the rolls in the chest I carried. What was in them and in no man's memory, I read out.*

Word for word: *The True Men laid [it], one to a haven, when Kael the Counter named them; and every man stood for answering, as under the banner. {{Halyna}} found the rolls in the chest that I carried. The thing that was in them and in the memory of no one, I read it aloud.*

- ***Re hadhant et vennuldath***: the headnotes' *Re hadh* X (Tier 3 §5.1) with a plural noun subject, so the plural ending (pilot G2). ***hos ul hos cumm***, "each for his own haven" (O3009, W02): *o humm sost* would be heard as *humm* "husband".
- ***Re weske {{Halyna}}***: the pair takes the dual (*wesk* is slender, so *-e*), which is what the English plural translates (§3.9 item 1). The pair-name is written `{{…}}`, the leaf-hand's sealing lintel, as in the English.
- ***ul dumol nahos***, "in the memory of no one": the Title's own first words, *Ul dumol*, said of what no one remembers. Seren reads out what memory lost; the Book's theme is in her preposition.
- ***et vestul sa re lymmym***: the Tier 3 report's chest (*vestul sa re lymmym dem susk et brunn*), shortened as the English is. ***re veskym o ul garm***, "I read it out": pilot I.1's words.

### Kael calls the roll

<!-- S05-III1-4 -->
> Hebb et tolm lom. Nath reskym; es stonom. Lo vollol stonant uldath.

Give me the stone. No, I will stand. At a roll men stand.

Word for word: *Give the stone at me. I do not sit; I will stand. At a naming, men stand.*

- Pilot I.1's answer to the offered seat, word for word (O0897 + *Nath reskym; es stonom*): v3 opens five leaves with it, so it is now a phrase row (S05-16), the same words in S01.
- ***Lo vollol stonant uldath***: a roll is a calling of names, *vollol*, the Naming's own word. *Ston* is the verb that closes every oath: the men who stand to answer tonight will each end on it (note 1).

<!-- S05-III1-5 -->
> Ul et dhess re orrom dem susk et taldow rerddhalden. Hy et taldow ull doss veskyl Orrow tul, dem et myst. Nath lusk hos odh umo dhidh dem susk.

At first light I went up the east tower. From there that coast may be seen yet, as far as the haze. Not one hearth on it sendeth up smoke.

Word for word: *In the first light I went up the eastern tower. From that tower there is a seeing of the Shore yet, as far as the grey. Not one hearth upon it looses smoke upward.*

- ***Orrow***, "the Shore", is "that coast" and the whole Shorelands at once; ***myst***, the haze, is the grey. Kael says neither twice and means both.
- ***doss veskyl … tul***, "may be seen yet": "there is a seeing", with no one named who sees. ***Nath lusk hos odh***: "not one hearth", the numeral before its noun. *dhess* "dawn" only after *et* (wf8 S05: it sounds as softened *tess*).

<!-- S05-III1-6 -->
> Amm sy hunna vollath et cummath, eth ketha so, hy vodh dossant lor.

Hear now the names of the havens, and hold them, for they are ours.

- The wf8 unit's line, kept word for word (*Amm sy hunna* is I.1's "Now hear"). Its second half is a phrase row (S05-17), because v3 says *hold them, for they are ours* again in V.5 and VI.1.

<!-- S05-III1-7 -->
> Hinnar uld ullen, nath gadhom o. Vollom et cumm, eth es dess uld et cumm sost, eth es ryt vodh es nald o, amm es mymm et cumm dem varn. El rytvard en. Kethym so.

Another man's city I tell not. I call the haven, and its own man shall answer, and swear what he will do when it is come home. Oath-keeper am I. I hold them.

Word for word: *The city of another man, I do not lay it. I name the haven, and the haven's own man will answer, and will swear what he will do, when the haven shall come home. Oath-keeper am I. I hold them.*

- Kael's "tell" is ***cadh***, "lay", as in pilot I.1, where *nath gadhom o los* is his rule: the fronted object is picked up by *o* (G6).
- ***es dess***, "will answer": the Invocation's *es dess et odh*. ***amm es mymm et cumm dem varn***, "when the haven shall come home" (*mymm dem varn*, V.3's title): the haven named a third time, a roll-call's refrain, because *amm es mymm o* was read "when he comes home" (back-translation). ***es nald o***, "he will do": *gald* is "build" and "do" in one, and a mason's deed is a building.
- ***El rytvard en***: the copula with its predicate first, I.1's *El nydherd en* (O2172). ***Kethym so***: he keeps the oaths as the hearth keeps the names (*ketha so* in the line before).

### Seren reads the rolls

<!-- S05-III1-8 -->
> *Hosast re veskym hy et scaevureleth, hosen re haral o. Ew galdardath ol nedheth, new wadhardath. Re yal ludal et wadh lunnat umont, eth re yaldant ul vodh ommant et brunnath dem et wadh lo ommolath sell; eth re hadhant orrolath um et brunnath hosen lorrdhrenneth. Cadh gor scaevurel et tolm hosast lo hos trenn:* Orl sy re yal et trenn hosast cadhat, eth hulter stinet dem et galdat. *El lyss garl et saevulath, eth vothen, hosen amm nath re vallant vyssorol et flenn. Ew galdat Stonwrytan gor hald ul noth cumm. Noth cumm, eth noth hulter.*

*First I read from the rolls, as he bade me. Builders were our fathers, and no seafarers. The open water they feared, and built where the mountains go down in long falls to the sea, and over the mountains they paved roads like veins. Each haven's roll beginneth with the one line:* This day was the first course laid, and a staff cut for the work. *Small is the clerks' hand, and patient, as if they were loath to waste the leaf. Every wall of the ten was Stonewright work. Ten havens, and ten staffs.*

Word for word: *First I read from the rolls, as he bade. Builders were our fathers, not seafarers. Fear of the open sea was upon them, and they built where the mountains fall to the sea by long fallings; and they laid roads upon the mountains like blood-courses. Every roll lays its first stone with one line:* This day was the first course laid, and a staff cut for the work. *Small is the hand of the clerks, and careful, as if they did not love the spending of the leaf. The work of the Stonewrights was every wall in the ten havens. Ten havens, and ten staffs.*

- ***ommant et brunnath … lo ommolath sell***: "go down in long falls" is the verb of the Fall, *omm*, the title of III.2 (*So Ommol*). The mountains fall in the first leaf before the havens fall in the second.
- ***Ew galdardath ol nedheth, new wadhardath***, ***Re yal ludal … umont***, ***re hadhant orrolath … hosen lorrdhrenneth***: the wf8 unit's sentences, kept: to pave is to lay, and veins are blood-courses.
- ***Cadh gor scaevurel et tolm hosast***, "every roll begins": the lexicon's *cadh et tolm hosast*, "lay the first stone", so the roll that records the first course lays one. "Each haven's roll" is "every roll": *gor cumm* would soften to *gor humm*, "every husband".
- **The rolls' line**, in roman as the English sets it: the passive with *re yal* and the participle; ***hulter stinet dem et galdat***, "a staff cut for the work", is the phrase III.2's law of the staff uses (*dem o yaldat*).
- ***garl***, "hand", is the hand and the handwriting. "Patient" is ***vothen***, "careful", the Plain Words sense. "Loath to waste the leaf" is "did not love the spending of the leaf".

### Tavow Hemm · Eldhythe

<!-- S05-III1-9 -->
> **Tavow Hemm, et cumm hemm sost.**

**Eldhythe, the Eldest Haven.**

- The haven's own name and the lexicon's by-name (O2910, wf8 S03 and S05), unchanged. The Book's English names are Seren's translations of these (`orrowen_v2` §4.7).

<!-- S05-III1-10 -->
> Corlen Dhavow. Pa vurr eth noth surr re lymm hyll et hedh et sultereth dem varn lo sest en davow, niss, dem et tavowlesteth dhaedh sost ul Morrow. Ol sulter hosast sa es mymm dem varn, es davom o. Ston.

Corlen of the harbour. Fifty years the evening tide brought the boats home past my quay, slow, to the warmest harbour-houses on the coast. The first boat of ours to come home, I will steer it in.

Word for word: *Corlen of the Harbour. Fifty years the tide of the evening brought the boats home beside my quay, slow, to the warmest harbour-houses in the Shore. Our first boat that shall come home, I will land it. It stands.*

- ***Dhavow · davow · davom***: Corlen's epithet (S·*tavow*), his quay (N·*tavow*) and his oath (N·*tav*, "land a boat"). The harbour and the landing are one word, said three ways in three lines.
- "I will steer it in" is ***es davom o***, "I will land it": *tav* is "come ashore, land a boat". ***Ston.***: note 1. ***pa vurr eth noth***, "fifty", two twenties and ten; *ul Morrow*, the name bedded (wf8 S05).

<!-- S05-III1-11 -->
> *Hy ull re resk Corlen, hemm sost et Delv, eth re heth o nind thenth dem susk, hosen gor fedh ul et kethyl um et hald. Nath noss helvhoss ul et hellur sy. Re dhyvullant et uldath tevel ul sestow. Re vysk o lont galat sa vysk o lo o wramman, amm maverent brammol: Tovva. Nath dunn et wadh tul.*

*Then Corlen sat, eldest of the Twelve, and wet a finger and held it up, as at every holding on the wall. There is no wind in this hall. The young men at the back laughed. To them he said what he saith to his gunners when they would fire: "Wait. The sea hath not finished."*

Word for word: *Then Corlen sat, the eldest of the Twelve, and held a wet finger up, as always in the holding upon the wall. There is no wind in this hall. The young men at the back laughed. He said to them the thing he says to his battery, when they want firing: Wait. The sea does not finish yet.*

- ***hemm sost et Delv***, "eldest of the Twelve", as Pellow's ***tevel sost et Delv***. ***et kethyl***, "the holding", is the wall's hour when the grey sails come (O1124).
- ***o wramman***, "his battery" (S·*bramman*, "the guns of one True Man"). ***helvhoss***, "wind", is "sky-breath": there is no breath of the sky in this hall.
- **The saying.** ***Nath dunn et wadh tul***, "the sea does not finish yet" (*nath … tul* is "not yet"), the words S08 gives it in V.5 (S08-08), and a phrase row here (S05-18). III.2 tells it in the past, *nath re dhunn*, and the Epilogue says it again. ***Tovva***, to many: he says it to the young men and his gunners (V.5's is *Tovv*, to one). The romanisation marks no speech (§6.7), so the Book's quotation marks stand as a colon.

### Cemm Hyll · Tidesmeet

<!-- S05-III1-12 -->
> **Cemm Hyll, ul vodh cemm et rhull et hyll.**

**Tidesmeet, where the river meeteth the tide.**

- The wf8 unit's line: the epithet says the name's own verb, *cemm*.

<!-- S05-III1-13 -->
> Tamm Sull Heskal. Um et pa hless re ston o, eth re lodhant sull heskal strom o um et rhull.

Tamm of the three spans. On both banks it stood, and three great spans bound it across the water.

Word for word: *Tamm of the Three Spans. Upon the two banks it stood, and three great spans bonded it across the river.*

- ***um et pa hless***: the article keeps the dual whole. After *um*, softened *pa* is *va*, which the tongue also hears as *va*, "your".
- ***re lodhant***: "bound" is the mortar's verb. "The water" is "the river", *rhull*: *nynth* reads first as "a spring".

<!-- S05-III1-14 -->
> *Re hadh Tamm sull tolm lo et vorr, ul so dhrenn, hosen lo o naeval gor vess.*

*Tamm set three stones out by the fire, in their order, as by his blanket every night.*

Word for word: *Tamm laid three stones by the fire, in their course, as by his blanket every night.*

- ***ul so dhrenn***, "in their order", is "in their course": three stones in a course, the spans of a city laid as a wall is laid.

<!-- S05-III1-15 -->
> El crynir hos heskal. El hinnar sull. Um et cless ul vodh re stonant, es gadhom so. Ston.

One span is a plank. Three is a city. On the bank where they stood, I will set them out.

Word for word: *[It] is a plank, one span. [It] is a city, three. Upon the bank where they stood, I will lay them. It stands.*

- Two copula lines in one shape, the predicate first (*El tolm et hald*), the second a word shorter. ***es gadhom so***: "set them out" is "lay them".

### Sedhnell · Fenholm

<!-- S05-III1-16 -->
> **Sedhnell, et hinnar sa re yal gor fedh ul felmyl.**

**Fenholm, the town that was always sinking.**

- The wf8 unit's line: *ul felmyl*, the progressive.

<!-- S05-III1-17 -->
> Marl Sedhen. Re ston ol gumm um yannath, eth re felm o ryssyth yarl gor hossol, eth re hadh gor dhesk trenn clenn um et trenn hemm. Uld sedhen sa nath dhyvull um ull, el uld wrunnel o.

Marl the fen-born. Our town stood on pilings, and sank a hand's breadth a lifetime, and every generation laid a new course on the old. A fen-man that cannot laugh at that had best go and live on a hill.

Word for word: *Marl the Fen-born. Our haven stood upon posts, and sank a hand's breadth every lifetime, and every generation laid a new course upon the old course. A fen-born man who does not laugh at that is a man of a hill.*

- "Our town" is ***ol gumm***, "our haven": *ol hinnar* shows no bite after a verb and reads "we, town" (G9). ***um yannath***, ***ryssyth yarl*** (O2947), ***gor dhesk trenn clenn um et trenn hemm***: the wf8 unit's.
- ***Uld sedhen … el uld wrunnel o***: Marl's own epithet (*Sedhen*, fen-born) turned on the man who cannot laugh, and "had best go and live on a hill" said as what such a man is. The hearth that hears it sits on a mountain.

<!-- S05-III1-18 -->
> Re hadh Ebba hemm, en scaelur, trenn dasken ol lest. Doss tolm hyo ul en havul, eth es lymmym ey sost, amm lusk ey en ul lymmyl. Doss ey lo ull, lo et vorr, ul ferryl. Es gadhom tolm Ebba um et trenn hemm. Ston.

Old Ebba, my grandmother, laid the last course of our house. A stone of it is in my pack, and I would carry her too, if she would let me. She is there by the fire, nodding. I am to lay her stone on the old course.

Word for word: *Old Ebba, my grandmother, laid the last course of our house. There is a stone from it in my pack, and I would carry her herself, if she let me in carrying. She is there, by the fire, in nodding. I will lay Ebba's stone upon the old course. It stands.*

- ***Ebba hemm***, "old Ebba", as S01 writes her (*Ebba hemm Sedhnell*): *hemm* the adjective, not *Hemma*, the rank. *Ebba* is new to the lexicon here and in S01, S03, S06, S09 (S05-01).
- "Her stone" is ***tolm Ebba***, the name as possessor: *ey tolm* after a verb reads "her, stone". ***ey sost***, "her herself", for "her too". ***trenn dasken ol lest***: the construct's head takes no article.
- "If she would let me" is ***amm lusk ey en ul lymmyl***, "if she let me carry" (*lusk* X *ul* VN, O3003): *en* is the object, for a possessive would bed *ul* to *mul*; *amm es lusk ey o lom* was read "when she lets it go to me", the stone (back-translation). ***lymmym … lymmyl***: the carrying twice. ***ul ferryl***, "nodding": *ferr* is bow and nod.

### Lurrvard · Holtward

<!-- S05-III1-19 -->
> **Lurrvard, et kethow ul vimm et crisur lurr.**

**Holtward, the keep under the canopy.**

- The wf8 unit's line: *crisur lurr*, "the roof of the forest", is Aske's epithet.

<!-- S05-III1-20 -->
> Aske Hrisur Lurr. Re yal o sost virr ul molt et mesketh, hy ull re orr orrard ul vimm, eth nath re vesk o hald. Nath re stiner hos gend; eth gor wremm re lunnar et haldorrol hy et flenneth lo yarl.

Aske of the canopy. It lay so close among the trees that a traveller might pass under it and see no wall. No bough would we cut, and every autumn we swept the wall-walk of its leaves by hand.

Word for word: *Aske of the Canopy. It was very close among the trees, so that a traveller went under, and did not see a wall. We did not cut one bough; and every autumn we cleared the wall-walk of the leaves by hand.*

- ***flenneth***, "leaves": *flenn* read of a tree, as W03 reads it (S05-03). The tree's *flenneth* and the Book's *flennath* part only by the harmony of the ending; Seren's ear hears the one in the other.
- "Swept" is ***lunn … hy***, "make room from, clear" (S05-13); there is no broom in the lexicon. "Might pass under it and see no wall" is said as what a traveller did. ***lo yarl***, "by hand": *lo* softens *garl*.

<!-- S05-III1-21 -->
> *Re firr kaelirvesk et vess lo et odh. Um gor hrynir hyo re hadh Aske o yarl, somm nath re orr o dem et vorr tul.*

*By the hearth lay the night's wreck-wood. On every plank of it Aske laid his hand, ere it went on the fire.*

Word for word: *The wreck-wood of the night lay by the hearth. Upon every plank of it Aske laid his hand, while it had not yet gone to the fire.*

- ***somm nath … tul***, "ere": "while not yet" (S05-15). *amm nath* is "unless" (O0015). ***re hadh … o yarl***: Aske *lays* his hand, as the tellers of III.2 lay theirs on the stone.

<!-- S05-III1-22 -->
> Ew mesketh so hos fedh. Nath narr dannwresk ul Lurrvard somm hossom. Ston.

These were trees once. No axe shall come into Holtward while I live.

Word for word: *They were trees once. No axe comes into Holtward while I breathe. It stands.*

- The vow in the present, as a law is (the Creed is present). There is no negative future in the grammar, and *nath* would bed *es* into *nes*. "While I live" is ***somm hossom***, "while I breathe".

### Kethow Stellath · Carnhold

<!-- S05-III1-23 -->
> **Kethow Stellath, kethow et stelleth.**

**Carnhold, the stronghold of the cairns.**

- The wf8 unit's line: the name, then the name as words. *Stellath* is the canon's broad slip; the word takes the rule's *stelleth*.

<!-- S05-III1-24 -->
> Garvel Runnowath. Re stannar o ul molt stelleth et stannardath hosast, eth re yal seskyl lo uld hy et bontol, vodh drunnow re yal ul vorr. Gor wramm um et hald sy, rernym o, hosen rern odha hoss gask. Myskent venn brammath sa wontant venn. Veth um runndholm ul Gethow Stellath es nald galat sa nel bramm, eth es hunnom o ul montol. Ston.

Garvel of the smithies. We hewed it among the cairns of the first quarrymen, and a man could tell by the ring which of its forges was at work. Every gun on this wall I listen to, as a mother to a cough. Guns that ring true speak true. But at an anvil of Carnhold I will make a thing that is not a gun, and hear it ring.

Word for word: *Garvel of the Smithies. We quarried it in the heart of the cairns of the first quarrymen, and a man could know from the ringing which forge was alight. Every gun upon this wall, I listen to it, as a mother listens to a cough. Guns that ring true say true. But upon an anvil in Carnhold I will make a thing that is not a gun, and I will hear it ringing. It stands.*

- ***re stannar o … et stannardath***: "hewed" is "quarried", among the quarrymen's cairns: the verb twice, and Stannard's name is the word (I.1).
- "At work", of a forge, is ***ul vorr***, "alight". A cough is ***hoss gask***, "a broken breath" (S05-10). ***Myskent venn …***: the verb first, a gunner's proverb.
- ***runndholm***: *drunndholm*, "an anvil, hammer-stone" (S05-07, the form S09 reached too), softened after *um* (*dr-* > *r-*). ***ul Gethow Stellath***: as a possessor the name would soften to *Hethow*, heard as *heth* "spring" + *-ow* (pilot I.1).

### Tavow Vorr · Emberhythe

<!-- S05-III1-25 -->
> **Tavow Vorr, et drunnow um et orrow sa vorr.**

**Emberhythe, the forge on the burning shore.**

- The wf8 unit's line: *et orrow sa vorr* is Harl's own shore (O2949).

<!-- S05-III1-26 -->
> Harl Orrow sa Vorr. Re ston o ul vodh orr dhidh et brunn dem et wadh, eth re yal et trun sost dhaedh, hy ull re haskant ol devath lo visketh naesket um et sulv. Es gethym pawast et orrow sa vorr, eth es vorrom et myst, eth et wadh sost, amm grik o vorr. Ston.

Harl of the burning shore. It stood where the mountain smoketh into the sea, and the ground was so warm that our children ran barefoot in the snow. My shore I will have again, and the grey I will burn, and the sea too, if it would take fire.

Word for word: *Harl of the Burning Shore. It stood where the smoke of the mountain goes to the sea, and the ground was very warm, so that our children ran with uncovered feet upon the snow. I will hold again the shore that burns, and I will burn the grey, and the sea itself, if it catch fire. It stands.*

- ***ol devath***: the bite shows the possessive. "Barefoot" is ***lo visketh naesket***, "with uncovered feet" (*naesket*, the lexicon's; the same word is III.2's bare ground).
- "My shore I will have again" is ***Es gethym pawast et orrow sa vorr*** (*keth pawast*, "win back"): the shore of his epithet, and *en morrow* (N·*orrow*) avoided. ***et wadh sost***, "the sea itself", for "the sea too". Fire three times, *sa vorr*, *vorrom*, *vorr*: his temper (notes_v3 §1.8).

### Grullsorth · Sandreach

<!-- S05-III1-27 -->
> **Grullsorth, et kethow rytet ul hald et thykir.**

**Sandreach, the citadel carved in the canyon wall.**

- The wf8 unit's line: *hal*, the living rock.

<!-- S05-III1-28 -->
> Ulden Salereth. Nath re yal tavow lo. Veth re vollar o: cumm, hy vodh re var o ol. Nath re yal galdat, veth paskat, saler ul vimm saler ul et hal unth. Gor vess dem et Ommol re aedhym ul vimm et brunn tunn, hosen ul vimm en grisur, eth en rern um et pressir; eth re hunnom o ul gethyl. Ul vimm et crisur ull es naedhym pawast. Ston.

Ulden of the galleries. Sandreach had no harbour. We called it a haven all the same, for it sheltered us. Dug it was, not built, gallery below gallery into the red rock. Every night till the havens fell I slept with the whole mountain for my roof, and mine ear to the floor, and heard it hold. Under that roof I will sleep again.

Word for word: *Ulden of the Galleries. There was no harbour at it. But we named it: haven, for it guarded us. It was not built, but dug, gallery under gallery in the red bedrock. Every night until the Fall I slept under the whole mountain, as under my roof, and my ear upon the floor; and I heard it in holding. Under that roof I will sleep again. It stands.*

- ***Nath re yal tavow lo***, "there was no harbour at it": *lo Grullsorth* would soften the name to *Rullsorth*. ***re vollar o: cumm***: the naming colon (wf8 S05, S2).
- *cumm* is "any walled place of shelter", so "for it sheltered us" is the word's own sense; Ulden says it with *par*, "guard". ***Nath re yal galdat, veth paskat***: "not … but" (§3.8), the wf8 unit's.
- ***dem et Ommol***, "until the Fall" (O1869). ***ul gethyl***, "in holding": the mountain held, as the wall holds and as Kael holds the oaths.

### Frennvar · Rimewatch

<!-- S05-III1-29 -->
> **Frennvar, et par um et lynth crentet.**

**Rimewatch, the watch on the frozen mere.**

- The wf8 unit's line: *par*, a watch, is the name's *-var*.

<!-- S05-III1-30 -->
> Hesk Lynth. Lo dhrenn et grem re ston et hellur strom um et crest, eth re nask et hinnar ul vimm o hrisur ul hylenn, eth re yal dhaedh. Hedh eth hedh re orr tymornel disker eth disker, eth re lestirent et grellereth et sugornath um et crest dem varn. Re voth en modha et grellereth ull. Doss hos hyont ul vorr um et hald bridlernen vess sy. Pawast es syndalom o um et crest. Ston.

Hesk of the mere. All winter its great hall stood out on the ice, and the town feasted under its roof, and was warm. Every dusk a taper went from window to window, and the fishers out on the ice steered home by the lamps. My mother kept those lamps. One of them is lit on the north wall tonight. On the ice I will light it again.

Word for word: *Hesk of the Mere. Through the winter the great hall stood upon the ice, and the town ate under its roof in gladness, and was warm. Dusk after dusk a taper went window after window, and the lamps guided the fishers upon the ice home. My mother tended those lamps. One of them is alight upon the northern wall tonight. Again I will light it upon the ice. It stands.*

- ***Hedh eth hedh … disker eth disker***: the distributive twice, for "every dusk … from window to window". III.2 says *Disker eth disker* of the fire that went the same way, and *Hedh eth hedh* of the lamp on the wall. *gor hedh* would also read "every who" (S·*cedh*).
- "Steered home by the lamps" is "the lamps guided the fishers home" (*lestir*), the verb of III.2's night-watch. "Feasted" is "ate in gladness". ***tymornel***, "a small torch", the taper.
- ***en modha***, "my mother": the plain grammar (*en* beds a vowel to *m-*); wf8 S04 stepped round it as English-looking, which the standing rule no longer asks. ***Pawast*** stands first: *o pawast* reads "its second".

### Taldow Helv · Highreach

<!-- S05-III1-31 -->
> **Taldow Helv, taldow et tolmath sa haemerent.**

**Highreach, the tower of the floating stones.**

- The wf8 unit's line, with O2970's *tolmath sa haemerent*.

<!-- S05-III1-32 -->
> Lanner Gynten. Re yal et gynteth sost susk, hy ull re orr gor dhrenn dem susk um wemmerdeth, eth re haemer o um et helvhoss, hosen haemerent ganurnasath ul susk et trunow. Doss mummol helvhoss lom hos orl tevar. Hossvell luld et gynteth ull helvhoss dem susk, somm nath noss orrol lo tul. Es nystym pawast et gynt susk sost, eth es hossvellym dem et helvhoss. Ston.

Lanner of the crags. So high were its crags that every course went up on cranes, and hung on the wind as hawks hang over a field. A wind I can smell a day off. A boy of those crags can whistle one up ere he can walk. I will climb the highest again, and whistle for the wind.

Word for word: *Lanner of the Crags. The crags were very high, so that every course went up upon lifters, and it hung upon the wind, as hawks hang above the field. Smelling a wind is at me one day early. A boy of those crags whistles a wind up, while walking is not yet at him. I will climb again the highest crag, and I will whistle for the wind. It stands.*

- ***ganurnas***, "chase-bird", a hawk (S05-05). ***haemer***, "hang, float": the courses hang as the heading's stones float. ***um wemmerdeth***, *bemmerd* "a lifter", the crane (wf8 S05).
- ***hossvell***, "breath-song", a whistle (S05-06: the form S03, S08 and S09 reached). Lanner's breath-song calls up the sky-breath, *helvhoss*: the tongue makes the whistle and the wind one breath.
- ***Doss mummol helvhoss lom***, "I can smell a wind": the "at" idiom of being able; ***somm nath noss orrol lo tul***, "ere he can walk": the same idiom, not yet. III.2 answers *hos orl tevar* with *Hos orl tunn tevar hyont*.

### Sirrvell · Glasspire

<!-- S05-III1-33 -->
> **Eth Sirrvell, et dasken eth et deth sost.**

**And Glasspire, the last and proudest.**

Word for word: *And Glasspire, the last and the very proud.*

- The superlative by *sost*, as *cumm hemm sost*; *dasken* "last", *deth* "proud". The wf8 unit had "the last work we raised, and the proudest" in a sentence; v3 gives it as a heading.

<!-- S05-III1-34 -->
> Pellow Vell. Re yald dhom tesk o, eth re rik o et rerd hosen preller hrest. Lo wisk re ston et sirr strom, sa re rig et rerd farrol orl dem neld ul et myst. Re rasor en nedh o. Galat hyo sa re lymmym dem susk et brunn lo wroclinth, re rasorom o ul ol sirreth dem neld. Amm es mymm Sirrvell dem varn, hosast es veskym o. Ston.

Pellow of the spire. Seven generations raised it, and it caught the sun like a blade of ice. At the top stood the great glass, that threw the sun a day's sail out into the grey. My father ground it. What I carried of it up the mountain in straw, I have ground into our far-glasses. When Glasspire is come home, mine shall be the first eye on it.

Word for word: *Pellow of the Spire. Seven generations raised it, and it caught the sun like a blade of ice. At the head stood the great glass, that threw the sun a day's sail far into the grey. My father polished it. The thing from it that I carried up the mountain in straw, I polished it into our far-glasses. When Glasspire shall come home, first I will see it. It stands.*

- ***re rik o et rerd hosen preller hrest***, ***re rig et rerd … ul et myst***: the wf8 unit's. ***farrol orl***, "a day's sail" (O2956). "Ground" is ***dasor***, "polish, rub smooth": *re rasor en nedh o*.
- ***sirreth dem neld***, "far-glasses" (S05-08, S02's *sirr dem neld*). ***lo wroclinth***, "in straw": *ul* would bed *broclinth* into *mr-*. ***dem susk et brunn***: the Book's "up the mountain".
- "Mine shall be the first eye on it" is ***hosast es veskym o***, "first I will see it", I.1's *Hosast re veskym o*. *vesk* is also "read".

<!-- S05-III1-35 -->
> *Hy ull re heth Pellow, tevel sost et Delv, o sirr dem susk lo et vorr, hosen keth o o dem susk gor fedh, eth lo o naskol sost. Re vysk o lo et tevath ul lern: Veska virr.*

*Then Pellow, youngest of the Twelve, held his glass up to the fire, as he holdeth it up to everything, his supper among the rest. To the children at the front he said, "Look close."*

Word for word: *Then Pellow, the youngest of the Twelve, held his glass up at the fire, as he holds it up every time, and even at his eating. He said to the children in front: Look close.*

- "To everything, his supper among the rest" is "every time, and even at his meal" (*sost*, "even"): a softening word before *gor* is unsettled in the grammar (pilot I.1), and *lo gor yalat* would be *lo yor yalat*.
- ***Veska virr***, "look near" (S05-19); III.2's *Re veskym virr* answers it.

## III.2 · OF THEIR FALL (*SO OMMOL*)

*A stone leaf. Kael opens it and closes it with the Road; each True Man lays his hand on the stone and tells his city's fall; Seren speaks for the guild after Pellow gives her his place. All in Seren's single ink.*

### Title

<!-- S05-III2-1 -->
> ## *So Ommol*

**III.2 · Of Their Fall**

- The lexicon's *So Ommol* (O2940), "their falling", unchanged.

### Dateline

<!-- S05-III2-2 -->
> *Grem pawast, ul Hyll Pawast.*

*The second winter, in the Second Tide.*

- *pawast* twice, the winter and the Tide (*Hyll Pawast*, O-row): the counts chime as the English does not.

### Seren's headnote

<!-- S05-III2-3 -->
> *Re hadhant et vennuldath pawast, gor uld um ommol o hinnar sost, ul vess frenn duss, amm re wrident et brammath, eth nath re hyn et lodh tul. Re voll Kael Nydherd so, eth re vyskym hosen brodow et Stonwrytan.*

*Laid by the True Men again, each for the fall of his own city, on a night of hard frost when the guns were cooling and the mortar was not yet set. Kael the Counter called them, and I spake for the guild.*

Word for word: *The True Men laid [it] again, every man about the fall of his own city, in a night of hard frost, when the guns cooled, and the mortar had not yet set. Kael the Counter named them, and I spoke as the mouth of the guild.*

- ***re wrident***, "cooled": *brid* "cold; cool" as a verb; its progressive after *ul* would bed to *mr-*. ***nath re hyn et lodh tul***: *rhyn*, "set fast (of mortar)", is Rhyna's verb (*rh* softens to *h*).
- ***um ommol o hinnar sost***: *um* "about", what a thing is told upon. "I spake for the guild" is ***re vyskym hosen brodow et Stonwrytan***, "I spoke as the guild's mouth": *brodow*, "the word-place", Eldhythe's harbour mouth (*lo wrodow et tavow*) given to the guild, whose part Seren speaks as each True Man speaks his city's. *re vyskym dem et Stonwrytan* was read "I spoke to the guild" (back-translation).

### Kael opens the falling

<!-- S05-III2-4 -->
> Hebb et tolm lom. Nath reskym; es stonom. Nel dem reskyl et trenn sy.

Give me the stone. No, I will stand. This is no tale to sit for.

Word for word: *… It is not for sitting, this tale.*

- The copula with its predicate first; *trenn*, a tale, is also a course, and a course is not for sitting on.

<!-- S05-III2-5 -->
> Vollath et cummath, re hethys so ul et bremm. Ketha so amm sy lo dhrenn et ommol, eth nath veska dem neld, hy vodh nath re vesker dem neld ul et brodhol.

Their names ye held in the autumn. Hold them now through the falling, and look not away, for we looked not away in the telling.

Word for word: *The names of the havens, you held them in the autumn. Hold them now through the falling, and do not look away, for we did not look away in the telling.*

- "Their names" is ***Vollath et cummath***, fronted and picked up by *so*: *re hethys so vollath* would read "you held, they, names". ***lo dhrenn et ommol***: the title's word.
- ***nath veska dem neld***, ***vesk dem neld*** (O2957), the wf8 unit's. The tellers are the True Men, so the plural *vesker* (wf8 had Halyna's dual).

<!-- S05-III2-6 -->
> Ul heth dasken et sollan, murr bress tevar hy et movenn, re yaldant et lodhnasath nasowath ul grernil gor yanna. Nath re hresk hos hy et cummath nasow fedh. Nath re yal scethan yorrol lo et cummath. Re yalant haldath et cummath galdat dem dernil eth dem helv. Re yal gor yanna ryss, eth re ston lunnat ul vess.

In the last spring of the peace, twenty months ere the banner, the swallows nested in the arches of the gates. No man of the havens ever broke a nest. The havens had no fleet of war. Their walls were built for the high days and the weather. Their gates were wide, and stood open at night.

Word for word: *In the last spring of the peace, twenty months early from the banner, the mortar-birds built nests in the arch of every gate. Not one from the havens ever broke a nest. There was no war fleet at the havens. The walls of the havens were built for rite and for weather. Every gate was wide, and stood open in the night.*

- ***lodhnas***, "mortar-bird", the swallow (S05-04): it builds its nest of mud under an arch, so the masons' tongue names it a builder, and no one of the havens breaks a builder's work. ***nasow***, "a nest", the lexicon's "bird-place" (S05-11).
- ***ul grernil gor yanna***, ***gor yanna ryss***: the wf8 unit's "every gate" (*gannath* is "posts"). ***scethan yorrol*** (O2952), ***dem dernil eth dem helv***: "the high days" are the rite's.

<!-- S05-III2-7 -->
> Hy et myst re rarrant et wemmeth domm, eth nath re yal peltir. Nath re vellor luskat sa re hyrril hos, veth drunnol hy et myst, hosen brammol helv; eth nath re hess o. Vollant et hesperdeth um et hald sy o: et drunnatath. Re raevular crenn um et scetheth ull, eth cryrull, eth hos uld lo hos bunth. Re ston nahos um et pressireth. Rytom um ull. Ston.

Out of the grey, without warning, came the dark sails. No envoy came that any man could understand, but a knocking out of the grey, like thunder, on and on. The soldiers on this wall call it the drums. We looked for a captain on those ships, and a crew, and one man at one rope. No one stood on the decks. I swear to that.

Word for word: *Out of the grey came the dark sails, and there was no warning. No envoy came in whom anyone understood, but a knocking from the grey, like thunder; and it did not stop. The soldiers upon this wall name it: the drums. We sought a captain upon those ships, and a crew, and one man at one rope. No one stood upon the decks. I swear upon that. It stands.*

- "On and on" is ***eth nath re hess o***, "and it did not stop": *hess* is the Guest's first word, and the knocking that would not stop is answered, books later, by a mouth that says it.
- ***wemmeth domm*** (O2950), ***luskat sa re hyrril hos***, ***brammol helv***, ***et drunnatath***, ***Re ston nahos***: the wf8 unit's. ***Rytom um ull. Ston.***: Kael's oath on a fact, as S01 gives it in I.1 (S05-21).

Word for word: *So we named it: the ghost fleet; and the senders: Mystaeri, a name from the mist. A man needs a name for his death.*

- "Must call his death by some name" is ***Maver uld voll dem o sennyl***, "a man needs a name for his death": *sennyl* is the Guest's third word.

## NOTES FOR THE NEXT HANDS

### 1. *Ston.* after the ten oaths [Jack]

`orrowen_v2` §3.9: "An oath closes with the one-word vow *Ston.*" III.1 is the night the True Men swear (*and swear what he will do when it is come home … Ten have sworn tonight … ten oaths I hold*), and each man's last sentence is his oath. In Orrowen an oath without its close is not finished, so each of the ten ends ***Ston.***, "It stands": Corlen, Tamm, Marl, Aske, Garvel, Harl, Ulden, Hesk, Lanner, Pellow. Stannard swears nothing and has none; his knock on the hearthstone stands where the close would. Kael's *I swear to that* in III.2 closes so too (*Rytom um ull. Ston.*, as S01 gives it in I.1). It is the liberty pilot I.1 took with Kael's two oaths (its note 1), and the reading is the same: Seren's English renders the whole oath, and English has no closing word. ### 6. Collisions the leaves step around

- ***gor cumm*** > *gor humm* "every husband"; ***o cumm*** likewise: the generic *et cumm*, or *hos ul hos cumm* (O3009).
- ***gor hedh*** reads S·*cedh* "who": *Hedh eth hedh*.
- ***nath osk*** beds to ***mosk***, the Creed's word: Stannard *gives no trust to a stone*, *Nath hebb o osk lo dholm*.
- ***re cemm*** "gathered" softens to *hemm* "old": *re vest o*, "it grew many".
- ***hemmath*** is "Elderesses" (*Hemma* + *-ath*): the old are ***hemmeth***.
- ***mest hy*** is "more than": "many of the children" is *tevath mest*.
- ***derd nind*** "finger-bone" softens to *rerd* "the sun" after *lo*: the knuckle is *nindyorn*.
- ***o grellereth*** drops its *g* and is heard as *rellor* "a sign": *et grellereth*.
- ***o pawast*** reads "its second", ***o griket***, ***o baeg*** as possessives missing their bite: the *o* is left out or the word moved (*Pawast es syndalom o …*, *re yal griket*, *re yal baeg*).
- ***ul orl*** > *ul morl*, ***ul orrol*** > *ul morrol* "in going": *Lo orl dasken*; the smiths' street is *ul hinnarorrol* (S05-23), for *um orrol et drunnardath* was heard "upon the going of the smiths" (BACK-TRANSLATION).
- ***dem*** before a person is heard "to" before "for": Seren speaks *hosen brodow et Stonwrytan*, "as the guild's mouth". ***re heth Tamm o*** leaves *o* between a hand and a stone: *et tolm*.
- ***ul broclinth*** > *mr-*: *lo wroclinth*. The progressive of *brid* (*ul mridol*) likewise: *re wrident*, "cooled".
- ***nynth*** "water" reads first as "a spring": Tidesmeet's spans cross *et rhull*.
- ***hethow*** (S·*kethow*), ***Rullsorth***, ***Damm***: note 4.
