## II.1 · *KETHOW SA RE YAL NADHUMAT*, OF THE KEEP THAT WAS FORGOTTEN

### Title

> ## *Kethow sa re yal nadhumat*

**II.1 · Of the Keep That Was Forgotten**

*The Keep that was un-remembered.* (Lexicon O1118, unchanged: *nadhumat* is *na-* on the participle of *tum*, the Title's "remember".)

### The dateline

> *Syst hosast et tynur.*

*The first summer of the siege.*

Word for word: *First summer of the siege.*

- A dateline is a naming, verbless, noun first (§3.11 D4's shape, here in ink). *Syst hosast et tynur* is a construct with a definite possessor, so its head takes no article (§3.2), as wf8 S04's *surr hosast et tynur*, "the first year of the siege". The three first-year datelines of v3 (I.4, II.1, III.1) should all take this shape.

### Seren's headnote

> *Re hadh Seren Pa Luth. Doss ulo flenn et galdardath hosast; doss o lont, eth nath noss o lom: re veske {{Halyna}} o lom hy et Hal, eth nath re werdym clennyl wrodath ull.*

*Laid by Seren Two-Inks. The founders' leaf within it is theirs and not mine: {{Halyna}} read it me out of the old hand, and those words I have not dared to mend.*

Word for word: *Seren Two-Inks laid [it]. There is in it the leaf of the first builders; it is at them, and it is not at me: Halyna read it to me out of the Hal, and I did not dare the mending of those words.*

- ***Re hadh Seren Pa Luth***: the headnote's frame, "X laid" (pilot I.1), with the canon's *Seren Pa Luth*.
- "theirs and not mine" is the having idiom (§3.7): ***doss o lont, eth nath noss o lom***, "it is at them, and it is not at me".
- "the old hand" is ***et Hal***, the Hal (O1884, *Orrowen Hal*): the first builders' register and the hand it is cut in (§4.1). The founders' leaf is in the Hal on its slate (notes §6.14: "all in the Hal"); Halyna "read it me out of" it.
- "mend" is ***clenn***, "make new, mend": Seren mends every teller's words (K5), and these alone she did not make new. "dare" is *berd*; the object of the verbal noun is its softened possessor, ***clennyl wrodath ull*** (pilot I.1 G3).

### Seren's telling

> Keth et tolm, Seren.

Hold the stone, Seren.

- The Invocation's canon ***Keth et tolm*** and the vocative; *Seren* does not soften (*s*). The Book says this line in eight tales (I.4, II.1, IV.1, V.1, V.2, V.7, VI.3, the Epilogue) and in the Book of Knowings: **one form for all**, *Keth et tolm, Seren.*

> El ryt surr et Kethow sy, hosen re hethent et galdardath hosast o, eth hosen nath re hether o.

Here is the annal of the Keep, as the founders kept it, and as we kept it not.

Word for word: *This is the annal of the Keep, as the first builders kept it, and as we did not keep it.*

- **The Keep is the holding-place.** *Kethow* is made from *keth*, "hold, keep", so the annal of the Keep is the annal of what was kept, *hosen re hethent … eth hosen nath re hether* (from S03). Seren's first play, and the leaf's spine: *keth* comes back with the gate no one kept, the songs that held it, the stone that kept its vow, and whoever holds it now.
- "Here is" is the copula with ***sy***, "this", as subject (I.1's *El gor yalat vennuld ull*). "the founders" is ***et galdardath hosast***, "the first builders" (Tier 3 §5.2, already one). The "we" who kept it not is the people (1pl), not the pair.

> Et flenn hosast, re ryte {{Aldwena}} o um halflenn ul et orlath hosast, ul dolmath lyss hosen fymmetath.

Its first leaf {{Aldwena}} cut on slate in the First Days, in letters no bigger than grains of barley.

Word for word: *The first leaf, Aldwena cut it on slate in the first days, in letters small as seeds.*

- The fronted object taken up by *o* (pilot I.1 G6); ***re ryte***, the dual: a pair-name takes a dual verb (§3.10).
- **"letters" are *tolmath*, "stones"**: every letter is a stone (§1). Aldwena's letters are stones no bigger than seeds. The lexicon has no barley; *fymmet*, "seed, a sown thing", carries the grain.
- ***ul et orlath hosast***, "in the first days" (the Reckoning's age of the Keep): the article keeps *ul* from bedding *orlath* into *morlath*.

### The founders' leaf

*The words of Aldwena, as Halyna read them out of the Hal and Seren set them down, unmended. In Seren's ink; the words are theirs. The English is in blockquotes because the Book sets it so.*

> Cadhan sy, olna sa re hadhan et ganna.

> We two set this down, that laid the gate.

Word for word: *We two lay this, we two who laid the gate.*

- **The leaf is in the living tongue** because Halyna read it out in the living tongue, and Seren wrote what they read: the ruling of wf8 S02 for the Rite (Halyna "tell it here in the living tongue, and Seren writes what they told"). On the slate it is the Hal, word-signs with no endings and no small words (§4.5, §6.9); see note 3.
- To set down a line is to lay it: ***Cadhan sy***, "we two lay this". *sa re hadhan*: the relative of a pair, in the dual.

> Dem susk um et sorth re vellorar, eth re wesker et sorth treskat hy o wisk dem o rivull, hosen amm re omm hesp strom umo. Ul et tresk re yaldar kethow hy dholm et sorth sost. Re stannant et stannowvardath et tolm; eth re ryt et hosast hyont voll ul hosk et bystir rerdsennen, eth re lusk o et tolm ul vimm o lo o hassan.

> Up onto the ridge we came, and found it cleft from its crown to its root, as though a great blade had come down on it. In the cleft we made a hold of the ridge's own stone. The wardens of the quarry cut the stone, and the first of them cut his name in the lintel of the western stair, and left the stone below it for his sons.

Word for word: *Up onto the ridge we came, and we found the ridge riven from its head to its foundation, as when a great sword fell upon it. In the cleft we built a keep out of the stone of the ridge itself. The quarry-wardens quarried the stone; and the first of them cut a name in the lintel of the western stair, and he let the stone under it be, at his sons.*

- ***re vellorar***, "we came", is the plural: all the first builders. "We two" is kept for the pair (*Cadhan*, *re hadhan*).
- "from its crown to its root" is ***hy o wisk dem o rivull***, "from its head to its foundation" (*bisk* softened is *wisk*; *visk* would be the foot). *Rivull* is the bottom course of a wall: the ridge is spoken of as a work.
- ***hosen amm re omm hesp strom umo*** and ***hy dholm et sorth sost*** are from S03 (there *tolm terril*, ash-grey; v3 says only "the ridge's own stone").
- ***Re stannant et stannowvardath et tolm***: the quarry-wardens *quarry* (*stann*, the root of *Stannard*). ***hosk et bystir rerdsennen***, "the lintel of the western stair", is Kael's oath-stone (pilot I.1).
- "left the stone below it for his sons" is ***re lusk o et tolm ul vimm o lo o hassan***, "he let the stone under it be, at his sons": *lusk*, "loose, let be", the word the Keep itself takes later (*Nath re yal o veth luskat*), and the having idiom (*lo*): the blank is theirs. "His name" is *voll* alone, since *o voll* could not show its bite (G9).

> Lo olna yemm garl re hadhan hosk et ganna ulvenn. Ul vimm o re ryten olna rellor, eth olna dhragul lo o sest, ul vodh es vesk nahos pawast o, somm ston et hald.

> With our four hands we two laid the capstone of the inner gate. Under it we cut our mark, and our vow beside it, where no eye will see it again till the wall fall.

Word for word: *With our-two four hands we two laid the capstone of the inner gate. Under it we two cut our mark, and our promise at its shoulder, where no one will see it a second time, while the wall stands.*

- ***Lo olna yemm garl***: *olna* softens *gemm* (as S03's *lo sona yemm garl sost*). ***hosk et ganna ulvenn*** is the Book's one form (Tier 3 §5.2 no. 6).
- "mark" is ***rellor***, "a sign, a mark" (the lexicon's; one word for the pair's mark in every leaf of this unit). After the dual verb *olna* can only be "our", though *r* takes no bite.
- **"vow" is *tragul*, "promise", throughout II.1** (and *ryt*, "a cut vow", inside Jack's formula and in the Covenant's name): the possessor shows on it (*olna dhragul*, *o dhragul*, *ol dragul*), and S03's hinge line turns on that. See note 1.
- **"till the wall fall" is *somm ston et hald*, "while the wall stands".** "Until" before a clause has no clean form (*dem amm* would bed into *dem mamm*, pilot I.1 §3), and the founders' own next words are *Es ston et hald sy*: the leaf runs from *somm ston et hald* into the vow. The same English comes back in II.3 and the Epilogue: one form (note 1).

> Es ston et hald sy. Re hadhan o, eth tessen o, somm el tolm tolm. Ston.

> This wall will stand. We two laid it, and we two answer for it, while stone is stone. It stands.

Word for word: *Will stand the wall this. We two laid it, and we two answer [for] it, while is stone stone. It stands.*

- **The Stonwryt, canon, letter for letter** (`orrowen_v2` §11.6 (b); Tier 3 §5). On the slate and under the capstone it is the Hal, §11.6 (a), *ESAN STONOS ETAS XALDOS SIU · …*, which unit S01 draws on the foreword's surface. A vow is set whole, every word (§3.11 D9), which is why Seren could write the founders' words at all.

### Seren's telling, again

> Ul vimm et tolm sy doss rellor ull eth tragul ull. Nath noss o hylyl dem susk lom, hy vodh el tolm o sa vaver pa. Desir, amm doss nahos lo et vorr, re hadhom en narl ul vimm o, eth re fennym sona rellor lo hos nind.

Under this stone are that mark and that vow. Turn it I cannot, for it is a stone that taketh two. Late, when none is at the fire, I have put mine hand under it, and traced their mark with one finger.

Word for word: *Under this stone there is that mark and that promise. Its turning up is not at me, for it is a stone that needs two. Late, when no one is at the fire, I have laid my hand under it, and I followed their mark with one finger.*

- "Turn it I cannot" is the "at" idiom of ability, negated (§3.7): ***Nath noss o hylyl dem susk lom***, "its turning up is not at me". *Kyl* is "change" before it is "turn", and bare (*o hylyl lom*) it was read "I cannot change it", a vow she may not alter; *dem susk*, "up", makes it a stone turned over, face to the light, as the pair turn it in the snow (back-translation fix). **Seren's play:** *kyl* is "turn", and the dry cut's NOT is *tolm kylet*, "the turned stone" (§7.6). The stone she cannot turn is the one that says *not* to her.
- ***el tolm o sa vaver pa***, "it is a stone that needs two", from S03 ¶9. "traced" is ***fenn***, "follow". ***Desir***, "late, after the time".
- **The hands are counted.** Their four hands (*yemm garl*), her one finger (*hos nind*), and, at the end, her two hands (*pa yarl*) on the stone that needs two.

> Stonwryt, vollant et prassath o: *ryt hoskat ul hal, nethilet lo vyskorn eth prassoth*, eth nath lenull styvil o. Veth re runnant prassyannath et cummath, um serull, so rellorath, eth re rikent uldath et serull hosen re rikent et rellor: *osk, hoskat ul dolm*. Nath noss rellor lom. Es rytym hos nafedh.

The Stonwryt the masters call it: *a vow sealed in living rock, earned by toil and skill*, and no gold buyeth it. Yet the master-pairs of the havens struck their marks on coin, and men took the coin as they took the mark: *trust, sealed in stone*. I have no mark. Never shall I cut one.

Word for word: *Stonwryt, the masters call it: a vow lintelled in bedrock, earned by toil and mastery; and gold does not buy it. But the master-pairs of the havens struck, upon coin, their marks; and men took the coin as they took the mark: trust, lintelled in stone. No mark is at me. I will cut one never.*

- **Jack's two lore lines are quoted formulas** (voice rule: "set as quoted formulas after a colon"), in the masters' own words and in italics, as the English sets them: ***ryt hoskat ul hal, nethilet lo vyskorn eth prassoth*** (from S03 ¶4, where it was *El ryt o, …*) and ***osk, hoskat ul dolm*** (S03 ¶5). *Hosk* is "seal" and "lay the lintel": the vow is lintelled in the living rock (*hal*), and the coin's trust in stone.
- "no gold buyeth it" is ***nath lenull styvil o***: *styvil*, "gold, the metal" (S03's coin was *saed styvil*); *bell* is the colour.
- ***re runnant***, "struck": *drunn*, the mallet, softened (*dr-* > *r-*), as S03. ***prassyannath***, "master-pairs" (O1979: a master-pair is a master-gate). *um serull* stands between commas so that *et cummath so rellorath* is not read as a construct with an article on its head (§3.2; the analyzer's guard).
- "I have no mark. Never shall I cut one." is ***Nath noss rellor lom. Es rytym hos nafedh.***: the future with *nafedh*, "at no time", as the pilots' plain past takes it (pilot IV.4); the grammar has no *nath es*.

> Ul vimm voll et pard hosast re rytent o hassan so vollath, hos ul vimm ullen; eth ul vimm et dasken doss et tolm dasorat. Doss Halvard Tolmvard hy dhrenn ull, eth doss Rhyna Yanna Ulvenn hy dhrenn {{Aldwena}}. Ew hos pa dhrenn, sell tevar hy sona hos tevow.

Under the first warden's name his sons cut theirs, one beneath another, and beneath the last the stone is smooth. Of that line is Halvard Stone-Warden, and Rhyna of the Inner Gate is of {{Aldwena}}'s. Two lines were one, long ere those two lay in their one cradle.

Word for word: *Under the name of the first warden his sons cut their names, one beneath another; and under the last the stone is dressed smooth. Halvard Stone-Warden is from that course, and Rhyna of the Inner Gate is from Aldwena's course. Two courses were one, long before their-two one cradle.*

- ***hos ul vimm ullen*** (S03-06); ***dasorat***, "rubbed smooth, dressed": a dressed face waiting for a name (the Epilogue cuts Halvard's there).
- **A line of descent is a course** (*trenn*, S03-20/21): "Two lines were one" is the copula, ***Ew hos pa dhrenn***, "one were two courses". *trenn* is also a tale, and a column of people: the leaf's second spine.
- ***sell tevar hy sona hos tevow***, "long before their one cradle" (O3005).

> Hy ull re nayyst et lunt dem et tavowath dhaedh, eth et Stonwrytan ul o sestow. Tevar hy so orrol, re hadhant et galdardath hosast halflenneth et Kethow eth o hulter hosast ul et vestul domm, hosen cadh galdard o yarlelath ul hedh. Um et orrow re yaldant Tavow Hemm, sa vollant uldath et cumm hemm sost. El et hemm sost o ul molt et cummath hosel.

Then went the folk down to the warm harbours, and the guild after them. Ere they went, the founders laid the Keep's slates and its first staff in the black chest, as masons lay by their tools at dusk. On the coast they raised Eldhythe, that men call the Eldest. It is the eldest only of the havens.

Word for word: *Then the people climbed down to the warm harbours, and the guild at its back. Before their going, the first builders laid the slates of the Keep and its first staff in the black chest, as a builder lays his tools at dusk. Upon the shore they built the Old Landing, which men call the eldest haven. It is the eldest among the havens alone.*

- **"went down" is *nayyst*, "climb down"** (the reversative of *gyst*): the folk *un-climb*. Their children climb back (*re yyster*, after Glasspire).
- **One verb lays the slates, the tools and the tale**: ***re hadhant … hosen cadh galdard o yarlelath ul hedh***. A builder lays by his tools as a teller lays a tale (*Cadhom o …*). *garlel*, "a tool, a small hand"; *ul hedh*, "at dusk".
- ***et vestul domm***, "the black chest"; ***hulter hosast***, "the first staff" (I.4's and III.2's staff). ***Tavow Hemm*** and ***et cumm hemm sost*** are S03's (O2910): Eldhythe's own name already says "old", and Seren lets the eldest be eldest ***ul molt et cummath hosel***, "among the havens alone": *hosel* after its noun is "only" (as *luth hosel*, "but ink"), and the Keep it does not name is older. S03's *Nel et hemm sost o veth ul molt et cummath* was read "it is not the eldest, but one among the havens", the opposite (back-translation fix).

> Ul sestow et dasken hyont, sa nath noss so vollath um halflenn, re vellor et helvhoss ul vimm et ganna sa re heth nahos.

Behind the last of them, whose names are on no slate, the wind went in under the gate that no one kept.

Word for word: *Behind the last of them, whose names are not upon slate, the wind came in under the gate that no one held.*

- ***sa nath noss so vollath um halflenn***: the relative with its possessor resumed (pilot I.1 G5). ***helvhoss***, "the wind, the sky's breath" (I.1).
- "the gate that no one kept" is ***et ganna sa re heth nahos***: *keth* again. The holding-place no one held.

> Re omm o nafedh. Nath re yal o veth luskat.

It had never fallen. It had only been left.

Word for word: *It fell never. It was not but let be.*

- From S03 ¶7, unchanged: *nafedh* after the plain past, and "only" of a clause as *nath … veth* (G8). *lusk*, the Captain's third call.

> Hy dhumol re orr o, eth nath re heth hos o veth et velleth. Nath re yyst ganna dem susk, dem glennyl et *brellirlodh*, eth re wesk et helvnynth gor wrellir. Vallom et brod ull. Ul sestow et helvnynth re vellor et frenn, eth ul sestow et frenn, et crest.

Out of mind it went, till only the songs held it. No pair came up to *point* its mortar, and the rain found every joint. I love that word. After the rain came the frost, and after the frost the ice.

Word for word: *From memory it went, and no one held it but the songs. No pair climbed up to mend the joint-mortar, and the rain found every joint. I love that word. After the rain came the frost, and after the frost, the ice.*

- ***Hy dhumol***, "from memory": the Title's *tumol*. ***nath re heth hos o veth et velleth***, "no one held it but the songs": *keth*, and the "only" of G8.
- "No pair" is ***ganna***, "a gate": a pair is a gate (S03-08).
- **The word she loves.** "to point its mortar" is ***dem glennyl et brellirlodh***, "to mend the joint-mortar" (S03, with the slender verbal noun *clennyl*). In her own tongue the word is ***brellirlodh***, *brellir* "a joint between stones" + *lodh* "mortar; the bond": **the bond lives in the joint**, and no pair came to renew it. She italicises it as the English italicises *point*.
- ***gor wrellir***, "every joint" (*gor* softens *brellir*). The last clause is verbless, as the English: *eth ul sestow et frenn, et crest.*

> Ul et grem sa re nydh nahos, re orr hosk et ganna ulvenn dem vimm hy et pa yann.

In some winter that none told, the capstone of the inner gate came down from its two posts.

Word for word: *In the winter that no one counted, the capstone of the inner gate went down from the two posts.*

- **"none told" is *re nydh nahos*, "no one counted"**: the Book's play on *tell* (count and recount) lives in Orrowen in *nydh*, "count", the annal's own verb (an annal counts its years, and this winter is missing from the count).
- ***Ul et grem***: the article, because *ul* would bed *grem* into *nr-*, an onset the tongue forbids.
- **"its two posts" is *et pa yann***: *ganna*, the gate, is the old dual "the two posts" (§3.2), so the capstone comes down from the gate's own two.

> Re hunn nahos o ul mommol.

No one heard it fall.

Word for word: *No one heard it in falling.*

- From S03 ¶9, unchanged: *ul mommol*, "in falling" (*ul* beds *ommol*).

> Ommol et cummath, re hunn Orrow tunn o. Ul sestow Sirrvell re yyster dem et reskow hosel sa es geth, hosen re vyskent et velleth; eth re wesker dhenn.

The Fall of the havens the whole coast heard. After Glasspire we climbed to the one place the songs said would hold, and found a shadow.

Word for word: *The Fall of the havens, the whole Shore heard it. After Glasspire we climbed to the only place that would hold, as the songs said; and we found a shadow.*

- ***Ommol et cummath***, "the Fall of the havens" (*Ommol*, the name sense of O1869), fronted and taken up by *o*: **one root turns the two sentences**, *ul mommol* (no one heard) and *Ommol* (all heard).
- ***dem et reskow hosel sa es geth, hosen re vyskent et velleth*** is S03 ¶8's (*keth* once more: "would hold"). ***dhenn***, "a shadow" (S03).

> Re yald et nadhumol galat sa nath re yal o yaldol lo wynd fedh.

Forgetting had done what no enemy ever could.

Word for word: *Forgetting did a thing whose doing was not ever at an enemy.*

- From S03 ¶8, unchanged. *gald* is "build" and "do": Forgetting *built* what no enemy could. *nadhumol*, "un-remembering", the Title's word turned by *na-*.

> Ul hedh orl et movenn, re vellora Halvard eth Rhyna ul vimm et ganna ulvenn, eth et trenn ul sona sestow. Virr ul sona sestow re vellorom, lo et nynthsast. Nath re seskym sona vollath.

At dusk on the day of the banner, Halvard and Rhyna came under the inner gate with the column at their backs. Close behind came I with the water-skin. Their names I wist not.

Word for word: *At the dusk of the day of the banner, Halvard and Rhyna came in under the inner gate, and the column at their-two back. Near at their back I came in, with the water-skin. I did not know their names.*

- **The cradle-names, with the dual.** "Their names I wist not": Seren cannot yet write the pair-name, so the leaf does not. ***re vellora Halvard eth Rhyna***: a dual verb with its two names for subject.
- ***orl et movenn***, "the day of the banner"; ***et trenn***, "the column" (a column of people is a course, I.1).
- ***nynthsast***, "a water-skin" (an addition: *nynth* "water" + *sast* "hide", as *helvnynth*). "wist" is ***sesk***, "know a fact"; *keth* would be "know by holding".

> Ul et ganna re yal et hosk ul firryl. Re rasor Halvard et sulv hyo lo o hovvel. Um o wisk re dhev et feth, stacorn hosen frenn um yann.

In the gateway lay the capstone. Halvard swept the snow from it with his sleeve. On its upper face the bloom was grown as pale as frost on a gatepost.

Word for word: *In the gate the capstone was lying. Halvard rubbed the snow from it with his sleeve. On its head the moss had grown, pale as frost upon a post.*

- ***ul firryl***, "lying" (S03). "swept" is ***rasor*** (*dasor* "rub smooth" softened): the lexicon has no sweep, and *dasor* is the verb of the smooth stone under the last name. ***covvel***, "a sleeve, a small cloak": the Torn Cloak's word.
- "the bloom" is ***feth***, moss (the lexicon has no lichen; *sinth* is a flower). "its upper face" is ***o wisk***, "its head, its top": the stone lies face down, its vow to the earth.
- ***hosen frenn um yann***, "as frost upon a post": the frost that rived the Keep comes back as the picture, and *gann*, "a post", is one of the two the gate is made of.

> Re lumma sona ul sulv, eth re hadha sona yemm garl ul vimm o. Re hyle sona o, hosen kylent sugornath ul Gemm Hyll sulter sa re yal mammat sell.

They kneeled down in the snow and set their four hands under it. They turned it as fishers of Tidesmeet turn a boat long given up for lost.

Word for word: *They two knelt in snow, and they two laid their four hands under it. They two turned it, as fishers in Tidesmeet turn a boat that was long lost.*

- *sona* after each dual verb: a dual verb always has a subject (§3.4), and *re lumma* alone sounds like the plural imperative. ***sona yemm garl***, their four hands.
- ***ul Gemm Hyll*** (wf8 S04's: the construct would give *Hemm Hyll*, which clashes with *hemm* "old"). ***sulter***, "a boat"; "long given up for lost" is ***sa re yal mammat sell***.

> Ew sona dholm o.

It was their own.

Word for word: *It was their-two stone.*

- **The hinge, plain** (voice rule: "Plain at the hinge"): the copula, predicate first. "Their own" is *their stone*: Aldwena's line laid it and Halvard's fathers cut it.

> Re heth et vimmlern rellor {{Aldwena}}, eth et tragul. Ew gand so, hosen ul et orl amm re yalant rytet. Re yal dest wresketh et pardath lo so sest. Re hadh Rhyna ey nindeth um rellor ey vadullath. Re hadh Halvard nind strom ul nest o yedheth.

Its underside bare the mark of {{Aldwena}}, and the vow. Sharp they were, as on the day they were cut. Beside them was the bite of the wardens' chisels. Rhyna laid her fingers on the mark of her forebears. Halvard set his thumb in the bite his fathers left.

Word for word: *The underside held the mark of Aldwena, and the promise. Sharp were they, as on the day when they were cut. The bite of the chisels of the wardens was at their shoulder. Rhyna laid her fingers upon the mark of her forebears. Halvard laid a thumb in the bite of his fathers.*

- "bare" is ***keth***, "held". "Sharp they were" is the copula, which already puts its predicate first: ***Ew gand so***.
- ***dest wresketh et pardath*** is S03's *dest wresketh et stannowvardath*, with *pard*, "warden", as v3 shortens it. ***ey vadullath*** / ***o yedheth***, "her forebears" / "his fathers" (S03: *ey* never mutates).
- ***nind strom***, "a thumb, the great finger" (an addition); "his" is left to the next phrase, since *o nind* could not show its bite (G9).

> Ew tunn o. Ul et parow re vellor et lunt tul. Nath re yaldant et misketh hunnat um et sulv.

It was whole. Out in the ward the folk came in yet. Their feet made no sound on the snow.

Word for word: *It was whole. In the ward the people came in still. The feet did not make a sound upon the snow.*

- ***Ew tunn o*** (S03). ***parow***, "the ward" (I.1). ***hunnat***, "a sound, a heard thing".

> Re heth et tolm sost o dhragul. Ew ol sa nath re hether ol dragul.

The stone kept its vow. It was we who did not keep ours.

Word for word: *The stone itself kept its promise. It was we who did not keep our promise.*

- **From S03 ¶10, unchanged.** *tragul* shows both possessors, softened for the stone (*o dhragul*) and bedded for us (*ol dragul*): the two lines differ by one letter. *et tolm sost*, "the stone itself", parts the construct the analyzer would otherwise read.

> Somm re yal et hedh lo et sulv tul, re harma sona et Stonwrytan dem susk.

Ere the light was off the snow, they cried the guild to its feet.

Word for word: *While the last light was still at the snow, they two called the guild up.*

- ***hedh***, "the last light, dusk": the banner's own dusk. With *lo*: *doss X um Y* is "Y feels X" (§3.7).
- "they cried the guild to its feet" is ***re harma sona et Stonwrytan dem susk***: *carm*, the Cry's word (I.2 tells the Cry, which ends *Talda!*).

> Amm re yal et Ternil tunnat, re dhald et tolm clenn dem et ganna. Et tolm hemm, re lymment et vennuldath o ul et hellur sy, eth re hadhant o ul baltur hald odh.

When the Rite was done, the new stone went up into the gate. The old stone the True Men bare into this hall, and laid in the niche of the hearth-wall.

Word for word: *When the Rite was completed, the new stone rose to the gate. The old stone, the True Men carried it into this hall, and laid it in the niche of the hearth-wall.*

- S03 ¶11, with the True Men for subject, as v3 has it (I.4 tells the new stone set mark down). ***re dhald***, "rose" (*tald*), for "went up". ***ul baltur hald odh*** (S03).

> Gor sa heth o, keth tragul et galdardath hosast, eth kaemul so dhevath. Vess sy cadhom pa yarl umo.

They that hold it hold the vow of the first builders, and the shame of their children. Tonight it lieth under my two hands.

Word for word: *Whoever holds it, holds the promise of the first builders, and the shame of their children. This night I lay two hands upon it.*

- ***Gor sa heth o, keth …*** is S03's close, with *tragul*.
- **"Tonight it lieth under my two hands" is *Vess sy cadhom pa yarl umo*, "tonight I lay two hands upon it".** The stone that needs two lies under the two hands of one; and her laying of hands is the next line's laying of the tale (*Cadhom o …*). *en ba yarl*, "my two hands", is avoided: whether *pa*, once bedded by *en*, still softens is unsettled, and the analyzer does not read it (note 2).

> Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

- The laying formula in the singular (O0238; Tier 3 §5.1): Seren lays II.1 herself. No ink but her own.

> *Eth re vysk et odh:* **Tumar.**

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

- *Tumar* is canon; the frame as pilot I.1 sets it (Tier 3 §5.1).

## II.3 · *LODHAN ETH TOLMATH VARDH*, OF THE BONDED AND THE THEOLITHS

### Title

> ## *Lodhan eth Tolmath Vardh*

**II.3 · Of the Bonded and the Theoliths**

*The Bonded and the stones of God.* (Lexicon O1225, unchanged. *Tolmath Vardh* is a construct, so it never takes the article.)

### The dateline

> *Molt syst hosast, vess hoskol {{Enrella}}.*

*The first high summer, {{Enrella}}'s sealing night.*

Word for word: *The heart of the first summer, the night of the sealing of Enrella.*

- "high summer" is ***molt syst***, "the heart of summer" (O2960). *hosast* after it makes it the heart of the first summer, which is the first high summer.
- ***Enrella*** is a new pair-name by the canon's rule (§3.10): *En-* (Enno) + softened *Dell-* (*d* > *r*) + the lintel *-a*, exactly as *Idrenna* (*Id-* + S·*Denn-* + *-a*). An addition, with *Enno* and *Della*.

### Seren's headnote

> *Ryst ull re hoska {{Idrenna}} Enno eth Della ul vimm et ganna ulvenn, et hoskol hosast ul et Kethow hy et galdardath hosast; eth re hethym et greller. Vess ull re hadha {{Halyna}} sy lo et tevath, eth re vessa sona lom rellorol cedh re vysk vodh, hy vodh vallant tevath seskyl.*

*That morning {{Idrenna}} had sealed Enno and Della under the inner gate, the first sealing at the Keep since the founders, and I held the lamp. That night {{Halyna}} laid this for the children, and would have me mark whose words are whose, for children like to know.*

Word for word: *That morning Idrenna sealed Enno and Della under the inner gate, the first sealing in the Keep since the first builders; and I held the lamp. That night Halyna laid this for the children, and asked of me the marking of who said what, for children love knowing.*

- ***re hoska***: *hosk*, "seal; lay the lintel": the sealing is the lintel laid on two posts (§3.10). ***eth re hethym et greller***: *keth*; Seren holds the lamp.
- **"would have me mark" is *re vessa sona lom*, "they two asked of me"**, and *vess* is also "night": ***Vess ull … re vessa***. The canon's own homophony, which pilot I.1's headnote lets stand (*ul et vess hosast sa re vess et odh*).
- "whose words are whose" is ***cedh re vysk vodh***, "who said what" (v1.3's "whose paragraph is whose" was *cedh re wrodh gor dhrenn*).

### Halyna's telling, to the children

> *Lo sona yarl um et tolm, re hadha sona et tolm hosast:*

*With their hands on the stone they began it:*

Word for word: *With their-two hands upon the stone, they two laid the first stone:*

- **"began" is *cadh et tolm hosast***, "lay the first stone" (O2859): with their hands on the stone, the stone works twice. Its fellow closes the leaf, *re hadha sona et hosk umo*, "they laid the capstone upon it". Seren's line, fully italic: no ink of Halyna's.

**¶1** *(first ink)*

> Kethen et tolm olen,

"We hold the stone together,"

**¶2** *(second ink)*

> hosen kethen gor yalat.

"as we hold all things."

Word for word: *We two hold the stone together, / as we two hold everything.*

- wf8 S02's I.4 opening, *Kethen et tolm olen, hosen kethen gor yalat* (O2992), split at its wedge for two voices as the Book splits it: the first ink to the comma, the second the rest (§3.9 item 5). The ink labels are the builder's alternation, before the Book's `MARKED`.

> **Rhyna.** Darra virr, gor hos, ul vodh doss weskyl va lerneth lo et vorr. Es noss et lern lo et tevath, eth doss lunn rernyl lo et hemman, amm dossant velv. Ryst sy re veskys Enno eth Della: re lumma pa, eth re dhald hos. Amm sy el {{Enrella}} sona.

**Rhyna.** Come closer, all of you, where the fire can find your faces. The children shall have the front, and the old may listen, if they be quiet. This morning ye saw Enno and Della kneel down two and rise up one. Now are they {{Enrella}}.

Word for word: *Rhyna. Come near, every one, where the finding of your faces is at the fire. The front will be at the children, and the leave of listening is at the old, if they are quiet. This morning you saw Enno and Della: two knelt, and one rose. Now they two are Enrella.*

- "where the fire can find your faces" is the idiom of ability (§3.7), ***ul vodh doss weskyl va lerneth lo et vorr***.
- ***Es noss et lern lo et tevath***: the having idiom in the future, "the front will be at the children". ***doss lunn rernyl lo et hemman, amm dossant velv*** is from S04 v1.3.
- **"kneel down two and rise up one" is *re lumma pa, eth re dhald hos***: **the grammar does the sealing.** The dual verb kneels (*lumma*, with *pa*, "two"), and the singular rises (*dhald*, with *hos*, "one").
- ***Amm sy el Enrella sona***: the pair-name as the copula's predicate, "they two" its subject.

> *Re vess nemmel lonta: Vodh doss umos? Re vysk Enno: El hosen hos uld; eth re dhunn Della o: lo yemm garl, eth nahos ullen dem nastathulol. Es naldan lest hos gebb, re vyske sona, eth es ryten olna rellor ul vimm o dholm hosast, hy vodh nath noss tovvol et dasken lona. Re dhyvull et odh. Hy ull re vess et nemmel lom: Amm vodh es noss hoskol loth? Nath re yal tessyl lom, eth re serym.*

*A small girl asked them how it felt. "It is like being one person," said Enno, and Della ended it: "with four hands and no excuses." They would build a house of one door, they said, and cut their mark under its first stone, for they could not wait for the last. The hearth laughed. Then the girl asked me when I should be sealed. I had no answer, and wrote on.*

Word for word: *A little girl asked them two: What is upon you? Enno said: It is like one person; and Della made it whole: with four hands, and no other to blame. We two will build a house of one door, they said, and we two will cut our mark under its first stone, for the waiting for the last is not at us two. The hearth laughed. Then the girl asked me: When will a sealing be at you? An answer was not at me, and I went on.*

- "how it felt" is ***Vodh doss umos?***, "what is upon you?": a feeling is laid on a person (§3.7). The speeches are given as spoken (§6.7).
- **"Della ended it" is *re dhunn Della o*, "Della made it whole"**: the verb of the Book's one form for "one of them said, and the other finished" (*re vysk hos, eth re dhunn ullen*, O2984).
- **"no excuses" is *nahos ullen dem nastathulol*, "no other one to blame"**: with four hands there is no one else to blame, and the Bonded have no other, for *ullen* is the word Rhyna will use for the other of a pair (*et garl ullen*, the other hand). *Nastathul*, "blame", is "un-praise" (the reversative). The lexicon has no excuse; *pessyl*, "a plea", would soften the jest; and the bare *nahos ullen* of the draft was read "and no one else", which lost it (back-translation fix).
- ***Es naldan lest hos gebb***: *es* beds *gald* (*naldan*); a house of one door is the Bonded's one way in. "for they could not wait for the last" is ***hy vodh nath noss tovvol et dasken lona***: the first stone against the last, the capstone, where the guild cuts its marks.
- **"and wrote on" is *eth re serym*, "and I went on"**: *ser*, the word the guild hears in her name (§4.6). She says it of herself twice in this leaf, here and at the cloak. Seren's own ink.

> **Halvard.** New Stonwrytan gor Orrowan, veth ew Orrowan gor Stonwrytan. Carm et Stonwrytan tev ul et tevow, hy odh sy ell hy odh ull; eth hos fedh, myskent et scaevureleth, hy lest vernul ul Gethow Stellath. Re nahebb odha ell gedh o nafedh, hy vodh re heth et Stonwrytan vennoth Orrow hosen re heth haldath Orrow.

**Halvard.** Not all Shorelanders were Stonewrights, yet all Stonewrights were Shorelanders. To the guild a child is called in the cradle, from any hearth, and once, the rolls say, from a gaoler's house in Carnhold. No mother nor father ever refused it, for the guild kept the faith of the land as it kept its walls.

Word for word: *Halvard. The guild was not all the Shore-folk, but the Shore-folk were all the guild. The guild calls a child in the cradle, from this hearth or from that; and once, say the rolls, from the house of a gaoler in Carnhold. No mother or father refused it ever, for the guild kept the faith of the Shore as it kept the walls of the Shore.*

- The chiasmus, the gaoler, the refusal and the faith are S04 v1.3's (*New Stonwrytan gor Orrowan, veth ew Orrowan gor Stonwrytan*; *re nahebb … nafedh*; *vennoth Orrow hosen re heth haldath Orrow*), cut to v3's length.
- **"is called" is *carm***, "call, cry out": the guild calls a child as it cries the Cry. "from any hearth" is ***hy odh sy ell hy odh ull***: *gor* after a preposition would soften to *yor* (pilot I.1 §3).
- ***et scaevureleth***, "the rolls" (O2964). ***Gethow Stellath***, Carnhold bedded after *ul* (the canon's broad *-ath*, which the analyzer notes and does not fault).

> **Rhyna.** Kethen: re lodh Mardh gor yanna ul hos, amm nath re hossa sona tul. Orrant veskerdeth ul molt et tevowath lo reller, eth ferrent, eth veskent galat sa re yal rytet sell tevar hy ol devyl. Nath naelurant. El Lodh Helv sy. Um o dhevow eth um en devow re ferr veskerd hosen ull, eth hy vess ull re firren ul hos, ul lest o odha eth ul lest en modha, hos eth ullen. Re aedhen hos vess ul ba lest nafedh, ul olna hossol tunn. Rellora sy, hy vodh es maverys o: doss et lodh galdat tevar hy et tevow; vesk veskerd o ul et tevow; eth nath noss o tunn veth lo et hoskol.

**Rhyna.** We hold that God made each pair one ere either drew breath. The seers go among the cradles with a lamp, and bend low, and read what was written before ever we were born. They choose not. This is the Aetherbond. Over his cradle and mine a seer bent so, and from that night we lay in one, in his mother's house and in mine by turns. Never in our lives have we two slept a night under different roofs. Mark this, for ye will need it: the bond is made before the cradle, read in the cradle, and made whole only at the sealing.

Word for word: *Rhyna. We two hold [it]: God mortared every pair into one, when they two had not yet breathed. Seers go among the cradles with a lamp, and bow, and read the thing that was written long before our birth. They do not choose. That is the Aetherbond. Upon his cradle and upon my cradle a seer bowed so; and from that night we two lay in one, in the house of his mother and in the house of my mother, by turns. We two slept one night in two houses never, in our whole life. Mark this, for you will need it: the bond is made before the cradle; a seer reads it in the cradle; and it is not whole but at the sealing.*

- **"We hold that" is *Kethen:***, the verb of the stone (*Kethen et tolm*) for the creed, then a colon, for Orrowen has no "that" before a clause (pilot I.1). ***re lodh Mardh***: God *mortars* the pair, and *Lodh Helv* is "the mortar of the sky". ***amm nath re hossa sona tul***, "when they two had not yet breathed" (S04 v1.3).
- **One verb bows**: ***ferr***, "bow, bend low, lean, nod". The seers bend low (*ferrent*), Halvard leans and the children with him, and Idrenna's nod is *ferr*, withheld and then given. In English three gestures; in Orrowen one.
- ***lo reller***: *lo* softens *greller* (*g* before *r* drops). ***Nath naelurant***: *naelur*, "choose" (S04).
- ***o dhevow eth … en devow***, "his cradle and mine": the softening and the bedding of one word, a letter apart. ***en modha***: *en* beds *odha*.
- ***ul ba lest***, "in two houses" (S04 v1.3). "made … read … made whole": a participle *vesket* would be heard as *vesket*, "a colour", so the middle clause has a verb: ***vesk veskerd o ul et tevow***.

> **Halvard.** Ew {{Idrenna}} olna Dholmath Vardh, sa reske lo et gebb vess sy. Re ammada sona olna stinyl, eth cadhol, eth rhynyl, eth ammelyl, eth kethyl nydhyl ul et Hal. Et ryt, re volla sona o lona: Ryt Dhunnoth, eth nayalat ullen; eth nath hebben galat ullen. Es hyrrilys o, amm doss o los. Es ferre {{Idrenna}} amm cadhan sy venn.

**Halvard.** Our Theoliths were {{Idrenna}}, that sit by the door tonight. They taught us to cut and lay and set, to pray, and to keep the count in the old hand. The vow they named to us, the Covenant of Completion, and no more; and no more give we. Ye will understand it when it is yours to understand. {{Idrenna}} will nod when we two tell it right.

Word for word: *Halvard. Idrenna were our-two stones of God, who sit at the door tonight. They two taught us two cutting, and laying, and setting, and praying, and keeping the count in the Hal. The vow, they two named it to us two: the Covenant of Completion, and nothing other; and we two give no other thing. You will understand it when it is at you. Idrenna will nod when we two lay this true.*

- ***Ew Idrenna olna Dholmath Vardh*** (S04 v1.3). ***rhynyl***, "setting fast": the verb in Rhyna's name (§4.6), said by Halvard.
- "in the old hand" is ***ul et Hal***, as II.1's headnote. ***Ryt Dhunnoth*** is canon, and its words stay withheld.
- "and no more; and no more give we" is ***eth nayalat ullen; eth nath hebben galat ullen***: *ullen* twice, for the English's twice.
- ***amm doss o los*** (S04). ***amm cadhan sy venn***: Kael's "lay this plumb" (pilot I.1, *es gadhom sy venn*); *cadhan o venn* would read *venn* as a softened noun after *o*.

> *Lo et gebb re reske {{Idrenna}}, bisk sulv lo wisk sulv. Nath re ferre sona.*

*By the door {{Idrenna}} sat with their two white heads together. They nodded not.*

Word for word: *At the door Idrenna sat, white head at white head. They two did not nod.*

- **"with their two white heads together" is *bisk sulv lo wisk sulv*, "white head at white head"**: the second head is softened by the first's *lo*, so the mutation is the touch. (*sona pa visk* would set *pa* after a softening possessive, *va*.)
- ***Nath re ferre sona***: the nod withheld. Seren's own ink.

> **Rhyna.** Lo et hoskol mysk et Stonwrytan ul garm galat sa re vysk et tevow hosast. Cadhant et prassath hos voll um vana, galdat hy vana, hosen doss ganna galdat hy pa yann eth hy dholm cadhat umonta. Eth hebbent lo voll et rellor sa re veskent et veskerdeth: voll lo wrodow, eth rellor lo dholm. Nath ryt hos o veth sona yemm garl, eth es gresk ganna ullen o nafedh. Hosen ull re yalan hoskat, eth vollat {{Halyna}}. El humm Hyna o, eth el verr Halvard en; eth ew hos ganna olna sell tevar hy hummoth ell verryth.

**Rhyna.** At the sealing the guild saith aloud what the cradle said first. On the two the masters lay one name made of both, as a gate is made of two posts and the stone laid across them. With it they give the mark the seers read: a name for the mouth, and a mark for the stone. No hand but theirs may cut it, and no other pair will break it. So were we two sealed, and named {{Halyna}}. He is mine husband and I am his wife, and we were one pair long ere we were either.

Word for word: *Rhyna. At the sealing the guild says aloud the thing that the cradle said first. The masters lay one name upon both, made from both, as a gate is made from two posts and from a stone laid upon the two. And they give with the name the mark that the seers read: a name at the mouth, and a mark at the stone. No one cuts it but their four hands, and another pair will break it never. So we two were sealed, and named Halyna. He is the husband of Rhyna, and I am the wife of Halvard; and we two were one pair long before husbandhood or wifehood.*

- ***ul garm***, "aloud" (O-phrase); ***galat sa re vysk et tevow hosast*** (S04).
- ***hos voll um vana, galdat hy vana***, "one name upon both, made from both". **The gate explains itself** (S04 v1.3): ***hosen doss ganna galdat hy pa yann eth hy dholm cadhat umonta***.
- **"a name for the mouth, and a mark for the stone" is *voll lo wrodow, eth rellor lo dholm***: *brodow*, "the mouth, the word-place"; each half softens once after *lo*, and they chime.
- "No hand but theirs may cut it" is ***Nath ryt hos o veth sona yemm garl*** ("only", G8); "no other pair will break it" is ***es gresk ganna ullen o nafedh***.
- ***Hosen ull re yalan hoskat, eth vollat Halyna***: the pair-name received, once (notes §7.2 call 21).
- **"He is mine husband and I am his wife" is *El humm Hyna o, eth el verr Halvard en***: the two constructs laid crosswise (v1.3's), now in her first person (*Hyna*: *Rhyna* softened as possessor). "long ere we were either" is ***sell tevar hy hummoth ell verryth***: *hy humm* would also read *hy* + S·*cumm*, "from a haven".

> *Re ferr Halvard um et odh, eth re ferrent et tevath hosen o.*

*Halvard leaned to the hearth, and the children leaned with him.*

Word for word: *Halvard leaned over the hearth, and the children leaned like him.*

- *ferr* again, with ***um et odh***, "over the hearth": he bends to the ash to draw in it. *Dem et odh*, with *ferr* and *dem* in their first senses, was read "bowed as low as the hearth", a bow of honour (back-translation fix). ***hosen o***, "like him". Seren's own ink.

> **Halvard.** Doss et vorrdhuss lilv vess sy. Nenda et nind sy, hy vodh es rytym o hos fedh hosel. El olna rellor sy. Ul vess et Ternil re ryten o ul vimm et hosk clenn, ul vodh es vesk nahos pawast o, somm ston et hald. Veska hos fedh.

**Halvard.** The ash is soft tonight. Watch my finger, for I will draw it but once. This is our mark. On the night of the Rite we two cut it under the new capstone, where no eye will see it again till the wall fall. Look once.

Word for word: *Halvard. The ash is soft this night. Watch this finger, for I will draw it one time only. This is our mark. On the night of the Rite we two cut it under the new capstone, where no one will see it a second time, while the wall stands. Look one time.*

- ***vorrdhuss***, "ash, fire-dust"; ***lilv***, "soft".
- **"draw" is *ryt*, "cut letters; draw; swear"**: Halvard *cuts* the mark in the ash with the word for cutting a vow. ***hos fedh hosel***, "once only" (*hosel* after a noun). ***Nenda et nind sy***: *en nind* could be read *en* + N·*tind*.
- **"This is our mark" is *El olna rellor sy***, and *rellor* takes no bite: it reads "This is our mark" and "This mark is we two". **Left so, as a word that works twice.**
- ***et hosk clenn***, "the new capstone" (Tier 3 §5.2 no. 6). "where no eye will see it again till the wall fall" as in II.1: ***ul vodh es vesk nahos pawast o, somm ston et hald***.

### The native drawing (Halvard's mark in the ash)

- **The surface is Halyna's Stonwryt mark**, the ligature of their two cradle-names under one lintel, **HAL | RHYN** (`orrowen_v2` §6.8, "The Stonwryt ligature"), drawn with one finger in the ash. It holds no words to translate.
- **The caption is the reader's furniture, in English**, as unit S01 treats the caption of the foreword's Stonwryt. An Orrowen caption, if the Original tab wants one, is in note 4 (checked with the rest, 0 reported); it is not in the blind file.

> *Nath re varr hos tev. Ul et vorr re sol brenth.*

*No child stirred. In the fire a coal settled.*

Word for word: *Not one child stirred. In the fire a coal came to rest.*

- ***re varr***, *marr*, "shift, move; tremble". ***re sol brenth***: *sol*, "rest, lie still", the root of *sollan*, peace; *brenth*, "coal", the Captain's word. Seren's own ink.

> **Rhyna.** Amm sy dasorom o lo yarlremull, eth nath noss o. Doss vorrdhuss lo et helvhoss. Doss rellor lo dholm, eth es geth tolm.

**Rhyna.** Now I sweep it out with my palm, and it is gone. Ash is for the wind. A mark is for stone, and stone will hold.

Word for word: *Rhyna. Now I rub it smooth with a palm, and it is not. Ash is at the wind. A mark is at stone, and stone will hold.*

- "sweep it out" is ***dasorom o***, "I rub it smooth" (II.1's verb for the snow); ***garlremull***, "a palm, the hand-hollow". "and it is gone" is ***eth nath noss o***, "and it is not".
- **Belonging is the "at" idiom**: ***Doss vorrdhuss lo et helvhoss. Doss rellor lo dholm***, as R3's *voll lo wrodow, eth rellor lo dholm*. ***es geth tolm***: *keth*, the stone's own verb, last.

> **Halvard.** Um et hald ul et kethyl re veskys olna: hos galdat, eth brod nafedh. El galdat relv galdat yanna, hosen re nydhent prassath hemm o. El Hemm Halvard en, eth el Hemma Rhyna ey; eth nel hemm hy et ullen en ell ey. Re hadh ey tolm ul en sestow nafedh.

**Halvard.** On the wall in the holding ye have seen us: one work, and never a word. The work of a pair is the work of twelve, as the old masters counted it. Elder Halvard am I, and she is Elderess Rhyna, and neither is the elder of the other. There was never a stone she laid second.

- *Merge (wf12), H4:* "on the wall in the holding" is *um et hald ul et kethyl*, the holding as the hour of the siege day (O1124), as I.2 says it (S02). (The unit had *Um et hald ul gethyl re veskys olna*.)

Word for word: *Halvard. Upon the wall in the holding you have seen us two: one work, and a word never. The work of a pair is the work of twelve, as the old masters counted it. I am Elder Halvard, and she is Elderess Rhyna; and neither I nor she is older than the other. She laid a stone at my back never.*

- ***ul gethyl***, "in the holding": *et kethyl*, the hour of the siege day when the wall must stand (S02). ***hos galdat, eth brod nafedh***, verbless, as the English.
- ***El galdat relv galdat yanna***: the copula's predicate first; *delv*, the guild's twelve, softened as possessor. (v1.3's "twelvefold" was *delvoth*; v3 says "the work of twelve".)
- ***Hemm Halvard***, ***Hemma Rhyna*** (wf8 S01). *hemm* is "old" and "Elder" in one word, so "neither is the elder of the other" is the tongue's plain sense (S04). **"There was never a stone she laid second" is *Re hadh ey tolm ul en sestow nafedh***, "she never laid a stone at my back". v1.3's *tolm pawast fedh* says "a stone a second time" (*pawast fedh*, as *hos fedh* "once"), and was read so (back-translation fix). *Sestow* is "the back, the shoulder-place": she lays at his shoulder (*lo o sest*), never at his back; and *ul X sestow* is also "after X" (*ul sestow et helvnynth*), so the stone she never laid is the one laid second.

> *Re nydh luld ul lern delv lo nindeth, eth re raskant et nindeth.*

*A boy at the front counted twelve on his fingers, and ran out of fingers.*

Word for word: *A boy in front counted twelve on fingers, and the fingers ended.*

- ***luld***, "a boy"; ***ul lern***, "in front". "ran out of fingers" is ***re raskant et nindeth***: *dask*, "end", the Cry's last word. Seren's own ink.

> **Rhyna.** Amm stinym en narl ul et taldow bithlernen, ledh Halvard o um et hald bridlernen, eth es gadh o los vodh garl. Re yal myskyl umo lo vrassath hemm. Grest um hos, grest um vana.

**Rhyna.** If I cut mine hand in the south tower, he feeleth it on the north wall, and he can tell you which hand. The old masters had a saying for it. Harm to one is harm to both.

- *Merge (wf12), H9:* "the north wall" is *et hald bridlernen*, the adjective (O0156), as III.2, V.5 and VI.1 say it (S05, S08, S09); "the south tower", *et taldow bithlernen* (O2972) likewise. (The unit had *ul et taldow withlern, ledh Halvard o um et hald wridlern,*.)

Word for word: *Rhyna. When I cut my hand in the tower of the south, Halvard feels it upon the wall of the north, and he will lay to you which hand. A saying about it was at the old masters. Harm upon one, harm upon both.*

- S04 v1.3's sentence, in Rhyna's first person (*stinym*, *en narl*). ***withlern***, ***wridlern*** (S04). "he can tell you" is ***es gadh o los***: *cadh* for "tell", since the future's bedding would make *brodh* begin *mr-* (pilot I.1 §3).
- ***Re yal myskyl umo lo vrassath hemm*** (S04). ***Grest um hos, grest um vana.*** canon (§11.7), here with no signpost, as v3.

> **Halvard.** Doss et Lodhan hy ullen merr fedheth, eth deld nafedh. Nath re yal lodh cresket fedh, ul ryteth surr et Stonwrytan. Treskol, nath re lusk Mardh o.

**Halvard.** The Bonded are seldom apart, and never far. No bond was ever broken, in all the annals of the guild. Separation, God did not permit.

Word for word: *Halvard. The Bonded are apart few times, and far never. A bond was not ever broken, in the annals of the guild. Separation, God did not let it.*

- S04 v1.3, with the annals folded into the second sentence as v3 folds them. ***Treskol, nath re lusk Mardh o.*** (O2937).

> *Re senn et vorr hos nind. Re reske Enno eth Della, garl ul narl; eth re yal et nemmel ul naedh lo ey gerd.*

*The fire was down a finger's width. Enno and Della sat hand in hand. The small girl was asleep against Della's knee.*

Word for word: *The fire went out by one finger. Enno and Della sat, hand in hand; and the little girl was asleep at her knee.*

- **"The fire was down a finger's width" is *Re senn et vorr hos nind***: *senn* is "go out, as a fire in peace" and "die". The fire dies by a finger just before Rhyna tells how the Bonded die, in the same verb (*Hy ull senn et Lodhan olen*).
- ***garl ul narl***, "hand in hand" (an addition). ***lo ey gerd***, "at her knee": *Della* after any preposition would mutate (*Rella*, *Nella*), so the leaf says "her", the woman last named. Seren's own ink.

> **Rhyna.** Hy ull senn et Lodhan olen, eth nath noss ludal umona. Amm doss hos hy yanna hurrat, nath noss grestet lo et ullen, eth nath noss cathal umo. Veth el cresket et molt, hy vodh sesk o. El solat et derdsast, hy vodh doss o yaldat tunn. Thell orl, myskent uldath; nath re hoss hos hosel fedh sell hy ull. Clemeth, mysk et Stonwrytan, sa heth et nydhyl. Re hossant mest so hoss dasken ul hos clemel, bost lusul hy ullen; eth re sesk gor hos orrol ullen. Eth re fenn gor hos. Amm senn et garl ullen, ho nath senn et garl venn?

**Rhyna.** So the Bonded die together, and we two fear it not. If one of a pair be killed, there is no wound on the other, and no sickness. But broken is the heart, for it knoweth. Still is the body, for its work is done. Eight days, men say, is the longest that ever one lived on alone. Hours, say the Stonewrights, that keep the count. Many have drawn their last breath in the same moment, a mile apart, and each wist that the other was going. And each went too. When the left hand dies, does not the right one die too?

Word for word: *Rhyna. So the Bonded die together, and fear is not upon us two. When one of a pair is killed, there is no wound at the other, and no sickness upon him. But broken is the heart, for it knows. Stilled is the body, for its work is whole. Eight days, say men; no one alone ever breathed longer than that. Hours, says the guild, that keeps the count. Many breathed their last breath in one moment, a mile from the other; and each knew the going of the other. And each followed. When the other hand dies, does not the true hand die?*

- ***nath noss ludal umona***, "fear is not upon us two" (*doss ludal um*). "no wound on the other" takes ***lo***: with *um* it would read "the other feels no wound" (§3.7).
- **The English inversions are the copula's own order**: ***Veth el cresket et molt***, ***El solat et derdsast***. ***doss o yaldat tunn***, "its work is whole": *tunn*, the word of *Ryt Dhunnoth* (S04).
- ***Clemeth, mysk et Stonwrytan, sa heth et nydhyl***: the guild *keeps* (*keth*) the count. ***mest***, "many", as subject, as I.1's *merr*, "some"; *mest hy et Lodhan* would read "more than the Bonded".
- ***bost lusul hy ullen***, "a mile from the other"; ***re sesk gor hos orrol ullen*** and ***Eth re fenn gor hos***, "and each followed" (S04 v1.3).
- **Jack's line**, ***Amm senn et garl ullen, ho nath senn et garl venn?***, exactly (O2935).

> **Halvard.** Amm sy et galat dasken, eth hy ull dem lodull. Fedheth re senn hos hy dhevow tevar hy et hoskol, eth re hoss ullen, hy vodh nath re yal et lodh tunn tul. El nalodhat tev hosen sy, eth doss o hebbet lo odh lodhat, hosen sullast et odh. Keth et nalodhat hos voll, eth ul molt et Stonwrytan el lommol hos voll. Veth re vyskent prassath hemm: nath lymm Mardh et hos, veth amm lusk galdat lo et ullen.

**Halvard.** Now the last thing, and then to bed. Sometimes one of a cradle died ere the sealing, and the other lived, for the bond was not yet whole. Such a child is unbonded, and is given to a bonded hearth as its third. The unbonded keep one name, and among the guild one name is a mourning. But the old masters said: God taketh not the one without leaving the other a work to do.

Word for word: *Halvard. Now the last thing, and then to bed. Sometimes one of a cradle died before the sealing, and the other lived, for the bond was not yet whole. Such a child is unbonded, and it is given at a bonded hearth, as the third of the hearth. The unbonded one keeps one name, and among the guild one name is a mourning. But the old masters said: God does not carry off the one, but when he leaves a work at the other.*

- S04 v1.3, cut to v3: *Amm sy et galat dasken, eth hy ull dem lodull*; *hy vodh nath re yal et lodh tunn tul*; *hosen sullast et odh*; *el lommol hos voll*; the old masters' "only" (*nath … veth*, G8).
- ***tevar hy et hoskol***, "before the sealing". ***doss o hebbet***: *hebbet* is "given" and "a gift"; the unbonded child is both.

> **Rhyna.** El olna sullast Seren, sa re heth et greller ryst sy, eth sa ross ul rytyl sy. Re firre ey eth Tarnel ul hos tevow: Tarnel, hass vynull ul Gemm Hyll. Gor orl et orrol sell re wysk Tarnel piskur, bysket um wysket, somm re orr o, dem lyntheth et brunnath, amm es mellorar so. Re ammad o ey et bysket. Ul vess et hiskenn re rik et bridyth tevath mest, eth merr hy et hemman. Re firre Tarnel eth ey ul vimm hos covv. Re yern ey lo o sest.

**Rhyna.** Seren, that held the lamp this morning, and is writing this down, is our third. She lay in one cradle with Tarnel, a boatwright's son of Tidesmeet. All the long road he knotted a net as he walked, to fish the mountain tarns when we came to them. He taught her the knot. On the night of the pass the cold took many of the children, and some of the old. Tarnel and she lay down under one cloak. She woke beside him.

- *Merge (wf12), H28:* "he knotted a net as he went" is I.2's verb (S02), *re wysk … piskur*, "knotted (S·*bysk*) a net", with this leaf's own *bysket um wysket*. (The unit had *re yald Tarnel piskur, bysket um wysket,*.)

Word for word: *Rhyna. Our third is Seren, who held the lamp this morning, and who is writing this. She and Tarnel lay in one cradle: Tarnel, son of a boatwright in Tidesmeet. Every day of the long road Tarnel made a net, knot upon knot, while he went, for the meres of the mountains, when we would reach them. He taught her the knot. On the night of the pass the cold took many children, and few of the old. Tarnel and she lay down under one cloak. She woke at his shoulder.*

- ***sa ross ul rytyl sy*** (S04 v1.3; IV.3 says it of Seren in the same words). ***hass vynull***, "a boatwright's son" (*pynull* softened as possessor): v3's boatwright, to meet VI.3's Tarnard.
- **"he knotted a net" is *re yald Tarnel piskur, bysket um wysket*, "Tarnel made a net, knot upon knot"**, built as the Cry's *tolm um dholm* (an addition): his net is laid as a wall is. *re vysk*, "knotted", would be heard as "said" (pilot I.1), and *re yald o piskur* would read *o* as "his" (the analyzer asks *viskur*), so the name stands for "he". *Tarnel* means "little net" (canon, in the notes only); the living word for a net is *piskur*.
- "the mountain tarns" are ***lyntheth et brunnath***, "the meres of the mountains". ***et orrol sell***, the long road. ***vess et hiskenn***, "the night of the pass".
- **One verb lays them down twice**: ***re firre … ul hos tevow*** and ***re firre … ul vimm hos covv***, in one cradle and under one cloak. ***Re yern ey lo o sest*** (S04 v1.3): plain at the hinge.

> **Halvard.** Hy ull re hoss ey, eth el hos ey. Re vysk Tarnel loy: Piskur, el bysketeth hosel o, garl ul narl. Eth lo o wysket doss et flennath sy bysket. Nath noss kethyl et mesk loy, hy vodh doss kethyl kethet hosen kethe pa yarl tolm sa nath noss o yaldol lo hos. Veth nath senn ey hy sennyl ullen; eth doss orrol loy, hosel, ul vodh nath noss orrol lona; eth doss seryl et flennath sy loy amm sennen. Re yal gor ryterd demoy hy yanna; eth amm re orr ganna, re orr sona flennath dem nelter ul sona sestow. El luth hosel flennath sa wrodh nahos. Veska: nath hess ey dhenth. Doss maver um et flennath sy: hossol sell hy o wrodhardath. Hy ull el Seren sa ryt o.

**Halvard.** So she lived, and she is one. "A net is only knots holding hands," Tarnel told her, and with his knot is this Book bound. The wood she cannot know, for a knowing is held as two hands hold a stone that one cannot lift. But she will not die of another's death, and she can go where we two cannot, alone, and carry this Book on after us. Every scribe before her was of a pair, and when a pair went, their book went into the vault behind them. A book that none telleth is but ink. Look, her pen is not stopped. This Book must outlive its tellers. That is why it is Seren that writeth it.

Word for word: *Halvard. So she lived, and she is one. Tarnel said to her: A net, it is knots alone, hand in hand. And with his knot this Book is knotted. The holding of the wood is not at her, for a holding is held as two hands hold a stone whose raising is not at one. But she does not die of the death of another; and the going is at her, alone, where the going is not at us two; and the carrying-on of this Book is at her when we two die. Every scribe until her was of a pair; and when a pair went, their book went to the vault behind them. A book that no one tells is ink alone. Look: her pen does not stop. Need is upon this Book: a life longer than its tellers. So it is Seren who writes it.*

- ***Hy ull re hoss ey, eth el hos ey*** (S04 v1.3): *hos*, the number; her name is "a single sorrow that overcomes".
- **Tarnel's saying** is a fronted topic, *Piskur, el bysketeth hosel o*, and its knots are ***garl ul narl***, hand in hand, as Enno and Della sat. ***bysket*** is "a knot" and "knotted fast": the Book is knotted with his knot, in one word (*lo o wysket … bysket*).
- "The wood she cannot know" is the canon's shape (Seren's line, §11.4): ***Nath noss kethyl et mesk loy***. **The knowing is held as two hands hold**: ***doss kethyl kethet hosen kethe pa yarl tolm***, *keth* four times; ***sa nath noss o yaldol lo hos*** (S04 v1.3, *lo hos*, "at one").
- ***Veth nath senn ey hy sennyl ullen***; ***doss orrol loy, hosel, …***; ***doss seryl et flennath sy loy amm sennen*** (S04 v1.3; *seryl*, S04-01 of wf8, O2923).
- ***Re yal gor ryterd demoy hy yanna***, "every scribe until her was of a pair"; the vault and the ink (S04 v1.3).
- **"her pen is not stopped" is *nath hess ey dhenth***: *hess*, "stop", the Guest's first word; *ey dhenth* is "her pen" and, read the other way, "she … the pen": neither stops. *dhenth*, "a feather", gains the sense "a quill, a pen" (an addition).
- **"This Book must outlive its tellers" is *Doss maver um et flennath sy: hossol sell hy o wrodhardath***, "need is upon this Book: a life longer than its tellers". The idiom *doss maver um* (O0488) is said whole and its need after the colon; split round the need (v1.3's order, *Doss maver hossol … um et flennath sy*) it was read "a wish for a long life lies upon this Book from its tellers" (back-translation fix). *Hy* is "than" (§3.6), as Rhyna's *sell hy ull*. ***Hy ull el Seren sa ryt o*** (S04).

> *Lo et covv re hess en dhenth. Re hastom o ul et luth, eth re serym. Lo et gebb, re ferre {{Idrenna}}.*

*My pen had stopped at the cloak. I dipped it, and wrote on. By the door, {{Idrenna}} nodded.*

Word for word: *At the cloak my pen stopped. I plunged it in the ink, and went on. At the door, Idrenna nodded.*

- *hess* again, for the pen. "dipped" is ***hast***, "push, plunge". ***eth re serym***, as at the girl's question. ***re ferre Idrenna***: the nod, given. Seren's own ink.

> *Olen re hadha sona et hosk umo, lo vyskyl hemm sost et Stonwrytan:*

*Together they ended it, with the oldest saying of the guild:*

Word for word: *Birth mortared them two; their separating was not at death.*

### 2. Traps met, and how the leaves step round them

- ***en ba yarl***, "my two hands": the analyzer reads *va* (S·*pa*) as still softening the next word (*lo va yarl* passes) but not *ba* (N·*pa*) (*en ba yarl* is reported, *en ba garl* passes). The grammar does not say whether a bedded *pa* still softens. II.1's close is recast (*cadhom pa yarl umo*). **For the grammar's owner.**
- ***o* + a noun after a verb**: the analyzer always reads *o* as "his" when a mutable word follows (*re yald o piskur* asks *viskur*), so a subject pronoun *o* cannot stand before an unmutated object. A name, an article or another order is used.
- ***visk* / *wisk***: S·*misk* "foot" is *visk*, S·*bisk* "head" is *wisk*. "its crown", "its upper face" are *o wisk*.
- ***vesket***: "seen, read" and "a colour" (I.1's *vesket*) are one form; a participle "read" is avoided.
- ***mest hy*** is "more than"; "many of X" is not *mest hy X*.
- ***ul grem*** would bed into *nr-*; ***hy humm*** also reads S·*cumm*; ***cadhan o venn*** reads *venn* as a softened noun; ***en nind*** also reads N·*tind*. Each is recast.
- **Names that mutate after every preposition** (*Della* > *Rella*, *Nella*; *Enno* > *nEnno*): the leaf keeps them in subject place, or says "her".
- **Readings the blind back-translation took** (each mended; see BACK-TRANSLATION): *kyl* is "change" before "turn" (say which way: *dem susk*); *nel … veth* with a copula is heard "not X but Y", so "only" of a predicate is *hosel* after its noun; *pawast fedh* is "a second time", never "second"; *ferr dem* is heard "bow as low as" (say *um*, "over"); an idiom split round its object (*doss maver … um*) is not seen as the idiom.
- Harmony slips the analyzer caught in the drafts: *halflenneth*, *wesker*, *yyster*, *rikent*, *hebbent*, *hebbet*, *ryten* (1du of the slender *ryt*: *rytan* is silently read as the collective *ryt* + *-an*, so the analyzer cannot catch it; check every *-an* on a slender stem by eye), *Cadhant*, *raskant*.
