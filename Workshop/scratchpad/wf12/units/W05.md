## 1 · SEREN'S FRAME AND HALYNA'S READING, IN ORROWEN (THE LEAF-HAND)

### 1.1 The title

> **Kaelir sa re vymm dem varn**

**V.3 · The Wreck That Went Home**

- **Gloss:** wreck · REL(+S) · PST(+S) · S·return · to(+N) · home.
- The lexicon's title (O1089), as the wf8 unit has it: *vymm* is *mymm* "return, come back", softened after *re*. The Book sets the title as its heading, `### V.3 · The Wreck That Went Home`; the English above is that heading, set in bold as the house style sets a title (merge: the unit had dropped *V.3 ·*).
- *Dem varn*, "to home", is also the carving's own two words (*Dem varn, amm grestet*, *Home when wounded*, line 3 of the reading): the wreck went home as the carving bade it.

### 1.2 The dateline

> **Ul nunn et grem sullast.**

*Deep in the third winter.*

- **Gloss:** in(+N) · N·deep · the · winter · third.
- **Word for word:** *In the deep of the third winter.*
- *Gunn*, "deep; (n.) the deep", is the head of a construct, so it takes no article and its definite possessor keeps one (*et grem sullast*, the ordinal after its noun, as *et tolm hosast*); *ul* nasalises it, *g* > *n*.
- **The word works three ways.** *Gunn* is the depth of the winter, the deep the wreck lay in until the tide worked it loose, and Seren's own word for what Halyna can or cannot hold of the wood (*Nath noss tul kethyl et gunn sy lo Halyna*, her untold-leaf line). On this leaf they held the deep.

### 1.3 The headnote

> **Re yalant garlath Halvard um et sceth sy; nath re femm Rhyna o, eth stinel dhant lunnat ul ey garlremullath, veth re heth ey o hosen re heth o o, hosen keth lonn lonn.**

*Halvard's hands were on this hull; Rhyna, with the salt-cuts open in her palms, touched it not, and knew it as he knew it, as kin know kin.*

- **Gloss:** PST(+S) · S·be-3PL · hand-PL · Halvard · upon(+S) · the · hull · this; NEG · PST(+S) · touch · Rhyna · it, and · stroke · S·salt · open · in(+N) · her · palm-PL, but · PST(+S) · S·hold · she · it · as · PST(+S) · S·hold · he · it, as · hold · kin · kin.
- **Word for word:** *The hands of Halvard were upon this hull; Rhyna did not touch it, and the salt-cuts open in her palms, but she held it as he held it, as kin holds kin.*
- *Re yalant garlath Halvard*: the past of the state verb, plural for its plural subject (as V.1's *Re yalant haralath …*); *garlath Halvard* is a construct, "the hands of Halvard", a name needing no article.
- **"With the salt-cuts open in her palms"** is a clause with no verb laid beside the other with *eth*, as I.1's *eth so wresketh ul so simmeth* ("their swords in their sheaths"): v3 gives no "for", so the Orrowen gives none. *Stinel dhant* (O2376) is literally "strokes of salt", *stinel* the same word as the strokes of the grain (*Stinel Hosel*, the Lone Stroke): her palms are cut as the wood is cut, and by the same salt the reading opens on (*Tant*). *Garlremullath*, "hand-hollows".
- **The verb that works twice.** *Keth* is "hold, keep" and, of the wood, "know by touch". *Nath re femm … veth re heth*: she did not touch it, and yet she *held* it, as he held it with his hands on it. The English says "knew"; the Orrowen says the one thing both ways at once.
- *Veth* ("but") where the English has "and": the tongue sets the not-touching against the holding, which is the point of the line. *Re heth ey o hosen re heth o o* mirrors IV.4's *re heth o o hosen re heth ey o*, the two turned about, as the wf8 unit had it.
- ***Hosen keth lonn lonn***, "as kin holds kin", in four words, the shape of *Somm el tolm tolm* ("while stone is stone"). *Lonn* is "kin by blood"; *keth* again: kin know kin, and kin hold kin.
- ***ul ey garlremullath***: a possessive after a bedding preposition is not bedded (the house rule, wf8 S04's table row 6; pilot IV.4's *ul o rask*), so not *ul ney*; the analyzer agrees. The blind reader asked; the line stands.

### 1.4 The reading (Halyna, as the wood gave it up; set down by Seren as it fell)

The Book's `<!-- READING -->` block: eight fragments, one line each, in the two inks by turns. Each line is its own blockquote over its own line of the Book, so the builder can lay the block whole (its English is the eight lines in order).

*(first ink)*

> **— Tant. Tant ul grynir eth crynir. Brid, fedh sell.**

*— Salt. Salt in every board. Cold, a long while.*  

- **Gloss:** salt · salt · in(+N) · N·board · and · board · cold · time · long.
- *Crynir eth crynir*, "board and board", is the distributive "every board" (as *grem eth grem*, "every winter", O2904; pilot I.1's *hos eth hos*). *Ul gor* would nasalise *gor* to *nor*, which the analyzer does not follow into the next word (NOTES 7).
- *Brid*, "cold"; *fedh sell*, "a long time", as *fedh lyss* (O2930) is "a short while".

*(second ink)*

> **— Tolm, ul drodol. Dusstyvil, vimm, ulo eth hyo. Et wadh ulo.**

*— A stone, flashing. Iron, low and through. The sea in.*  

- **Gloss:** stone · in(+N) · N·flashing · iron · low · in-it · and · from-it · the · sea · in-it.
- *Ul drodol*, "in flashing": the progressive (§3.4), *drod* "a flash"; a nasalised *d* before *r* stays *d*.
- **"Through" is *ulo eth hyo***, "into it and out of it": the conjugated *ul* and *hy*, the way a shore-man says a shot went in and out. *Et wadh ulo*, "the sea in it": the same *ulo*, and no *hyo* after it.

*(first ink)*

> **— Dem varn, amm grestet. Dem varn, niss. Brodhol dem lonn.**

*— Home when wounded. Home, slow. Telling its kin.*  

- **Gloss:** to(+N) · home · when · wounded · to(+N) · home · slow · telling · to(+N) · kin.
- ***Dem varn, amm grestet*** is the carving's name exactly as the Book of Knowings gives it (S11, E1-07; and V.2's soldiers' heads): Halyna name what woke.
- *Niss*, "slow, slowly"; *brodhol*, "telling", the verbal noun; *dem lonn*, "to kin".

*(second ink)*

> **— Vesk hosast —**

*— Look before —*  

- **Gloss:** look(2SG.IMP) · first.
- **The caution, broken across the two inks** as the English breaks it: *Vesk hosast —* / *— eth hy ull osk*, "look first, and then trust". It is Kael's own line of V.5 (*Veskent hosast, eth hy ull oskant*, "they look before they trust") in the bidding form: the same caution, seen from the wall.

*(first ink)*

> **— eth hy ull osk.**

*— you trust.*  

- **Gloss:** and · from(+S) · that · trust(2SG.IMP).
- *Hy ull*, "from that, then" (the lexicon's phrase); *osk*, "trust, rest one's weight on".

*(second ink)*

> **— Pawast. Et wadh ull sost. Hosel, eth niss.**

*— Again. The same water. Alone, and slow.*  

- **Gloss:** again · the · sea · that · self · alone · and · slow.
- *Pawast*, "again, a second time"; ***et wadh ull sost***, "that same sea": *X ull sost*, "that very X", is the house's "that same X" (*et tolm ull sost*, S01; *orl ull sost*, S04). *Hosel*, "alone, one".
- **The word works twice.** *Wadh* is line 2's sea (*Et wadh ulo*, "the sea in it"): the sea that came in at the wound is the very sea they came back to, and the two lines answer each other, *wadh ulo* / *wadh ull*. The Book says "water" here and "sea" there; the Orrowen has one word for both, and the one word is the point.
- *(Mended after the blind back-translation: the line had* et nynth sost*, and* nynth *is first "a spring, a well"; the blind reader read "the very spring", and could not tell what it pointed to.)*

*(first ink)*

> **— Hos vorr, driget. Ul vodh re omm o — [ ]**

*— One fire, thrown. Where it fell — [ ]*  

- **Gloss:** one · fire · thrown · in(+N) · what · PST(+S) · fall · it.
- *Hos vorr, driget*, "one fire, thrown" (*drig* "throw", its participle). ***Ul vodh re omm o***, "where it fell", stands beside the Hasty head *Ul vodh re rod o*, *Where it flashed* (S11): the wall's name for a carving, and here the place the grain does not hold. *Omm* is "fall" and "fail": the fire fell, and failed, and nothing of it is held.
- The gap is the Book's `[ ]`, marked and not mended; the romanisation keeps it.

*(second ink)*

> **— Tolm, ul drodol. Dusstyvil, vimm, ulo eth —**

*— A stone, flashing. Iron, low and —*

- **Gloss:** stone · in(+N) · N·flashing · iron · low · in-it · and.
- Line 2 again, word for word, and broken where the English breaks: after the *eth*, before *hyo*. The second time the iron does not come out.

## 3 · WHAT THE TRANSLATION DID, AND WHY

**The grain.**

- **One ring to a paragraph** (§11.1–11.2): v3's story has twenty paragraphs where v1.3's had eleven, so the round is 2 / 14 / 4 = 20 rings, its band-rules at the Book's two `RING` markers, and the seal in the bark. Every sentence is carved once (§10.11): one mark to a content word, a referent already cut pointed at (`@f`) or run to.
- **Kept from the wf8 round, where the meaning is the same** (`wf8/units/W05.md`, as its back-translation mended it): the root STERN; ring 1's Ralenthae, the words cut for a memory and a caution (A9's heart substitutes) and the bearer ahead; the low wound and the iron through it; the sea's own going in; *Home when wounded* cited by its root; the two questioned marks of "we know not which"; the knowings sent, not gone (`>GO`); the dread as the band; the likeness of sap; `BEARER+HEART`; the heart's carving cut whole; the stone's yesterday and today (now one questioned mark to a file, below); `!TURN@2` because of the sea; the iron's finding and its hollow count; the lee of the stone; the next hulls; the last ring's chain of *then* runners; the seal. Its six compounds are used as merged (§21.9.2).
- **Changed where v3 changed the meaning.** "Now are we broken wood, and the salt is in every board" and "Twice in one cold season came we to your wall" are new (rings 1–2). The green wood's lesson says *the iron*, not *a gun*, and stands outside band I, so it is the cut-pocket §18 always gave it. "Your wall let us go" gives the act to the wall. "Needed no words" is ours. The sendings are counted, *First … Then … Then … Last*. "In the night" replaces "While you mended your wall". The second coming is told close: we went alone and slow, knew the place, threw one fire, found what the grain does not hold, and our kin stood off on flat water (rings 10–12, new). The halt and its payoff (rings 13–14) turn on a stone the grain had not held. "Low and through" comes again in ring 15; ring 16 says ring 7's last line again, in the breath. The hulls that lived bear it home themselves, root to root, without the grey. "Long we lay" and the keels of our kin are new. **Gone with v1.3:** the likeness of the two who feel each other's hurt across a city, and the whole of v1.3's ring 10 (what the groves learn, and the hand behind the wall).
- **The same one, and another** (rays, v1's "the same one"). The stone that flashed (r3) is the stone we turned toward (r4), the stone we threw fire at (r11) and the stone that did not flash (r14): three rays on file 0. The stone that did flash in ring 14 stands on another file with no ray to anything, and the silent stone of ring 9's lesson on a third, placeless file, no ray: any stone. Three rays make the wound of rings 5, 7, 15 and 16 one wound; one makes the sea of rings 5 and 15 one sea; one makes the place where wood dies (r8) the place we knew (r10).
