- *re rik*: *grik* "pick up" softened after *re*, the *g* dropping before a consonant (§2.5), as W03 and IV.4 have it. *Et molt sy*, "this heart": the noun first, the demonstrative after (*et gunn sy*).
- **"Had but its shape" is the "at" idiom for having** (§3.7) in the past, with the set phrase *et lern hosel*, "the shape only" (O3015, W03's addition after its blind test read *lern* as "face"). *Hosel* after the noun is "only" of the noun.
- *Grem sy*, "this winter", is the lexicon's adverb (O2866), set first and parted by a comma, as I.1 sets *Amm sy* first. *Ul grem sy* would bed *grem* into *nrem*, an onset §2.5 forbids.
- *re rarr o tunn ul Halvard … eth uloy*: "it came whole into Halvard … and into her", IV.4's *re rarr o uloy* ("it came into her"). *Uloy* is the conjugated *ul*, feminine: the headnote names the two of Halyna apart, as the Book does, and the pair comes back as one in the dual *ulonta*.
- **Seren's play.** *Molt* is "a heart" and "the middle" (O1417), and *ul molt* is "among" (O2633): the heart came whole to Rhyna *ul molt et tevath*, "in the heart of the children". *Tunn* "whole" and *trenn* "a tale" answer each other across the sentence.
- *Ew sull vess et brodhol*: the copula puts its predicate first (§3.4), "three nights was the telling"; a numeral before a singular (§3.1).
- **"Not as a tale" turns on *trenn***, canon's "a tale laid in one night": a telling of three nights, in them as a wound (*grestet*, the word of the Book of the Wound), was not a *trenn*. Kept from W03, word for word, with *ulonta*, "in them two": Halyna take the dual (§3.9).
- v3 drops the old headnote's two formulas (*Re wrodha Halyna o … re hadh Seren o* and *Amm re yal et mesk cresket …*): the foreword says them once (notes_v3 §1.5).

- *Tumol stinet lo so dhrenn dem et molt*, "a memory cut by their course to the heart": *lo dhrenn* is "through, by way of" (O1217), here with its possessor *so* "their" between (*so* softens *trenn*). *Their course* is also *their rank*: the memory goes through the ranks along the rank.
- *Galat sa re heth hos gann, re heth gor dhrenn o*: "what one pillar held, every rank held it": *keth* is how wood feels and knows (§3.7; the grain's HOLD, "to know by touch"). *Gor dhrenn*, every rank (*gor* softens *trenn*), keeps the reading's *trenneth*. *Hy veskrivull dem meskrivull*, "from root to root": *meskrivull* "a root" ('tree-foundation', O1389) softened after *hy*.
- *Et brodow kylet*, "the turned mouth" (W02-02): *brodow* is the mouth as 'the word-place' (O0188), *kylet* "turned" (O1131), the word of the dry cut's own NOT mark, *tolm kylet*, the turned stone (O2555). Both hands say *not* by turning a mark, and Seren uses the one to name the other.
- **"Tied back across the rings" is *lodhat***, "bonded; mortared" (O1227): the runner is the wood's mortar, and the silence is bonded to the plea, as the Bonded are bonded. *Um et scaevurath*, "across the rings" (*scaevur*, "a ring, a round", as S02 counts a log's rings). *Dem bessyl rytet um et tolm*: "to the plea cut upon the stone", *pessyl* nasalised after *dem*; *rytet* is "cut letters, inscribed", the guild's word for a vow cut in stone.
- *Nath re dhess o*, "it did not answer", is the closing formula's own verb (*Eth re dhess nahos*): the stone's silence and the hearth's are one word.

### 1.5 The closing line (Seren's ink)

> **Eth re dhess nahos.**

*And no one answered.*

Word for word: *And answered no one.*

```text
Eth  re       dhess     nahos.
and  PST(+S)  S·answer  none .
  = Eth re dhess nahos: And no one answered ('and answered no one')
```

- The lexicon's formula (O0563), as every wood leaf closes. It answers the note's *Nath re dhess o*: the plea the stone did not answer, and the hearth that does not answer the wood.

## 3 · WHAT THE TRANSLATION DID, AND WHY

**The Orrowen.**

- **No new word and no new root.** Every word is in the shared lexicon or made by its grammar (the plurals *trenneth, nestullath, stineleth, gendeth, rellorath, gannath, scaevurath*; the conjugated *uloy, ulonta, lonta*; the participles *nydhet, stinet, rytet, lodhat, kylet*). Two set phrases are added so that a reader takes two words in the sense the leaf means (W02-01 *lern et mesk*, the wood's shape; W02-02 *brodow kylet*, the turned mouth).
- **The old unit's words, where v3 means the same.** W03's *Dannwresk ul Meskdhrenn*, *re rik Rhyna … um et dhysal*, *et lern hosel lo Halyna*, *re rarr o tunn … uloy*, *ul molt et tevath*, *ew sull vess et brodhol, hy vodh re yal o ulonta hosen grestet, eth nath re yal hosen trenn*, and the closing *Eth re dhess nahos* (O0563) stand as they were.
- **The Tier 3 report's wood formulas are not in v3's IV.2.** *Hosen tum Mesk Myst: kethet hy …*, *Re wrodha Halyna o, hos eth ullen, ul ba luth; re hadh Seren o.*, *Amm re yal et mesk cresket …* and *doss o ul lern* X (§5.1) belonged to the wood headnotes of v1.3; v3 says them once, in the foreword (notes_v3 §1.5), and its headnote here is two sentences. None of the stone formulas (*Tumar*, the Title, the Cry, the Stonwryt, the Guest's words, the laying formula) occurs in this leaf.
- **Halyna's reading is theirs, not Seren's** (notes_v3 §1.5: Seren "sets their pieces down as they fell"): fragments, mostly nouns, alternating line by line in the two inks, starting with the first. Its Orrowen keeps their trade: the ranks are *trenneth*, courses of a wall; the strokes are *stineleth*, cuts, and *nydhet*, counted and numbers; the sky *lunn*, opens and makes room. The breaks fall where the Book's fall (the sky's triple broken across the two inks before *wide*), and the gaps are the Book's two drawn marks.
- **Seren's craft in her own lines**, within the grammar: *sullast*, the third winter and the unbonded third; *molt* and *ul molt*, the heart that came to Rhyna in the heart of the children; *tunn* against *trenn*; *brodow kylet*, the turned mouth, named by the dry cut's own *not* (*tolm kylet*); *lodhat*, the wood's runner as mortar, the silence bonded to the plea; and the note's *Nath re dhess o* answered by the leaf's last line, *Eth re dhess nahos*. Her economy: the caption and the note are namings with colons, as captions are, and not copulas.
- **The standing rules.** *Halyna* is one name for two, with the dual (*ulonta*, *lonta*); the headnote names Halvard and Rhyna apart (*ul Halvard … uloy*), as the Book does. The warden does not occur in this leaf. The wood knows only the wood's side: the reading and the note hold nothing the wood did not give. No word or note says the two tongues are kin. No custom of burning occurs.

**The grain.**

- **Every paragraph of the story is a ring, and every sentence is carved once** (§10.11), but for one constraint: v1 §3.2 and the validator's H05 cap a round at 24 rings, and v3's IV.2 has 25 story paragraphs. Ring 24 holds the last two (§2.5, ring 24's note); it is shown in two blocks, each over its own paragraph. **[Jack]**
- **The fragments are the first rings, read.** The literal reading of each ring the reading draws on says the fragment's own words: *Ranks. Tall, dark of bark. Holding the breath down.* (r1: the long ranks; bark in the dark; a high pillar; hold the grey, in the earth) · *Oars. Many oars. Then iron, in the bark.* (r4: the iron's coming with many hulls, many hands; r7: they send the iron into the pillar) · *One, and — [ ]* (r5: one pillar feels; the first drawn mark) · *Strokes. Counted. Every stroke, counted.* (r3: count all; r7: the iron strikes again; we count all of it) · *Each fell, and the sky came through. Pale —* (r8–r10: it lets go; the grey opens in the white; we fall, once, then again) · *and pale, and hard. Shoots. Cut, and cut again.* (r10: hard light opening, in the white; r11: the stumps grow true shoots; the iron comes again, by turns) · *Soft letters, on the mute thing —* (r15: we carve, in the stone; r16: we carve gently, with our words) · *and it did not [ ]* (r18: the stone does not speak to us; the second drawn mark).
- **The two drawn marks are the grain's own** (the note): the ranks with a memory ray cut through them to the heart (ring 5), and the mouth mirrored, `!MOUTH`, tied back across three ring-lines by a *then* runner from the carving of the plea (rings 15 to 18).
- **Kept from W03** wherever v3 says the same: rings 1, 3 to 5, 9 to 11, 13 to 17, 19 and 22, most of 23 and the first paragraph of 24 (each ring's note says which of W03's knowings it keeps), the plea's and the belief's pockets, the braid `aba`, both blinds, the hollow pole and likenesses, and W03's two compounds (`THIN+NEW`, `BENEATH+NEW:stump`). **New for v3:** ring 2's cause (*for none think of breath*), ring 3's `EDGE+EYE`, the first pillar's felling told close (rings 6 to 8), the sky's triple broken (rings 9, 10, 12), ring 14's *never* and *over the water*, ring 19's edge and breath, ring 20's future, ring 21's *years of the iron*, ring 23's *forty summers* and the council as people, and ring 24's rays, *ever the same*.
- **The heart stays plain** (§3, A9): ring 1 carries no fringe and no device but runners; band I (rings 1 to 3) carries no pocket, braid, bind or break, and the girth gives it one cell. The fringe begins in ring 2, the first pocket and the braid in the sapwood.

- **A reading that holds without Seren's apparatus.** The blind round has no `# file` lines, so the validator names each file by its first thing-sign (§11.2, A16). The round keeps them right where it can: the grey's file (11) never takes a thing-sign (`FLASH+OPEN`, `THIN+LIVE`: act heads); the mute thing has a file of its own (7); acts stand first on the first pillar's file; the axe in our rings stands on the iron's file.
