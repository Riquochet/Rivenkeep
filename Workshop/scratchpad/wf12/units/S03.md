## I.4 · *TERNIL ET GORNDHOLM*, THE RITE OF THE CORNERSTONE

### Title

> ## *Ternil et Gorndholm*

**I.4 · The Rite of the Cornerstone**

Word for word: *The rite of the cornerstone.*

- The lexicon's title row (O2499), as the old unit gave it. *Ternil* is "a rite, a thing done in order; a custom": the leaf ends by finding that the hearth's custom is a *ternil* too, and the youngest apprentice's leaf says so in the Rite's own chest.

### Dateline

**¶1**

> *Heth hosast et tynur.*

*The first spring of the siege.*

Word for word: *Spring first of the siege.*

- ***Heth hosast et tynur***: the old headnote's *ul heth hosast et tynur* without its *ul*, a naming with no verb (a dateline is a label). *Heth* is "spring, the lengthening"; the construct head takes no article.

### Seren's headnote

**¶2**

> *Re hadh Seren Pa Luth, sullast odh {{Halyna}}. Ul et gemmnoth vess re yalom tev et Stonwrytan, eth nath re oskant galat lom veth et nynth.*

*Laid by Seren Two-Inks, the third of the hearth of {{Halyna}}. In the Fourteen Nights I was a child of the guild, and they trusted me with naught but water.*

Word for word: *Laid Seren Two-Inks, the third of the hearth of Halyna. In the fourteen nights I was a child of the guild, and they did not trust a thing to me but the water.*

- The stone headnote's frame, *Re hadh* X, X's epithet (pilot I.1), with Seren's own canon epithet *Seren Pa Luth* (O2266).
- ***sullast odh Halyna***, "the third of the hearth of Halyna": *sullast* is the lexicon's "a third; the unbonded third of a hearth" (O2421), so the word that counts her is the word that names what she is. A construct: *odh* heads the possessor *Halyna*, and neither takes the article.
- ***Ul et gemmnoth vess***, "in the Fourteen Nights": a numeral before a singular noun (§3.1); the article keeps *ul* from bedding *gemmnoth* into *nemmnoth*.
- ***nath re oskant galat lom veth et nynth***: "only" of a clause is *nath … veth* (pilot G8). *Et nynth*, "the water", comes back in I.5, where the least child of the guild is the one *sa re lymm et nynth*, "that carried the water": Seren.

### Seren's telling

**¶3**

> Keth et tolm, Seren. Myskym o lom gor fedh.

Hold the stone, Seren. I tell myself so every time.

Word for word: *Hold the stone, Seren. I say it to me every time.*

- ***Keth et tolm***: the first half of Halyna's canon words to the child Seren (`orrowen_v2` §11.11, *Keth et tolm. Keth o, eth es geth tho.*), with her name called after it (a vocative softens, and *s* does not change). The second half is not said here; the Epilogue says both halves alone.
- ***Myskym o lom***, "I say it at me": the conjugated preposition is the reflexive ("I tell myself"), as *Hebb et tolm lom* is "give me". *Mysk* stands in the present, the habitual, where nothing beds it (pilot I.1 §3).

**¶4**

> Ul et vess sullast ul sestow et carm, re varr baegyth, eth re rynnym. Re yal Rhyna rynnet, eth ul et hoss sost re rik Halvard et greller. Nath re harmom. Re orr et baegyth hyom dem vimm, kylyl um hylyl, dem drod ul nynth.

On the third night after the cry a light moved, and I woke. Rhyna was risen, and in the same breath Halvard took up the lamp. I called not out. Down from me the light went, turning by turning, till it was a spark in a well.

Word for word: *In the third night after the cry a light shifted, and I woke. Rhyna was woken, and in the very breath Halvard took the lamp. I did not call. The light went from me downward, turning upon turning, down to a spark in a well.*

- ***re varr baegyth***: *marr* is "shift, move; shake; tremble". The leaf uses the one verb four times and lets it work each way: the light *moved* here; Halvard *shook* each flask (¶10); *naught moved* in them (¶11); and Idrenna's hands *shook* at everything but the flasks (¶30).
- ***Re yal Rhyna rynnet***, "Rhyna was risen": *rynnet*, "woken, risen from sleep", beside *re rynnym*, "I woke". *Rhyna* ("she who sets fast", *rhyn-*) and *rynn* ("wake") are different roots that sound alike, and Seren sets them side by side.
- ***ul et hoss sost***, "in the very breath": S02's *ul et clemel sost*, "in the very moment", with the Book's own word.
- ***kylyl um hylyl***, "turning upon turning": shaped on the Cry's *tolm um dholm* and S02's *bystir um wystir*; it comes back word for word in ¶43, when Seren goes down alone.
- ***dem drod ul nynth***, "down to a spark in a well": *dem* is "until, as far as", and a nasalised *d* before *r* stays *d* (§2.5), so the light simply goes *as far as* a spark. *Nynth* is "a spring, a well; water".

**¶5**

> Um sona hos tevow re vell odha Hyna vell dhesketh {{Aldwena}}, eth o haeveret, re vess nahos o:

Over their one cradle Rhyna's mother had sung the rhyme of {{Aldwena}}'s line, and what it meant, none had asked:

Word for word: *Upon their one cradle Rhyna's mother sang the song of the line of Aldwena, and its meaning, no one asked it:*

- ***Um sona hos tevow***, "over their one cradle": the lexicon's idiom for a bonded pair is *cadhat ul hos tevow*, "laid in one cradle" (O2908); *sona* is the dual possessive.
- ***re vell odha Hyna vell***, "sang … the song": the verb and its object are one word (*vell*, "song; sing"). *Odha*, "a mother", is "she of the hearth". *Hyna* is *Rhyna* softened as a possessor, as S01's *vadullan Hyna*.
- ***vell dhesketh Aldwena***, "the rhyme of Aldwena's line": *tesketh* is "the generations, the old line" (O2509), softened as the possessor.
- ***eth o haeveret, re vess nahos o***: a fronted object taken up by *o* (pilot G6); the bite on *haeveret* shows *o* is "its"; *re vess nahos o* is a positive verb with *nahos*, as *Re varr nahos o*.

**¶6**

> *Ul vimm et hellur, ul vimm et pressir,*
> *ul vimm et bystir sa hess,*
> *dem vimm, ul vodh re lusk et bunth et tolm hosast,*
> *kethe pa hosast galat sa seske pa hosast.*

> *Under the hall and under the floor,*  
> *under the stair that goes no more,*  
> *down where the rope let the first stone go,*  
> *the first two keep what the first two know.*

Word for word:

> *Under the hall, under the floor,*  
> *under the stair that stops,*  
> *down, where the rope let go the first stone,*  
> *the first two keep the thing that the first two know.*

- **The form.** Four lines falling on their first syllables. The first two rhyme on their stressed syllables, ***PRESS**ir · **HESS***; the last two rhyme on their own word, *hosast*, "first", as the English says "the first" three times and a cradle-rhyme repeats itself. The first three lines open on the same two words (*ul vimm*, *ul vimm*, *dem vimm*), so the rhyme goes down as it is sung.
- ***et bystir sa hess***, "the stair that goes no more": *hess* is "stop". The tongue has no "no longer" (pilot G10), so the stair that goes no more is the stair that *stops*. ¶7 says it in the past, *sa re hess*; the Epilogue's *I know the stair that goes no more* should take the present, as here.
- ***kethe … seske***: the first two *hold* (*keth*, the Keep's verb, and the wood's) what they *know* (*sesk*, a fact).

**¶7**

> Ul vimm et bystir sa re hess, re sesk Rhyna ul vodh dem veskyl, eth re orr Halvard dem vimm ul et myger. Re lusk ey et bunth, eth nath re vyske sona hos brod.

Under the stair that went no more, Rhyna wist where to look, and down the shaft went Halvard. She paid out the rope, and neither spake a word.

Word for word: *Under the stair that stopped, Rhyna knew where for looking, and Halvard went downward into the shaft. She loosed the rope, and they two did not say one word.*

- ***re lusk ey et bunth***, ***nath re vyske sona hos brod***: the old unit's words for the same two acts (S02, I.4 ¶2).
- ***ul vodh dem veskyl***, "where to look": *dem* with a verbal noun is purpose (S02's note), and *ul vodh* is the relative "where" (pilot G4).

**¶8**

> Lo visk, hosen re wrodha {{Halyna}} o lom, re ston vestul hy dholm domm, eth tuss um o waek, gunn hosen nind. Re nalymm Halvard et tuss lo o hovvel.

At the foot, as they told it me, stood a chest of black stone, the dust a finger deep on its lid. Halvard swept it with his sleeve.

Word for word: *At the foot, as Halyna told it to me, there stood a chest of black stone, and dust upon its lid, deep as a finger. Halvard took away the dust with his sleeve.*

- ***hosen re wrodha Halyna o lom***: a dual verb says its subject (§3.4), so the pair-name stands where the English has "they"; it is also Halyna's own way, to speak of themselves by name.
- ***eth tuss um o waek, gunn hosen nind***: a clause with no verb (I.1's *rellor … rytet ul o waek*); *baek* "lid" is softened after *o*.
- ***Re nalymm Halvard et tuss***, "took the dust away", for "swept it": the lexicon has no "sweep", and *nalymm* is its reversative of "bring". *Covvel*, "a sleeve", is "a small cloak".

**¶9**

> Ew rellor {{Aldwena}}.

It was the mark of {{Aldwena}}.

Word for word: *It was the mark of Aldwena.*

- The copula with its predicate first and the subject left unsaid (§3.4).
- (**The chip that follows**, the `legend_stonwryt_aldwena` surface, is the mark itself: the same **ALD | BEN** under one lintel that the tale-stone's underside carries (`legend_stonwryt_hal`, S01). A mark is matched, not read (wf10 notes), so there is no Orrowen to give; its caption is the reader's furniture and stays in English, as S01 and S10 keep the captions of their surfaces.)

**¶10**

> Ulo re sol et Ternil um halflenneth, halflenneth yaldol dem stemir, ryt surr et galdardath hosast, eth hulter lo dhrenn et pressir. Ul et gorn re ston sull hellenn hy dholm. Re varr Halvard gor hos hyont lo et greller.

Within lay the Rite on leaves of slate, the slates of building for battle, the founders' annal, and along the floor a staff. In the corner stood three stone flasks. Halvard shook each by the lamp.

Word for word: *In it lay the Rite upon slates, slates of building for battle, the annal of the first builders, and a staff along the floor. In the corner stood three flasks of stone. Halvard shook every one of them by the lamp.*

- ***halflenneth yaldol dem stemir***, "the slates of building for battle": the old unit's *yaldol dem stemir*. *Halflenn*, "slate", is "rock-leaf"; *flennath* is avoided because it is also "the Book".
- ***ryt surr et galdardath hosast***, "the founders' annal": *ryt surr* "an annal" (O2162) with the canon's *galdardath hosast*, "the first builders", for the founders.
- ***hulter lo dhrenn et pressir***, "a staff along the floor": *lo dhrenn* is "along" ("at the course of"), as I.5's *lo dhrenn et orrow*.

**¶11**

> Re varr nayalat ulont.

Naught moved in them.

Word for word: *Nothing shifted in them.*

- *Re varr nayalat ulont*: the verb of ¶10 again, now with *nayalat*, "nothing". Halvard shook them; nothing moved: one word for both.

**¶12**

> Lo et vorr re veske {{Halyna}} et halflenneth dem so haeveret, trenn eth hoss eth trenn, eth re hadhom et nunth, eth re nadhumom o.

At the fire {{Halyna}} read the slates for their meaning, a line and a breath and a line, and I set my bucket down and forgat it.

Word for word: *At the fire Halyna read the slates for their meaning, a line and a breath and a line, and I laid the bucket down, and I forgot it.*

- ***trenn eth hoss eth trenn***: *trenn* is a line of writing and a course of stone, *hoss* the breath between: the slates are read as they were laid.
- ***et nunth***, "the bucket": *nunth* is "a butt, a vessel for water". It takes the article, not *en*, because *en* before *n* shows no bite and would read "I" (§3.5).
- ***re nadhumom o***: *nadhum*, "forget", the verb of the Keep that was forgotten.

**¶13a** *(first ink)*

> El sy Ternil et Gorndholm,

"This is the Rite of the Cornerstone,"

Word for word: *This is the Rite of the Cornerstone,*

- **Halyna's two halves.** The Book wraps the pair's quoted lines in `<!-- HALYNA -->`; the builder restarts the alternation at each wrap, so each pair of lines is *first ink*, *second ink*. The halves are given as they fall; the Orrowen splits where the English splits.
- ***El sy Ternil et Gorndholm***: "this is …" puts *sy* straight after the copula, as the old I.5's *El sy en dessyl* and *El ull ternil et odh sy*.

**¶13b** *(second ink)*

> sa hadhar ketherd um yaldat strom lo, dem o dhaldol eth dem o hethyl, dem o stonol.

"by which a keeper is set over a great work, to raise it and to keep it, till it stand."

Word for word: *which we lay a holder over a great work by, for its raising and for its holding, for its standing.*

- ***ketherd***, "a keeper": the canon's *Ketherd* is "the holder: the Commander" (O1114). The Rite's own word for its keeper is the word that will name the Captain in ¶36, and *dem o hethyl*, "to keep it", is the same verb. In Orrowen the slate already says what he will be called.
- ***sa hadhar ketherd um yaldat strom lo***: the preposition stays behind in the relative and goes to the end of it (pilot G5, as *sa yorrom demo* in I.5 ¶9), "by which we lay a keeper over a great work"; *we* is the guild, whose slates they are. To set a keeper over a work is to *lay* him on it, as a capstone is laid. **Back-translation fix.** The first draft left *lo* straight after *ketherd*, where it met *um* (*ketherd lo um yaldat*), and the blind reader heard "a holder at it, upon a great work" and lost the *by which*; the stranded *lo* now closes the clause, as every other stranded preposition of the unit does.
- ***dem o dhaldol eth dem o hethyl, dem o stonol***: three purposes in one shape. *Dem* with a verbal noun is purpose, and *dem o stonol*, "for its standing", is also "until it stand" (*dem*, "until"): the one word does both of the English's jobs.

**¶14**

> Re ston haral et hulter vimm hyo.

Lower stood the law of the staff.

Word for word: *The law of the staff stood lower than it.*

- *Haral* is "an order; a law"; *vimm hyo*, "lower than it", is the comparative with *hy* (§3.6).

**¶15a** *(first ink)*

> Doss hulter stinet dem o yaldat, eth nath morr o hyo,

"A staff is cut for its work, and leaveth not its work,"

- *Merge (wf12), H6:* the law of the staff is one saying in I.4 and III.2: *Doss hulter stinet dem o yaldat, eth nath morr o hyo* (O3057, S05): *stin* is the cut of wood (a staff is cut, not lettered), and *orr hy*, "go out of", is "leave". (The unit had *Doss hulter rytet dem o yaldat, eth nath lusk o yaldat,*.)

Word for word: *A staff is cut for its work, and does not leave its work,*

- ***Doss hulter rytet***: the state verb and a participle, the passive. *Ryt* is "cut, carve".
- ***nath lusk o yaldat***: *lusk* is "loose, let go; leave, let be": a staff that does not *let go of* its work.

**¶15b** *(second ink)*

> eth amm doss et galdat galdat, doss et hulter cadhat ulo.

"and when the work is done, the staff is laid down in it."

Word for word: *and when the work is worked, the staff is laid in it.*

- ***amm doss et galdat galdat***: *galdat* is "a work" and "done" (O0657, with S02's sense row). The slate says it twice and means it two ways: the work, worked.
- ***doss et hulter cadhat ulo***, "the staff is laid in it": *cadh*, the mason's "lay". The staff ends in its work as a stone ends in its wall.

**¶16**

> Lo et halflenneth vimm hyont re sell et hoss ul lodh et trenneth. Hald ferret dem gylyl vedh. Ganna ferret ul lern et stromrunn. Trenn cadhat sa omm vathur ulo hyvenn. Re seskent et galdardath hosast gorrol. Ul lern vodh, nel rytet um et halflenneth.

At the slates beneath, the breath between the lines grew long. Walls sloped to turn a shot. Gates set crooked against the ram. Courses laid that a breach should fall outward. The first builders had known war. Against what, the slates say not.

Word for word: *At the slates below them the breath in the mortar of the lines stretched. A wall bent, for the turning of a shot. A gate bent, in the face of the ram. A course laid that a breach in it falls outward. The first builders knew war. In the face of what: it is not cut upon the slates.*

- ***re sell et hoss ul lodh et trenneth***, "the breath between the lines grew long": *sell* is "long" and "stretch" (O2241), and *ul lodh* is "between", "in the mortar of". The pause sits where the mortar words sit.
- **The three secrets are a mason's list**, verbless and in the generic singular, as the old unit cut them: *hald ferret dem gylyl vedh* (*kylyl vedh*, the Captain's second call made a noun); *ganna ferret ul lern et stromrunn*; *trenn cadhat sa omm vathur ulo hyvenn*. The plural of *ganna* would be *gannath*, "posts" (I.5 uses that word for posts).
- ***Ul lern vodh, nel rytet um et halflenneth***: *ul lern* is "against, in the face of". Where the English says "the slates say not", the Orrowen says what a slate can do: it is not *cut* there.

**¶17**

> Doss maver sull lernul um et Ternil, sa re yald nahos hossen.

Three oils the Rite must have, that none living had made.

Word for word: *Need of three oils is upon the Rite, that no living one had made.*

- ***Doss maver … um et Ternil***: "must" is the "upon" idiom (O0488).
- ***sa re yald nahos hossen***: *nahos*, "none", with a positive verb; *hossen* is "living, of breath".

**¶18**

> Dem et maress, re nesk Stannard {{Halyna}} lo dhrenn et sulv dem et meskan ul vodh re yal et hulter hosast rytet. Nath re yostant mesk hossen. Um et mesk ommat re runn Stannard lo o nind, eth re rern o.

For the cedar, Stannard led {{Halyna}} through the snow to the stand where the first staff was cut. A living tree would they not fell. On the fallen trunks Stannard knocked with his knuckle, and listened.

Word for word: *For the cedar, Stannard led Halyna along the snow to the grove where the first staff was cut. They did not fell a living tree. Upon the fallen trees Stannard knocked with his finger, and he listened.*

- ***Nath re yostant mesk hossen***: *gost*, "fell a tree" (O0779), the verb of IV.1's title. The plural keeps the sentence from reading as an order (a dual verb with no subject reads as the imperative).
- ***re runn Stannard***: *drunn*, "knock", softened after *re* (*dr* > *r*). I.5 uses the same verb when Stannard knocks once on the hearthstone. ***lo o nind***, "with his finger": the lexicon has no "knuckle".
- ***re rern o***: *rern* is "an ear; to listen".

**¶19**

> Seskym vodh hyont es geth.

"I know which of these will hold."

Word for word: *I know what of them will hold.*

- ***es geth***, "will hold": *keth*, the Keep's verb. Stannard is no Bonded; he knows by the knock, *sesk*, and the wood that will hold is the wood that will *keth*.

**¶20**

> Re wamma {{Halyna}} lernul hy et meskvolt lo et odh sy, eth ul vess thenth hossant tolmath et odh o tul.

They rendered the heartwood at this hearth, and on a wet night its stones breathe it out yet.

Word for word: *Halyna drew oil from the heartwood at this hearth, and on a wet night the stones of the hearth breathe it still.*

- ***Re wamma Halyna lernul hy et meskvolt***, "they rendered the heartwood": the lexicon has no "render", so the Orrowen says what rendering is, "drew oil from" (*bamm*, "pull, draw"). The pair-name says who "they" are.
- ***hossant tolmath et odh o tul***: *hoss* is "breathe" and "live on, outlive" (O1004): the stones breathe the cedar out, and it outlives the night it was made on.

**¶21**

> Sinth, vollant et tolmardath et sast stacorn um dholm dasorat sa re yal navothat tesk eth tesk, hy vodh el sinth o ul vodh mellor nahos.

Bloom the masons call the pale crust on worked stone untended for lives of men, for it flowereth where none come.

Word for word: *Bloom, the masons name the pale crust upon dressed stone that was untended generation after generation, for it is a flower where no one comes in.*

- ***Sinth***, "bloom", is fronted as the English fronts it; the second oil's name, *kyvilsinth*, is "granite-bloom".
- ***sast stacorn***: *sast* is "skin, a hide; a crust" (O2209). ¶23 takes the same word the other way: the children scrape the stone's *sast* and lose their own.
- ***tesk eth tesk***, "generation after generation" (O2903), for "lives of men".

**¶22**

> Hy ull re vellorar, et tevath, lo wresketh hemm, eth re ferrer lo dhrenn et tolmath nadhumat hosen derrerdeth lo dhrenn derrow derret. Ew ull et pyskorn hosast hebbet lom sa new nynth.

Then came we children with old chisels, and bent along the forgotten stones as gleaners along a stubble-field. It was the first work given me that was not water.

Word for word: *Then we came in, the children, with old chisels, and we bent along the forgotten stones as gatherers along a reaped reaping-field. That was the first toil given at me that was not water.*

- ***hosen derrerdeth lo dhrenn derrow derret***, "as gleaners along a stubble-field": *derrerd* is "one who reaps or gathers in", *derrow* "a reaping-field", *derret* "reaped, gathered in" (O0421); the lexicon has neither "glean" nor "stubble", and gatherers on a field already gathered in are what gleaners are. The one root three times over is Seren's: the children gather where the gathering is done. **Back-translation fix.** Without *derret* the blind reader saw "reapers through a reaping-field".
- ***et tolmath nadhumat***: "the forgotten stones", with the participle of the Keep that was forgotten.
- ***lo wresketh hemm***: *bresk* "a chisel" is slender, so its plural is *bresketh*; *lo* softens it.
- ***et pyskorn hosast hebbet lom sa new nynth***, "the first work given me that was not water": the participle *hebbet* ("given"; also "a gift") and a relative on the copula's past negative (*sa new*, as ¶37's *sa nel ryt*), in the English's own order. The last word is still the headnote's *nynth*. **Back-translation fix.** The first draft coordinated it (*… sa re yal hebbet lom, eth new nynth o*), and the blind reader read "the first toil that was given to me, and it was not the water": the first work of all, which the headnote's water denies.

**¶23**

> Re yal nindeth nasastat lom hyo.

My fingers were raw with it.

Word for word: *Unskinned fingers were at me from it.*

- ***nasastat***, "raw" (addition S03-06): *na-* + *sast* + *-At*, "not provided with skin", by the rules that give *lernulat* "oiled" and *nawrodhat* "untold". The children took the crust off the stone and the skin off their fingers, and Orrowen has one word for both.
- ***lom hyo***: the "at" idiom ("I had"), and *hyo*, "from it", for "with it".

**¶24**

> Nath re yal hebbyl styvess lo et brunn. Re lymme {{Idrenna}} hos hellenn lo dhrenn et orrol tunn, dem muldath sennet et Stonwrytan. Um et hiskenn, nath re rik et trun so. Amm re dhaedh Rhyna et styvess ul et maress, re yal et hellur velv, hosen doss orrol velv dem et uld sennet. Lo styvess uldath sennet et orrol, es noss et Crenn lernulat.

The myrrh the mountain could not give. One flask had {{Idrenna}} carried the whole road, for the dead of the guild. On the pass the ground would not take them. When Rhyna warmed the gum into the cedar, the hall fell quiet, as a street doth for a bier. With the myrrh of the road's dead the Captain would be anointed.

Word for word: *Giving of myrrh was not at the mountain. Idrenna carried one flask along the whole road, for the dead men of the guild. Upon the pass the ground did not take them. When Rhyna warmed the myrrh into the cedar, the hall was quiet, as a road is quiet for the dead man. With the myrrh of the dead men of the road the Captain will be anointed.*

- ***Nath re yal hebbyl styvess lo et brunn***: "could not" is the "at" idiom in the past (§3.7), *the giving of myrrh was not at the mountain*.
- ***dem muldath sennet et Stonwrytan***: *dem* beds *uldath* to *muldath* (§3.3: a broad vowel takes *m-*).
- ***nath re rik et trun so***, "the ground would not take them": I.5's *nath mellor paskard et trun*, "the ground will not take a spade", is the same ground told the other way.
- ***hosen doss orrol velv dem et uld sennet***, "as a street doth for a bier": the lexicon has no street and no bier; a road is quiet for a dead man carried on it.
- ***es noss et Crenn lernulat***: the future from inside the past ("would be anointed"); *lernulat* is "oiled, anointed" (O1189).

**¶25**

> Re seskym cedh sa re yal o lymmet demo.

I wist for whom it had been carried.

Word for word: *I knew who [it was] that it had been carried for.*

- ***cedh sa re yal o lymmet demo***: the preposition stays behind (pilot G5). *Dem cedh* would bed to *dem gedh*, "for a father", so the question word stands first and its *dem* goes to the end as *demo*. The notes tie this line to Tarnel and Wendhessa (notes_v3, the oils); the Orrowen, like the English, does not name them.

**¶26**

> Lo dhrenn et vess, re ston hellenn {{Idrenna}} pondul um et odhdholm, eth re femm nahos hyor o. Desir re vellor et Crenn hy et hald, lodh dem et sestyarl, eth re vesk o umo, eth pawast re orr o.

All night the flask of {{Idrenna}} stood empty on the hearthstone, and none of us would touch it. Late came the Captain in off the wall, mortar to the wrist, and looked on it, and went out again.

Word for word: *Through the night the flask of Idrenna stood empty upon the hearth-stone, and no one of us touched it. Late the Captain came in from the wall, mortar up to the arm, and he looked upon it, and again he went.*

- ***re femm nahos hyor o***, "none of us would touch it": *femm*, "touch", kept apart from *keth*, which is "hold" and also "know the wood by touch".
- ***lodh dem et sestyarl***, "mortar to the wrist": "up to the arm" (*sestyarl*, "shoulder-hand"); the lexicon has no wrist.
- ***eth pawast re orr o***: *pawast* goes first because after *o* it would read as "his second" (*vawast*).

**¶27**

> Ul et orl gemmnothast, re vellor rerd bynt, et hosast hy et movenn. Ul et parow re sol et hosk clenn sa re stann Stannard, eth re rasora {{Orvenna}}. Lo o sest re reske {{Idrenna}}, eth et hellenneth um sona simm. Re ston et Crenn ul vodh re haral et halflenn et nenderd dem stonol, eth o yarl um et tolm, eth o lern dem et lunt.

On the fourteenth day came a thin sun, the first since the banner. In the ward lay the new capstone that Stannard had quarried and {{Orvenna}} dressed. Beside it sat {{Idrenna}} with the flasks in their laps. The Captain stood where the slate bade the overseer stand, his hand on the stone and his face to the people.

Word for word: *On the fourteenth day a thin sun came in, the first since the banner. In the ward lay the new capstone that Stannard quarried and Orvenna dressed. At its shoulder Idrenna sat, and the flasks upon their laps. The Captain stood where the slate bade the overseer to standing, and his hand upon the stone, and his face to the people.*

- ***Orvenna*** (addition S03-01): the pair-name of Ormund and Penna by the rule of §3.10 (*Or-* + softened *Penn-* + the lintel *-a*), as *Halyna*, *Aldwena*, *Idrenna*; it takes the dual (*re rasora*, *dasor* "dress, polish" softened).
- ***re stann Stannard***: *stann*, "quarry", is the root of *Stannard*'s name, "quarrier" (§4.6): the quarrier quarried it.
- ***um sona simm***: *simm* is "the belly; a lap"; *sona* is the pair's own possessive.
- ***ul vodh re haral et halflenn et nenderd dem stonol***: *haral*, "bid"; the slate *bade* the overseer, where the old unit had the Rite's standing rule (*doss maver stonol um nenderd*).

**¶28**

> Nath re vall o ull. Re ston o lo ull.

He did not like it. He stood there.

Word for word: *He did not love that. He stood there.*

- The old unit's two sentences, unchanged. *Vall* is the tongue's only verb of liking, a shade stronger than "like".

**¶29**

> Um et ganna re wemm et covv treskat hos fedh, eth re omm o. Re hunnom et crestnynth ul haskol ul et praevarath hemm.

Above the gate the torn cloak lifted once, and fell. I heard the snow-water running in the old gutters.

Word for word: *Upon the gate the riven cloak lifted one time, and it fell. I heard the meltwater in running in the old channels.*

- ***re wemm***: *bemm*, "lift", softened. ***crestnynth***, "meltwater" (O0317), for the snow-water; ***praevarath***, "channels", for the gutters.

**¶30**

> Hy ull re dhalda {{Idrenna}}, hos ul ferryl um sest ullen, eth lo yemm garl hemm re ferre gor hellenn um o wisk ferret. Ul et grem ull re varrant sona yarlath um gor yalat ullen. Um et hellenneth nath re varrant.

Then {{Idrenna}} rose, the one leaning on the other, and with their four old hands tipped each flask over his bowed head. That winter their hands shook at everything else. On the flasks they shook not.

Word for word: *Then Idrenna rose, one in leaning upon the shoulder of the other, and with four old hands they tipped every flask upon his bowed head. In that winter their hands shook at everything else. Upon the flasks they did not shake.*

- ***re ferre gor hellenn um o wisk ferret***: *ferr* is "bow; tip; lean" (O0594), and its participle *ferret* is "bowed". The flasks bow over the bowed head: one word for the pourers and the poured-on. *F* does not soften, so *re ferre* keeps its *f*.
- ***hos ul ferryl um sest ullen***, "the one leaning on the other": the progressive (*ul* + verbal noun) of the same verb, and the other's shoulder (*sest ullen*, a construct) to lean on. **Back-translation fix.** *hos ul ferryl um ullen* was read "each bowing to the other", a courtesy where the English has two old women holding each other up; the shoulder makes the *ferr* a leaning.
- ***lo yemm garl hemm***: *lo* softens the numeral *gemm*.
- ***re varrant … nath re varrant***: ¶4's *marr* a third and fourth time.

**¶31**

> Re voll hos gor lernul, eth ullen o haeveret:

The one named each oil, and the other its meaning:

Word for word: *One named every oil, and the other its meaning:*

- The old unit's line (*Re voll hos et lernul, eth ullen o haeveret*) with "each" (*gor*). Idrenna speak in halves, so the line that follows is theirs, split at the wedge; it is not a Halyna block, and the Book gives it no ink.

**¶32**

> Maress, dem hydull. Kyvilsinth, dem mylenn. Styvess, dem havul hrennyth.

"Cedar, for endurance. Granite-bloom, for strength. Myrrh, for the burden of command."

Word for word: *Cedar, for endurance. Granite-bloom, for strength. Myrrh, for the burden of captaincy.*

- The three oils as the old unit gave them, one voice to the comma and the other the rest (§3.9 item 5). *Hydull* is "patience; to endure", *mylenn* "strength", *havul hrennyth* "the burden of command" (*crennyth* softened as possessor, *hr-*, pilot G7). I.5's last paragraph puts the same *havul* on Marl's back.

**¶33**

> Re hask et lernul ul o hovv, eth re lusk o ull. Re wemmer, et tevath ul lern, ol narlath nasastat hos um ullen, eth re yal hylenn umor, ul et fedh hosast hy et hiskenneth.

The oil ran into his collar, and he wiped it not. We children at the front held up our raw hands to one another, and smiled, for the first time since the passes.

Word for word: *The oil ran into his cloak, and he let that be. We lifted, the children in front, our unskinned hands one to the other, and gladness was upon us, in the first time since the passes.*

- ***ul o hovv***, "into his collar": the lexicon has no collar; the oil runs into his cloak.
- ***re lusk o ull***, "he wiped it not": *lusk*, "let be". The lexicon has no "wipe".
- ***ol narlath nasastat***: ¶23's word for their fingers, now for their hands.
- ***re yal hylenn umor***, "and smiled": the "upon" idiom of feeling (§3.7), *gladness was upon us*; the lexicon has no "smile".

**¶34**

> Re hethe {{Idrenna}} hulter et Stonwrytan dem lern. Hosen sy voll et halflenn o: *hulter hy vesk dasorat, hoskat lo dholm dasorat, et gell sost, sa re lymm gor nenderd yaldat strom et Stonwrytan hy et rivull*. Ew et hulter hosast sost. Re vesk et Crenn umo fedh sell, eth hy ull re sell o yarlath.

{{Idrenna}} held out the staff of the guild. The slate nameth it so: *a rod of polished wood capped with the finest polished ashlar, borne by every overseer of a great Stonewright work since the founding*. It was the first staff of all. A long while the Captain looked on it ere he put out his hands.

Word for word: *Idrenna held the staff of the guild forward. Like this the slate names it: a staff of polished wood, capped with polished stone, the very best, that every overseer of a great work of the guild bore from the founding. It was the very first staff. The Captain looked upon it a long time, and then he stretched his hands.*

- ***Re hethe Idrenna … dem lern***, "held out": *keth*, "hold", with *dem lern*, "forward".
- **The slate's words** are the old unit's, exactly (S02 I.4 ¶8), in italics where the English has them.
- ***Hosen sy voll et halflenn o***: *Hosen sy* goes first because *et halflenn o* would read as "the slate of it", the article on a construct's head (§3.2).
- ***fedh sell … re sell o yarlath***: *sell* is "long" and "stretch" (O2241). The long while and the reaching out are one word, said twice.

**¶35**

> Re hadhant gemm garl o, eth re rike pa yarl o.

Four hands laid it in two.

Word for word: *Four hands laid it, and two hands took it.*

- The old unit's recast, kept: "in two" would be *ul ba yarl*, and the analyzer does not read the bedded *pa* as the trigger that softens *garl* (S02 Notes 4). The dual subject *pa yarl* takes the 3du *re rike*.

**¶36**

> Lo et hulter re yal o vollat Ketherd Hethow Dhresk, eth re ston o sol ul et bontoth.

By it he was named Commander of Rivenkeep, and stood still in the noise.

Word for word: *By the staff he was named Commander of Rivenkeep, and he stood still in the loudness.*

- ***Ketherd Hethow Dhresk*** is the lexicon's (O2896), the Rite's name for him; the slate's *ketherd* (¶13) is the same word.
- ***re ston o sol***: *ston*, the oath's "It stands", and *sol*, "still, at rest".

**¶37**

> Ul et vess ull re hethym et greller somm re ryte {{Halyna}} sona rellor eth sona Stonwryt ul vimm et tolm clenn. Re hadh hos et bresk, eth re runn ullen. Nath noss lunn lom dem rytyl sona rellor. Lo sest et Stonwryt re ryte brodath sa nel ryt, rytet hy et covv, stinel eth stinel.

That night I held the lamp while {{Halyna}} cut their mark and their vow under the new stone. The one set the chisel, and the other struck. Their mark is not mine to draw. Beside the vow they cut words that are not a vow, copied from the cloak stroke for stroke.

Word for word: *In that night I held the lamp while Halyna cut their mark and their Stonwryt under the new stone. One laid the chisel, and the other struck. Freedom is not at me for drawing their mark. At the shoulder of the Stonwryt they cut words that are not a vow, cut from the cloak, stroke and stroke.*

- ***Re hadh hos et bresk, eth re runn ullen***: the old unit's words.
- ***Nath noss lunn lom dem rytyl sona rellor***: "their mark is not mine to draw" is the lexicon's "may" (*doss lunn lo* Y *dem* VN, O0486), negated: *freedom is not at me to draw their mark*, the leave that is not hers. *Ryt* is also "draw (a sign)". *Lunn* is the Title's fourth thing, *ol lunnath*, cut a paragraph later. **Back-translation fix.** The first draft had the "cannot" idiom (*Nath noss rytyl sona rellor lom*), and the blind reader read "I cannot write their sign", a want of skill where the English says a want of right.
- ***re ryte brodath sa nel ryt***: "they cut words that are not a cut vow". *Ryt* is the verb "cut letters" and the noun "a vow", so the English's turn (*cut … not a vow*) is one word in Orrowen, said twice.
- ***stinel eth stinel***, "stroke for stroke": *stinel*, "a stroke, a single cut", in the distributive *X eth X* (*hos eth hos*, *trenn eth trenn*).

> Re wemment et vennuldath et tolm ul et ganna, et rellor dem vimm. Ul norn et gannalest re ferr et Crenn et hulter, hy vodh ew o yaldat et hald eth et gorrol. Re orr o dem et hald pawast, eth saed hovv um o sestow.

No one hath moved the staff. If you doubt the tale, go and look at the dust on it.

**¶43**

> Amm re yal et tolm cadhat, eth et garleleth midhet, re luska {{Halyna}} en dem vimm hosel lo et greller, dem gadhol et halflenneth pawast. Re orrom dem vimm, kylyl um hylyl, ul vodh re nendym sona waegyth ul morrol. Re wemmym et hellenneth pondul.

Word for word: *When the stone was laid, and the tools cleaned, Halyna sent me downward alone with the lamp, for laying the slates again. I went downward, turning upon turning, where I watched their light in going. I lifted the empty flasks.*

- ***amm re yal et tolm cadhat, eth et garleleth midhet***: the old unit's words, which the slate's line in ¶46 says again in the present (*Amm doss et tolm cadhat …*). *Garlel*, "a tool", is "a small hand".
- ***re luska Halyna en***: *lusk* is also "send (off)".
- ***kylyl um hylyl***: ¶4's turning, Seren's own feet now on it. ***ul morrol***: the progressive, "in going" (*ul* beds *orrol*).

**¶44**

> Re yal hos flenn tul.

There was one leaf more.

Word for word: *There was one leaf yet.*

- *Tul*, "yet, still", for "one more", as the old unit had it.

**¶45**

> Re yal o dholmath strom eth vothen, hosen doss tolmath dhev, eth hosen doss en dolmath tul. Re lymmym o dem susk ul vimm en govv, eth re yal o brid umom. Lo et vorr re veske {{Halyna}} o lom: flenn et prassel tevel sost, ul et orlath hosast.

Its letters were large and careful, as a child's are, and as mine are yet. I carried it up inside my shirt, cold against my skin. At the fire {{Halyna}} read it me: the leaf of the youngest apprentice of the First Days.

Word for word: *Its letters were great and careful, as a child's letters are, and as my letters are still. I carried it upward under my cloak, and it was cold upon me. At the fire Halyna read it to me: the leaf of the youngest prentice, in the first days.*

- ***o dholmath***, "its letters": every letter of the course-hand is a *tolm*, a stone (§1). A child's letters are large stones.
- ***ul vimm en govv … brid umom***: the lexicon has no shirt and no skin-word that would not read as the stone's crust (¶21), so the leaf rides under her cloak and is cold upon her.
- ***et prassel tevel sost***, "the youngest apprentice": *sost* after an adjective is "very", the superlative (S02's *et gell sost*).

**¶46a** *(first ink)*

> Amm doss et tolm cadhat, eth et garleleth midhet, resker lo et odh, eth cadh hos hyor trenn,

"When the stone is set and the tools are cleaned, we sit at the hearth, and one of us layeth a tale."

Word for word: *When the stone is laid, and the tools cleaned, we sit at the hearth, and one of us lays a tale,*

**¶46b** *(second ink)*

"As a course is laid on a wall, one course each night, so the wall and the telling rise together."

- Seren tells this leaf, so "we" is the plural throughout (*re nadhumar*, *re wesker*), where the old unit, in Halyna's mouth, had the dual for the finding. *Pawast* stands last because after *o* it would read "its second" (S01).

**¶48**

> Ul et vess fennet re ston Kael, hy vodh re nahebb o reskyl, eth re hadh o et trenn hosast. Re yal o um et Crenn.

The next night Kael stood, for he would not sit, and laid the first tale. It was of the Captain.

Word for word: *In the next night Kael stood, for he refused sitting, and he laid the first tale. It was upon the Captain.*

- ***re ston Kael***: Kael's own *Nath reskym; es stonom* (pilot I.1). ***Re yal o um et Crenn***: "of the Captain" is *um*, "upon, about" (S02's *um uldath sennet*).
- ***hy vodh re nahebb o reskyl***, "for he would not sit": *nahebb*, "refuse" ('un-give', O1523), with the verbal noun, as another leaf's *re nahebb ey myskyl hy vodh*, "she would not say why". The man who will not give the hearth his sitting gives it its first tale. **Back-translation fix.** *hy vodh nath re resk o* was read "because he had not sat": the refusal was lost.

**¶49**

> Re harm et lunt o: Ketherd. Ul o volt sost, ew tul hosel et Crenn o.

The people called him Commander. In his own heart he was still only the Captain.

Word for word: *The people called him: Commander. In his own heart, he was still only the Captain.*

- The old unit's last paragraph, unchanged: *Re harm et lunt o: Ketherd. Ul o volt sost, ew tul hosel et Crenn o.*

## I.5 · *VOLLOL ET TRESKAN*, THE NAMING OF THE RIVENMEN

### Title

> ## *Vollol et Treskan*

**I.5 · The Naming of the Rivenmen**

Word for word: *The naming of the Treskan.*

- The lexicon's title (O2777), as the old unit gave it.

### Dateline

**¶1**

> *Et grem vranast, hedh et orrol dem vimm.*

*The sixth winter, the eve of the going-down.*

Word for word: *The sixth winter, the eve of the going downward.*

- ***hedh et orrol dem vimm***, "the eve of the going-down": the old unit's phrase (wf8 addition S03-22, *orrol dem vimm dem et cummath*), its havens left to ¶39. *Hedh* is "the last light, dusk; the eve".

### Seren's headnote

**¶2**

> *Re hadh Voss sa re vess, hy Dhavow Hemm, vennuld wramman et tavow, sa re dhev ul davowlest o scaelur um et tavow.*

*Laid by Voss who asked, of Eldhythe, True Man of the harbour battery, that was raised in his grandmother's harbour-house on the quay.*

Word for word: *Laid Voss who asked, from the Old Landing, True Man of the battery of the harbour, who grew in the harbour-house of his grandmother upon the quay.*

- The headnote frame with the canon epithet *Voss sa re vess*. ***hy Dhavow Hemm***, "of Eldhythe": the haven's Orrowen name is *Tavow Hemm*, "the old landing" (`orrowen_v2` §4.7), softened after *hy*.
- ***vennuld wramman et tavow***: *bramman*, "a battery" (O0124), softened as the possessor of *vennuld*.
- ***sa re dhev ul davowlest o scaelur um et tavow***: *tev*, "grow" (and "a child"), for "was raised"; *tavowlest*, "a harbour-house" (O2495), bedded by *ul*; *scaelur*, "a grandmother, an old mother of the hearth". *Tavow* is harbour, landing and quay in one word, and Voss's headnote says it three ways.

### Voss's telling

**¶3**

> Hebb et tolm lom. Tessym tessyl lo et odh sy hy fedh sell.

Give me the stone. I have owed this hearth an answer a long while.

Word for word: *Give the stone at me. I owe an answer at this hearth, from a long time.*

- The old unit's ¶1, exactly: I.1's opening, and *tessym tessyl*, Voss owing an answer with the verb of answering (canon *tess*, "answer; stand surety for, owe").

**¶4**

> Virr lo rask et grem hosast, re lammom memm um et lern rerddhalden, ul vimm stell, hy vodh nath mellor paskard et trun lo ull. Amm re yalar tevath, re hyler et bunth olen um et tavow, somm re hunnerent et tevath lyss. Et tolm dasken umo, re hadhom o, en sost.

Near the end of the first winter I buried a friend on the east face, under a cairn, for the ground there will not take a spade. As boys we had turned the rope together on the quay, while the little ones jumped. The last stone on him I laid myself.

Word for word: *Near at the end of the first winter I buried a friend upon the eastern face, under a cairn, for a spade does not enter the ground there. When we were children, we turned the rope together upon the quay, while the small children skipped. The last stone upon him, I laid it, I myself.*

- ***Virr lo rask et grem hosast***, "near the end of the first winter": *virr* "near" with *lo*, as I.5 ¶28's *virr sost lo et kesketh*; *dask*, "the end", softened after *lo*. The same root closes the paragraph: *et tolm dasken*, "the last stone", is the stone of the end. **Back-translation fix.** The first draft said it with the lintel (*Lo hosk et grem hosast*), and the blind reader took the lintel for the head of the winter, its onset: the end read as the beginning.
- ***nath mellor paskard et trun lo ull***: the old unit's words, now in the present, as the English ("will not take a spade"). *Grik*, "take", is not used after *nath*: it would bed to *nrik*.
- ***re hyler et bunth***: *kyl*, "turn" (Halyna *turned* the tale-stone, S01). ***re hunnerent***: *hunner*, "skip, leap" (the lexicon's skipping-song is *sodhvell*).
- ***Et tolm dasken umo, re hadhom o, en sost***: a fronted object taken up by *o* (pilot G6), and *en sost*, "I myself" (S01's *Nath gethym et mesk, en sost*).

**¶5**

> Ew Yory o voll.

His name was Jory.

Word for word: *His name was Jory.*

- ***Yory*** (addition S03-02). The tongue has no *j* (§2.5), and the analyzer rejects one. Orrowen's only /j/ is the *y* the romanisation writes before a vowel (§2.4), so the Book's *Jory*, said /ˈjori/, is *Yory* in the ink, and Seren's English writes its first sound with a J. The leaf-hand has no letter for /j/ but the softened *g* (§6.3), so it writes his name with the softening bite under its first letter, the one name in the Book that carries a bite. **[orchestrator]** Every leaf that names him (I.3, IV.5, V.1, V.5) should take one form; this is the proposal.
- ***Ew Yory o voll***: the copula, predicate first. *O* before *voll* shows no bite (§3.5), but after a copula and its predicate it can only be the possessive.

**¶6**

> Ul et vess ull re vessym pessyl lo et vorr sy. Re yalom brid, eth re haeverym o hosen pessyl duss.

That night at this fire I asked a question. Cold I was, and I meant it hard.

Word for word: *In that night I asked a question at this fire. I was cold, and I meant it as a hard question.*

- ***Ul et vess ull re vessym***: "night" and "asked" are the canon's homophones (*vess*, *re vess*), and the man who asked is *Voss sa re vess*.
- ***re haeverym o hosen pessyl duss***: the old unit's "I meant it as a hard question". *Re haeverym o duss* would put *duss* after *o*, where it reads "its hardness" (*o russ*).

**¶7**

> Hy vodh gorrar dem dolm eth crest?

"Wherefore fight we for ice and stone?"

Word for word: *Why do we fight for stone and ice?*

- The old unit's question, word for word: *dem crest* would bed *crest* to *grest*, "harm", so the stone goes first, where the bedding is harmless (*tolm* > *dolm*). Voss asks it so both times (¶37).

**¶8**

> Hos fedh re runn Stannard um et odhdholm, eth re dhess o lom.

Once on the hearthstone knocked Stannard, and answered me.

Word for word: *One time Stannard knocked upon the hearth-stone, and he answered me.*

- ***re runn Stannard***: the knock of I.4 ¶18, the quarrier's hand on the wood, now on the hearthstone (*odhdholm*, O1856). ***re dhess o lom***: *tess*, "answer", softened.

**¶9**

> Nel et crest sa yorrom demo.

"It is not the ice I fight for."

Word for word: *It is not the ice that I fight for.*

- The old unit's line, word for word: the negative cleft, and the preposition left behind in the relative (*sa yorrom demo*, pilot G5).

**¶10**

> Ew ull tessyl gell, veth ew o dhessyl. En dessyl, re rytym o lo dhrenn et gorrol tunn.

That was a good answer, but it was his. Mine hath taken me the whole war to cut.

Word for word: *That was a good answer, but it was his answer. My answer, I cut it along the whole war.*

- ***Ew ull tessyl gell, veth ew o dhessyl***: the old unit's words; the bite on *dhessyl* shows the possessive.
- ***En dessyl, re rytym o***: a fronted object (G6). *Ryt* is "cut letters; swear": Voss's answer is cut as a vow is, and in ¶18 the naming *is* one (*El ryt o*).

**¶11**

> El hyll niss tessyl niss, hosen re wrodh en scaelur o lom: mellor o um et tolmath sost pawast.

A slow answer is a slow tide, so my grandmother told it me: it cometh in over the same stones.

Word for word: *A slow answer is a slow tide, as my grandmother told it to me: it comes in upon the very stones again.*

- ***El hyll niss tessyl niss***: the copula with its predicate first (§3.4): "a slow tide [is] a slow answer". The two halves stand on the same adjective, as the English's.
- ***mellor o um et tolmath sost pawast***: *mellor*, "come in"; *et tolmath sost*, "the very stones": *sost* after a noun is "self, very; the same" (as I.4 ¶4's *ul et hoss sost*, "in the same breath"). *Tolmath* is also "letters", so the slow tide comes in over the same stones as an answer comes over the same words. **Back-translation fix.** Without *pawast*, "again", the blind reader heard a tide that comes "up to the very stones", one that arrives; the English's tide goes over the same ground once more, and *pawast* stands last, away from *o* (where it would read "his second").

**¶12**

> Re vosk o ul et dynd ul sestow et Ternil, et Kethow tunn ul et parow ul vimm et covv treskat. Re stonom ul lodh et kesteth ul et spenn sulven, eth Yory lo en sest. Hos fedh ul o hossenyth nath re yal o ul hossvellyl. Re ston et Crenn lo ol sest, eth et hulter ul o yorn.

It began the week after the Rite, the whole Keep in the ward under the torn cloak. I stood among the companies in the slush, and Jory at my shoulder. For once in his life he was not whistling. The Captain stood with us, and the staff in its corner.

Word for word: *It began in the week after the Rite, the whole Keep in the ward under the riven cloak. I stood in the mortar of the companies in the snowy mud, and Jory at my shoulder. One time in his life he was not in whistling. The Captain stood at our shoulder, and the staff in its corner.*

- ***Re vosk o***, "it began": *mosk*, "begin" (O1425), softened after *re*. *Mosk* is the Creed's "attack" only after a nasaliser (*bosk* bedded, lexicon §8.2); after *re* it can only be *mosk*. ¶19 closes on the same words. **Back-translation fix.** The first draft said a beginning *rose* (*re dhald o*, the old units' figure), and the blind reader read "it rose", with no beginning in it.
- ***ul lodh et kesteth***, "among the companies": the lexicon's *ul lodh* (O2632), "in the mortar of", is "between"; *kest* is slender, so *kesteth*.
- ***ul et spenn sulven***, "in the slush": "in the snowy mud" (*spenn*, "mud"; *sulven*, "snowy").
- ***ul hossvellyl***, "whistling" (addition S03-04): *hossvell*, "breath-song", a compound by the rule of *vennuld*, *Halvard*: a whistle is a song of the breath alone, and Jory whistled "the tune and never the words". *Hossenyth*, "life", in the same sentence, is the same breath. **[orchestrator]** I.3, IV.5, V.1 and V.5 whistle too.
- ***Re ston et Crenn lo ol sest***, "the Captain stood with us": *at our shoulder*, the place where Jory stands two sentences before (*Yory lo en sest*): Jory at my shoulder, the Captain at ours. **Back-translation fix.** *Re ston et Crenn lor*, "at us", was read "before us", a captain set apart from his people where the English stands him among them.
- ***eth et hulter ul o yorn***: *gorn*, "a corner", softened after *o*.

**¶13**

> Re yyst Kael Nydherd et tolmath ommat lo et ganna. Re yal hos galat lo, eth re vysk o hos fedh.

Kael the Counter climbed the fallen stones by the gate. One thing had he to say, and he said it once.

Word for word: *Kael the Counter climbed the fallen stones by the gate. One thing was at him, and he said it one time.*

- ***Re yal hos galat lo, eth re vysk o hos fedh***: the counter counts: one thing, one time. *Dem myskyl*, "for saying", would read as N·*byskyl*, "for tying" (pilot I.1 §3), so the second clause carries the saying.

**¶14**

> Re yorr et Ketherd.

"The Commander fought."

Word for word: *The Commander fought.*

- Canon (`orrowen_v2` §11.11), unchanged.

**¶15**

> Eth re dhunn et Stonwrytan tunn o, ul hos carm, hy {{Idrenna}} dem et tev lyss sost sa re lymm et nynth:

And the whole guild ended it, in one voice, from {{Idrenna}} down to the least child that carried its water:

Word for word: *And the whole guild finished it, in one voice, from Idrenna to the very small child that carried the water:*

- ***re dhunn et Stonwrytan tunn o***: *tunn* is "whole" and "finish, make whole" (O2607), the verb of *tunn ullen*, "the other finishes" (O2902). The whole guild makes the line whole: one word, said twice.
- ***ul hos carm***: *carm*, "call, cry; voice" (S01's *en garm*).
- ***sa re lymm et nynth***: I.4's headnote's water. The least child that carried it is the one who lays the Book.

**¶16**

> Re yald et Stonwrytan.

"The Stonewrights built."

Word for word: *The Stonewrights built.*

- Canon (`orrowen_v2` §11.11), unchanged.

**¶17**

> Ew ull et brodan tunn. Re yal loskat. Re vesker hos um ullen, hesperd eth tolmard. New pa lunt ol, sa re saede kaelir. Ew hos ol, hosen kyle pa dhrenn ul hos hald amm rhyn et lodh.

That was all the speech there was. It was enough. We looked at one another, soldier and mason. We were not two peoples sharing a ruin. One were we, as two courses become one wall when the mortar taketh.

Word for word: *That was the whole speech. It was enough. We looked one upon the other, soldier and mason. We were not two peoples who shared a ruin. We were one, as two courses change into one wall when the mortar sets.*

- The old unit's words, cut to the new English: *pa lunt … sa re saede kaelir* (the dual, and the relative agreeing with it); ***New pa lunt ol … Ew hos ol***, "not two peoples were we … one were we": two copula lines, *two* against *one*.
- ***amm rhyn et lodh***: *rhyn*, "set fast, of mortar", is the root of *Rhyna*.

**¶18**

> Ul et orl ull re hadhar voll et Kethow um et gaed. El Kethow Dhresk et cummath kethet lo et wemmeth myst, eth el cless et Kethow cless et wadh. El ryt o.

That day we laid the Keep's name on the land. Every haven the grey sails hold is Rivenkeep, and the edge of the sea is the edge of the Keep. It is a vow.

Word for word: *On that day we laid the name of the Keep upon the land. The havens held by the grey sails are Rivenkeep, and the edge of the sea is the edge of the Keep. It is a vow.*

- ***re hadhar voll et Kethow um et gaed***: the English now *lays* the name, so the verb is the mason's *cadh* (the old unit's *re hebber*, "gave").
- ***el cless et Kethow cless et wadh***, ***El ryt o***: the old unit's words. "Every haven" is the plural, "the havens": *gor cumm* would soften to *gor humm*, "every husband".
- ***El Kethow Dhresk et cummath kethet lo et wemmeth myst***, "every haven the grey sails hold is Rivenkeep": the participle *kethet*, "held", with *lo* for the holder, as ¶36 of I.4 has *lo et hulter* for the staff he was named by. The havens the grey sails *keth* are the *Kethow*: the holding-place is wherever the holding is wrong, and ¶20 ends *re yal et Kethow kethet*, "the Keep was held". **Back-translation fix.** The old unit's relative (*et cummath sa hethent et wemmeth myst*) has two plurals and one gap, and the blind reader took the havens for the holders, "the havens that hold the grey sails"; the participle leaves only one way to read it.

**¶19**

> Re vollar ol sost Treskan. Ol nedheth, vollar so: Orrowan, hy vodh ew Orrowan so; eth vollar ol sost: *ol*. Ul et parow ull re vosk o.

We named ourselves the Rivenmen. Our fathers we call Shorelanders, for so they were, and ourselves but *we*. In that ward it began.

Word for word: *We named ourselves Treskan. Our fathers, we name them: Orrowan, for Orrowan they were; and we name ourselves: we. In that ward it began.*

- **The recurring rendering** (Tier 3 §5.2 row 12): *vollar so: Orrowan … vollar ol sost: ol*, as the foreword writes it, with "our fathers" fronted and taken up by *so*. The English's "but" ("ourselves but *we*") is in the plainness of the pronoun; the formula is kept letter for letter.
- ***Ul et parow ull re vosk o***: "it began", as ¶12, with ¶12's back-translation fix (*re vosk o* for *re dhald o*).

**¶20**

> Lo dhrenn et orl ull re orrar dem susk, hos eth hos, dem et tolm ul vimm et hagess. Re hadh gor hos garl umo, eth re ryt o, hosen byskent uldath so wunthath um et tavow ul hedh. Re orr Yory dem susk ul lern, eth re orr o dem vimm ul hossvellyl. Amm re vellor et vess, re yal et Kethow kethet.

All that day we went up one by one to the stone under the pike. Each laid a hand on it and sware, as men make fast their ropes on the quay at evening. Jory went up ere me, and came down whistling. By dark the Keep was held.

Word for word: *Through that day we went upward, one and one, to the stone under the pike. Every one laid a hand upon it, and he swore, as men tie their ropes upon the quay at evening. Jory went upward in front, and he went downward in whistling. When the night came in, the Keep was held.*

- ***hos eth hos***, "one by one" (pilot I.1).
- ***hosen byskent uldath so wunthath***, "as men make fast their ropes": *bysk*, "tie".
- ***Re orr Yory dem susk ul lern***, "Jory went up ere me": *ul lern*, "ahead, in front" (O2631); a pronoun after *ul lern* is unsettled.
- ***re yal et Kethow kethet***: the holding-place held. *Kethow* is *keth* + *-ow*, so the Keep and its holding are one word.

**¶21**

> Re ryter: Es gether pawast gor dholm. Nel hos tolm sa nath re yal lor. Veth gor hos sa re yal.

We sware to take back every stone. Not one stone that was not ours. But every one that was.

Word for word: *We swore: We will hold every stone again. It is not one stone that was not ours. But every one that was.*

- The old unit's words: *Es gether pawast gor dholm* (O2914, *keth pawast*, "take back"), and the negative cleft with *lor*, "at us", for "ours".

**¶22**

> Nafedh re vallant et hemmeth sost et voll clenn, eth re vyskent nayalat. Medhent tul ul so naevalath: Orrow.

The eldest never liked the new name, and said naught. Into their shawls they murmur "the Shorelands" yet.

Word for word: *Never did the very old love the new name, and they said nothing. They murmur still into their blankets: The Shore.*

- ***et hemmeth sost***, "the eldest": *hemm* "an elder" is slender, so *hemmeth* (the old unit's back-translation fix), with *sost*, "very".
- ***Medhent tul ul so naevalath: Orrow***: *naeval*, "a blanket, a wool cloth", serves for the shawl; ¶28 puts it on Ebba and on the sleepers both. *Orrow* is "the Shore", and the tongue is named for it.

**¶23**

> Ul et grem sy, amm re reskent et noth scethel strom ul et cummath hosen nasath ul et venth, re vessym Ebba hemm hy Sedhnell hy vodh keth ey o. Nath re vesk ey dem susk hy et vorr, veth re heth ey garlath demo.

This winter, when the ten great hulls had sat down in the havens like herons in the shallows, I asked old Ebba of Fenholm wherefore she kept it. She looked not up from the fire, but held her hands to it.

Word for word: *In this winter, when the ten great hulls sat in the havens like birds in the shallow water, I asked old Ebba from Fenholm why she keeps it. She did not look upward from the fire, but she held hands to it.*

- ***et noth scethel strom***: a numeral before a singular noun; *scethel* is "one hull of a fleet" (§3.2, the singulative).
- ***hosen nasath ul et venth***, "like herons in the shallows": the lexicon has no heron; *venth* is "shallow water".
- ***Ebba hemm hy Sedhnell***: *Ebba* (addition S03-03); Fenholm's Orrowen name is *Sedhnell*, "fen-islet" (§4.7).
- ***hy vodh keth ey o … re heth ey garlath demo***: she *keeps* the name and *holds* her hands to the fire, one verb.

**¶24**

> El voll dem mymmyl o, amm es ston et hald dasken, eth es firr et Crenn Myst dasken cresket.

"It is a name to come home to, when the last wall shall stand, and the last Mystarch lie broken."

Word for word: *It is a name for the homecoming, when the last wall will stand, and the last Mystarch will lie broken.*

- The old unit's line without its *nayess*: v3 gives "Perhaps" a paragraph of its own (¶26). *Voll dem mymmyl*, "a name for the homecoming": *mymmyl* is also the Homecomings of III.1.

**¶25**

> Lo et vorr, nel lom, re vysk ey hos brod tul.

To the fire and not to me she said one word more.

Word for word: *To the fire, not to me, she said one word yet.*

- ***nel lom***: *nel* is the "not" of a thing named (*Nel et hesp, veth et tolm*).

**¶26**

> Nayess.

"Perhaps."

Word for word: *Perhaps.*

- *Nayess*, "perhaps ('unsure')" (O1746). Ebba's one word is one word in Orrowen too.

**¶27**

> Nath re hyrrilym o amm ull. Kaeverym: hyrrilym amm sy.

I did not understand it then. I think I do now.

Word for word: *I did not understand it then. I think: I understand now.*

- The old unit's words: "I think I do now" is the yes-rule (§3.4), the verb said again.

**¶28**

> Ul lern et gemm vess sy, re resk ey desir um et prenthrask ull, virr sost lo et kesketh, eth ey naeval dem et biskyann. Re orrar dem ol naevalath, eth re luskar ey ul naedh. Ul et vess re vorr et odhvorr dem vimm, eth re hlenn nahos o.

Four nights since, she sat late on that bench-end, nearest the coals, her shawl to her chin. We went to our blankets and left her sleeping. In the night the fire burned down, and no one mended it.

Word for word: *Before these four nights, she sat late upon that bench-end, very near the embers, and her blanket up to the neck. We went to our blankets, and we left her in sleep. In the night the hearth-fire burned downward, and no one mended it.*

- ***Ul lern et gemm vess sy***, "four nights since": "before these four nights", the phrase ¶31 takes up (*ul et gemm vess sy*, "these four nights").
- ***um et prenthrask ull*** (addition S03-05): *prenth* "a bench" + softened *dask* "end", as the lexicon's *orlrask* "nightfall" and *surrask* "the year's end".
- ***re luskar ey ul naedh***: "asleep" is *ul naedh*, "in sleep" (pilot I.1's *ul naedh um so visketh*).
- ***re vorr et odhvorr dem vimm, eth re hlenn nahos o***: S10's "the fire burned down", and *clenn*, "mend", softened (*hl-*, as I.4 ¶40).

**¶29**

> Ul et ryst nath re rynn ey.

In the morning she did not wake.

Word for word: *In the morning she did not wake.*

- ***nath re rynn ey***: I.4's waking verb (*re rynnym*, *rynnet*). There Rhyna was risen; here Ebba does not rise.

**¶30**

> Ul hedh re hadhar ey um et lern rerddhalden lo et hesperdeth. Re vyske {{Idrenna}} et ammelyl dem muldath sennet, saed eth saed. Re hadh Marl, tev ey tev, et tolm dasken. Umo re vysk o et voll hemm: Orrow. Re hyl nahos hyor o. Re wysk et Crenn saedel hy o hovv um hagess umoy.

At dusk we laid her on the east face by the soldiers. {{Idrenna}} said the prayer for the dead in two halves. Marl, her grandson, laid the last stone. Over it he said the old name, "the Shorelands." None of us corrected him. The Captain bound a strip of his half-cloak on a pike above her.

- *Merge (wf12), H32:* "a strip (of his half-cloak)" is *saedel*, "a small part" of the half, as V.5, V.7 and the Epilogue say it (S08, S09, S10); *ryssyth yarl* stays for the Book's *a hand's breadth*. (The unit had *Re wysk et Crenn ryssyth yarl hy o hovv um hagess umoy.*.)

Word for word: *At dusk we laid her upon the eastern face by the soldiers. Idrenna said the prayer for the dead men, half and half. Marl, her child's child, laid the last stone. Upon it he said the old name: Shore. No one of us changed it. The Captain tied a hand's breadth from his cloak upon a pike above her.*

- ***re hadhar ey***: the dead are *laid*, as stones and tales are, with the mason's *cadh*.

### 2. Cross-unit choices, for the orchestrator to make one [orchestrator]

1. **Jory is *Yory*** (S03-02). ### 3. Craft: the words that work twice

Seren's Orrowen keeps the English's turns and finds its own, always inside the grammar:

- ***ryt*** "cut letters; a vow": *they cut words that are not a vow* is *re ryte brodath sa nel ryt* (¶37).
- ***galdat*** "a work; done": the law of the staff ends *amm doss et galdat galdat* (¶15b).
- ***ketherd***: the slate's keeper (¶13b) is the Captain's Rite-name (¶36), and *to keep it* is *keth* again.
- ***sell*** "long; stretch": the breath between the lines *grows long* (¶16); the Captain looks *a long while* and *stretches out* his hands (¶34).

### 4. Collisions and tool limits stepped round

- **The softened *cl-*** is written *hl-* (*re hlenne*, I.4 ¶40; *re hlenn*, I.5 ¶28), as the canon's softened *cr-* is *hr-* (pilot G7). §2.5 lists neither onset; the analyzer accepts both. **[Jack / spec]**: if *hl-* should not stand, the plain alternative is *re hyle* (*kyl*, "change") for "mended".
- ***f* does not soften**: *re ferr*, *re ferre*, *re ferrer* (I.4 ¶22, ¶30, ¶41), never *verr*.
- **A dual verb with no subject reads as the imperative plural** (§3.4), so the pair is named (*hosen re wrodha Halyna o lom*, ¶8) or the plural taken (*Nath re yostant mesk hossen*, ¶18).
- ***mysk* after a nasaliser reads as *bysk*** (pilot I.1 §3): Kael's *one thing had he to say* says it with *re vysk* in the second clause (I.5 ¶13), not *dem myskyl*.
- ***grik* after *nath*** beds to *nrik*: I.5 ¶4 says *nath mellor paskard et trun* (the old unit's verb).
- **The bedded *pa*** is not read as a trigger by the analyzer (S02 Notes 4): *in two* (I.4 ¶35) keeps the old recast, *in two halves* (I.5 ¶30) is *saed eth saed*.
- ***dem cedh*** would bed to *dem gedh*, "for a father" (I.4 ¶25): the preposition goes to the end, *… demo*.
- **A stranded preposition closes its relative** (pilot G5): set straight after the object it met the next preposition (*ketherd lo um yaldat*, I.4 ¶13b) and was read as "at it"; it now stands last (*… um yaldat strom lo*), as *demo* does in I.4 ¶25 and I.5 ¶9 (back-translation).
- **A relative with two plurals and one gap** does not show which is the holder (*et cummath sa hethent et wemmeth myst*, I.5 ¶18): the participle and *lo* for the agent (*et cummath kethet lo et wemmeth myst*) leave one reading (back-translation).
- ***o* before a letter that does not soften** shows no possessive (§3.5): *pawast* is fronted after *o* (I.4 ¶26), the half-cloak is *saed covv um o sestow* (¶41), the slate *Hosen sy voll … o* (¶34).
