## 1 · V.2 · THE NIGHT OF THE NAMING

### Title

`S08-V2-00`

> ## *Vess Vollol*

**V.2 · The Night of the Naming**

Word for word: *The Night of the Naming.* (The lexicon's title, O2738: *vollol* is *voll* "a name; to name" with the verbal noun.)

### Dateline

`S08-V2-01`

> *Desir ul et syst pawast.*

*Late in the second summer.*

Word for word: *Late, in the second summer.*

- The dateline is a naming, verbless, as the stone grammar's headings are; *desir* "late, after the time" stands first, as the English puts *Late*. *ul et syst pawast*: the ordinal follows its noun (*Hyll Pawast*, *et grem hosast*).

### Seren's headnote

`S08-V2-02`

> *Re hethym et greller vess ull, eth re hadhom sy lo et odh ul hedh et ryst. Solant et crynireth sa wrodhom umont ul et nelter vimm sost tul; orr hos dem vimm amm maver, eth cadh hos o yarl umont.*

*I held the lamp that night, and laid this at the hearth on the evening after. The planks I tell of lie yet in the lowest vault, and whoso will may go down and lay a hand on them.*

Word for word: *I held the lamp that night, and I laid this at the hearth in the evening of the morrow. The planks that I tell of rest in the very lowest vault still; one goes down when one wishes, and lays one's hand upon them.*

- ***Re hethym et greller***: Seren holds the lamp with *keth*, the verb in which Halyna hold the wood (`orrowen_v2` §3.7). The foreword's picture (*When they know the wood, I hold the lamp*) is one verb in Orrowen, and Seren lets it work twice.
- ***ul hedh et ryst***, "in the evening of the morrow": *ryst* is "the morning; the morrow". *lo hedh* would be read as *lo* + S·*cedh* "who" (the analyzer reads it so), so the evening takes *ul*, which leaves *h* alone.
- The second sentence is old S07's (V.2's headnote in v1.3), with the teller turned to the first person: *sa wrodhom umont*, "that I tell upon", a relative with its preposition left behind (pilot I.1 G5). The v3 headnotes carry no facing line, so S07's *Doss flenn et mesk ul o lern* is not here.

### Seren's telling

**¶3** · `S08-V2-03`

> Keth et tolm, Seren.

Hold the stone, Seren.

Word for word: *Hold the stone, Seren.*

- The Invocation's command (`orrowen_v2` §11.10), the bare opener of every leaf Seren lays. A name called softens (§3.3); *s* does not change.

**¶4** · `S08-V2-04`

> Lo dhrenn et grem hosast ull, re dhyvull et vorr sy um et wemmeth myst. Re vysk Kael: Re naelurant et kethow navenn; eth re dhyvullom hosen et ullen.

All that first winter this fire laughed at the grey sails. "They have picked the wrong fortress," said Kael, and I laughed with the rest.

Word for word: *Through that first winter this fire laughed upon the grey sails. Kael said: "They chose the wrong keep"; and I laughed as the others did.*

- ***dhyvull um***, "laugh upon": the butt of a laugh is what it is laid upon, as a feeling is laid upon a person (§3.7). *lo et ullen* would be "at the others", so "with the rest" is *hosen et ullen*, "as the others".
- **The jest is Kael's in Count I of V.5 in the same words** (*Re naelurant … et kethow navenn*), and ¶30 answers it with the same verb: *Nath re naelurant galat*.
- ***et vorr sy***, "this fire": the hearth's company by its fire. The last plank's word (¶27) is *Vorr*.

**¶5** · `S08-V2-05`

> Ul vess desir ul Hyll Pawast, re orra {{Halyna}} dem vimm, dem et nelter hemm sost ul vimm et hellur sy, ul vodh re yal et ternil wesket. Re fennym sona, eth et luth eth et grellereth lom. Doss o brid ul et syst hosen ul et grem. Re solant et trenneth, myst um vyst, hosen trunow kylet sa re hebb tolmath.

One night late in the Second Tide, {{Halyna}} went down to the oldest room under this hall, where the Rite was found. I came behind with the ink and the lamps. As cold it is in summer as in winter. The rows lay grey on grey, like a field the plough hath turned up stony.

Word for word: *On a late night in the Second Tide, Halyna (two) went down, to the oldest vault under this hall, where the Rite was found. I followed them two, with the ink and the lamps at me. It is cold in the summer as in the winter. The rows lay, grey upon grey, like a turned field that gave stones.*

- ***re orra {{Halyna}}***: the pair takes the dual (3du *-a*, §3.9 item 1), here and throughout.
- ***et trenneth***, "the rows": the plural of *trenn* is "the courses of a wall; **the tales of a night**" (O2586). The rows of planks are the night's tales, and Seren uses the word for them all through the leaf.
- ***myst um vyst***, "grey upon grey", is built as the Cry's *tolm um dholm*.
- ***hosen trunow kylet sa re hebb tolmath***, "like a turned field that gave stones". The tongue has no plough; a ploughed field is a turned one (*kylet*, the participle of *kyl*, as in *tolm kylet*, the turned stone). The field *gave* stones as the planks *gave* words (¶7, the same verb).
- ***Doss o brid ul et syst hosen ul et grem***: "in the winter" keeps its article, because *ul grem* would bed to *nrem* (S07's table).

**¶6** · `S08-V2-06`

> Re yal lo ull pa vurr eth sullnoth crynir et grem hosast, eth gor hos rytet. Re lymment et haskardath so dem varn, pa haskard lo hos crynir, hy ul vodh re hebb et wadh so, eth et tant ul vidhyl um so sestyarlath. Re yal et ullen lo vorrath et hesperdeth, eth lo et hyll. Re voll et hald so: et flenneleth. Ew hosel crynireth so.

Fifty-three planks of the first winter lay there, and every one carved. The runners had carried them home two to a plank, from wherever the sea gave them up, the salt running down their arms. The soldiers' fires had the rest, and the tide. The wall called them the tablets. They were but planks.

Word for word: *There were there fifty-three planks of the first winter, and every one carved. The runners brought them home, two runners to one plank, from where the sea gave them, and the salt pouring upon their arms. The rest were at the fires of the soldiers, and at the tide. The wall named them: the tablets. They were only planks.*

- ***pa vurr eth sullnoth***, "two twenties and thirteen", is fifty-three in the Shore's twenties (O1899, S07).
- ***pa haskard lo hos crynir***, "two runners at one plank": *two to a plank* is two runners to each.
- ***Re yal et ullen lo vorrath et hesperdeth***: "had the rest" is the at-idiom of having (§3.7). The fires are the soldiers' warmth; nothing here is a custom.
- ***et flenneleth***, "the little leaves", is the wall's word for the tablets (O0626). Seren writes each word "to a leaf" (¶7): the wall's name and her page are one word.

**¶7** · `S08-V2-07`

> Gor hos hyont veth hos, re hebb o hos brod lo {{Halyna}}; eth re rytym et brodath, hos brod lo flenn. Re hebb et hos nayalat, veth re yal o rytet gell hosen gor hos. Dyndeth ul sestow et hosast re lymm et wadh o.

Each but one had given {{Halyna}} one word, and each word I wrote on a leaf of its own. That one had given naught, though it was carved as finely as any. The sea brought it in weeks after the first.

Word for word: *Every one of them but one, it gave one word to Halyna; and I wrote the words, one word to a leaf. The one gave nothing, but its carving was good like every one. Weeks after the first, the sea brought it.*

- ***Gor hos hyont veth hos, re hebb o …***: the fronted subject taken up by *o* (pilot I.1 G6), as S07.
- ***hos brod lo flenn***, "one word to a leaf", is built as ¶29's *hos brod lo sceth*, "one word to a hull".
- **The time is fronted** (*Dyndeth ul sestow et hosast re lymm et wadh o*): set after the object pronoun *o*, *dyndeth* would be read as "its weeks" (the analyzer reads it so).

**¶8** · `S08-V2-08`

> Re lymment crynireth Hyll Pawast pa rellor gor hos, rytet virr, hosen rytent vollath virr um hosk hemm. Um et dhysal re rystulla {{Halyna}} kethyl vana olen, eth re yal tresk ul et brodhol gor fedh. Nath re hlennym so lo haevereth. Dem vess ull, re orr nahos dem vimm dem bessyl et pa vurr eth sullnoth: ho ross mest?

The planks of the Second Tide bare two signs apiece, cut close, as names are cut close on an old lintel. On the shingle {{Halyna}} learned to hold two at once, and there were gaps in every telling. I have not mended them with guesses. Till that night no one had gone down to ask the fifty-three for more.

Word for word: *The planks of the Second Tide bore two signs apiece, cut near, as they cut names near upon an old lintel. Upon the shingle Halyna learned the holding of both together, and there was a gap in the telling every time. I did not mend them with guesses. Until that night no one went down to ask the fifty-three: is there more?*

- ***gor hos***, "apiece" (O0763); ***rytet virr***, "cut near".
- ***hosen rytent vollath virr um hosk hemm***: the lintel of the western stair, where names stand close; it is also the lintel under which a pair's one name is cut, and two signs cut close are read here by a pair.
- ***Nath re hlennym so lo haevereth***: *clenn* "mend" softened (*hl-*, as in the canon formula's *nahlennet*); *lo* softens *kaevereth*. The foreword's own *I have not mended it with guesses* would take the same words.
- **The asking is given as asked**, *ho ross mest?* (the question of the state verb, O2982): Orrowen has no "that" before a clause.

**¶9** · `S08-V2-09`

> Ew nasell et vess, eth ew sell et trenneth; hy ull re hadha et tolm hosast ul et pa rask, Rhyna lo et bystir eth Halvard lo et hald deld. Re veskym {{Halyna}} nafedh ul gethyl hy ullen, dem vess ull. Eth nath re yala deld hy ullen: sellyth et nelter.

The night was short and the rows were long, so they began at the two ends, Rhyna at the stair and Halvard at the far wall. Never ere then had I seen them work apart. They were not far apart: the length of the vault.

Word for word: *The night was short, and the rows were long; so they two laid the first stone at the two ends, Rhyna at the stair and Halvard at the far wall. I had never seen Halyna holding apart, until that night. And they two were not far from each other: the length of the vault.*

- ***re hadha et tolm hosast***, "they two began", is "lay the first stone" (O2859).
- ***Re veskym {{Halyna}} nafedh ul gethyl hy ullen, dem vess ull***: *nafedh* "never" after a plain past (IV.4); the pair's work is holding (*keth*, nasalised after *ul*). The rest is S07's sentence.

**¶10** · `S08-V2-10`

> Re rik Rhyna et crynir hosast sost, et fodh sa re vysk *orrow*, eth hos cless sell treskat lo. Re rik Halvard et hos nawrodh, eth re yal cless sell treskat lo, hosen lo et hosast.

Rhyna took up the first plank of all, the wave that had said *shore*, with its one long edge split raw. Halvard took up the silent one, and its long edge was raw also.

Word for word: *Rhyna took the very first plank, the wave that said* shore, *and one long edge riven at it. Halvard took the mute one, and there was a long edge riven at it, as at the first.*

- ***et fodh sa re vysk* orrow**: the wave's own word is set in italics and lower case, as the Book sets *shore*.
- **"Split raw" is *treskat*, "riven"**, the word of the Torn Cloak and of the Keep's own name. A raw edge is a riven one, and the riving is the point of the leaf.
- ***hos cless sell treskat lo***, "one long edge riven at it": the at-idiom of having again. "Also" is said by likeness: *hosen lo et hosast*, "as at the first".

**¶11** · `S08-V2-11`

> Re stonom ul lodh {{Halyna}}, eth et greller lom. Nath mellor helvhoss dem et gunn sy, eth re dhald et vem venn ul en narl.

I stood between them with the lamp. No wind cometh so far below, and the flame stood up straight in mine hand.

Word for word: *I stood in the mortar of Halyna, with the lamp at me. No wind comes into this deep, and the flame rose plumb in my hand.*

- ***ul lodh {{Halyna}}***, "in the mortar of Halyna", is S07's: *ul lodh* is "between", and Seren stands where the mortar stands.
- ***dem et gunn sy***, "into this deep", is the phrase of Seren's own untold line (*Nath noss tul kethyl et gunn sy lo Halyna*, `orrowen_v2` §11.4). The vault is a deep, and so is what they are about to hold.
- ***et vem venn***, "the flame plumb": *venn* is true and plumb, a mason's straight.

**¶12** · `S08-V2-12`

> Re wemm Rhyna ey crynir. Lo et hald deld re wemm Halvard o hrynir. Hosen sy taldant et bysketeth deld olen eth et bysketeth virr, hy vodh el hos piskur tunn o.

Rhyna lifted her plank. At the far wall Halvard lifted his. So rise the far knots of a net with the near, for it is all one net.

Word for word: *Rhyna lifted her plank. At the far wall Halvard lifted his plank. Like this the far knots rise together with the near, for it is one whole net.*

- ***re wemm***, "lifted": *bemm* softened after *re*. It sounds as *wemm* "a sail"; after *re* only the verb can stand.
- ***ey crynir***, "her plank": *ey* never mutates (§3.5), so her plank shows no bite; ***o hrynir***, "his", shows it.
- ***bysketeth***, "knots", are *bysk* "bind" made things: the net is held by its bindings. ***hosen sy***, "like this" ("so"); ***hy vodh el hos piskur tunn o***, "for it is one whole net".

**¶13** · `S08-V2-13`

> Ul hos clemel re hyle et pa lern.

Their two faces changed in the same moment.

Word for word: *In one moment the two faces changed.*

- ***re hyle et pa lern***: a dual subject takes the dual verb (3du of *kyl*), as ¶32's *cemme pa saed*. *so pa lern* "their two faces" would soften *pa* to *va*, which is "your"; the article says it plainly.

*To shore. As root to water, go. Wait on no bough.*

Word for word: *To shore (bedded). As a root to water, go. Do not wait upon a bough.*

- **Two writings stand for this paragraph.** The chip above is the carving on the plank: E1-01, the root alone in green wood from a war-pith, reused verbatim from old S07 and the coverage pass (0 errors, 0 warnings); its literal reading is the Book's line word for word. **¶16** · `S08-V2-16`

> Hy et dask deld, eth et crynir nawrodh lo, re vysk Halvard o olen, brod um wrod.

From the far end, with the silent plank in his hands, Halvard said it with her, word for word.

Word for word: *From the far end, with the mute plank at him, Halvard said it together, word upon word.*

- ***eth et crynir nawrodh lo***: a clause with no verb, the at-idiom ("the mute plank at him"); ***brod um wrod***, "word for word" (O2971, S07).

**¶17** · `S08-V2-17`

> Hy ull re wrodh gor hrynir. Re hadhar so ul drenn, hosen re hebb et wadh so; eth vess ull re hebb gor hos o dhrenn sost. Et vess tunn re orra {{Halyna}} lo dhrenn et trenneth, Rhyna dem Halvard eth Halvard dem Rhyna; eth re orrom ul lodh, eth re rytym. Ul molt et trenneth re yal grest um en narl dem et brellir, eth nath re hessym. Dossant lo sy et ullen, hosen re rytym so:

After that every plank spake. In the order the sea gave them up we had laid them, and that night each gave up its own. All night {{Halyna}} went along the rows toward each other, and I went between and wrote. By the middle rows mine hand ached to the wrist, and I stopped not. Here are the others, as I wrote them:

Word for word: *Then every plank spoke. We had laid them in course, as the sea gave them; and that night each gave its own course. The whole night Halyna went along the courses, Rhyna toward Halvard and Halvard toward Rhyna; and I went in the mortar, and wrote. In the middle of the rows there was hurt upon my hand up to the joint, and I did not stop. Here are the others, as I wrote them:*

- **The English plays on *order*** (a sequence, a command). Orrowen plays on *trenn*: the sea's order is *ul drenn*, "in course" (O2977), and what each plank gave up is ***o dhrenn sost***, "its own course", which is also "its own tale" (*trenn* is a tale laid in one night). `S08-V5-02`

### 3. Choices to look at (each reversible)

1. **The carvings in V.2 are written twice**: the plank's chip in GN v2 (old S07's practice: the wood's own words are grain), and, in the blockquote, the order as Halyna spoke it and Seren wrote it, in Orrowen and in the wood's role (`<!-- W -->`). v3 gives Halyna's spoken reading in Orrowen on every wood leaf, and V.2 says *Here are the others, as I wrote them*: so Seren's leaf holds the spoken words, and the plank holds the carving. If the builder wants only the grain for a W passage, the blockquote can be dropped and nothing else moves. **[Jack]**
2. **Kael's oath closes with *Ston.*** (V.5 ¶9), as his two oaths in I.1 do: pilot I.1's one liberty, still Jack's call.
3. **"His belt" is *o simm*, "his belly"** (V.5 ¶71; O2296, also "a lap"). The tongue has no belt. The draft had *o volt*, "his middle", which is also "his heart"; the blind reader heard only the heart, and the cloak's length went with it, so the fact now stands and the overtone is given up (§BACK-TRANSLATION). A word for a belt could still be coined. **[Jack]**
4. **"The wood can fear" is *Doss ludalol lo et mesk*** (the at-idiom of being able), where old S08 had *Keth et mesk ludal* for v1.3's *knows fear*. If the hearth should hear the wood's knowing verb (*keth*) here, the old line can return, at the cost of *can*.
5. **"The fleet hath not finished" is "does not finish yet"** (*nath … tul*). A blind reader may hear "the sea is not yet whole"; *tunn* as a verb is attested only in *re dhunn ullen* (O2984).
6. **The part heads** of V.5 write the numerals as words (*Hos · Hyll Pynt* …), so every head is clean Orrowen; the Book's Roman numerals are the English's.
7. **The saying at the fire** is in the plural (*Naelura et molt sa luskos lont*), because v3's Kael says it *to the young gunners* with *ye*; O2980 keeps the singular. Both are regular; no row is added for the plural.
8. **Jory is *Yory*** (S08-01). The tongue has no *j* and no native /j/ but the worn *g*; *Yory* keeps the name's sound as near as Orrowen can, and the English's J- renders its y- as English renders a foreign y-. Four other leaves name Jory (I.3, I.5, IV.5, V.1): their units should take this row, or the merge should choose one form. **[Jack]**
9. **"Mad", "a coward", "hunger"** are *dend* "wild", *nawerd* "un-brave", *maver* "want; a need". The tongue has words for none of the first three; *maver* is chosen because it is also "require", and the leaf's point is that the hulls were obeying.

### 5. Seren's craft, where the Orrowen makes it

Seren writes both leaves (Kael's telling is his; the words are hers). Where the tongue offered a word that works twice inside the grammar, the leaf takes it, and the English carries the plain sense:

- ***trenneth***, the rows of planks, is "the tales of a night"; ***o dhrenn sost***, what each plank gave up, is "its own course" and "its own tale"; ***trenn dasken*** is the last row and the last tale.
- ***re hebb***: the sea *gave* the planks, the planks *gave* words, and the turned field *gave* stones.
- ***flenneleth***, the wall's "tablets", are "little leaves", and Seren writes *hos brod lo flenn*, "a word to a leaf".
- ***keth***: Seren *holds* the lamp in the verb in which Halyna *hold* the wood; the wood *held* one word to a hull; the hulls *held* the walls; Corlen *held* as the Captain would hold, and Voss *held* as Corlen held; looking *kept* the wall; the poor *keep* the thinnest share, and the hearth is bidden *hold* the dead.
- ***ul lodh {{Halyna}}***: Seren stands in the mortar between them; ***dem et gunn sy***, "into this deep", is the phrase of her own untold line.
- ***Vorr***, the Emberhythe plank's word, is "fire", the word of *et vorr sy*, the fire that laughed.
- ***maver***, the wall's name for the hulls' hunger, is also "require".
- ***pynt sost***, "quickest", is the Hasty Tide's own word; and the worst of the wood *has not come in yet* (*nath … tul*) and is growing *still* (*tul*): one word on either side of the full stop.
- ***kyl***: the fleet *turned* onto the east road, and only the bearer's own *turned* wild (*kyl ul*, "become"), into the wall's own name for the Wild of V.2.
- In V.5: the sounding hull *counts* the water (*nydhyl*), as Kael counts; Kael *counts* (*nydh*) the grey sails and *lays* (*cadh*) ours; the strip is a *saedel*, a little half of the half, bound with the bond-word *lodh*; Pellow's glass is the glass of Glasspire (*Sirrvell*), and he is the one who *looks* (*vesk*).
