## 1 · V.7 · *VESKSIRR UL MESKDHRENN*, THE MIRROR IN THE GRAIN

### Title

> ## *Vesksirr ul Meskdhrenn*

**V.7 · The Mirror in the Grain**

- The lexicon's title (O2734), kept from v1.3: *vesksirr* "a mirror" ('see-glass'), *meskdhrenn* "the grain" ('the wood's course').

### Dateline

> *Syst gemmest, heth leskest, eth grem vranast.*

*The fourth summer, fifth spring and sixth winter.*

Word for word: *Summer fourth, spring fifth, and winter sixth.*

- Ordinals follow the noun, as *ul et vess hosast* (I.1): *gemmest*, *leskest* (lexicon), *vranast* by the rule (I.1's verse). *heth* is "spring, the lengthening" (reserve) and also S·*keth* "held"; with no trigger before it, it is the season.

### Seren's headnote

> *Re hadhom o, Seren Pa Luth, ul sull trenn, gor dhrenn ul et vess sa re yal et rytet kethet. Ul et sull re hethym et greller um et dhysal, eth el et greller sy o.*

*Laid by me, Seren Two-Inks, on three nights, each the night its carving was known. On all three I held the lamp on the shingle, and it is this lamp.*

Word for word: *I laid it, Seren Two-Inks, in three courses, each course in the night that the carving was held. In the three I held the lamp upon the shingle, and it is this lamp.*

- **Active, in the first person**, as every stone headnote is active (*Re hadh* X …, Tier 3 §5.1): *Re hadhom o*.
- ***ul sull trenn, gor dhrenn*** is v1.3's: a *trenn* is "a tale laid in one night" (canon), so three nights are three courses, and the part heads stay *Trenn Hosast, Pawast, Sullast* (O2989–O2991). One word carries *night* and *course*. "Known" is *kethet*, "held".
- ***el et greller sy o***: the copula, predicate first, as I.1's *Ew hosel covv hesperd o*.

### Part I

> **Trenn Hosast: Sull Orrol, Hos Clem**

**Part I · Three Roads, One Hour**

- O2989, unchanged: v3 keeps v1.3's part titles (notes_v3).

> Keth et tolm, Seren.

Hold the stone, Seren.

- The Invocation's own first words (*Keth et tolm. Cadh et trenn.*), said to herself, as Kael opens his leaves with *Hebb et tolm lom*. *Seren* does not change in the vocative (*s*).

> Lo dhrenn et vess nasell ull re runnant et drunnatath ul et myst, hosen drunnath ul stannow deld. Lo o dhrenn re reske Enno eth Della ul sona lest hos gebb. Re sol rellor {{Enrella}} ul vimm o dholm hosast. Re hadh Enno et bresk, eth re runn Della. Re ston o hos trenn susk; susk hy ull, nafedh.

All that short night the drums knocked in the grey, like mallets in a far quarry. Through it sat Enno and Della in their house of one door. Their mark lay under its first stone. Enno had set the chisel, and Della struck. One course high it stood, and higher it stood never.

Word for word: *Through the course of that short night the drums knocked in the grey, like mallets in a far quarry. Through it Enno and Della (two) sat in their two's house of one door. The mark of Enrella rested under its first stone. Enno laid the chisel, and Della struck. It stood one course high; higher than that, never.*

- **One root three times**: the drums are *drunnatath* ('struck things', the soldiers' word, O0513), they *re runnant* (*drunn* "knock", *dr-* > *r-*, the 3pl with its plural subject, as *re rarrant et ganurardath* in Part II), like *drunnath*, mallets. The mason hears his own tool in the enemy's drums. *Back-translation fix*: the draft's bare *re runn* was heard as an unnamed striker beating the drums, every other plural subject in the unit taking *-Ant*.
- ***rellor Enrella***: the mark is named by the pair-name (the English names it in the caption below). *sona rellor* could not show its possessive (*r*; §3.5).
- ***Re ston o hos trenn susk; susk hy ull, nafedh***: the English chiasmus kept, the second half verbless.

> Garl ul narl re vellora sona ul et parow ull, ul vodh re vellorom hosel.

Hand in hand had they come into that courtyard, where I came in alone.

Word for word: *Hand in hand they two came in into that courtyard, where I came in alone.*

- ***re vellorom … hosel*** is I.2's own clause (v1.3, S01: *re vellorom ul et parow ull hosel*), which V.7 now answers. *garl ul narl*, "hand in hand": *garl* bedded after *ul*. The dual *re vellora* takes *sona* so that it is not heard as the imperative plural (S07).

> Ul et dhess re rarr et scethan lo sull orrol ul hos clem, eth re hresk o et hald lo rerddhald eth lo wridlern. Re yal gor yanna lodhat ul vathur, eth re yal vathurath tul. {{Orvenna}}, prassyanna et hald, sa nath garma fedh: re yalda sona, nawrodh, galat sa nath re yaldant prassath fedh tevar.

At dawn the fleet came by three roads in one hour, and broke the wall east and north. There were more breaches than pairs. {{Orvenna}} the wall-masters, that never raise their voices, did without a word what no masters ere then had done.

Word for word: *At the first light the fleet came by three roads in one hour, and it broke the wall at the east and at the north. Every bonded pair was in a breach, and there were breaches yet. Orvenna, the master-pair of the wall, who never cry out: they two built (did), mute, a thing that masters had never built (done) before.*

- ***lo wridlern***, not *ul*: the nasalising *ul* would bed *bridlern* into *mr-* (§2.5).
- **"More breaches than pairs" is told, not compared**: *gor yanna lodhat ul vathur*, "every bonded pair in a breach" (*ganna lodhat*, 'a bonded gate', O2907: a pair is a gate), and breaches left over. *gannath*, the plural, is "posts" (S05, S07), so the pairs are counted one gate at a time. *Back-translation fix*: the draft's bare *gor yanna* was heard "every gate was in breach", the gates of the wall and not the pairs; *lodhat*, the Bonded's own word, names the gate a pair.
- ***prassyanna*** "a master-pair" (lexicon) is "the wall-masters" in one word, dual by its making. "That never raise their voices" is *sa nath garma fedh*, "who never cry out"; "without a word" is *nawrodh*, "mute". **Seren's play**: *gald* is "build" before it is "do" (O0655), so the masters *build* what no masters had done: the one thing the wall-masters ever raised that parts the Bonded. The blind reader heard "built", and the line stands.

> Re luska {{Orvenna}} ganna hy ullen.

They sent a pair apart.

Word for word: *Orvenna loosed a pair apart.*

- ***lusk*** "loose, let go; send" is the verb II.3 gives God, *Treskol, nath re lusk Mardh o*, "Separation God did not permit" (O2937): the masters permit what God did not. *hy ullen*, "apart (from the other)". The pair-name as subject marks *luska* as the dual (*re luska sona ganna* would read "their pair", *sona* before an unbitten *g*).

> Re hebb Ormund et vathur rerddhalden lo Enno, eth re hebb Penna et bridlern lo Rella. Lo wisk et bystir re harm Enno um o sest: El hosen hos uld; eth re rig Della et ullen lo, ul haskol: lo yemm garl, eth nahos ullen dem nastathulol.

Ormund gave Enno the east breach, and Penna gave Della the north. At the stair-head Enno called back, "It is like being one person," and Della threw him the rest, running: "with four hands and no excuses."

Word for word: *Ormund gave the eastern breach to Enno, and Penna gave the north to Della. At the head of the stair Enno called over his shoulder: It is like one man; and Della threw the rest at him, in running: with four hands, and no other to blame.*

- ***lo Rella***: names mutate after a trigger like any word (S05 S3).
- ***lo wisk et bystir***, "at the stair-head" (*bisk* "head"), one letter from *visk*, "foot".
- **The jest, made to work twice**: *El hosen hos uld* / *lo yemm garl, eth nahos ullen dem nastathulol*, II.3's words exactly (S04, after its back-translation): "with four hands, and no other to blame". *Nastathul* "blame" is "un-praise" (O1671). After *garl* the ear also hears *garl ullen*, the left hand ("the other hand", O2886): four hands, and not one of them the other. What Della throws him is *et ullen*, "the rest": the same word sets the jest up. *Back-translation fix*: the draft's bare *nahos ullen* was heard "and no other", which lost the excuse, as S04's blind reader lost it in II.3.

> Doss et Lodhan hy ullen merr fedheth, eth deld nafedh. El deld bost lusul hald.

Seldom apart are the Bonded, and never far. A mile of wall is far.

Word for word: *The Bonded are apart a few times, and far never. It is far, a mile of wall.*

- The first sentence is v1.3 II.3's own (S04: *Doss et Lodhan hy ullen merr fedheth, eth deld nafedh*), which V.7 now quotes. *bost lusul* "a mile" ('four hundred paces'); the copula sets *deld* first.

> Ul rerddhald re rik pedh et hald, eth Enno lo. Nath re veskym o. Re vesk Hale.

In the east a shot took the wall, and Enno with it. I did not see it. Hale did.

- *Merge (wf12), H16:* "I did not see it. Hale did." is V.5's words (S08), *Nath re veskym o. Re vesk Hale.*. (The unit had *Nath re veskym o. Re vesk Hale o.*.)

Word for word: *In the east a shot took the wall, and Enno with it. I did not see it. Hale saw it.*

- "Hale did" is the yes-rule: the verb said again (§3.4).

> Lo wridlern re yalom lo sest Rella, eth et lodh lom. Re hyl ey et tolm dasken, dem rytyl et rellor ul o vimm. Re hadh ey sost et bresk, eth re runn ey; eth nath re varr et garl.

At the north I was beside Della with the mortar. The last stone she had turned over, to cut their mark beneath it. She set the chisel herself, and struck, and her hand shook not.

Word for word: *At the north I was at the shoulder of Della, and the mortar at me. She turned the last stone, for the cutting of the mark under it. She herself laid the chisel, and struck; and the hand did not shake.*

- ***eth et lodh lom***, "and the mortar at me", the clause with no verb (v1.3's *eth et greller lom*).
- ***re hyl ey et tolm***: *kyl* "turn". The turned stone, *tolm kylet*, is the dry cut's mark for *not* (§7.6): the last stone is turned before it is cut.
- "Her hand" is *et garl*: *ey* never mutates, so *ey garl* would read "she, hand" (G9).

> Hy ull re hess et drunn ul et helv, ul et garl.

Then the mallet stopped in the air.

Word for word: *Then the mallet stopped in the air, in the hand.*

- *hess* "stop" is the Guest's first word. *ul et helv*, "in the upper air". ***ul et garl***, "in the hand": her hand, as in the line before (*nath re varr et garl*), for *ey* cannot show the possessive (G9). The hand did not shake; the mallet in it stopped.
- *Back-translation fix*: without *ul et garl* the line was heard "the beating in the sky stopped", the night's drums (*drunnatath*) falling silent (*drunn* is "mallet" and "blow"), and not Della's stroke stopping mid-air as Enno fell.

> Re vesk ey dem rerddhald. Hy ull re ferr ey pawast, eth re ryt ey et stinel dasken, eth re hadh ey et bresk lo sest et rellor, eth re firr ey, lern um dholm.

She looked east. Then she bent again, and cut the last stroke, and laid the chisel by the mark, and lay down with her cheek against the stone.

Word for word: *She looked to the east. Then she bowed again, and cut the last stroke, and laid the chisel at the side of the mark, and lay down, face upon stone.*

- ***lern um dholm*** answers the Cry's *tolm um dholm*, "stone upon stone", with her face for the first stone. *stinel* "a stroke; a single cut".

> Re hadhom et nind strom um et lodh, hosen cadhant tolmardath. Nath re hyn o tul.

I put my thumb to the mortar, as masons do. It was still soft.

Word for word: *I laid the thumb upon the mortar, as masons lay [it]. It had not yet set.*

- ***nind strom*** "the thumb" ('the great finger', added). There is no helper "do": *hosen cadhant tolmardath* says the verb again (as Part III's *hosen ryter*).
- "Still soft" is the mason's own word, ***nath re hyn o tul***, "it had not yet set": *rhyn* "set fast (of mortar)", the root of *Rhyna*; *nath … tul* is "not yet" (§3.8). Part I ends on the same word: Halvard's mortar *ul rhynyl*, setting.

> Re senn ey.

She was gone.

Word for word: *She went out.*

- ***senn*** "die: go out, as a fire in peace" (canon). Part III's lamp goes out with the same verb.

> Re rarr Hale ul haskol, eth et brod hy rerddhald lo; eth nath re vysk o o. Nath re rarr haskard desir fedh.

Hale came at a run with the word from the east, and said it not. Never before had a runner come too late.

Word for word: *Hale came in running, and the word from the east at him; and he did not say it. A runner had never come late.*

- *desir* "late, after the time" is "too late". *nath re … fedh*, "never".

> Ul ryteth surr nath noss rellor ullen sa re dhunn hos garl o. Ul hedh re raevulom fedh sell et stinel sa re ryt Della hosel.

Theirs is the only mark in the annals that one hand finished. By the last light I looked long for the stroke Della cut alone.

Word for word: *In the annals there is no other mark that one hand finished. In the last light I sought a long time the stroke that Della cut alone.*

- "Theirs is the only mark" is "there is no other mark": the possessor is the sentence before.
- ***ul hedh***, not *lo hedh*: after *lo* the ear (and the analyzer) hears S·*cedh*, "who?". *hedh* is "the last light" itself (reserve). *raevul* "seek".

**The mark** (`::: native legend_stonwryt_enrella chip`, notes_v3 §5.6). The drawing is Enrella's mark, whole, on the underside of the north stone. > Rellor {{Enrella}}, tunn, um vimmlern et tolm bridlernen, hosen re vesk Seren o lo et baegyth dasken

The mark of {{Enrella}}, whole, on the underside of the north stone, as Seren looked upon it by the last of the light

- *Merge (wf12), H29:* "the last of the light" is I.2's *et baegyth dasken* (S02). (The unit had *hosen re vesk Seren o lo rask et baegyth*.)

- **V7-06** *Lo dhrenn et vess nasell ull re runnant et drunnatath ul et myst …* "Through that short night the drums knocked in the grey …" The 3pl takes its plural subject, as *re rarrant et ganurardath* and *re vellent et tolmardath* do in this leaf; the reader's flag (his "possible text issue") was right. *runn* stays the mallet's root, so the mason still hears his tool in the drums.
- **V7-08** *Re yal gor yanna lodhat ul vathur, eth re yal vathurath tul.* "Every bonded pair was in a breach, and there were breaches yet."
