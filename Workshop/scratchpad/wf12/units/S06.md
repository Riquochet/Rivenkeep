## IV.1 · OF THE FELLING OF THE BLACK PILLARS (*GOSTOL ET GANNATH DOMM*)

*A stone leaf, laid by Seren from the Harvest rolls, in her one ink. Only the two lines Halyna would not read past (¶3–¶4) are theirs, a half each in their two inks, as the Book's `HALYNA` marker sets them; "They would not have me mark whose is whose" (the foreword), so the inks are named first and second. In v1.3 this leaf was Halyna's by turns; in v3 it is Seren's, so it closes in her singular.*

### Title

> ## *Gostol et Gannath Domm*

**IV.1 · Of the Felling of the Black Pillars**

Word for word: *The felling of the black posts.*

- The lexicon's title (O0783), unchanged from the Tier 3 unit. *Gannath* is the canon's word for the pillars (*Gannath Myst*, the Mystholders, "the posts of the grey"); *gostol* is the felling of a tree.

### Dateline

> *Et heth pawast, ul Hyll Pawast.*

*The second spring, in the Second Tide.*

Word for word: *The second spring, in the Second Tide.*

- *heth* is "spring, the lengthening" (O0962); the ordinal follows its noun, as *Trenn Hosast*. *Hyll Pawast* is the lexicon's Second Tide. The two *pawast* answer each other, as the English's two *second* do.

### Seren's headnote (her one ink)

> *Re hadh Seren Pa Luth, hy scaevureleth et Stonwrytan um et Derryl. Re veske {{Halyna}} so lom ul vesseth, eth re rytym hyonta, pynt sost; re ferrent en dolmath hosen linth ul helvhoss.*

*Laid by Seren Two-Inks, out of the Harvest rolls of the guild. {{Halyna}} read them me by night, and I copied after them so fast that my letters leaned like reeds in a wind.*

Word for word: *Seren Two-Inks laid [it], from the rolls of the guild about the Harvest. Halyna read them to me in the nights, and I wrote after them two, very fast; my letters bowed like grass in a wind.*

- *Re hadh Seren Pa Luth*: the headnotes' *Re hadh* X (pilot I.1), with the canon epithet (O2266). Seren names herself in the third person, as the English does, and then says *lom* and *re rytym*.
- *et Derryl*, "the Harvest": the felling's own name, made as the lexicon makes *et Ommol*, "the Fall" (a new sense of *derryl*, S06-15). *um*, "upon, about", is what a roll is written upon.
- *hyonta*, "after them two": *hy* conjugated in the dual, for Halyna's reading.
- **The picture.** *re ferrent en dolmath hosen linth ul helvhoss*: *ferr* is "bow, bend low, lean", and *tolmath* are her letters (every letter is a stone). Reeds have no word of their own; grass in a wind keeps the picture. The same verb comes back in ¶18, where she bends the lamp (*re ferrym et greller*).
- *pynt sost; re ferrent*: "so fast that" is two clauses side by side, the second the first's result, as the leaf-hand lays them.

### ¶1 · Seren's ink

> Keth et tolm, Seren.

Hold the stone, Seren.

Word for word: *Hold the stone, Seren.*

- **The call that opens her own leaves** (craft §2.1). *Keth et tolm* is the Invocation's canon line, to one hearer; *Seren* is called by name, and *s* does not soften. It is an addition as a set phrase (S06-06), so the other units that open her leaves can use the same four words.

### ¶2 · Seren's ink

> Re vesker hos surr, amm ul vess sa re lilv et crest re rarrar dem vimmow vestul rytow. Re yal bunth et scaevurel hemm sost lilvet, eth re hresk o lo nindeth Hyna. Nath re veske {{Halyna}} hos trenn ulo lom tul; hosast re rytym sy:

A year had we read, when on a night of thaw we came to the bottom of the archive chest. The cord on the oldest roll was rotten, and broke in Rhyna's fingers. No line of it would {{Halyna}} read me till I had written this:

Word for word: *We read one year, when, on a night that the ice went soft, we came to the bottom of the archive chest. The rope of the very old roll was rotted, and it broke at Rhyna's fingers. Halyna did not yet read me one line of it; first I wrote this:*

- "We" is Seren and the pair, three, so the plural (*re vesker*, *re rarrar*); the dual is kept for Halyna alone.
- **A night of thaw** is *vess sa re lilv et crest*, "a night that the ice went soft": *lilv* is "soft; rot, go soft", and the next sentence's cord is *lilvet*, "gone soft". Seren lets the thaw and the rot be one word, and they are one thing: the old roll gives way as the ice does.
- *vimmow vestul rytow*, "the bottom of the archive chest": *vestul rytow* is the lexicon's archive chest (O2743), possessor of *vimmow*, "a low place, a bottom".
- *lo nindeth Hyna*, "at Rhyna's fingers": *Rhyna* softened as possessor (*rh* > *h*). Not *ul nindeth*: after *ul* the analyzer reads *nindeth* as nasalised *tindeth*, "thick ones".
- *Nath … tul*, "not yet" (canon, Seren's line), and *hosast*, "first": "would not read me … till I had written this" said as "did not yet read me … first I wrote this". The colon hands the leaf to Halyna's two lines.

### ¶3 · first ink (Halyna)

> Nath re hossan o,

"We two lived it not,"

Word for word: *We two did not live it,*

- The dual, as the Tier 3 unit had it (*re hossan*, 1du of *hoss*, "breathe, live"): a pair speaks of its own acts as two. The line is split at the comma, one ink to each half (`orrowen_v2` §3.9 item 5).

### ¶4 · second ink (Halyna)

> veth re hossant gedheth ol nedheth o. Re rytent o, eth nath re veskent fedh galat sa re rytent.

"but our fathers' fathers lived it. They wrote it down, and never read what they had written."

Word for word: *but the fathers of our fathers lived it. They cut it (wrote it), and they did not ever read the thing that they had cut.*

- *gedheth ol nedheth*, "our fathers' fathers" (O2965): the forefathers are the Shore's, so *ol*, the plural, and their verbs are plural (*re hossant*, *re rytent*).
- *ryt* is "cut letters, write": the forefathers *cut* the rolls and never *read* (*vesk*) them. *nath … fedh* is "not ever", as in the Tier 3 unit's IV.3.

### ¶5 · Seren's ink

> Ul scaevurel hemm sost doss et myst rytet hosen esket dommel ul gless et wadh, eth nayalat ullen. Hy ull el pessyl o, sa re vessent ul vessvarath, eth sa re dhess nahos fedh: vodh ew o, eth cedh re hoss o. Amm re rarr et tessyl, nath re yal o rytet ul ol narl.

In the oldest roll the grey is but a blackish veil on the rim of the sea. After that it is a question, asked in the night-watches and never answered: what it was, and who breathed it. The answer, when it came, was not written in our hand.

Word for word: *In the very old roll the grey is written as a blackish veil on the bank of the sea, and nothing else. After that it is an asking, which they asked in the night-watches, and which no one ever answered: what it was, and who breathed it. When the answer came, it was not written in our hand.*

- "but a blackish veil" is *rytet hosen esket dommel …, eth nayalat ullen*, "written as a blackish veil, and nothing else": the same "only" Brenn uses of the Guest's speech in IV.3 (*hosen sy brodhant so, eth nayalat ullen*).
- **A word that works twice.** *re vessent ul vessvarath*, "they asked in the night-watches": *pess* "ask" softens to *vess*, which is "night" (the canon's own homophony). The rolls ask in the night, and the asking sounds like the night.
- *sa re dhess nahos fedh*, "which no one ever answered": the frame of the wood leaves' close, *Eth re dhess nahos* (O0563).
- *vodh ew o, eth cedh re hoss o*: the questions set down as questions, the question word first and the verb after it.
- *ul ol narl*, "in our hand" (*ol* beds *garl*), as in ¶22.

### ¶6 · Seren's ink

> Hy ull re rarr molt syst, amm re yal lyrness um et cummath. Re orrant et scetheth dem neld, ul vodh nath re werdent so yedheth, eth ul gless et myst re weskent et gannath domm ul so dhrenneth, venn hosen venndhrenn. Re vollant et higileth so: Gannath Myst, hy vodh re hethent et myst dem susk, hosen re veskent, hosen kethent gannath crisur. Re yal et mesk domm duss hy russvesk, eth lelv hy russtyvil, eth re vemess o lilvyl.

Then came the high summer of the havens' pride. Farther than their fathers had dared went the ships, and at the hem of the grey they found the black pillars in their ranks, straight as a plumb-line. The ship-masters named them the Mystholders, for they seemed to hold the grey up as posts hold a roof. Harder than oak was their timber, and lighter than iron, and proof against rot.

Word for word: *Then came the heart of summer, when pride was upon the havens. The ships went far out, where their fathers had not dared, and at the bank of the grey they found the black posts in their courses, plumb as a plumb-line. The ship-masters named them: the Posts of the Grey, for they held the grey up, as they seemed, as posts hold a roof. The black wood was harder than the hard-tree (oak), and lighter than the hard-metal (iron), and it warded off rot.*

- *molt syst*, "the heart of summer" (O2960), and pride in the lexicon's idiom, *amm re yal lyrness um* X (O0487), now upon *et cummath*, the havens, as the English has it.
- "Farther than their fathers had dared" is *ul vodh nath re werdent so yedheth*, "where their fathers had not dared" (*berd* softened, *b* > *w*; *so* softens *gedheth*, *g* > *y*).
- *ul so dhrenneth*, "in their courses": the ranks are laid as a mason lays courses (*trenn* softened after *so*). *venn hosen venndhrenn*, "plumb as a plumb-line" (O2716).
- *Re vollant et higileth so: Gannath Myst*: a name given after a colon, as the Council names its fortune below, because *so Gannath* would read "their posts".
- Oak and iron as the Tier 3 unit made them: *duss hy russvesk … lelv hy russtyvil*, "harder than the hard-tree, lighter than the hard-metal".

### ¶7 · Seren's ink

> Lo dhrenn pa dhesk re lusk Redul Serdul et dannwresketh dem neld. Kethent et scaevureleth et nydhyl, havul eth havul. Hunna o amm sy, eth ketha o, hy vodh doss o lor.

Two generations the Trade Council sent out the axes. The rolls keep the count, cargo by cargo. Hear it now, and hold it, for it is ours.

Word for word: *Along two generations the Trade Council loosed the axes away. The rolls hold the count, pack and pack. Hear it now, and hold it, for it is at us (it is ours).*

- *havul eth havul*, "cargo by cargo" (O2959). **One word for four things**: *havul* is "a pack, a bundle; a load; a cargo". The black wood goes out cargo by cargo here; in IV.3 the gift and the lie go into one *havul*, Brenn's pack; Wendhessa seal his *havul*, his bundle; and he dies *hosen cadh uld havul*, as a man sets down a load. Seren never points at it.
- *Hunna o … eth ketha o*: the plural imperative, to the hearth. *keth*, "hold", is also "keep" and "know the wood". *hy vodh doss o lor*, "for it is at us": the having idiom (§3.7) for "it is ours".

### ¶8 · Seren's ink

> Mesk domm, dem homarath eth heskalath. Mesk domm, dem sull heskal ul Gemm Hyll, eth dem nannath Sedhnell. Mesk domm, dem grisurvesketh Lurrvard, eth dem memmerdeth Dhaldow Helv. Mesk domm, dem salereth Rullsorth, eth dem hellur um et crest lo Frennvar. Mesk domm, vorrat niss ul drunnowath Dhavow Vorr. Eth ul Gethow Stellath re yalant et dannwresketh drunnat, eth re vymment dem gless dem stinyl mest.

Black wood, for buttresses and bridges. Black wood, for the three spans at Tidesmeet, and the pilings of Fenholm. Black wood, for the roof-trees of Holtward, and the cranes of Highreach. Black wood, for the galleries of Sandreach, and the hall on the ice at Rimewatch. Black wood, burned slow in the forges of Emberhythe. And at Carnhold the axes were beaten out, and went back to the edge for more.

Word for word: *Black wood, for buttresses and bridges. Black wood, for the three spans in Tidesmeet, and for the posts of Fenholm. Black wood, for the roof-trees of Holtward, and for the lifters of Highreach. Black wood, for the galleries of Sandreach, and for the hall upon the ice at Rimewatch. Black wood, burned slow in the forges of Emberhythe. And in Carnhold the axes were hammered, and they went back to the edge for more cutting.*

- **The count as the rolls keep it**: each line opens on its stone, *Mesk domm*, and has no verb, a naming (D4), as a cargo-list is set down. *dem* + N is "for" (purpose).
- **The havens.** A haven softens as a possessor: *Lurrvard* and *Sedhnell* do not change; *Taldow Helv* > *Dhaldow Helv*; *Grullsorth* > *Rullsorth* (*g* drops before a consonant, as *um rull thenth* in the Tier 3 IV.5); *Tavow Vorr* > *Dhavow Vorr*. Tidesmeet is *ul Gemm Hyll* ("in Tidesmeet") and Carnhold *ul Gethow Stellath*, as the Tier 3 units set them; Rimewatch is *lo Frennvar*, "at Rimewatch" (*f* does not change).
- *dem grisurvesketh … dem memmerdeth*: *dem* beds *crisurvesketh* and *bemmerdeth* (the crane is *bemmerd*, "lifter"). *dem nannath Sedhnell*: the pilings are *gannath*, posts, bedded.
- *vorrat niss*, "burned slow": *niss* "slow; slowly" after the participle.
- *dem stinyl mest*, "for more cutting": "for more" given its object. *dem mesk mest* ("for more wood") was tried; after *dem* the analyzer reads *mesk* as bedded *besk*, "a stranger".

### ¶9 · Seren's ink

> Hy ull, et galatath sa nath re vaverent galat hosen ull.

Then the things that needed naught of the kind.

Word for word: *Then, the things that did not want a thing like that.*

- Verbless, as the English is: *galatath*, "things" (*galat* "a thing"), and *maver* "want, need". *galat hosen ull*, "a thing like that", for "naught of the kind".

### ¶10 · Seren's ink

> Mesk domm, dem bressir vilvhellur vithul Dhavow Hemm.

Black wood, to floor the dancing-hall of a lord of Eldhythe.

Word for word: *Black wood, for the floor of the dancing-hall of a lord of Eldhythe.*

- A construct chain, each possessor softened: *bressir* (*pressir* "a floor", bedded by *dem*) + *vilvhellur* (*v* unchanged) + *vithul* (*pithul* "a lord") + *Dhavow Hemm* (*Tavow Hemm*, Eldhythe). The tongue has no verb "to floor"; the floor is named instead.

### ¶11 · Seren's ink

> Mesk domm, dem serdulyentheth et tavowlesteth, eth so nulesshrynireth.

Black wood, for the trade-tables of the harbour-houses, and their dice-boards.

Word for word: *Black wood, for the trade-tables of the harbour-houses, and their dice-boards.*

- *serdulyentheth*, *tavowlesteth* and *nulesshrynireth* are the lexicon's words, as the Tier 3 unit used them; *et tavowlesteth* is a definite possessor, so it keeps its article and does not soften.

### ¶12 · Seren's ink (the stained place)

> *[Lo sy doss et scaevurel luthat, eth doss sull trenn mammat.]*

*[Here the roll is stained, and three lines are lost.]*

Word for word: *[Here the roll is stained, and three lines are lost.]*

- Reused word for word from the Tier 3 unit: *luth* is ink and stain at once, *mammat* "lost". Italic and bracketed, as the English is.

### ¶13 · Seren's ink

> Lo rask sost, re rann vaskorn ul Gemm Hyll hos gann ul hos lodull.

Last of all, a merchant of Tidesmeet had one pillar hewn into a single bed.

Word for word: *At the very end, a merchant in Tidesmeet hewed one post into one bed.*

- *Lo rask sost*, "at the very end" (*dask* softened after *lo*), for "Last of all". *re rann*: *dann* "hew" softened. *hos gann ul hos lodull*, "one post into one bed": the two *hos* stand where the English has *one* and *single*.

### ¶14 · Seren's ink

> Re aedh o ulo, eth re yal lyrness umo.

He slept in it, and was proud.

Word for word: *He slept in it, and pride was upon him.*

- The litany ends on the wrong thing (notes §1.6): *lyrness*, the havens' pride of ¶6, now upon one man, in the same idiom.

### ¶15 · Seren's ink

> Re hyl Halvard et flenn, eth re vymm o dem o.

Halvard turned the leaf, and turned it back.

Word for word: *Halvard turned the leaf, and he went back to it.*

- **Fixed after the blind reading** (see BACK-TRANSLATION). The first draft said *eth pawast re hyl o*, "and again he turned it", and the reader heard "turned it a second time", which is onward, not back. Now *eth re vymm o dem o*, "and he went back to it": *mymm* is "return, go back" (O1448), the verb the axemen *go back* to the edge with (¶8, ¶16), so Halvard goes back to the leaf as they go back to the stumps.
- *Re hyl Halvard et flenn*: *kyl*, "turn", softened; *flenn* is the leaf the letter lies on (¶28).

### ¶16 · Seren's ink

> Kethent et scaevureleth nydhyl ullen lo sest ull, dyss hyo. Ul vodh re yal gann gostat, re dhald gendan hy et brynt, venn eth domm. Re vymment et gostardath dem stinyl o, hy vodh re rarrant gannath gell hyo; eth re lenull et Stonwrytan so dem brynur.

Beside that count the rolls keep a stranger one. Where a pillar was felled, the stump put up new growth, straight and black. Back went the axemen to cut it, for it made good poles, and the guild bought them for scaffold.

Word for word: *The rolls hold another count beside that, stranger than it. Where a post was felled, a shoot rose from the stump, plumb and black. The axemen came back to the cutting of it, for good poles came out of it; and the guild bought them for scaffold.*

- *Kethent et scaevureleth nydhyl ullen lo sest ull, dyss hyo*: the Tier 3 sentence, kept: *lo sest*, "at the shoulder of, beside".
- *gendan*: *gend* is "a shoot from a stump" (O0720). *re dhald* (*tald* "rise" softened).
- *dem stinyl o*, "to the cutting of it": the poles are cut with *stin* (a stroke), not felled with *gost*. *re lenull … dem brynur*, "bought … for scaffold", as the Tier 3 unit.
- **Fixed after the blind reading.** "for it made good poles" was *hy vodh re yal gannath gell hyo*, and *hy* is both "from" and "than": the reader took *gell hyo* as "better than it". Now *hy vodh re rarrant gannath gell hyo*, "for good poles came out of it": with a verb of coming, *hyo* can only be "out of it". (*re hebb o gannath gell*, "it gave good poles", was tried: the analyzer softens a noun after the subject *o*, §3.5.)

### ¶17 · Seren's ink

> Re stinent o, eth re dhev o. Re stinent o, eth re dhev o. Re stinent o.

They cut it, and it grew. They cut it, and it grew. They cut it.

Word for word: *They cut it, and it grew. They cut it, and it grew. They cut it.*

- **By three, and the third breaks** (craft §2.1): the same five words twice, and the third time the second half is gone. *re dhev o*: *tev* is "a child" and "grow, sprout", so the stumps grow as children do.

### ¶18 · Seren's ink

> Ul vimm nydhyl gannath re hess nind Hyna. Re ryt saevul hos trenn mest, lyss sost: pa fedh re ferrym et greller dem o, eth hy ull re vesk ey o.

Below a count of poles Rhyna's finger stopped. A clerk had written one line more, so small that twice I bent the lamp to it ere she could read it.

Word for word: *Under a count of posts Rhyna's finger stopped. A clerk had cut one line more, very small: two times I bowed the lamp to it, and then she read it.*

- *re hess nind Hyna*, "Rhyna's finger stopped": *hess*, "stop", the Guest's first word. In IV.3 Brenn says *Nath re hessym so*, "I did not stop them".
- *lyss sost: pa fedh re ferrym et greller dem o*: "so small that twice I bent the lamp to it" said as a quality and then its proof. *ferr*, "bend, bow", is the verb her letters lean with in the headnote. *pa fedh*, "two times" (*fedh* does not soften). "ere she could read it" is *eth hy ull re vesk ey o*, "and then she read it".

### ¶19 · the clerk's line

> *Lo dhrenn et cless tunn, re dhald nayalat hy et brynteth.*

*All along the edge, the stumps put up nothing.*

Word for word: *Along the whole bank, nothing rose from the stumps.*

- The clerk's line, in his own words, italic as the English is. It answers ¶16's *re dhald gendan hy et brynt* ("a shoot rose from the stump") with the same verb and *nayalat*, "nothing": the shoots stop by the grammar of the line that told them.

### ¶20 · Seren's ink

> Re ryt nahos hy vodh, eth re vess nahos. Nath re vysk et scaevurel cedh ew et garl. Amm re vysk o, es gethym voll et garl.

No man wrote why, and no man asked. Whose hand it was, the roll saith not. I would have kept his name.

Word for word: *No one wrote why, and no one asked. The roll did not say who the hand was. Had it said, I would keep the name of the hand.*

- *Re ryt nahos hy vodh, eth re vess nahos*: the Tier 3 sentence.
- **The hand.** "Whose hand it was" is *cedh ew et garl*, "who the hand was": *garl* is a hand and a hand of writing, the clerk himself. *Amm re vysk o, es gethym voll et garl*, "had it said, I would keep the name of the hand": *es* after an *amm* clause is the tongue's "would", as Brenn's *amm re hess o lo ull, es morr ol hossol ullen*. **Fixed after the blind reading**: the first draft had *Es gethym voll et garl* alone, and the reader heard a vow, "I will keep the hand's name"; the condition, said in the roll's own verb (*Nath re vysk … Amm re vysk o*), makes it the regret the English means, and *vysk* works twice: the roll did not say; had it said. The roll's tense is past (*Nath re vysk*): after *nath* the analyzer reads present *mysk* as bedded *bysk*, "tie".

### ¶21 · Seren's ink

> Fedh eth fedh re wynt et myst, eth re voll et Redul o: Pussal. Re raevulom et brod ull ul vimm en serr, eth nath re heth o, hosen grull hy lodh hemm.

Season by season the grey thinned, and the Council named it fortune. I tried that word under my tongue, and it gave, like mortar gone to sand.

Word for word: *Season by season the grey thinned, and the Council named it: Fortune. I tried that word under my tongue, and it did not hold, like sand out of old mortar.*

- *fedh eth fedh* (O2958) and *Re voll et Redul o: Pussal* are the Tier 3 unit's.
- **Fixed after the blind reading: the grey thins.** The Tier 3 *re hyl et myst dem myntyth*, "turned toward thinness", leaned on *byntyth*, which the lexicon gives only as "narrowness" (O0224): the reader heard "the grey closed in", the opposite of the English. Now *re wynt et myst*, "the grey thinned": *bynt*, "narrow; fine, thin", used as a verb, as *lilv* "soft" is "go soft" (a new sense, S06-19). It is the Thin Sky's own word (*Helv Bynt*, V.4), and it answers ¶2's thaw, *re lilv et crest*, "the ice went soft": the ice went soft, and the grey went thin.
- **Her picture, in the Book's own verb.** "It gave" is *nath re heth o*, "it did not hold": *keth* is the word the whole Book holds by (*Keth et tolm*), so the word *fortune* fails exactly as a word should not. *serr* is the tongue of the mouth (O2267), not *brodhen*, the tongue one speaks.
- *hosen grull hy lodh hemm*, "like sand out of old mortar": "mortar gone to sand" would put *grull* after *dem* or *ul*, which bed it to an onset the tongue does not allow (*nrull*).

### ¶22 · Seren's ink

> Re veltir et Stonwrytan et Redul. Doss o ul ol narl sost, um flenn sa nath re yal domm hy nindeth fedh:

The Stonewrights warned the Council. It is in our own hand, on a leaf that no thumb had darkened:

Word for word: *The guild warned the Council. It is in our own hand, upon a leaf that was not ever dark from fingers:*

- *Re veltir* (*peltir* "warn" softened). *ul ol narl sost*, "in our own hand", as in the Tier 3 unit.
- "a leaf that no thumb had darkened" is *flenn sa nath re yal domm hy nindeth fedh*, "a leaf that was never dark from fingers": the tongue has no thumb and no verb "to darken"; the unread leaf keeps its picture.

### ¶23 · the guild's warning (quoted)

> *Tevent et mesketh sy ul drun dyss. Lymment et ryteteth sa wesker lo so sest brodhen sa nath noss veskyl lor. Ul vodh naross poduloth, hessyth ol lestir.*

*"These trees grow in strange soil. The carvings we find beside them bear a tongue we cannot read. Where wisdom is absent, let restraint be our guide."*

Word for word: *"These trees grow in strange ground. The carvings that we find at their shoulder bear a tongue that we cannot read. Where wisdom [is] absent, restraint [is] our guide."*

- **Fixed matter**, word for word as the Tier 3 unit set it: the warning is the guild's own, from the rolls, and the Book keeps its English letter for letter. *Ul vodh naross poduloth, hessyth ol lestir* (O2966) is verbless, as the guild's sayings are; *hessyth*, restraint, is *hess* "stop" made abstract.
- Italic and quoted, as the English is: a fully italic line, which takes no ink in the builder.

### ¶24 · Seren's ink

> Veth tevar re resk serdul ul reskow strom, eth re yal ol niltenn hosen galat sa vyskent scaelurath lo et vorr. Hos fedh re yal et peltir vesket ul garm, ul redulhellur Dhavow Hemm. Hy ull re hadhant o um yenth.

But trade sat in the high seat by then, and our counsel was a thing grandmothers said by the fire. Once was the warning read aloud, in the Council hall at Eldhythe. Then was it laid on a shelf.

Word for word: *But already trade sat in the great seat, and our counsel was like a thing that grandmothers say at the fire. One time the warning was read aloud, in the council-hall of Eldhythe. Then they laid it on a shelf.*

- **A word that waits.** "the high seat" is *reskow strom*, "a great seat, a throne" (O2092), and *resk* is "sit": trade sits on a throne. The grey's Thrones are *Reskowath Strom* (O3007) in Book Five. Seren does not say so.
- *tevar*, "early, already", for "by then". *galat sa vyskent scaelurath lo et vorr*: present, for what grandmothers always say.
- *Hy ull re hadhant o um yenth*, "then they laid it on a shelf": active, because *re yal o cadhat* reads "its laid" (§3.5). *genth* "a table, a shelf", softened.

### ¶25 · Seren's ink

> Hy vodh re yal ryteteth. Lo visketh et gannath re weskent et gostardath tolmath cadhat venn, rytet ul rellorath kylet, rerr hosen frenn um risker. Re yal veskyl lo nahos.

For there were carvings. At the feet of the pillars the axemen found stones set upright, cut with curling letters, as beautiful as frost on a window. No man could read them.

Word for word: *For there were carvings. At the feet of the posts the axemen found stones laid plumb, cut in curling signs, fair as frost upon a window. Reading was at no one (no one could read them).*

- *tolmath cadhat venn*, "stones laid plumb", the mason's word for upright, and *rellorath kylet*, "curling signs": the Shore calls what it cannot read *rellorath*, signs; its own letters are *tolmath*. *rerr hosen frenn um risker*, as the Tier 3 unit.
- *Re yal veskyl lo nahos*: the "at" idiom of ability, "reading was at no one".

### ¶26 · Seren's ink

> Re haemerent merr ul davowlesteth, kylet dem rerrel. Re fimment uldath ul vimm et tolmath, eth re nydhent. Re roskur et Stonwrytan et ullen ul rytowath, eth et peltir sost lont.

Some hung in the harbour-houses, reduced to ornament. Men drank under them, and did their reckoning. The rest the guild locked in its archives, and its own warning with them.

Word for word: *A few hung in harbour-houses, turned into ornament. Men drank under the stones, and they counted. The guild locked the rest in archives, and the very warning with them.*

- *haemer* is "float" and "hang" (the Tier 3 note on IV.1 ¶4). *kylet dem rerrel*, "turned into ornament".
- *eth re nydhent*, "and they counted": *nydh* is "count; reckon; weigh". The men at the dice reckon under the stones; in IV.3 Brenn counts the blows and weighs his cleverness with the same verb.
- *et peltir sost lont*, "the very warning with them": the guild's own warning is *sost*, "self, very"; *lo* conjugated, "at them, with them".

### ¶27 · Seren's ink

> Solant merr hyont lo yerd, ul et vestul sa re lymmym dem susk et brunn. Re hadha {{Halyna}} gemm garl umont, eth re hebbent nayalat lonta. Nath noss brodlymmyl lo dholm.

Some of those lie in the chest at my knee, that I carried up the mountain. {{Halyna}} have laid their four hands on them, and been given naught. Stone does not translate.

Word for word: *A few of them rest at [my] knee, in the chest that I carried up the mountain. Halyna laid four hands upon them, and they (the stones) gave nothing to them two. Stone cannot translate.*

- *vestul sa re lymmym dem susk et brunn*, "the chest I carried up the mountain": the Tier 3 rendering, checked as one across units. *Solant*, "they rest" (*sol*, "rest, lie still").
- *lo yerd*, "at the knee", unpossessed: *lo en nerd* ("at my knee") reads as bedded *derd*, "bone", in the analyzer. The knee is hers by the sentence. **Fixed after the blind reading**: set last, after the relative, *lo yerd* was read with *re lymmym*, "carried up the mountain on my knees"; it now stands by *Solant*, before the chest, so the stones rest at her knee.
- *Re hadha Halyna gemm garl umont*: the dual verb; *eth re hebbent nayalat lonta*: the plural verb is the stones', and *lonta*, "at them two", is Halyna. The grammar keeps the giver and the given apart without a name.
- *Nath noss brodlymmyl lo dholm* (O2967), the Tier 3 line, unchanged: "translating is not at stone".

### ¶28 · Seren's ink

> Ul molt nydhyleth hos syst desir re wesk Halvard flenn hy et deldvar hyvenn sost. Re ryt saevul o ul lodh nydhyl gannath eth nydhyl nestullath, ul hos vothol. Re vesk Halvard o hosen re vesk o et nestullath.

Among the counts of one late summer Halvard came on a letter from the outermost post. A clerk had copied it between a count of poles and a count of oars, with the same care. Halvard read it as he had read the oars.

Word for word: *In the midst of the counts of one late summer Halvard found a letter from the outermost outpost. A clerk had cut it in the mortar between a count of posts and a count of oars, in one tending (with the same care). Halvard read it as he read the oars.*

- *flenn*, "a leaf, a sheet", is the letter (a new sense, S06-13): the same word as the leaf Halvard turns in ¶15, which is where the letter lies.
- **Between is in the mortar.** *ul lodh* X *eth* Y is "between" ("in the mortar of"): the letter is laid between two counts as a stone between courses, and in ¶32 Seren copies it there again.
- *ul hos vothol*, "in one tending": *hos* "one" for "the same", as *ul hos garl*, "in the same hand", in ¶31. *nestull*, "an oar" (O1789).

### ¶29 · the warden's letter (no ink of its own)

> *Besk nasesket. Dask bynden. Hesset. Sceth vorrat eth luskat.*

*Unknown intruder. Hostile intent. Neutralized. Vessel burned and released.*

Word for word: *Stranger unknown. End hostile. Stopped. Hull burned and released.*

- **Fixed matter**: the warden's letter, word for word as the Tier 3 unit set it (O2934), and the same in IV.3. Four verbless namings in the clerk's register; *Hesset*, "stopped", is the participle of *hess*, the Guest's own first word. Italic, as the English is: the builder gives a fully italic line no ink.

### ¶30 · Seren's ink

> Lo o visk doss voll stinet: ▒▒▒▒. Re hadh Rhyna ey nind um et luth, eth re rik ey o hyo.

At its foot a name is cut out: ▒▒▒▒. Rhyna laid her finger on the blot, and took it away.

Word for word: *At its foot is a name cut: ▒▒▒▒. Rhyna laid her finger upon the blot, and she took it from it.*

- **The blot** is written as the Book writes it, ▒▒▒▒, in the Orrowen too: four blots, never a name. *luth*, "ink, stain", is the blot itself (a new sense, S06-12); IV.3's annal counts the same blots (*Luth eth luth*).
- *lo o visk*, "at its foot" (*misk* softened by *o*). *ey nind*, "her finger": *ey* softens nothing, and after the subject *Rhyna* it can only be possessive.

### ¶31 · Seren's ink

> Um et flenn fennet doss pa sulter et mesk domm stinet hy nydhyl havul, eth lo so sest, ul hos garl: *mammat ul gless.*

On the next leaf two timber-boats are struck off a cargo-list, and beside them, in the same hand: *lost at the edge.*

Word for word: *Upon the next leaf are two boats of the black wood cut from a cargo-list, and at their shoulder, in one hand: lost in the bank (at the edge).*

- *pa sulter et mesk domm*, "two boats of the black wood", for "two timber-boats": the dual, and the wood they carried named.
- *stinet hy nydhyl havul*, "cut from a cargo-list" (the lexicon's *nydhyl havul*). *mammat ul gless*: *mamm* "lose", and *cless*, the edge, bedded. Italic as the English is.

### ¶32 · Seren's ink

> Re rytym o hosen re ryt et saevul o, ul lodh et gannath eth et nestullath.

I copied it as the clerk had, between the poles and the oars.

Word for word: *I wrote it as the clerk wrote it, in the mortar between the posts and the oars.*

- "Copied" is *ryt*, "cut, write", as the clerk's own verb: she lays the letter in the same mortar he did.

### ¶33 · Seren's ink

> Nath re seskym vodh re hethym.

What I held, I wist not.

Word for word: *I did not know what I held.*

- **The turn, in two verbs** (notes §1.4: *What I held, I wist not.*). *keth* is to hold, and to know the wood by touch; *sesk* is to know a fact. She held it, *re hethym*, and did not know it, *nath re seskym*.

### ¶34 · Seren's ink

> New poduloth sa re hess et Derryl, veth kylyth uldath: re yalant et gannath virr merr, eth re hebbent brunneleth et bridlern mesk mest naryll. Hy surr et sulter sa vorr, nath re vaver hos garlan lussol ul veskyl et myst, eth nath re vyskent et scaevureleth hy vodh. Hy ull re hessent et dannwresketh. Re naelur nahos o.

Word for word: *It was not wisdom that stopped the Harvest, but the fickleness of men: the near posts were few, and the hills of the north gave cheaper wood. From the year of the boat that burns, not one crew wanted rowing within sight of the grey, and the rolls did not say why. Then the axes stopped. No one chose it.*

- *New poduloth sa …, veth kylyth uldath*: the cleft (§3.1) in the past negative, "it was not wisdom that … but"; *kylyth uldath* is the Tier 3 harmonised form (§5.2 #10).
- *merr*, "few; scarce". *mesk mest naryll*, "cheaper wood" ("wood more cheap").
- *surr et sulter sa vorr*, "the year of the burning boat": *Sulter sa vorr* is the lexicon's title of IV.5 (O2425), so the rolls point at the next leaf without a word. *hos garlan*, "one crew" (*garlan*, "a crew of hands"); *lussol*, "rowing".
- *nath re vyskent et scaevureleth*, past for "the rolls say not": present *nath myskent* reads as bedded *byskent*, "they tie". *Re naelur nahos o*: the Tier 3 line.

### ¶35 · Seren's ink

> Hy ull re nadhumant et cummath. Ul susk et braemur eth et nuless re haemerent et tolmath tul, eth re vessent tevath vodh myskent so, eth re yal mysket lont nayalat. Ew et tevath ull merr hyos, sa reskys lo et vorr sy.

Then the havens forgat. Over the ale and the dice the stones hung on, and children asked what they said, and were told naught. Some of you that sit at this fire were those children.

Word for word: *Then the havens forgot. Above the ale and the dice the stones hung yet, and children asked what they say, and it was said to them: nothing. Those children were some of you, who sit at this fire.*

- *Hy ull re nadhumant et cummath*: *nadhum*, "un-remember", the Title's *tumol* with *na-*. The havens forget as a plural.
- *re haemerent … tul*, "hung yet", for "hung on".
- *Ew et tevath ull merr hyos, sa reskys lo et vorr sy*: the copula, predicate first ("those children were some of you"), and the relative agreeing in the second person plural, as pilot I.1's *sulv um et odh sa reskys lo amm sy*. *resk*, "sit", is the verb trade sat on its throne with in ¶24.

### ¶36 · Seren's ink

> Desir, desir sost, re hyl scaevur lyrness. Re yalant lesteth Vardh clennet, eth re yystent uldath ol lusulath crystil pawast, dem bessyl niltenn, sumess hosen uldath sa hlennent hald wadh ul vonul. Re vess nahos um et tolmath, eth re vysker nayalat. Ew ol sa re hyler et roskur. El ol saed ull.

- *Re vess nahos um et tolmath*: *um*, "upon, about"; *re vess* is "asked" and sounds as *vess* "night", as in ¶5.

> Veth re rarr nestornoth desir.

Word for word: *But humility came after its time.*

- The Tier 3 line: *desir* is "late, after the time", the lexicon's own sense for "too late".

Word for word: *The first black sails were already upon the sea.*

## IV.3 · THE WARDEN'S DOOR (*GEBB ET PARD*)

*A stone leaf. Seren's headnote and the seal's caption are in her one ink. Then everything between the Book's two `HALYNA` markers, Halyna's two halves (¶1–¶2) and Brenn's words (¶3–¶41), takes their two inks by turns, a paragraph each, from the first; the letter, fully italic, takes none, and the turn steps over it. From ¶3 to ¶41 the "I" is Brenn's, and the Orrowen is in his singular: every deed of the killing is in the third person plural, and Brenn's own verbs are standing, hearing, seeing, counting, running and saying (K7). Seren's annal (¶42–¶48, ¶51) is in her one ink, Halyna's two closing halves (¶49–¶50) in theirs, and the three lines after the hearth's answer in hers.*

### Title

> ## *Gebb et Pard*

**IV.3 · The Warden's Door**

Word for word: *The door of the warden.*

- The lexicon's title (O0697), unchanged. Pilot IV.4's headnote names this leaf in the same words, softened as a possessor: *Yebb et Pard*.

### Dateline

> *Et grem sullast, ul Hyll Dhumol.*

*The third winter, in the Tide of Remembering.*

Word for word: *The third winter, in the Tide of Remembering.*

- *grem sullast*, "the third winter"; *Hyll Dhumol*, the lexicon's Tide of Remembering (O1046).

### Seren's headnote (her one ink)

> *Brodath Wrenn Reldvar, hosen re rike {{Wendhessa}} so hy o wrodow ul hemmyth, ganna et Stonwrytan ul Davow Hemm. Re veske {{Halyna}} so lo et odh sy, eth re hadhom ryt surr ul so sestow.*

*The words of Brenn of the outpost, as {{Wendhessa}} of the guild at Eldhythe took them from his mouth in his old age. {{Halyna}} read them at this hearth, and the annal after them is mine.*

Word for word: *The words of Brenn of the Outpost, as Wendhessa took them from his mouth in old age, a pair of the guild in Eldhythe. Halyna read them at this hearth, and I laid an annal behind them.*

- A naming first, *Brodath Wrenn Reldvar*, as the English opens on a noun: *brodath Wrenn* is the Tier 3 "the words of Brenn" (*b* > *w* as possessor), and *Brenn Reldvar* the name with its epithet (O2924).
- **Wendhessa are two**: *re rike Wendhessa*, the dual of *grik*, "take" (*re* drops the *g* before *r*; slender *-e*). The pair-name is an addition (S06-01) and carries its sealing lintel, `{{Wendhessa}}`, as `{{Halyna}}` does. *ganna et Stonwrytan ul Davow Hemm*, "a pair of the guild in Eldhythe", stands after the clause, because *et Stonwrytan … so* would read "their". *ganna*, the gate, is the pair (the Tier 3 IV.3's *ganna lodhat*).
- *ul hemmyth*, "in old age": not *ul o hemmyth*, which the analyzer reads as softened *cemmyth*, "a meeting".
- "the annal after them is mine" is *re hadhom ryt surr ul so sestow*, "I laid an annal behind them": *ryt surr* (O2162), the inscription of a year, and *ul so sestow*, "at their back". 35 words, against the English 36.

### ¶1 · first ink (Halyna)

> Kethen et tolm,

### ¶3 · first ink (Brenn)

> Re yal rostnoth surr lom. Re yalar delv, eth et pard, ul neldvar dasken et gaed, ul vodh hask et hal dem et myst. Re yal Tobe hemm piskurard lo et cless noth eth murr surr. Word for word: *Nineteen years were at me. We were twelve, and the warden, in the last outpost of the dry land, where the rock runs to the grey. - **Brenn's words.** From here to *So far the words of Brenn* the "I" is Brenn's, in short plain sentences, and the paragraphs take Halyna's two inks by turns, as the Book's `HALYNA` marker sets them.

- **Words that work twice.** *hask* is "run": the rock runs to the grey as the runner runs, and Brenn will run the lie. *piskurard*, Tobe's trade, is "the one of the net", and in ¶25 the dead man is cast *hosen drig piskurard piskur treskat*, "as a netman throws a torn net". *noth eth murr*, "ten and twenty", is thirty; *lesknoth*, fifteen.
- *Merge (wf12):* **the runners' oath** is one formula in I.3 and IV.3, I.3's (O3035): *Galat sa re veskym, lymmym o. Nath lymmym galat ullen. Ston.*, "What I saw, I carry it. I do not carry another thing. It stands.": the English's own chiasm (the two *carry* meet across the full stop) and the close of every oath (§3.9). The unit's own form, withdrawn (S06-07), and its note, kept: *Lymmym galat sa re veskym. Lymmym nayalat ullen.*, "I carry the thing that I saw. I carry nothing other." Both lines open on *Lymmym*, "I carry", as the English opens and closes on *carry*; it is an addition as a set phrase (S06-07). Retold, it takes no *Ston.* (the Tier 3 IV.5 rule: the pilot closes an oath that is sworn, not one that is told). *tolm et haskardath*, "the runners' stone" (S06-09), softened after *um*.

Word for word: *▒▒▒▒ had laid the black wood in the whole outpost: in the posts of the door, in his table, in the frame of the bed. > Re hadhant o ul vorr, eth nath re vorr o hosen vorr mesk. Re yal scyndalol ulo. Re yal et besk sennet. New uld sa re scyndal. Ew et mesk sost.

They set it alight, and it burned not as wood burneth. There was a screaming in it. The stranger was dead. It was no man that screamed. It was the wood itself.

Word for word: *They laid it in fire, and it did not burn as wood burns. There was screaming in it. The stranger was dead. It was not a man that screamed. It was the wood itself.*

- *Re hadhant o ul vorr*, "they laid it in fire" (the Tier 3 phrase), *hosen vorr mesk*, "as wood burns" (the present, a thing always so).
- *New uld sa re scyndal. Ew et mesk sost.*: the cleft twice, the second answering the first, as Brenn weighs it (notes §1.7).

### ¶29 · first ink (Brenn)

> Re hastant o dem neld lo et gannath sost. Re orr Tobe hemm ul et wadh dem o yerdeth, eth re ridh o et gannath. Re stonar et ullen um et dhysal, eth nath re vaverer veskyl, eth re vesker.

With the same poles they shoved it off. Old Tobe went into the sea to his knees and washed the poles. The rest of us stood on the shingle, and would not look, and looked.

Word for word: *They pushed it far off with the very poles. Old Tobe went into the sea up to his knees, and he washed the poles. We, the rest, stood upon the shingle, and we did not want looking, and we looked.*

- *lo et gannath sost*, "with the very poles" (*sost*, "self, very"), for "the same poles".
- *dem o yerdeth*, "to his knees" (*gerd* softened by *o*). *re ridh o et gannath*: *ridh*, "wash". Tobe says nothing.
- *Re stonar et ullen …, eth nath re vaverer veskyl, eth re vesker*: the plural is the post's, Brenn in it; the turn is the English's, "would not look, and looked".

### ¶30 · second ink (Brenn)

> Ul neld, re hyl et sulter. Re orr o ul lern et helvhoss, eth ul lern et kess, eth ul et myst, ul vorr.

Well out, the boat turned. Into the wind it went, and against the current, and into the grey, burning.

Word for word: *Beyond, the boat turned. It went in the face of the wind, and in the face of the current, and into the grey, alight.*

- *Ul neld*, "beyond" (O2635), for "Well out". *ul lern et helvhoss, eth ul lern et kess*: "against" is "in the face of", as the Tier 3 IV.5. *ul vorr*, "alight". VI.3 repeats this sentence without its last word.

### ¶31 · first ink (Brenn)

> Orl ull sost re harm ▒▒▒▒ lo saevul, eth re ryt et saevul dem et Redul. Re yalom lo sest, eth doss o vyskyl lom hy volt:

That same day ▒▒▒▒ had the clerk write to the Council. I stood by, and I can say it by heart:

Word for word: *That very day ▒▒▒▒ called to the clerk, and the clerk wrote to the Council. I was at [his] shoulder, and the saying of it is at me from the heart:*

- The Tier 3 sentences: "had the clerk write" is *re harm ▒▒▒▒ lo saevul, eth re ryt et saevul*; *doss o vyskyl lom hy volt*, "I can say it by heart" (the "at" idiom, *hy volt*).

### The letter (no ink of its own)

> *Besk nasesket. Dask bynden. Hesset. Sceth vorrat eth luskat.*

*Unknown intruder. Hostile intent. Neutralized. Vessel burned and released.*

Word for word: *Stranger unknown. End hostile. Stopped. Hull burned and released.*

- **Fixed matter**, the same four namings as IV.1 ¶29 (O2934). A fully italic line takes no ink in the builder, so the alternation steps over it.

### ¶32 · second ink (Brenn)

> Nath noss brod um et hebbet. Nath noss brod um et lummol.

No word of the gift. No word of the kneeling.

Word for word: *There is no word about the gift. There is no word about the kneeling.*

- The Tier 3 pair, *Nath noss …* twice.

### ¶33 · first ink (Brenn)

> Ul ryst fennet, amm re aedh gor hos tul, re rikym et tolm hy et tuss. Nath seskym hy vodh re yaldom o. Lo o yebb re hebb ▒▒▒▒ et flenn lom. Hosen sy re orrant et hebbet eth et nycul ul hos havul, eth re haskom o.

On the morrow, ere any woke, I picked the stone out of the dust. Why I did it, I know not. At his door ▒▒▒▒ gave me the letter. So the gift and the lie went into one pack, and I ran it.

Word for word: *On the next morning, when every one still slept, I took the stone from the dust. I do not know why I did it. At his door ▒▒▒▒ gave the letter to me. So the gift and the lie went into one pack, and I ran it.*

- *amm re aedh gor hos tul*, "when every one still slept", for "ere any woke". *re rikym et tolm hy et tuss*: pilot IV.4's *sa re rik Brenn hy et tuss*, in his own mouth. *Nath seskym hy vodh re yaldom o*: Brenn's mark, unchanged.
- *et flenn*, "the letter" (S06-13). *ul hos havul*, "into one pack": *havul*, the cargo of IV.1, the bundle of the seal, the load of the annal (IV.1 ¶7). *re haskom o*, "I ran it": the runner's own verb, transitive.

### ¶34 · second ink (Brenn)

> Re haskom hos orl eth hos vess, eth bost lusul eth bost lusul re vyskym et ryt pawast.

A day and a night I ran, and every mile I said the oath over.

Word for word: *I ran one day and one night, and mile by mile I said the oath again.*

- *bost lusul eth bost lusul*, "mile by mile" (S06-10): *bost lusul* is the lexicon's mile (O0117), "four hundred paces", and the distributive is *fedh eth fedh*'s. *gor vost lusul* ("every mile") reads "every brother step" in the analyzer.

### ¶35 · first ink (Brenn)

> Ul Davow Hemm re vesk saevul hemm et Redul o, luth um o nindeth, eth re vesk o dem susk lom.

At Eldhythe an old clerk of the Council read it, with ink on his fingers, and looked up at me.

Word for word: *In Eldhythe an old clerk of the Council read it, ink upon his fingers, and he looked up at me.*

- *saevul hemm et Redul*, "an old clerk of the Council". *luth um o nindeth*: a clause with no verb laid beside the others, as pilot I.1's *eth so wresketh ul so simmeth*.

### ¶36 · second ink (Brenn)

> Ho ross o hosen sy?

"Is it so?"

Word for word: *"Is it so?"*

- *Ho ross o hosen sy?*: *ho* softens *doss* to *ross*, "is it thus?". Orrowen has no *yes*: it is answered by saying the verb back, *Doss*.

### ¶37 · first ink (Brenn)

> Nath re vyskym: Doss o hosen sy. Re vyskym: Doss o rytet. Pa vurr surr re nydhym et kylyl lyss ull.

I said not that it was so. I said, "It is written." Forty years have I weighed that small cleverness.

Word for word: *I did not say: It is so. I said: "It is written." Two twenties of years I have weighed that small turning.*

- **The small cleverness, in the grammar.** The clerk asks *Ho ross o hosen sy?*, and the tongue's yes is the verb said back (`orrowen_v2` §3.4): *Doss*. Brenn says *Doss o rytet*, "It is written": he says the yes-word, and then makes it a yes only to the writing. Every hearer of Orrowen hears the yes he did not give. (Addition S06-17.)
- *Pa vurr surr re nydhym et kylyl lyss ull*: *nydh*, "count; weigh", the verb of the blows; *kylyl*, "a turning" (*kyl*, "turn, swerve, hook"; a new sense, S06-16), the small swerve of his answer, and a cousin of IV.1's *kylyth uldath*, the fickleness of men.

### ¶38 · second ink (Brenn)

> Re lymmym et nycul ul hos orl eth hos vess. Re lymmym et vennoth pa vurr surr, eth re hebbym o lo nahos.

I carried the lie in a day and a night. I carried the truth forty years, and gave it to no one.

Word for word: *I carried the lie in one day and one night. I carried the truth two twenties of years, and I gave it to no one.*

- **The mirror** (notes §1.6): *Re lymmym et nycul ul hos orl eth hos vess. Re lymmym et vennoth pa vurr surr*: the same verb, the oath's *lymm*, opens both. *vennoth* is "truth; faith".

### ¶39 · first ink (Brenn)

> Bremm ull re dhyvull et Orrow um sull gostard, sa re vymment dem varn um vivenn, eth sa re rytent um sulter sa vorr hy et myst. Nath re dhyvullom, hy vodh re veskym o ul gylyl. Veth re vyskym nayalat. Re yal o rytet lo et Redul.

That autumn the shore laughed at three axemen, home on a spar, that sware to a burning boat out of the grey. I laughed not, for I had seen it turn. Yet I said naught. The Council had it in writing.

Word for word: *That autumn the Shore laughed upon three axemen, who came back home upon a mast, and who swore upon a boat that burns, out of the grey. I did not laugh, for I had seen it in turning. But I said nothing. It was written at the Council.*

- *Bremm ull*, "that autumn", bare, as *orl ull*: *ul* would bed *bremm* to *mremm*, which the tongue does not allow. *um vivenn*, "upon a mast" (*mivenn* softened), for "on a spar". *sulter sa vorr*, IV.5's title again.
- *Re yal o rytet lo et Redul*, "the Council had it in writing": the having idiom with *rytet*, the word of his answer in ¶37.

### ¶40 · second ink (Brenn)

> Re hovv ▒▒▒▒ covveleth sell dem sennyl, lo dhrenn systeth bith ul Davow Hemm; eth seskym hy vodh. Grem sa re orr, re senn o ul lodull et mesk domm. Dossom hosel tul hy et delv.

▒▒▒▒ wore long sleeves the rest of his life, through the hot summers of Eldhythe, and I know why. Last winter he died in his bed of black wood. I am the last of the twelve.

Word for word: *▒▒▒▒ wore long sleeves until death, through the hot summers in Eldhythe; and I know why. The winter that went, he died in the bed of the black wood. I am alone yet, of the twelve.*

- *Re hovv ▒▒▒▒ covveleth sell*: *covv* is "a cloak; wear", and *covvel*, a sleeve, is "a small cloak": he wears little cloaks over the mark. The Tier 3 sentence otherwise.
- *Grem sa re orr*, "the winter that went", for "last winter" (S06-18). *senn* is "die: go out, as a fire in peace": he dies easy, in the black wood.
- *Dossom hosel tul hy et delv*, "I alone am yet, of the twelve", for "I am the last of the twelve".

### ¶41 · first ink (Brenn)

> Pa vurr surr re sol et tolm ul vimmow en havul, eth nath re hethym o hos fedh. Hebbym o amm sy lo et brodath sy, eth pessym haerilyl lo Vardh, hy vodh nath noss hos lom dem bessyl veth Mardh.

Forty years the stone hath lain at the bottom of my pack, and not once have I held it. I give it up now with these words, and I ask God to forgive me, for there is none left to ask but God.

Word for word: *Two twenties of years the stone rested at the bottom of my pack, and I did not hold it one time. I give it now with these words, and I ask forgiving of God, for there is not one at me to ask but God.*

- *ul vimmow en havul*, "at the bottom of my pack" (*vimmow*, as the archive chest's bottom in IV.1 ¶2). *nath re hethym o hos fedh*, "not once have I held it": *keth*, the verb of Halyna's knowing; it will be held at last in IV.4.
- The second sentence is the Tier 3 rendering, kept: *lo Vardh … veth Mardh*, God softened after *lo* and plain after *veth*.

### ¶42 · Seren's ink (the annal)

> Dem sy, brodath Wrenn. Luth eth luth, re yala {{Halyna}} nawrodh.

So far the words of Brenn. At every blot {{Halyna}} were silent.

Word for word: *Until this, the words of Brenn. Blot by blot, Halyna were mute.*

- *Dem sy, brodath Wrenn*: the Tier 3 "So far the words of Brenn".
- *Luth eth luth*, "blot by blot" (S06-11), for "At every blot": *lo* or *ul* would mutate *gor*. *re yala Halyna nawrodh*: the dual of the state verb, and *nawrodh*, "mute", the word of the mute stones (*tolmath nawrodh*). At the warden's name Halyna are as the stones are.

### ¶43 · Seren's ink

> Re lymm sestyarl et pard hos rellor: saed yalat, treskat ul molt. Re heth et Redul et flenn.

The warden's arm bare one mark: the half of something, cut off in the middle. The Council kept the letter.

Word for word: *The warden's arm bore one mark: the half of a thing, riven in the middle. The Council kept the letter.*

- *Re lymm sestyarl et pard hos rellor*: *lymm*, "bear, carry", the verb of the warning's "bear a tongue". *saed yalat, treskat ul molt*, "the half of a thing, riven in the middle": *galat* softened as possessor; *treskat*, the Riven Stone's word. Never a holy sign (notes §1.7).
- *Re heth et Redul et flenn*: *keth*, "hold, keep". The reader supplies *and forgot the Guest* (notes §1.9).

### ¶44 · Seren's ink

> Re senn Brenn, hosen cadh uld havul. Re lamm et Stonwrytan o ul vimm o voll. Re stin o voll et pard dem neld.

Brenn died, as a man setteth down a load. The guild buried him under his name. The warden's name it cut away.

Word for word: *Brenn died, as a man lays down a load. The guild buried him under his name. It cut away the name of the warden.*

- *hosen cadh uld havul*: Brenn's own simile from ¶7 turned on him, *cadh uld*, and the load is *havul*, his pack.
- *Re lamm*, "buried" (*lamm*, "bury, lay in earth"). *ul vimm o voll*, "under his name". *Re stin o voll et pard dem neld*: *o* is the guild; *stin*, "cut", the verb the axemen cut the poles with.

### ¶45 · Seren's ink

> Re hoska {{Wendhessa}} o wrodath, eth re wrodha so lo nahos. Ul et Ommol re lymme sona et vestul hy et cumm, o yarl um hos garlow, eth ey garl um et ullen. Um et Orrol re ommant sona sestyarlath. Re vyskent et tolmardath: Nath noss galat dyll ulo; eth re hebbent o lo pa dhevel sost: lo Dharnel, eth lom. Lo o yarl luskat re wysk Tarnel o viskur. Re veskym et bysketeth.

{{Wendhessa}} sealed his words, and told no one. At the Fall they bare the chest out, his hand on one handle, hers on the other. On the Road their arms failed. The masons said it held naught of worth, and gave it to the youngest, Tarnel and me. Tarnel knotted his net with his free hand. I watched the knots.

Word for word: *Wendhessa sealed his words, and they two told them to no one. At the Fall they two carried the chest from the haven, his hand upon one handle, and her hand upon the other. Upon the Road their arms failed. The masons said: There is no thing of worth in it; and they gave it to the two youngest: to Tarnel, and to me. With his loosed hand Tarnel knotted his net. I watched the knots.*

- *Re hoska Wendhessa o wrodath*: the dual (broad *-a*), and *o wrodath*, "his words" (*brodath* softened by *o*). *re wrodha so lo nahos*: *brodh*, "tell", in the dual.
- *Ul et Ommol*, "at the Fall" (O1869). *re lymme sona et vestul*: the dual of *lymm* (slender *-e*), and *sona*, "they two".
- **The handles.** *garlow*, "a handle", is new (S06-05): *garl*, a hand, and *-ow*, the place of a thing, "the hand's place". *o yarl um hos garlow, eth ey garl um et ullen*: *o* softens *garl*, *ey* does not, so the line shows his hand and hers by its mutation alone.
- *Um et Orrol*, "upon the Road" (*orrol*, "a going"). *re ommant sona sestyarlath*: *omm*, "fall", is the formula's "failed" (*ell re omm et brodhol*).
- *et tolmardath*, "the masons" (*tolmard*, "stone-one"). *lo pa dhevel sost*: the two youngest, a dual, so the adjective softens (§3.3: *pa yarl dhevel*). *lo Dharnel*: a name softens after *lo*.
- **Remember the good** (notes §1.9): *Lo o yarl luskat re wysk Tarnel o viskur*: *bysk*, "tie, knot", softened; *o viskur*, "his net" (*piskur*); "with his free hand" is *lo o yarl luskat*, "with his loosed hand", the hand let go of the handle, and Seren lets the tying hand be the loosed one (*lusk*, *bysk*). **Fixed after the blind reading**: the first draft had *Re wysk Tarnel o viskur lo o yarl ullen*, and the reader read "tied his net to his left hand" (*garl ullen* is the lexicon's left hand, O2886, and *lo* after *tie* is "to"); fronted, *lo* is the hand he knots with, and *luskat* says why it is free. Tarnel's own name is "little net" (`orrowen_v2` §4.6). *et bysketeth*, "the knots".

### ¶46 · Seren's ink

> Ul vess et hiskenn re sola {{Wendhessa}}, eth nath re dhalda. Lo et vestul re aedhen Tarnel eth en, o yarl um hos garlow, eth en narl um et ullen. Ul ryst re yal frenn um vana.

On the night of the pass {{Wendhessa}} lay down, and rose not. By the chest Tarnel and I slept, a hand each on a handle. At morning frost lay on both.

Word for word: *In the night of the pass Wendhessa lay to rest, and they two did not rise. By the chest we two slept, Tarnel and I, his hand upon one handle, and my hand upon the other. In the morning there was frost upon both.*

- *re sola Wendhessa*: *sol*, "rest, lie still", in the dual; *nath re dhalda*, "did not rise" (*tald*).
- **We two.** *re aedhen*, "we two slept": the guild keeps the dual for any two (§3.9 item 1), and Seren gives it to Tarnel and herself once, beside the pair it lost. *o yarl um hos garlow, eth en narl um et ullen* repeats Wendhessa's line with his hand and hers, as the English's *a hand each on a handle* does by its echo.
- **Frost upon both.** *frenn um vana*: *um vana*, "upon both", is the second half of the old masters' saying, *Grest um hos, grest um vana*, "harm upon one, harm upon both". Seren lays the frost where the harm lies in the proverb, and says nothing.

### ¶47 · Seren's ink

> Nath re rynn Tarnel.

Tarnel did not wake.

Word for word: *Tarnel did not wake.*

- **Plain at the grave**: *rynn*, "wake up, rise from sleep". Four words, as the English's four.

### ¶48 · Seren's ink

> Lo ull re hessym vess sy, en narlath um et tolm. Hy ull re hadha {{Halyna}} sona yarlath lo so sest.

There I stopped tonight, with mine hands on the stone. Then {{Halyna}} laid theirs beside them.

Word for word: *At that I stopped this night, my hands upon the stone. Then Halyna laid their hands at their side.*

- *re hadha Halyna sona yarlath lo so sest*: their four hands beside her two.

### ¶51 · Seren's ink

### The hearth's answer

Word for word: *And the hearth said: We remember.*

### After the answer (Seren's ink)

> *Nath re vysk et odh sy o fedh um hurrol sa re yald ol lunt sost. Re hadh Ebba hemm et tolm hosast, eth re yal et ullen niss dem fennyl. Veth re fennent.*

*Never before had this hearth said it over a killing by our own. Old Ebba began it, and the rest were slow to follow. But they followed.*

Word for word: *This hearth had not ever said it upon a killing that our own people did. Old Ebba laid the first stone, and the rest were slow to the following. But they followed.*

- *um hurrol sa re yald ol lunt sost*, "over a killing that our very people did": *gald*, "build; do, make", softened.
- "Old Ebba began it" is *Re hadh Ebba hemm et tolm hosast*, "Old Ebba laid the first stone": the lexicon's "begin" (O2859). *Ebba* is a name (S06-03).
- *re yal et ullen niss dem fennyl*: *niss*, "slow", and *fenn*, "follow". *Veth re fennent.*: the verb said back, which is the tongue's yes.

### After the answer (Seren's ink)

> *Eth re hadh et Crenn et biskhovv dem sestow.*

*And the Captain put his hood back.*

Word for word: *And the Captain laid the hood to the back.*

- *et biskhovv*, "the hood" (*bisk* "head" + *covv* "cloak"): *et Crenn o wiskhovv* is read by the analyzer as a construct with an article on its head. *dem sestow*, "to the back". The foreword's habit (the hood kept up) turns here, and again in the Epilogue; its unit may want the same words.

### After the answer (Seren's ink)

> *Ul ryst re rytent et haskardath et ryt pawast, eth re hadhant gemm brod umo: Nel hosen lymmyl Wrenn. Re ryt Wik, et tevel sost, o bont sost.*

*In the morning the runners sware their oath again, and added four words: Not as Brenn carried. Wick, the youngest, sware it loudest.*

Word for word: *In the morning the runners swore the oath again, and laid four words upon it: Not as the carrying of Brenn. Wik, the youngest, swore it loudest.*

- *re hadhant gemm brod umo*, "they laid four words upon it": *cadh* is "lay; add, lay on". The four words are four in Orrowen too: *Nel hosen lymmyl Wrenn* (S06-08), *nel* because there is no verb, *lymmyl* the oath's own *lymm*.
- *Wik* is the Orrowen spelling of the name the English writes *Wick* (S06-04: *k* after *i* at a word's end, §2.4). *bont sost*, "loudest"; *et tevel sost*, "the youngest" (*sost* for the most).

## NOTES FOR THE NEXT HANDS

### 1. What v3 changed in these leaves, and what the Orrowen does with it

- **IV.1 is Seren's leaf now.** v1.3 gave it to Halyna by turns and the Tier 3 unit closed it in their dual; v3 has Seren lay it from the rolls that Halyna read her, so its body is in her one ink, its "we" is the three of them (the plural: *re vesker*, *re rarrar*) or the guild's (*ol narl*, *ol niltenn*, *ol saed*), and it closes *Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom*. Only the two read lines (¶3–¶4) are Halyna's, in the dual (*Nath re hossan o*).
- **IV.3: Brenn only watched** (K7). The tongue carries this in its person endings: every deed of the killing is third person plural (*re rikent, re hreskent, re wammant, re rigent, re hadhant, re hastant*), and Brenn's verbs are *re stonom, re hunnom, re veskym, re nydhym, re haskom, re vyskym*. Where the English says *we* of the post (*We obeyed.*, *would not look, and looked*), the Orrowen says *-er* with him, and ¶23 says why.
- **No custom of burning** (K8). The burning is the warden's order, and his order is in the rhyme's own words, *Vorra o … hasta o … hebba o dem hyll*, so IV.5's rhyme keeps it without knowing it (notes §1.8). Nothing in either leaf explains a deed.

### 4. Where Seren plays (so a later hand does not "fix" it)

- **One word for four things.** *havul*: the cargo (*havul eth havul*, *nydhyl havul*), Brenn's pack (*ul hos havul*), his bundle under the seal (*hosk havul Wrenn*), the load he dies setting down (*hosen cadh uld havul*). *nydh*: the clerks' counts, the men's reckoning under the stones, Brenn's count of the blows, and his forty years' weighing of his answer. *hess*: Rhyna's finger stopping, the harvest stopping, Brenn's *Nath re hessym so*, and Seren's *re hessym* at the stone tonight, all the Guest's first word. *ser*, "go on", for both *on*s of IV.3 ¶20, which the guild hears in Seren's name.
- **The yes that is not one.** *Ho ross o hosen sy?* is answered by its verb; Brenn says the verb, *Doss o rytet*, and makes it a yes to the writing (IV.3 ¶36–¶37).
- **Hold and know.** *Nath re seskym vodh re hethym* (IV.1 ¶33): *keth* is to hold and to know the wood, *sesk* to know a fact. *nath re heth o* is the word that "gave" (IV.1 ¶21). Brenn never held the stone (*nath re hethym o hos fedh*); Halyna hold it in IV.4.
- **Pictures in the tongue's things.** letters bowing like grass in a wind (*ferr*, then the lamp bowed with the same verb); sand out of old mortar; men mending a sea-wall in the calm (*vonul*); a netman throwing a torn net (*piskurard piskur treskat*, Tobe's trade and the Torn Cloak's word); frost *um vana*, "upon both", the second half of *Grest um hos, grest um vana*; Halyna *nawrodh*, mute as the mute stones, at every blot; *luld* "a boy" inside *uldath* "the men"; trade sitting on a *reskow strom*, a throne, before the Thrones (*Reskowath Strom*) come; *re vessent ul vessvarath*, "asked in the night-watches", where *asked* sounds *night*; *ul lodh*, "in the mortar", for *between*, where the letter lies among the counts; the ice that went soft and the grey that went thin, one kind of verb each (*re lilv et crest*, IV.1 ¶2; *re wynt et myst*, ¶21, *bynt* the Thin Sky's word); the roll that did not say, and *had it said* (*Nath re vysk … Amm re vysk o*, ¶20); Tarnel knotting with the loosed hand (*Lo o yarl luskat re wysk*, IV.3 ¶45), *lusk* against *bysk*.
- **We two, once.** *re aedhen Tarnel eth en*, "we two slept, Tarnel and I" (IV.3 ¶46): the guild's dual for any two, given to the boy beside the pair Seren lost.
