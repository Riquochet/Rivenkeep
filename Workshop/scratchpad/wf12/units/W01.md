### 2.1 The title, the dateline and the headnote (Seren's ink)

> **Hoss et Mesk**

### II.2 · Of the Breath of the Wood

- *Gloss:* breath · the · wood. *Of the Breath of the Wood.* The lexicon's title (O1005), as the old unit has it. *Hoss* is the leaf's first word and the headnote's last.

> **Et grem leskest, Hyll sa re rystull nycul.**

*The fifth winter, the Tide that Learned Deceit.*

- *Gloss:* the · winter · fifth, Tide · REL · PST · learn · deceit.
- A dateline is a naming, so it has no verb (D4): *et grem leskest*, "the fifth winter", the ordinal after its noun as in *Hyll Pawast*, and slender (*leskest*, O1194, from *lesk*); then the Tide by its name (O1050), which takes no article, set beside the winter as the Book sets it.

> **Re vess tev: Ho ross lesteth lo lunt et arra? Lo dhrenn vesseth mest re hethe {{Halyna}} hebbet et arra, hos lo et odh, ullen ul naedh, eth gor fedh re rynn et aedherd ul gethyl: re hadhant ulo varn tunn, eth nath noss brod lo Orrowen um varn sa el hoss.**

*A child asked whether the Guest's people had houses. Many nights {{Halyna}} held his gift, one at the hearth, the other asleep, and ever the sleeper woke knowing; into it his people had put the whole of home, and for a home that is air our tongue hath no word.*

- *Gloss:* PST · S·ask · child: Q · S·be · houses · at · people · the · guest? At · S·course · nights · many · PST · S·hold-3DU · Halyna · gift · the · guest, one · at · the · hearth, other · in · N·sleep, and · every · time · PST · wake · the · sleeper · in · N·holding: PST · S·lay-3PL · in-it · home · whole, and · NEG · N·be · word · at · Orrowen · upon · home · REL · is · breath.
- *Word for word:* A child asked: Are there houses with the Guest's people? Along the course of many nights Halyna held the Guest's gift, one at the hearth, the other asleep, and each time the sleeper woke holding: they had laid home whole into it, and Orrowen has no word for a home that is breath.
- **The child's question is quoted**, as Orrowen reports speech (S02, S03): *Ho ross lesteth lo lunt et arra?*, the having idiom (§3.7) asked with *ho*, which softens *doss* (O2982, *ho ross*). The Book's "whether" is the particle's own sense (O0977). *Lest* is a house, a built thing; the leaf answers with *varn*, home, and the two words never meet.
- ***Lo dhrenn vesseth mest***, "along the course of many nights", is the old unit's phrase. *Trenn* is a course, and a tale laid in one night: the nights are laid as a tale is.
- ***re hethe Halyna***: the pair-name under its lintel takes the dual (*keth* + 3DU, softened after *re*), §3.7's own example. ***hebbet et arra***, "the Guest's gift", names the possessor: *o hebbet* after a verb would be heard "it, a gift", because *h* does not soften (§3.5). *Et arra* is IV.4's "the Guest"; v3's headnote says "the Guest", not "the Kneeling Guest", so the old unit's *arra sa re lumm* is not used.
- ***hos lo et odh, ullen ul naedh***, "one at the hearth, the other asleep": *ul naedh* is the lexicon's "asleep" (O2634).
- ***eth gor fedh re rynn et aedherd ul gethyl***: "ever" is *gor fedh*, every time (O0762). The sleeper woke *ul gethyl*, "in holding": *keth* is both "hold" and "know" (the wood is held, never *sesk*'d), so the one word says that the sleeper woke knowing and that the gift was still in hand. The Book's "held … knowing" is the same word twice.
- ***re hadhant ulo varn tunn***, "they had laid home whole into it". *Cadh* is "put", and also the verb of a tale (*cadh trenn*, "lay a tale"): his people laid their home into the gift as a teller lays a tale. "His people" is in the ending: the plural *-ant*, not Halyna's dual, can only be the Guest's folk of the first sentence.
- ***nath noss brod lo Orrowen um varn sa el hoss***, "Orrowen has no word for a home that is breath". "Our tongue" is the tongue's own name, *Orrowen*, "of the Shore": a tongue named for a shore has no word for a home that is breath. "Air" is *hoss*, as in the old unit (Orrowen has *helv*, the sky, and the breath, and the wood's air is its breath), so the headnote closes on the title's first word.
- The old wood headnotes' formulas (*Hosen tum Mesk Myst: kethet hy …*; *Re wrodha Halyna o, hos eth ullen, ul ba luth; re hadh Seren o.*; *Amm re yal et mesk cresket …*) are not used: v3's headnote is a child's question and the night's work, and the foreword says the formula once (notes_v3 §1.5).

- *Gloss:* old. Early · from · S·stone. Early · from · the · stone · first · quarried.

## 3 · WHAT THE TRANSLATION DID, AND WHY

**The Orrowen.**

1. **Seren's frame is new where v3 is new.** v3's headnote is a child's question and the night's work, not the old wood-leaf formulas, so it is translated fresh, from the old unit's own phrases: *lo dhrenn vesseth mest*, *hos … ullen*, *re rynn et aedherd ul gethyl*, *nath noss brod … um varn sa el hoss*. The title, the note on the name and the closing line are the old unit's (the note's gloss harmonised to O2995, *lunt et hoss*).
2. **Seren plays where her tongue lets her, and only there.** *Keth* is held and known at once (the gift held; the sleeper woke holding); *cadh* lays a home into the gift as a tale is laid; *trenn* makes the nights a course and a tale; *Orrowen*, "of the Shore", is the tongue that has no word for a home of breath; and the headnote ends on *hoss*, the title's first word. Fifty-two words for the Book's fifty.
