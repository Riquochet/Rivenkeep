## I.2 · THE CRY OF THE BONDED (*CARM ET LODHAN*)

### Title

> ## *Carm et Lodhan*

**I.2 · The Cry of the Bonded**

- *The cry of the Bonded.* The lexicon's title (O0246), as wf8 S01 gave it.

### Dateline

> *Et grem hosast, ul sestow et kiser hosast.*

*The first winter, after the first assault.*

Word for word: *The first winter, behind the first assault.*

- A dateline is a naming and has no verb. ***Et grem hosast***, "the first winter", the ordinal after its noun, as I.1's *et vess hosast*; ***ul sestow***, "after, behind" (O2637); ***kiser***, "an assault, a rushing-on". I.3's dateline is built the same way, so the two read as one season.

### Seren's headnote

> *En drenn sost, eth et hosast sa re hadhom fedh; et pawast sa re hunn et odh sy. Ul et orl sa wrodhom umo, re yal delv grem lom, eth ew tev et Stonwrytan en.*

*Mine own, and the first tale that ever I laid; this hearth had heard but one before it. On the day I tell of I was twelve winters old, and a child of the guild.*

Word for word: *My own tale, and the first that I ever laid; the second that this hearth heard. On the day that I tell of, twelve winters were at me, and a child of the guild was I.*

- Seren's own tale, so there is no *Re hadh* frame: she names the tale as hers, ***En drenn sost*** (*en* beds *trenn*).
- "This hearth had heard but one before it" is said as Seren's turn, from the other side of the same count: ***et hosast** sa re hadhom fedh; **et pawast** sa re hunn et odh sy*, the first she laid, the second the hearth heard. *fedh* is "ever" (pilot I.1's *re hyrril nahos fedh*).
- ***re yal delv grem lom***, "twelve winters were at me": the at-idiom of age (O2938 *doss surr lo* counts years), here with winters, as the hearth counts a child's age and the siege counts its time (addition W12-S02-08).
- ***sa wrodhom umo***, "that I tell of": the conjugated preposition left behind (pilot I.1 G5), as wf8 S01's *sa wrodh ey umo*. ***ew tev et Stonwrytan en***: the copula, predicate first, as Kael's *El nydherd yalatath en*.

### Seren's telling

> Vess sy re yal ludal wrodhol umom, eth re hadha {{Halyna}} en narlath pana um et tolm.

Tonight I was afraid to speak, and {{Halyna}} laid my two hands on the stone.

Word for word: *This night the fear of telling was upon me, and Halyna laid my hands, both, upon the stone.*

- ***re yal ludal wrodhol umom***: a fear is laid *upon* a person (§3.7, O0485), the verbal noun its softened possessor (wf8 S01).
- ***re hadha Halyna***: the dual, and the verb is *cadh*, "lay", the verb of every tale (*Cadhom o …*): Halyna lay her hands on the stone as a course is laid.
- ***en narlath pana***, "my hands, both", for "my two hands". The grammatical *en ba yarl* (*en* bedding *pa*, *pa* softening *garl*) is reported by the analyzer, which does not let a bedded *pa* keep its softening (wf8 S02 Notes 4); *pana*, "both", carries the two. The Epilogue says this line again, and its unit will meet the same limit.

*(first ink)*

> Keth et tolm.

"Hold the stone."

- Canon (`orrowen_v2` §11.11): Halyna's words to the child Seren, the first half in the first ink.

*(second ink)*

> Keth o, eth es geth tho.

"Hold it, and it will hold thee."

- Canon (§11.11), the second half in the second ink. ***tho***, the singular, to a child: the Book's *thee*. *es geth*, "will hold": *es* beds *keth*.

> Hy ull kethym o.

So I hold it.

- wf8 S01's line, the same words. *kethym o*: Seren answers the canon in the first person, with the canon's own verb.

> Ul hedh orl et movenn, re resk et Stonwrytan ul linth et parow hemm, merr lesk murr hyor, eth et bresketh ul ol simmeth. Re sol et baegyth dasken um et sulv, bynt hosen vorrdhuss. Um et hald re harmant et hesperdeth dem et movenn. Ul et linth re vysker nayalat.

At dusk on the day of the banner the guild sat in the grass of the old ward, some hundreds of us, with our chisels in our laps. Thin as ash lay the last light on the snow. On the wall the soldiers shouted for the banner. In the grass we said naught.

- *Merge (wf12), H1:* "At dusk on the day of the banner" is *Ul hedh orl et movenn*, as II.1 (S04) says it; *lo hedh* is also read *lo* + S·*cedh*, "at who" (S08's collision table), so the Book's *at dusk* is *ul hedh* everywhere. (The unit had *Lo hedh orl et movenn, re resk et Stonwrytan*.)

Word for word: *At the dusk of the day of the banner, the guild sat in the grass of the old ward, some hundreds of us, and the chisels in our laps. The last light lay upon the snow, thin as ash. Upon the wall the soldiers cried to the banner. In the grass we said nothing.*

- ***Ul hedh orl et movenn***: a double construct, "in the dusk of the day of the banner" (*ul* leaves *h* unchanged). *Merge (wf12):* the unit had *Lo hedh*.
- ***eth et bresketh ul ol simmeth***: a clause with no verb, laid beside the other, as I.1's *eth so wresketh ul so simmeth*. "Our chisels" would be *ol mresketh* (N + *br-*), an onset the tongue does not allow (wf8 S01): the article serves.
- ***merr lesk murr hyor***, "some hundreds of us": a hundred is five twenties and takes no plural (I.1).
- ***bynt hosen vorrdhuss***, "thin as ash" (*vorrdhuss*, "fire-dust"): Seren's picture, set after the light it paints.
- The last two sentences are a pair, each fronted by its place: ***Um et hald*** the soldiers cry, ***Ul et linth*** the guild is silent. *re vysker nayalat*, "we said nothing": a positive verb with *nayalat*, as *Re varr nahos o*.

> Lo ull re reskym, eth nath re yal prassoth lom tul veth lymmyl nynth. Re orrom et hiskenneth susk lo sest luld et Stonwrytan, sa re wysk piskur somm re orr o. Gor vess re aedhym lo o sest.

There sat I, that had no craft yet but the carrying of water. Over the high passes I had walked beside a boy of the guild, that knotted a net as he went. At night I slept at his side.

Word for word: *There I sat, and there was not yet a craft at me but the carrying of water. I walked the high passes at the shoulder of a boy of the guild, who knotted a net while he went. Every night I slept at his shoulder.*

- ***nath re yal prassoth lom tul veth lymmyl nynth***: wf8 S01's words: "yet" is *tul*, "only" is *nath … veth* (pilot I.1 G8).
- ***sa re wysk piskur***, "who knotted a net": *bysk*, "tie, knot fast", softened after *re* (*b* > *w*, §3.3, as the reversative *nawysk*, "untie"). *re vysk* is only "said" (S·*mysk*): pilot I.1's note that *re vysk* is also "bound" does not hold by the softening table. The boy is not named, as the Book does not name him; a Shore ear may hear Tarnel ("little net") in the net. *Back-translation fix*: the draft's *sa re vysk piskur* was heard "who said a net", that is, "who talked of nets".
- ***Gor vess***, "every night", for "at night": the habit, not one night.

> Re vellorom ul et parow ull hosel.

I came into that courtyard alone.

- wf8 S01's words; *hosel* after the verb is "alone" (G8).

> Fedh lyss ul en sestow re rarra pa dhev, sona yarlath cemmet: Enno eth Della, sa re yal lesknoth grem lonta. Nath re yyst ganna tunn hy et tevath et brunn, veth sona. Re reske sona olen, eth nath re luska sona.

A little after me came two children with their hands joined, Enno and Della, of fifteen winters. Of the children's pairs, none came up the mountain whole save those two. They sat down together, and held on.

Word for word: *A short while after me came two children, their two hands joined: Enno and Della, who were fifteen winters old. A whole pair of the children did not climb the mountain, but they two. They two sat together, and they two did not let go.*

- ***Fedh lyss ul en sestow***, "a short while after me" (*fedh lyss*, O2930).
- ***re rarra pa dhev***: a dual subject (*pa* + S·*tev*) takes the 3du (*darr*, softened, + *-a*). Enno and Della are named singly and take the dual; their pair-name, *Enrella*, is not yet theirs (II.3). ***sona yarlath cemmet***, "their two hands met": a clause with no verb (*cemm* "meet, come together"); it comes back at the leaf's end with *tul*.
- ***Nath re yyst ganna tunn … veth sona***: "none save those two" is *nath … veth* (G8). A pair is a gate (*ganna*, O0664), and a whole pair is a **whole gate**: *ganna tunn* is what Enno and Della will sing at the end of the night (*eth ston et ganna tunn*). *gannath* is not used for pairs: it is "posts" (O0671).
- "held on" is ***nath re luska sona***, "they did not let go": *lusk*, the Captain's third call. *lonta*, "at them two".

> Noth cumm, re vesker so ul sennyl ul murr bress. Re orrar dem neld hyont, hos eth hos, eth et bresketh ul ol havulath. Re wemm nahos hyor garl, hy vodh re nalusk et daevarat o: *Gald; nath nayald. Par; nath mosk.* Nath re yal hespeth lor veth lodh eth bresk, eth nath re yal so loskat.

Ten havens had we watched die in twenty months. From each we walked away with our chisels in our packs. No hand had we lifted, for our creed forbade it: *Build, do not destroy. Protect, do not attack.* Mortar and chisel were our only arms, and they had not been enough.

Word for word: *Ten havens, we saw them in dying, in twenty months. We went away from them, one by one, and the chisels in our packs. Not one of us lifted a hand, for the creed forbade it: Build; do not destroy. Protect; do not attack. There were no arms at us but mortar and chisel, and they were not enough.*

- ***Noth cumm, re vesker so …***: the havens fronted, as the English fronts them, and taken up again by *so* (pilot I.1 G6). ***ul murr bress***, "in twenty months" (*bress*, "a month, a moon's turn").
- From *Re orrar* on, wf8 S01's sentences: *re orrar dem neld hyont, hos eth hos*; *re wemm nahos hyor garl*; *re nalusk et daevarat o* ("our creed" would bed *daevarat* into *naevarat*); the Creed, canon (§11.11), in italics as the Book has it; *nath … veth* for "our only arms".

> Re resk vennoth relv tesk umor tul, hosen covv thenth. Re yal gor hos hy et hemmeth lo ull ul gylyl hos galat duss: haldath galdat dem sollan, ul et dask doss maver so hethyl lo lorr. Eth amm ew hosen ull, cedh sa hebb et lorr? Cedh sa wemm et garlath?

The faith of twelve generations sat on us yet, like a wet cloak. Every elder there turned over the same hard thing: that walls raised for peace must at the last be held with blood. And if that were so, whose blood? Whose hands?

Word for word: *The faith of twelve generations sat upon us still, like a wet cloak. Every one of the elders there was in turning one hard thing: walls built for peace, in the end there is need of their holding with blood. And if it was so, who gives the blood? Who lifts the hands?*

- ***vennoth relv tesk***: the canon's *delv tesk*, softened as the possessor (*d* > *r*, as in *pa relv*). The faith *sits* (*resk*) on them, as the guild sat in the grass: one verb for both.
- ***ul gylyl hos galat duss***: the progressive, "turning one hard thing" (wf8 S01). ***gor hos hy et hemmeth***, "every one of the elders": *gor hemm* is also S·*cemm*, "every meeting".
- The thought is given, not reported, and verbless at its head. "Held" is ***keth***, the third hour's verb and the guild's word (*Nel et hurrol, veth et kethyl*): ***so hethyl***, "their holding". *ul et dask*, "at the last". *doss maver*, "there is need" (wf8 S01).
- "Whose blood? Whose hands?" are asked as who gives and who lifts (wf8 S01): *cedh* as a possessor would soften to *hedh*, "dusk". ***wemm et garlath*** answers *re wemm nahos hyor garl* in the paragraph before.

> {{Halyna}}, nath re seskym sona amm ull, veth hosen Hemm eth Hemma sa re reske hy ullen ul vimm et ganna ulvenn cresket, eth re wrodha lo nahos, hy vodh nath re yal maver lonta. Re hadhant ey gedheth et ganna ull. Re stannant o yedheth o dholm hy et brunn. Ew humm eth verr sona, eth hos hy et tevow.

{{Halyna}} I knew not then, save as two elders that sat apart under the broken inner gate, and spake to no one, for they had no need. Her line had laid that gate. His had cut its stone out of the mountain. Husband and wife they were, and one since the cradle.

Word for word: *Halyna, I did not know them then, but as an Elder and an Elderess who sat apart under the broken inner gate, and spoke to no one, for there was no need at them. Her fathers laid that gate. His fathers quarried its stone out of the mountain. Husband and wife they were, and one from the cradle.*

- The object fronted, as the English fronts it, and taken up by *sona* (G6).
- ***Hemm eth Hemma*** (O2882), *hy ullen*, *re wrodha lo nahos*, *nath re yal maver lonta*: wf8 S01's words.
- "Her line" is ***ey gedheth***, "her fathers" (the old line, as the Invocation's *ol nedheth*): *ey* shows no bite, but the plural verb *re hadhant* cannot take "she" for its subject, so *ey* is read as "her" (G9). "His" is ***o yedheth***, where the bite shows. The two lines are one shape, *laid* and *quarried*.
- ***Ew humm eth verr sona, eth hos hy et tevow***: the second clause has no verb (wf8 S01 had *re yala sona hos*).

> Hy ull re rarr et Crenn dem vimm hy et hald. Re veskym et covv ul daldol, eth nath re seskym vodh ew o dem et Stonwrytan. New susk o. Re yal brenthromm um nindeth et Crenn tul, eth saed hovv um o sestow. Re vesk o um et hemmeth, eth hyont um et ganna cresket, eth re vess o hos pessyl. Then came the Captain down from the wall. I had seen the cloak go up, and wist not what it was to us. Not tall was he. The coal-black was yet on his fingers, and the half of a cloak on his back. He looked at the elders, and past them at the broken gate, and asked one question. It is the only speech of his that any at this hearth can say over:

Word for word: *Then the Captain came down from the wall. I saw the cloak in rising, and I did not know what it was to the guild. Not tall was he. There was coal-black still on the Captain's fingers, and the half of a cloak on his back. He looked at the elders, and from them at the broken gate, and asked one asking. This is his only speech that this hearth can say again:*

- Mostly wf8 S01's sentences. ***vodh ew o dem et Stonwrytan***, "what it was to the guild", for its *vodh ew o demor*: *dem* + a person with no motion is heard as "until" (wf8 S01's back-translation); Seren, a child of the guild, says *the guild* for *us*.
- ***um nindeth et Crenn***: *um o nindeth* would be heard as *umo nindeth*, "upon him, fingers" (wf8 S01). ***saed hovv***, "the half of a cloak": the possessor softened (*c* > *h*).
- ***eth hyont um et ganna cresket***, "and from them at the broken gate": the look leaves the elders for the gate, as *past them* does.
- ***sa ross myskyl pawast lo et odh sy***: the can-idiom in a relative (*mysk pawast*, "repeat", O1457).

> Ommant va haldath. Es naldos, ell es veskys eth es sennys?

"Your walls are falling. Will you build, or will you watch and die?"

Word for word: *Your walls fall. Will you build, or will you watch and will you die?*

- wf8 S01's eleven words, unchanged (the English is unchanged): the plural *va*, *-os*, to the whole guild; *es* beds *gald* into *nald*.

> Re dhess nahos o. Tumom et helvhoss ul et linth.

> Um et covv treskat re veske sona dem susk, eth re ferre sona dem et movenn ul hos hoss. Re dhaldant sona wresketh hosen hos.

To the torn cloak they looked up, and bowed to it in the same breath. Up went their chisels as one.

Word for word: *Upon the torn cloak they two looked up, and they two bowed to the banner in one breath. Their chisels rose as one.*

## I.3 · THE CAPTAIN'S LONG BREATH (*HOSS SELL ET CRENN*)

### Title

> ## *Hoss Sell et Crenn*

**I.3 · The Captain's Long Breath**

- *The long breath of the Captain.* The lexicon's title row (O1006), as wf8 S02 gave it. The breath comes back in the telling: Jory's tune, Kael's counting under his breath, the held breath let out.

### Dateline

> *Et grem hosast, ul o wress pawast.*

Word for word: *The first winter, in its second month.*

- Built as I.2's dateline. ***ul o wress pawast***: *o* softens *bress*, "a month" (*b* > *w*), so the bite marks "its"; the ordinal after its noun.

### Seren's headnote

> *Re hadh Hale Haskard, sa re yal dhomnoth surr lo, eth ul o ryndeth hosast um et hald. Re wrodh o et trenn ul stonol, eth pynt hosen re hask o fedh; re hadhom o niss.*

Word for word: *Laid Hale the Runner, who was seventeen years old, and in his first weeks upon the wall. He told the tale standing, and quickly, as he ever ran; I laid it slow.*

- The headnote frame, ***Re hadh*** X (pilot I.1), with ***Hale Haskard*** (O2895).
- ***sa re yal dhomnoth surr lo***: the at-idiom of age (O2938). ***ul o ryndeth hosast***: *o* softens *dynd*, "a week" (*d* > *r*); the relative runs on with no verb.
- ***re hadhom o niss***: "I have set it down slower" is the frame's own verb turned on Seren: Hale laid it, and she laid it, slow where he was quick (*pynt … niss*). The comparison is in the two halves of the sentence, not in a word.

### Hale's telling

> Hebb et tolm lom, hy ull. Nel brodhard en. Haskom.

Word for word: *Give the stone at me, then. Not a teller am I. I run.*

- wf8 S02's words: *Hebb et tolm lom* (O0897), *hy ull* for "then"; ***Haskom***, Hale's creed in one verb.

> Re yal tolm haskardath lo et cummath, cumm eth cumm. Ol dolm, re yaldar o ul et dynd hosast, hy dholm ommat lo et ganna ulvenn; eth umo re ryter et ryt hemm:

Word for word: *There was a runners' stone at the havens, haven and haven. Our stone, we made it in the first week, of a fallen stone by the inner gate; and upon it we swore the old oath:*

- ***tolm haskardath***, "the runners' stone" (addition W12-S02-14; IV.3 has it too).
- "Every haven" is ***cumm eth cumm***, the distributive *X eth X* (O2926 *trenn eth trenn*, O2862 *hos eth hos*): *gor cumm* would soften to *gor humm*, which is "every husband" (*humm*, I.2), and *lo gor* is unsettled (pilot I.1 §3).
- ***Ol dolm, re yaldar o***: the fronted object (G6), "Ours we made": *ol* beds *tolm*. *hy dholm ommat*: *hy*, "made of", softens *tolm*; *kyvildholm* after *hy* would be *hyvildholm*.
- ***umo re ryter et ryt hemm***: *ryt* is the verb and the noun: an oath is cut, as Kael's *Rytom um …* is.

> Galat sa re veskym, lymmym o. Nath lymmym galat ullen. Ston.

"What I saw, I carry. I carry nothing else."

Word for word: *The thing that I saw, I carry it. I do not carry another thing. It stands.*

- **The runners' oath** (addition W12-S02-15, for IV.3, where it is sworn again). The fronted object again (G6); *lymm*, "carry, bear".
- ***Ston.*** closes it, as an oath closes (§3.9 item 3): the one word the English does not carry, the liberty pilot I.1 took for Kael's two oaths. **[Jack]**: delete it and nothing else moves.

> Dasken re ryt Wik o, et tevel sost hyor eth et pynt sost. Nath re vall Wik galat gell hy lymmyl wrod dem et Crenn.

Wick sware it last, the youngest of us and the swiftest. Naught would he sooner do than carry a word to the Captain.

Word for word: *Last Wik swore it, the youngest of us and the quickest. Wik did not love a thing better than the bringing of a word to the Captain.*

- ***Dasken*** fronted, as the English fronts *last*. ***et tevel sost hyor***, ***et pynt sost***: *sost* for the most (wf8 S03's *et hemm sost hyont*).
- *Wik* is named twice: after *re vall o*, *galat* would be read as *o*'s possessed noun and asked for a bite (G9). ***lymmyl wrod***: the object of a verbal noun is a softened possessor (G3); *gell hy*, "better than".
- *Wik*: O3024. *Merge (wf12):* the unit had the Book's spelling *Wick*; the merge took the romanisation's, as S06 and S08 wrote it (§2.4: *k* after *i* at a word's end). The English keeps *Wick*. **[Jack]**

> Ul myst ryst et kiser hosast re stonom lo et brammath sa nesk Kael, gor yalat sa ross lor tul. Re hunn Kael kaever et Crenn: ul vodh es nend o, pedh vodh es gadh o, eth amm vodh es geth o. Hy ull re orr et Crenn dem neld, dem nask et hald. Re hadh Kael o yarl um en sest.

Word for word: *In the grey of the morning of the first assault I stood by the guns that Kael leads, all that is at us still. Kael heard the Captain's mind: where he will watch, what shot he will lay, and when he will hold. Then the Captain went far off, to the end of the wall. Kael laid his hand upon my shoulder.*

- ***ul myst ryst et kiser hosast***: *myst* is the grey of a morning and the grey of the sea-fog and of the fleet in it, one word (§5.5).
- **Kael stays out of every mutating slot** (wf8 S02 Notes 4): after *lo* or as a possessor *Kael* becomes *Hael*, a letter from *Hale*, the teller; after *dem*, *Gael*. So "Kael's guns" is ***et brammath sa nesk Kael***, "the guns Kael leads", and "The Captain gave Kael his mind" is ***Re hunn Kael kaever et Crenn***, "Kael heard the Captain's mind": the same exchange, from the other side.
- ***gor yalat sa ross lor tul***, "that are yet all we have". The three matters are wf8 S02's futures, *es nend o, es gadh o, es geth o* (*o*, "he", is Kael).
- ***dem nask et hald***: *dem* beds *dask*, "the end"; *rask* is only S·*dask* (I.1's *lo rask et prenth*). *Re hadh Kael o yarl um en sest*: laid, the leaf's verb.

> Nath gyl kaever ull veth o harm sost lo en sest, luld; eth nath noss stonol lo uld veth ul hos reskow.

"Naught may change that mind but his own voice at my side, lad, and a man can stand but in one place."

Word for word: *That mind does not change but for his own voice at my side, lad; and standing is not at a man but in one place.*

- Kael's two halves built alike, ***nath … veth*** twice (G8): what only the voice can do, and where only a man can stand. *nath gyl*: *nath* beds *kyl*. *nath noss stonol lo uld*: the can-idiom (§3.7). *luld*, "lad", a vocative.
- ***o harm sost***, "his own voice": *carm* is "a call" and "a voice", softened after *o*. The Captain gives the call *lo o harm sost* later, in the same words.

> *Hy ull re vyskent et cryrullath et sull carm, pawast eth pawast, hosen myskent tul:*

*Then the troops drilled the three calls, as they drill them still:*

Word for word: *Then the troops said the three calls, again and again, as they say them still:*

- "drilled" is ***re vyskent … pawast eth pawast***, the calls said over and over (addition W12-S02-13; *mysk pawast* is "repeat", O1457). *cryrullath*, "troops" (I.1); *et sull carm*, "the three calls" (canon). A pronoun before *tul* (*so tul*) would ask a bite of *tul*, so the object is left to the verb.

> *Marr tho dhrun, eth nend nynth clenn;*  
> *kyl tho vedh dem et wadh, et orrow, et helv;*  
> *keth, ell lusk, amm doss et Crenn lo sest.*

> *Shift your ground, and watch new water;*  
> *change your shot for the sea, the shore, the sky;*  
> *hold, or loose, when the Captain is by.*

Word for word:

> *Shift your ground, and watch new water;*  
> *change your shot to the sea, the shore, the sky;*  
> *hold, or loose, when the Captain is beside.*

- **The form.** The canon's three calls stand at the heads of the three lines word for word (§11.5: *Marr tho dhrun · Kyl tho vedh · Keth, ell lusk*), and each line adds what the drill adds. *tho*, the singular, as the Captain calls to a True Man.
- ***eth nend nynth clenn***, "and watch new water": the canon's own gloss on *trun* is the water a battery watches. ***dem et wadh, et orrow, et helv***: the article keeps *dem* from bedding *orrow* into *morrow*; *dem* is not said again in the list. ***lo sest***, with no possessor, is "beside [us]" (wf8 S01), the English's *by*.

> Amm re dhunnant, re ferr Yory Dhavow Hemm um hosk et hald rerddhalden, eth re hossvell et vell sa dhevirent et higileth hemm. Lo visk et bystir re yal Merrik ul scaevurol wunth, et hemm sost hyont. Re vysk o lo: Hess. Re dhyvull Yory dem vimm umo.

The drill done, Jory of Eldhythe leaned on the parapet of the east wall and whistled a tune that the old ship-masters hate. Merrick, eldest of them, coiling rope at the stair-foot, bade him stop. Jory laughed down at him.

- *Merge (wf12), H26:* "the parapet of the east wall": *et hald rerddhalden*, "the east wall", as IV.5 and V.5 say it (S07, S08), with the parapet as its capstone (*hosk*). (The unit had *um hosk hald et rerddhald,*.)

Word for word: *When they had finished, Jory of Eldhythe leaned upon the capstone of the east wall, and whistled the tune that the old ship-masters hate. At the foot of the stair Merrick was in rolling of rope, the eldest of them. He said to him: Stop. Jory laughed down upon him.*

- ***Amm re dhunnant***, "when they had finished" (*tunn*, "whole; finish"). ***Yory Dhavow Hemm*** (O3027): the epithet construct, *Tavow Hemm* softened, as *Corlen Dhavow*. ***um hosk hald et rerddhald***: the parapet is the capstone of the wall (wf8 S02's *hosk hald*), and the wall is the east's.
- *Merge (wf12):* **"whistled a tune" is *re hossvell et vell*, "whistled the tune"**, with the Book's one word for whistling, *hossvell*, "breath-song" (O3029: S03, S05, S08, S09; the article, as the tune is the one the ship-masters hate, IV.5's rhyme), where the unit had *re hoss vell*, "breathed a song" (W12-S02-09, withdrawn). The unit's reasoning, kept: the tongue has no verb for whistling and needs none; *hoss*, "breathe", is pilot I.1's, and the title's breath is in it. *o* is left out: *o vell* would be read "his spire" (S·*pell*). ***sa dhevirent et higileth hemm***: *tevir*, "hate", present, for they hate it still; *higil*, "a ship-master" ("one who knows the water").
- ***ul scaevurol wunth***: *scaevur*, "roll", for coiling; the object of a verbal noun is its softened possessor (*bunth* > *wunth*, G3). *lo visk et bystir*: *lo* softens *misk*, "foot". *et hemm sost hyont*, "the eldest of them".
- "bade him stop" is quoted, ***Re vysk o lo: Hess.***: Orrowen quotes rather than reports. ***dem vimm umo***, "down upon him".
- *Yory*, *Merrik*: O3025, O3026. *Merge (wf12):* the unit had the Book's spellings *Jory* and *Merrick*; the merge took the tongue's (no *j*, §2.5; *k* after *i*, §2.4), as S03, S07 and S08 wrote them. The English keeps *Jory* and *Merrick*. **[Jack]**

> El hosel tevvell o.

"It is only a rhyme."

Word for word: *It is only a children's rhyme.*

- *hosel* before the noun is "only" (I.1). ***tevvell***, "a children's rhyme": the tune is one (*Sulter Myst*, the Grey Boat, O2424), which the Book does not name here.

> Nath re dhyvull Merrik.

Merrick did not laugh.

- *dhyvull*, "laugh", does not change after *re*: the verb said back, as yes and no are.

> Somm nath re orr saed et ryst tul, re rarrant wemmeth en gethyl hosast hy et myst, pynt hy en nydhyl. Ul et cadhol orrant et clemeth niss, hy vodh doss tolm nalelv, eth ul et caldol, niss hy ull. Ul et kethyl orr clem ul moltrunn.

Ere the morning was half gone, the sails of my first holding came out of the grey faster than I could tell them. In the laying the hours go slow, for stone is heavy, and in the hauling slower. In the holding an hour is gone in a heartbeat.

Word for word: *While half the morning had not yet gone, the sails of my first holding came from the grey, quicker than my counting. In the laying the hours go slowly, for stone is heavy, and in the hauling, slower than that. In the holding an hour goes in a heartbeat.*

- ***somm nath … tul*** for "ere" (wf8 S02 S4); *saed et ryst*, "half the morning". ***wemmeth en gethyl hosast***: *en* beds *kethyl*. ***pynt hy en nydhyl***, "quicker than my counting" (wf8 S02): Hale's *tell* is a count.
- The three hours keep their articles (*et cadhol, et caldol, et kethyl*): after *ul*, a bare *caldol* would bed to *galdol*, "building" (wf8 S02). ***doss tolm nalelv***: a quality takes the state verb (wf8 S02 S1). ***niss hy ull***, "slower than that", the verb left out. *moltrunn*, "a heartbeat" ("heart-blow").

> Desir ul et kethyl, amm re yal et ullen vyssorat eth re yal et tolmardath um et vathur, re vesk begorn et bramman scethel ul sirr dem neld sa re rasor Pellow. Re dhovv o ul sestow ul et myst, ul vodh nath re dhovv hos fedh. Amm ull re vellor o, desir eth hosel.

Late in the holding, when the rest were spent and the masons were come out on the breach, Kael's lookout saw a hull in a far-glass of Pellow's grinding. It had hung back in the grey, where none had hung before. Now it came in, late and alone.

Word for word: *Late in the holding, when the rest were spent and the masons were upon the breach, the lookout of the battery saw a hull in a far-glass that Pellow polished. It had waited behind in the grey, where not one had ever waited. Then it came in, late and alone.*

- "Kael's lookout" is ***begorn et bramman***, the battery's: Kael kept out of the possessor's slot, and the battery is his (wf8 S02).
- ***ul sirr dem neld***, "in a far-glass" (W12-S02-12). ***sa re rasor Pellow***: *dasor*, "polish, rub smooth", softened (*d* > *r*): the Book's *grinding*.
- The second sentence is wf8 S02's. "Now" in a past telling is ***amm ull***, "then"; *desir eth hosel*, "late and alone".

> Ul lodh et Crenn eth et scethel ull re sol et dhidh. Re hebb Kael sull brod lom.

Between the Captain and that hull lay the smoke. Kael gave me three words.

Word for word: *In the mortar of the Captain and that hull lay the smoke. Kael gave me three words.*

- ***ul lodh***, "between" (O2632): the smoke is laid between them as mortar is. ***sull brod***: a numeral before a singular.

> Scethel ul sestow.

"Hull hangeth back."

Word for word: *A hull behind.*

- Kael's three words are three in the Orrowen: a naming with no verb, the stones of a message with its mortar left out, as the dry cut would cut it. *ul sestow* is the *behind* of *re dhovv o ul sestow ul et myst* two sentences before.

> Re haskom o.

I ran it.

- wf8 S02's words: *hask*, "run", with a message for its object.

> Haskom. Ul vimm et dhidh, bisk dem vimm, ul neld et tolmardath, ul neld et bunthath. Myskym dem nahos, eth nath hessym. Dem susk, dem et baegyth, ul susk et ganna.

I run. Under the smoke, head down, past the masons, past the ropes. To no man speak I, and I stop not. Up into the clear, above the gate.

Word for word: *I run. Under the smoke, head down, beyond the masons, beyond the ropes. I speak to no one, and I do not stop. Up, to the light, above the gate.*

- Hale's present, as the Book's. *bisk dem vimm*, "head down" (wf8 S02). ***ul neld***, "past, beyond" (O2635).
- ***Myskym dem nahos***: a positive verb with *nahos*; *mysk* is kept from *nath*, after which it is heard as *bysk*, "tie" (pilot I.1 §3). *nath hessym*: *hess*, the Guest's first word, is the runner's "stop".
- ***dem et baegyth***, "into the clear": the light out of the smoke; *ul susk et ganna*, "above the gate".

> Ston et Crenn sol.

The Captain standeth still.

Word for word: *Stands the Captain still.*

- Hale's present. *sol*, "lie still, rest", stands as the adverb of *ston* (wf8 S02's *Re ston o sol*).

> Tumom ull hosast. Re yal gor yalat um et hald ull ul morrol; nath re yal o. Re heth o hosk et hald lo hos garl, eth o lern dem et wadh. Nath re vesk o umom. Veth re hunn o gor wrod, dem et brod dasken.

That is what I remember first. Everything on that wall was moving but him. One hand he had on the parapet, his face to the sea. He looked not round at me. Yet he heard me to the last word.

Word for word: *That I remember first. Everything upon that wall was in going; he was not. He held the capstone of the wall with one hand, and his face to the sea. He did not look upon me. But he heard every word, to the last word.*

- The first two sentences are wf8 S02's: "but him" is the verb said back.
- "One hand he had on the parapet" is ***re heth o hosk et hald lo hos garl***, "he held the parapet with one hand": *keth*, the holding's verb, and the one hand the Book counts. *eth o lern dem et wadh*: a clause with no verb (wf8 S02's *eth o lern dem et lunt*).
- ***gor wrod … dem et brod dasken***: *gor* softens *brod*; *en mrodath*, "my words", is the onset the tongue will not have.

> Hy ull re orr o dem et brammath sa nesk Kael, lo o lusul sost, eth en lo o sest. Somm re orr o, re yal et clem ul sellyl.

Then he walked toward Kael's guns at his own pace, and I at his shoulder. As he walked, the hour began to stretch.

Word for word: *Then he walked to the guns that Kael leads, at his own pace, and I at his shoulder. While he walked, the hour was in stretching.*

- ***lo o lusul sost***, "at his own pace" (wf8 S02 used it of the world); *eth en lo o sest*, verbless. ***ul sellyl***: the progressive; *sell*, "long; stretch", is the title's word.

> Nath re hess et clem pynt. Re orr o niss, hosen saevar dholmath um wrunnel sell, dem et fedh sa re yal nydhyl yannath et scaevur lo luld ul sestow. Nath noss cadhol gell hy sy lom.

It stopped not short. It slowed, like a stone-cart on a long hill, till a boy behind might count the spokes. I have no better way to say it.

Word for word: *The hour did not stop quickly. It went slowly, like a cart of stones on a long hill, until the time that a boy behind could count the posts of the wheel. There is no better laying than this at me.*

- ***Nath re hess et clem pynt***, "the hour did not stop quickly": wf8 S02's *Nath re hess o pynt*, with the hour named. *Re orr o niss*, "it went slowly", is the morning's *orrant et clemeth niss* (the hours go slowly) in the singular. *Back-translation fix*: with *o* the blind reader took the Captain, the one walking, for the subject ("He did not stop quickly. He went slowly"), where the Book's *it* is the hour. ***saevar dholmath***, "a cart of stones", the possessor softened; *um wrunnel sell*, "on a long hill" (wf8 S02).
- "Till" is ***dem et fedh sa …***: *dem amm* would bed to *dem mamm* (wf8 S01). The spokes are ***gannath et scaevur***, "the posts of the wheel" (W12-S02-10), softened as the counting's object (*nydhyl yannath*, G3).
- The last sentence is wf8 S02's: to say is to lay.

> Re veskym et nynth ul vidhyl hy lern et scethel desir. Re nydhym et fodheleth ul morrol dem vimm lo dhrenn et wemm myst, hosen nydhys scaevurath um et mesk stinet: hos, eth hy ull ullen. Ew clemel gor hos, kaeverym. Re solant et nasath wadh. Re ston et dhidh. Re orr o tul.

I saw the water sheet off the late hull's bow. I counted the ripples going down its grey sail, as ye count the rings on a cut log: one, and then the next. Each, I think, was a minute. The gulls hung. The smoke stood. On he walked.

Word for word: *I saw the water in pouring from the bow of the late hull. I counted the ripples in going down along the grey sail, as you count rings upon the cut log: one, and then another. Every one was a minute, I think. The gulls lay still. The smoke stood. He walked on.*

- ***lern*** is "a face" and "the bow of a hull" (wf8 S02); *vidh*, "pour". *et scethel desir*, "the late hull".
- ***scaevurath***, the log's rings, and *scaevur*, the cart's wheel in the paragraph before, are one word: Hale's two pictures share it, and Seren leaves it as it falls. ***um et mesk stinet***: the article keeps *um* from softening *mesk* into *vesk*. *hosen nydhys*: the hearth addressed as many (wf8 S02).
- ***Ew clemel gor hos, kaeverym***: the copula, predicate first (wf8 S02). ***et nasath wadh***, "the gulls" (W12-S02-11); *re solant*, "lay still", for *hung*. ***Re orr o tul***, "on he walked", *tul* "still".

> Re yal fedh ludalol lom. Hy ull re yal fedh haemul lom um ull. Hy ull re yal fedh hessyl lom.

I had time to be afraid. Then I had time to be ashamed of it. Then I had time to stop.

Word for word: *There was a time of fearing at me. Then there was a time of shame at me, about that. Then there was a time of stopping at me.*

- wf8 S02's three sentences, the same line three ways: *fedh ludalol*, *fedh haemul* (S·*kaemul*), *fedh hessyl*.

> Re hess o, eth re yal Kael lo o sest. Re yal Kael ul nydhyl ul vimm o hoss. Hy ull re hebb et Crenn et carm, lo o harm sost.

At Kael's shoulder he stopped. Kael was counting under his breath. Then the Captain gave the call, with his own voice.

Word for word: *He stopped, and Kael was at his shoulder. Kael was in counting under his breath. Then the Captain gave the call, with his own voice.*

- "At Kael's shoulder" is turned so that Kael is the subject: *sest Hael* would put Hale's name in it. ***ul vimm o hoss***, "under his breath": the title's word again.
- ***et carm, lo o harm sost***: the call and the voice are one word, *carm*, and Kael's *o harm sost* comes back word for word.

> Keth.

"Hold."

- The third call's first word, sounded alone as the horn sounds it (§11.5): *Keth!* or *Lusk!*.

> Re heth et bramman. Ul vimm re hadhant et tolmardath tul, pa eth pa, eth nath re veskent dem susk. Re rarr et scethel desir tul lo dhrenn et dhidh, eth tul, dem et fedh sa re yal veskyl et nynth um o hrynireth lom.

The battery held. Below us the masons laid on, two and two, and looked not up. The late hull came on through the smoke, and on, till I could see the wet on its timbers.

Word for word: *The battery held. Below, the masons laid still, two and two, and did not look up. The late hull came still through the smoke, and still, until the time that I could see the water upon its planks.*

- ***Re heth et bramman***: the call obeyed in its own word. ***pa eth pa***, "two and two" (O2927); *re hadhant … tul*, "laid on"; *Ul vimm*, "below".
- ***tul … eth tul***, "on, and on". ***um o hrynireth***: *crynir*, "a plank", softened after *o* (*cr-* > *hr-*, G7); "the wet" is the water on them.

> Lusk.

"Loose."

- The call's second word (§11.5). *Lusk*, "loose", is the root the hearth hears in *lunn*, freedom.

> Niss re lusk et clem ol, hosen orr hoss kethet hyo. Re wrammant et brammath. Ul et dhidh re hresk et scethel, ul hosel reskow et nynth ull ul vodh nath re yal grest hyo um hos, ul vodh re haever et Crenn o dem greskyl.

The hour let us go, slow, like a held breath going out. The guns went. Out in the smoke the hull broke up, in the one stretch of that water where it could hurt no one, where the Captain had meant it to break.

Word for word: *Slowly the hour loosed us, as a held breath goes out of it. The guns fired. In the smoke the hull broke, in the only place of that water where there was no harm from it upon anyone, where the Captain meant it for breaking.*

- wf8 S02's sentences: *Niss* fronted so that *ol* ends its clause; ***hoss kethet***, "a held breath" (O2899); *re lusk*, the call's own verb; ***nath re yal grest hyo um hos***, the old masters' *Grest um hos* with no one to fall on.
- "where the Captain had meant it to break" is ***ul vodh re haever et Crenn o dem greskyl***, "meant it for breaking": *dem* + a verbal noun is purpose (wf8 S02 SG2). *o hreskyl*, "its breaking", was avoided: after *et Crenn* it reads as an article on the head of a construct.

> Nath seskym vodh re haever et Crenn. Seskym vodh re yald o.

I know not what the Captain thought. I know what he did.

- Two sentences of one shape, the second the first with *nath* taken away. *sesk*, "know a fact"; *re haever*, "thought"; *re yald*, "did" (*gald*, "do, make").

> Hedh ull re orrom et hylldhrenn, hosen orrar, ol haskardath, ul sestow gor hethyl. Ul et venth re veskym rellorath rytet ul grynir, eth re lymmym so dem susk ul neld et vorrath, dem {{Halyna}}:

That evening I walked the tide-line, as we runners do after every holding. In the shallows I saw marks cut in a plank, and carried them up past the fires to {{Halyna}}:

Word for word: *That evening I walked the tide-line, as we walk, we runners, after every holding. In the shallows I saw marks cut in a plank, and I carried them up beyond the fires, to Halyna:*

- ***et hylldhrenn***, "the tide-line" ("the tide's course"). ***hosen orrar, ol haskardath***: *ol* after a verb is the emphatic "we", with the runners in apposition. *ul sestow gor hethyl*: *gor* softens *kethyl*.
- *venth*, "shallow water"; ***ul grynir***: *ul* beds *crynir*. Hale carries the marks as he carries words: *lymm*, the runners' verb, the oath's verb.

> Rellorath um et mesk.

"Marks on the wood."

- Hale's message (W12-S02-16), verbless as Kael's was, four words to the English's four; V.1 has the same line.

> Re haskom o.

I ran it.

- wf8 S02's words, said a second time, as the Book says them.

> Ul et vess ull re vymmym lo reller Hesk, greller o odha, um hald et bridlern. Hyo re seskym: gern et bridlern.

That night I found my way back by Hesk's lamp, his mother's lamp, on the north wall. By it I wist the north was awake.

Word for word: *In that night I came back by the lamp of Hesk, the lamp of his mother, upon the wall of the north. From it I knew: the north wakes.*

- ***re vymmym***, "I came back" (*mymm*, "return"). ***lo reller Hesk***: *lo* softens *greller*, "a lamp", and a softened *g* before a consonant drops (§2.5), as in the lexicon's *baegyth reller*, "lamplight". *greller o odha*, in apposition, keeps its *g*. *bridlern*, "the north" ("the cold side").
- What he knew is given as known, not reported: ***gern et bridlern***, "the north wakes".

> Amm sy myskent et uldath o lo dhrenn et hald: *Lo et Crenn, clem sell hosen orl.* Kaeverym re hadhom o dholm hosast, veth mysk Voss: Doss et brodhat hemm hyoth. Myskent o hosen amm re hebb et Ternil o lo. Re vyskym o hosen ull, en sost; eth amm sy seskym gell hy ull. Re hlenn Kael o, eth lymmym o hosen re vysk o, eth nath lymmym galat ullen:

Now the men say it along the wall: "For the Captain, an hour lasteth as long as a day." I think it began with me, though Voss will have it older. They say it as though the Rite had given it him. So said I myself, and I know better now. Kael mended it, and I carry it as he said it, and nothing else:

Word for word: *Now the men say it along the wall: At the Captain, an hour long as a day. I think I laid its first stone, but Voss says: The saying is older than you. They say it as if the Rite had given it to him. I said it like that, myself; and now I know better than that. Kael mended it, and I carry it as he said, and I do not carry another thing:*

- The wall's saying, verbless (O2897), in italics as a quoted saying. The first stone and Voss's words are wf8 S02's (*re hadhom o dholm hosast*: *mosk* "begin" is the Creed's trap; *Doss et brodhat hemm hyoth*: Voss, wry, gives his own comparison).
- ***re hebb et Ternil o lo***: the Rite (*ternil*, I.4's title). *Re vyskym o hosen ull, en sost*; *seskym gell hy ull*: wf8 S02's. ***Re hlenn Kael o***: *clenn*, "make new, mend", softened (*cl-* > *hl-*, as *cr-* > *hr-*).
- The paragraph ends on the runners' oath, ***nath lymmym galat ullen***, as the English's *and nothing else* does.

> Nel et trun sa orr niss, luld. El et Crenn sa nath bynt. Et clemel sa re naelur o, ew et clemel sa re rarr.

"It is not the world that sloweth, lad. It is the Captain who will not hurry. The moment he chose was the moment that came."

Word for word: *It is not the world that goes slowly, lad. It is the Captain who does not hurry. The moment that he chose, it was the moment that came.*

- wf8 S02's three sentences, unchanged, for the English is the same: two clefts and a proverb-shaped close; *sa nath bynt*, *nath* bedding *pynt*.

> Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

- The laying formula, singular (O0238): Hale lays alone.

> *Eth re vysk et odh:* **Tumar.**

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

- The hearth's answer (O0564).

### 3. For Jack

- **Jory, Wick, Merrick: the Book's spellings break the Orrowen's.** The tongue has no *j* (§2.5) and the course-hand no letter for it; *ck* is not the romanisation's way with a final /k/ (§2.4: *Wik*, *Merrik*), and the token writer would give *Wick* a doubled k, which is no geminate of the tongue. This unit keeps the Book's spellings in the romanisation, as names are names, and gives the course-hand's spelling by sound in each row's dry cut (*y.o.r.y*, *w.i.k*, *m.e.r.r.i.k*). Say if the romanised Orrowen should instead read *Yory*, *Wik*, *Merrik*; it is three rows and a search.
- **The one word the English lacks**: *Ston.* after the runners' oath, as after Kael's two oaths in I.1.
- **Seren's turns in the ink**, all reversible: the headnote's *et hosast … et pawast* (the first tale she laid, the second the hearth heard) for *this hearth had heard but one before it*; *Re hadhom o niss* (I laid it slow) for *I have set it down slower*; *re hoss vell* (breathed a song) for *whistled a tune*; the leaf's last verb, *re hyn*, from Rhyna's name.

### 5. What was carried from the old units, and what changed

The v1.3 tellings were long, and their Orrowen was laid paragraph by paragraph against them.
