## 1 · IV.5 · THE BURNING BOAT (*SULTER SA VORR*)

*A stone leaf, laid by Voss, in Seren's one ink. Voss circles back in the same words (the question of ¶4 asked again in ¶19), and the leaf opens and closes on a screaming hull.*

### Title

**Title** · `S07-IV5-01`

> ## *Sulter sa vorr*

**IV.5 · The Burning Boat**

Word for word: *The boat that burns.*

- The lexicon's title (O2425), unchanged from the v1.3 unit (S06): the boat is named by what it does, as the shore remembers it.

### Dateline

**Dateline** · `S07-IV5-02`

> *Ul et syst pawast, ul Hyll Pawast.*

*The second summer, in the Second Tide.*

Word for word: *In the second summer, in the Second Tide.*

- A dateline is a naming (no verb), as the dry cut's headings are; the leaf-hand still writes its mortar words (*ul et*). *Hyll Pawast* is the lexicon's Second Tide (O1048). *pawast* twice, as the English says *second* twice.

### Seren's headnote (her one ink)

**Headnote** · `S07-IV5-03`

> *Re hadh Voss sa re vess, hosen re wrodh scaelur Voss, Elspet, o lo, lo ey vorr um dhavow Dhavow Hemm. El trenn o sa nath gadhant et higileth, eth vell sa nath hessent et tevath vellyl; re dhyvull et Orrow umo, eth nath re dhyvull et wadh.*

*Laid by Voss who asked, as his grandmother Elspet told it him at her fire on the quay at Eldhythe. It is a tale the ship-masters would not tell, and a rhyme the children would not stop singing; the shore laughed at it, and the sea laughed not.*

Word for word: *Voss who asked laid [it], as Voss's grandmother, Elspet, told it to him, at her fire on the quay of Eldhythe. It is a tale that the ship-masters do not lay, and a song that the children do not stop singing; the Shore laughed upon it, and the sea did not laugh.*

- *Re hadh* X is every stone headnote's opening (Tier 3 §5.1); *Voss sa re vess* is the canon epithet (O2790). v3 drops the rank, so *Kesterd* goes.
- *scaelur Voss, Elspet*: after a verb, *o scaelur* would read "he … grandmother" (§3.5: *s* never softens), so the possessor is said as a name, and her own name stands beside it.
- *um dhavow Dhavow Hemm*, "on the quay of Eldhythe": *tavow* twice, softened both times (after *um*, and as the possessor). Quay and haven are one word, and Seren lets the chime stand: Voss's grandmother kept her fire on the harbour of the Old Harbour.
- *nath hessent … vellyl*, "do not stop singing": *hess* is the Guest's first word, and it comes back at the end of the tale (¶28, ¶30, ¶31, ¶33). Seren plants it in her headnote.
- *eth nath re dhyvull et wadh*: Orrowen has no "no"; the verb is said again (§3.4).

### Voss's telling

**¶1** · `S07-IV5-04`

> Hebb et tolm lom. Nel en drenn sy. El trenn en scaelur; eth re yal o loy hy ey vost, eth re yal o lo hy et wadh.

Give me the stone. This one is not mine. It is my grandmother's, and she had it from her brother, and he had it from the sea.

Word for word: *Give the stone at me. This is not my tale. It is my grandmother's tale; and it was at her from her brother, and it was at him from the sea.*

- *Hebb et tolm lom* is the pilots' formula (O0897). *Nel en drenn sy* is the v1.3 unit's: *trenn* is the tale and the course of stones at once, so "this one" is both the stone he takes and the tale he lays.
- Having is the "at" idiom (*re yal o loy*, "it was at her"), and the chain is said twice in one shape, as Voss's English says it.

**¶2** · `S07-IV5-05`

> Ul gethyl hosast et gorrol sy, um et dhysal, re hunnom scethel ul vorrol. Re scyndal o. Lo dhrenn et hald re hylent et farrardath hemm lerneth hy et wadh, eth Merrik hosast. Um et hald rerddhalden re hossvell Yory.

In the first holding of this war, on the shingle, I heard one of their hulls burn. It screamed. Along the wall the old sailors turned their faces from the sea, and Merrick first. On the east wall Jory whistled.

Word for word: *In the first holding of this war, upon the shingle, I heard one hull of their fleet in burning. It screamed. Along the wall the old sailors turned faces from the sea, and Merrik first. Upon the eastern wall Yory whistled.*

- *kethyl*, "a holding", is the wall's word for an assault held, and the wood's word for a knowing (*keth*: hold, keep, know); V.1 opens on the same verb.
- *scethel*, the canon singulative, "one hull of a fleet", carries "one of their hulls" in one word.
- *re hylent … lerneth hy et wadh*, "turned faces from the sea": the faces are a bare plural object, since *so lerneth* after a subject reads as a construct (the v1.3 unit's S3).
- *Merge (wf12):* "whistle" is *hossvell*, "breath-song" (O3029), the Book's one word for it (S03, S05, S08, S09); the unit's *inthvell*, "lip-song" (S07-08, withdrawn), is the same thought. The unit's note on it, kept: *inthvell*, "whistle", is this unit's one new word (S07-08): *inth* "a lip; a kiss" + *vell* "a song", built as *sodhvell*. The lip of it is the kiss of the rhyme (*stinel hosen inth*), which Jory whistles without its words.
- **Names.** *Merrik* and *Yory* are the Book's Merrick and Jory in the Book's own romanisation: *k* at the end of a word after *i* (§2.4), and the tongue has no *j*, writing the glide as *y* before a vowel (§2.4, §2.5). See NOTES 1.

**¶3** · `S07-IV5-06`

> Re hyl tevvell ulom hosen bunth hunneryl. Amm re yal dhom surr lom, re hunnerym lo. Amm re yal pa vurr surr lom, re hyrrilym o.

A rhyme went round in me like a skipping-rope. At seven I had skipped to it. At forty I understood it.

Word for word: *A children's rhyme turned in me like a rope of skipping. When seven years were at me, I skipped to it. When two twenties of years were at me, I understood it.*

- *kyl* (*re hyl*), "turn", is the rope's verb, the boat's (¶12, the rhyme) and the rhyme's in Voss: one verb for all three turnings, as Seren hears it.
- **A word that works twice.** *hunner*, "skip", opens with *hunn*, "hear": the children *hunner* to the rhyme that the ship-masters will not *hunn* (¶4, ¶19). The two clauses are laid in one shape, seven against forty.
- Age is the "at" idiom; forty is *pa vurr*, "two twenties" (the Shore counts in twenties).

**¶4** · `S07-IV5-07`

> Amm ew luld en lo ey genth, re vessym en scaelur hy vodh nath hunnant et higileth o. Re dhess ey lom; ew trenn o. Nydha o, nalelv ell lelv.

A boy at her table, I asked my grandmother wherefore the ship-masters would not hear it. She answered me with a tale. Ye may weigh it.

Word for word: *When I was a boy at her table, I asked my grandmother why the ship-masters do not hear it. She answered me; it was a tale. Weigh it, heavy or light.*

- The question is embedded, as I.1's *nath re seskym hy vodh*; *hunnant* is the habitual present.
- *Re dhess ey lom; ew trenn o*: "she answered me with a tale" is cut in two. *ul drenn* ("in a tale") is the lexicon's idiom "in order, in line", and *lo dhrenn* is "along".
- *Nydha o, nalelv ell lelv*, "weigh it, heavy or light" (to many): *nydh* is "count; weigh, reckon", so the hearth is asked to weigh the tale with the verb Elspet counts the boats with (¶13). **Back-translation fix:** bare *Nydha o* was read "Count it"; *nalelv ell lelv* makes the verb a weighing, and leaves the verdict to the hearers, which is the English's *Ye may*. **Heavy is un-light.** *nalelv* is *lelv* with *na-*: Seren says the scale's two pans with one word and its undoing, as the tale's last line says *Nadhumar* against the hearth's *Tumar*.

**¶5** · `S07-IV5-08`

> Amm re yal delv surr loy, re heth ey et vorr ul davowlest ey odha. Re vellorant et higileth hy et helvnynth, eth et helv ul so vern. Ul susk et vorr re haemer sull tolm ryss myst, eth tolmath umont, bynt hosen miskryteth nasath um rull thenth. Re sesk nahos vodh re vyskent. Nath re vessent veth et tevath. Re runnant et higileth mygurath um et tolm vimm sost.

At twelve she kept the fire in her mother's harbour-house. In out of the rain came the ship-masters, with the weather in their beards. Over the fire hung three flat grey stones, with letters fine as the tracks of birds on wet sand. What they said, none wist. Only the children asked. The ship-masters knocked out their pipes on the lowest one.

Word for word: *When twelve years were at her, she kept the fire in her mother's harbour-house. The ship-masters came in from the rain, and the weather in their hair. Above the fire hung three flat grey stones, and letters upon them, fine as the tracks of birds upon wet sand. No one knew what they said. None asked but the children. The ship-masters knocked pipes upon the lowest stone.*

- *re heth ey et vorr*: she *kept* the fire with *keth*, the hold-and-keep verb.
- *eth et helv ul so vern*: a clause with no verb, laid beside the other, as the pilot's *eth so wresketh ul so simmeth*. *helv* is sky and weather at once; the lexicon has no beard, and *mern* "hair" carries the picture (*so* softens it, so the possessive shows).
- *tolm ryss myst*, "flat grey stone", never *tolm myst*, which is the canon's Myststone (the v1.3 unit's rule). **Stone upon stone:** the stones' letters are *tolmath*, for a letter of the course-hand is a stone; Seren does not explain it.
- *Nath re vessent veth et tevath*: "only" of a clause is *nath … veth* (§3.8).
- *Re runnant … mygurath*: the v1.3 unit's line (S3: a possessive after a subject reads as a construct).

**¶6** · `S07-IV5-09`

> Amm re vymm berderd hy et myst, eth trenn lo, re dhyvull ey, eth re losk ey et nunth. Kyl Mystow uldath venn dem nyculardath, re vysk ey.

At every bold man home from the grey with a tale she laughed, and filled his cup. "The Mystlands make liars of honest men," said she.

Word for word: *When a bold one came back from the grey, and a tale at him, she laughed, and filled the cup. The Mystlands turn true men into liars, she said.*

- "At every bold man" is said as a habit, *amm re vymm berderd …*: *um gor* would put *gor* under a softening (*um yor werderd*), which the grammar leaves unsettled.
- *et nunth*, "the cup": *o nunth* after a verb cannot show "his" (§3.5; the v1.3 unit's ¶6 fix).
- *Kyl Mystow uldath venn dem nyculardath* is the v1.3 unit's line. *kyl*, "turn", again; and *uldath venn*, "true men", is a breath from the *vennuldath*, the True Men, which no Rivenman misses.

**¶7** · `S07-IV5-10`

> Re vysk ey o um ey vost.

She said it of her brother.

Word for word: *She said it about her brother.*

- *um ey vost*: *um* "upon, about"; *ey* does not mutate, but after a preposition it can only be "her".

**¶8** · `S07-IV5-11`

> Ew gostard Daveth ul et gostol. Ul et syst ull re yal o ul mymmyl dem varn hy lern et myst, ul hos hy et sultereth dasken et mesk domm, vimm ul et wadh; eth sulter pawast ul o sestow.

Daveth was an axeman of the felling. That summer he was coming home from the edge in one of the last timber-boats, low with black wood, and a second in his wake.

Word for word: *Daveth was an axeman in the felling. In that summer he was in returning home from the face of the grey, in one of the last boats of the black wood, low in the sea; and a second boat in his back.*

- *Ew gostard Daveth*: the copula, predicate first (§3.4). *ul et gostol*, "in the felling", keeps "of the felling" from hanging Daveth on *gostol* as its possessor.
- "the edge" is *lern et myst*, "the face of the grey": the edge where the black pillars were felled is a face, as a wall has one (*hy hless* would be an onset §2.5 forbids).
- *sultereth … et mesk domm*, "the boats of the black wood", for "timber-boats … low with black wood": one possessor carries both.
- *ul o sestow*, "in his back", for "in his wake" (*sestow* is the back, and a hull's stern).

**¶9** · `S07-IV5-12`

> Re rarr sulter umonta hy et myst, ul vorr, ul lern et helvhoss eth ul lern et kess; nath noss darrol hosen sy lo sulter. Ul scyndalol re rarr o, hosen darrant wadhnasath um luldath sa rikent hy nasow. Vimm re runn o umonta, ul vodh re haemer et havul nalelv sost, eth re rik et mesk domm sa re lymme et vorr hyo. Ul sestow re rarr et helvnynth, um wisketh pa hryrull.

Out of the grey a boat came at them, burning, into the wind and against the current, which no boat can do. Screaming it came, as gulls come at boys that rob a nest. Low it struck them both, where the load rode heaviest, and the black wood in their holds took its fire. After came the rain, on the heads of two crews.

Word for word: *There came a boat upon the two of them from the grey, alight, against the wind and against the current; coming so is not at a boat. In screaming it came, as seabirds come upon boys who take from a rookery. Low it struck upon the two of them, where the load rode heaviest, and the black wood that they two bore took the fire from it. After came the rain, upon the heads of two crews.*

- The two timber-boats are a pair, so the boat comes *umonta*, "upon them two": the dual, which the tongue gives any two.
- *nath noss darrol hosen sy lo sulter*, "coming so is not at a boat": "which no boat can do" in the "at" idiom, as the v1.3 unit said it of the turning.
- *wadhnas*, "a seabird", for the gulls; *nasow*, "a rookery, a place of birds", for the nest (the lexicon has no nest; NOTES 4).
- *re haemer et havul*: *haemer* is "float; ride (on the water)", so the load *rides* low, as the English says.
- *Ul sestow re rarr et helvnynth*, "after came the rain": the couplet's own words (*eth helvnynth ul sestow*), so the unsung lines are already in the tale before the children sing them.
- *pa hryrull*: *cryrull* "a crew" softened after *pa*; the *hr* is the canon's own (*Hrenn* from *Crenn*).

**¶10** · `S07-IV5-13`

> Re vymment sull dem varn.

Three came home.

Word for word: *Three came back home.*

- *mymm*, "return, come back", is the word of the couplet's last line (*nath mymm dem varn*).

**¶11** · `S07-IV5-14`

> Re vymm Daveth dem varn um vivenn cresket, eth et wadh ul vidhyl hy o hovv um et pressir sa re vidh en scaelur. Hosen ull re wrodh ey o lom. Ul et bremm ull tunn re wrodhant et sull o, eth nafedh pawast re hadhant misk ul sulter.

Daveth came home on a spar, with the sea running out of his coat onto the floor my grandmother had swept. So she told it me. All that autumn the three told it, and never again set foot in a boat.

Word for word: *Daveth came back home upon a broken mast, and the sea in pouring from his cloak upon the floor that my grandmother had cleaned. Like that she told it to me. In that whole autumn the three told it, and never a second time laid foot in a boat.*

- "a spar" is *mivenn cresket*, "a broken mast" (no spar in the lexicon).
- **A word that works twice.** *re vidh*, "had cleaned" (*midh* softened), sounds as *vidh*, "pour": the sea *pours* (*ul vidhyl*) onto the floor she had *cleaned* (*re vidh*). The lexicon has no "sweep"; Seren takes the clean floor and the pun with it.
- *o hovv*: *covv* "cloak" softened after *o*, so the possessive shows; the lexicon's cloak is the coat.
- *ul et bremm ull tunn*: the article keeps *ul* from bedding *bremm* into *mr-* (§2.5). *nafedh pawast*, "never a second time", is "never again"; *cadh misk*, "lay a foot", is to set foot.

**¶12** · `S07-IV5-15`

> Hy ull re rytent uldath et deldvar lo so nunthath um sulter hastat ul vorr, eth uld sennet ulo. Ul neld, re rytent, re hyl o. Um et scyndal re rytent mest hy gor yalat, eth um sull brod orrowen sa re vysk et uld myst; veth um vodh sull, nath re hemme fedh pa hyont.

After them the outpost men sware in their cups to a boat shoved off burning, with a dead man in it. Well out, they sware, it turned. To the scream they sware above all, and to three words of ours the grey man spake; but which three, no two agreed.

Word for word: *Then the men of the outpost swore at their cups upon a boat pushed off alight, and a dead man in it. Beyond, they swore, it turned. Upon the scream they swore more than upon anything, and upon three Shore words that the grey man said; but upon which three, never did two of them meet.*

- *Re rytent … um*: the oath's own verb and preposition (*Rytom um …*, §3.9); *lo so nunthath* is the lexicon's "in their cups" (O2969). A drunken oath carries no *Ston.*
- *sull brod orrowen*, "three Shore words": *ol mrodath* would be an onset §2.5 forbids, so "of ours" is the Shore's (the v1.3 IV.1's way). *et uld myst*, "the grey man", is the Guest as the coast names him.
- *nath re hemme fedh pa hyont*, "never did two of them meet": *cemm*, "meet; agree" (the foreword's *Nath gemment et trenneth*), in the 3du with *pa hyont*, "two of them".

**The rhyme** (*Sulter Myst*, the Grey Boat) · `S07-IV5-27`

> *Sulter myst, sulter myst, hy et myst,*  
> *re lumm lo yebb et pard hos ryst;*  
> *stinel hosen inth, tolm hosen hebbet,*  
> *eth cemmyl hosen sy, nahunnat!*  
> *Vorra o, hasta o, hebba o dem hyll;*  
> *veth re harm sulter myst, eth re hyl.*

> *knelt at the warden's door one day;*  
> *a stone for a present, a cut for a kiss,*  
> *and who ever heard of a greeting like this?*

> *but the grey boat turned, and the grey boat cried.*

Word for word:

> *Grey boat, grey boat, out of the grey,*  
> *knelt at the warden's door one morning;*  
> *a cut as a kiss, a stone as a gift,*  
> *and a greeting as this, unheard-of!*  
> *Burn it, shove it, give it to the tide;*  
> *but the grey boat cried out, and turned.*

- **The rhyme is the v1.3 unit's (S06)**, for the English is unchanged, **but for line 3's two small words.** v1.3 had *stinel dem ninth, tolm dem hebbet*; read blind, *dem ninth* gave "a stroke to the lip" (*dem* is "until, as far as", and *inth* is "a lip; a kiss"). *hosen*, "as, the same as", cannot point a stroke at a lip: *stinel hosen inth* is a cut given as a kiss, in a kiss's place. **Three times *hosen*.** Line 4 already had it (*cemmyl hosen sy*), so lines 3 and 4 now run *hosen … hosen … hosen sy*, a cut as a kiss, a stone as a gift, a greeting as this: a children's rhyme beats on one word. Its name is the lexicon's *Sulter Myst* (O2424).
- *myst · ryst*: a true rhyme; "one day" is *hos ryst*, "one morning" (the Guest came in the morning, IV.3).
- *hebbet · nahunnat*: a **harmony rhyme**, the suffix *-At* in its slender and its broad shape, the same two letters in the leaf-hand; to get it the halves of line 3 change places and line 4 becomes an exclamation.
- *hyll · re hyl*: "tide" and "turned" differ only by the long *l*; to end on the turning, the last line says "cried out, and turned".
- *Vorra o, hasta o, hebba o*: the plural imperative; Harl's "Burn it" in V.1 is these first two words, *Vorra o*.

### Seren's telling

**¶1** · `S07-V1-04`

Word for word: *Hold the stone, Seren.*

- *Keth et tolm* is the Invocation's command and Halyna's word to the child Seren (§11.11), said to herself; a name called by name softens, and *s* does not.

**¶2** · `S07-V1-05`

> Um et dhysal re hethe {{Halyna}} et mesk, eth re hethym et greller: gemm garl lonta um et meskdhrenn, eth hos lom. Hosen ull re hadhar et tolm hosast; eth hosen ull ser o tul.

Word for word: *Upon the shingle Halyna held the wood, and I held the lamp: four hands at them two upon the grain, and one at me. Like that we laid the first stone; and like that it goes on still.*

- **One verb, two holdings.** *re hethe {{Halyna}} et mesk, eth re hethym et greller*: *keth* is "hold", and of the wood it is "know", so the pair *know* the wood in the same word that Seren *holds* the lamp with. The English says *held* twice; the Orrowen means two things by it.
- *gemm garl lonta …, eth hos lom*: the "at" idiom counts the hands without owning them (*sona* would soften *gemm*): four at them, one at me. Seren is unbonded, and "has one hand" (§11.4).
- "began" is *cadh et tolm hosast*, "lay the first stone" (O2859). "so it hath gone on" is *ser*, "go on, carry forward", the verb a folk etymology hears in *Seren* (§5.8): the work goes on in her name, and she does not say so.
