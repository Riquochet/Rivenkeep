## 1 · SEREN'S FRAME, IN ORROWEN (THE LEAF-HAND, HER ONE INK)

### 1.1 The title

> **Farrol et Aelvaren**

IV.6 · The Voyage of the Aelvaren

Word for word: *voyage the Aelvaren.*

- The lexicon's title (O0571), as the wf8 unit wrote it: *farrol* "a voyage; sailing" is *farr* + *-Ol*; *Aelvaren* is the Mystaeri name written by sound. Names are names. The reading's second line takes up *farrol* again (§2).

### 1.2 The dateline

> **Et syst sullast, ul Hyll Dhumol.**

*The third summer, in the Tide of Remembering.*

Word for word: *the summer third, in(+N) Tide S·Remembering.*

- A naming, with no verb, as a date set at the head of a leaf is; Orrowen lays a verbless clause beside a sentence as I.1 does (*eth so wresketh ul so simmeth*). The ordinal follows its noun, as I.4's *ul et vess sullast* "on the third night" (S02): *et syst sullast*, "the third summer" (*syst*, reserve "summer").
- *Ul Hyll Dhumol*, "in the Tide of Remembering", is IV.4's and IV.2's: *tumol* softened as possessor. The nasalising *ul* leaves *h* as it is.

### 1.3 The headnote

> **Re wemm Halvard et molt sy ul et nelter; re hast Rhyna, um et hald, pa yarl ul et nunth helvnynth, eth re nahebb ey myskyl hy vodh. Um et sellhossan myskym vodh nath noss kethyl lo et mesk: nath re seskent ew naseskyl.**

*Halvard lifted this heart in the vault; Rhyna, on the wall, thrust both hands into the rain-butt, and would not say why. Of the long-lived I say what the wood cannot: They did not know it was ignorance.*

Word for word: *PST S·lift Halvard the heart this in(+N) the vault; PST plunge Rhyna, upon(+S) the wall, two(+S) S·hand in(+N) the rain-butt, and PST refuse she saying from(+S) what. Upon(+S) the long-lived say-1SG what NEG(+N) N·be holding at(+S) the wood: NEG(+N) PST know-3PL was not-knowing.*

- **The two halves of the first sentence answer each other**, as Seren lays them: *re wemm Halvard … ul et nelter; re hast Rhyna … ul et nunth helvnynth*, he lifting (*bemm*, softened after *re*: *b* > *w*) in the vault below, she plunging (*hast*, "push; plunge; shove") on the wall above, and the one small word *ul*, in and into, for both. The leaf never says why her hands went into the water; the reading's sixth line is as near as anyone comes (§2).
- *Rhyna, um et hald*, set off between commas, is the wf8 headnote's *um et hald*, "on the wall". **"Both hands" is the dual itself**, *pa yarl* (orrowen_v2 §3.2's own example): the dual already says "both". *Nunth helvnynth*, "a rain-butt" (O1824), and *re nahebb ey myskyl hy vodh*, "she refused the saying of why", are the wf8 unit's.
- ***sellhossan*, "the long-lived", is the one new word of the leaf** (W04-01): *sell* "long" + *hoss* "breath; live" (a compound, the head softened, which *h* does not show) + the collective *-an*, "the folk of", as *mystan*, *Orrowan*, *Lodhan*. Seren takes over the wood's own word for its people, and in her tongue it is a word that works twice: *hoss* is both breath and life, so "the long-lived" are also "the folk of the long breath", and the breath is the Mystaeri's own name for themselves (*Naelear*, "those of the breath").
- ***Um et sellhossan***, "of the long-lived", is fronted, as the English fronts it: *um* is "upon; about, concerning" (O2661, what a thing is told upon), and Orrowen fronts a phrase for weight as W03's *Ul Hyll Dhumol re hadh …* fronts its time.
- **"What the wood cannot" says what the wood cannot hold.** The English leaves the verb out; the Orrowen fills it with the wood's own verb of knowing, *keth* "hold": *vodh nath noss kethyl lo et mesk*, "what holding is not at the wood" (the "at" idiom for *cannot*, §3.7, in an indirect question after *myskym*, "I say what …": the tongue's relative *sa* needs a head noun, and *vodh* is the "what" of a question, §3.8). Her tongue makes the pun for her, and she keeps it: the wood knows by holding (§3.7's last row), and this it never held, because it is the shore's side (the standing rule: the wood knows only the wood's side).
- **"They did not know it was ignorance"** is *nath re seskent ew naseskyl*: the negative past (*nath re*, G1) of *sesk*, "know a fact", 3pl *-ent* (slender), and *naseskyl*, the lexicon's "ignorance", which is "not-knowing" (*na-* + *sesk* + *-Ol*). So the line turns on one root, *sesk* against *naseskyl*, and the headnote's second sentence on three negations, *nath noss … nath re … na-*. Orrowen has no "that" before a clause (pilot I.1); the colon stands where the English has its own, and the copula needs no subject (*ew naseskyl*, "it was ignorance"), as *El et tolm sa heth*.

## 2 · THE READING, IN ORROWEN (HALYNA, IN THEIR TWO INKS)

*As the Book sets it under `<!-- READING -->`: eight lines, each opening with an em-dash, the two inks alternating line by line and neither named. Halyna speak in the present, in fragments, mostly nouns; the reading "runs easy and breaks only at the fire" (notes_v3 §1.5), and stops at the turn, so that the story fulfils what the reading could not finish.*

*(first ink)*

> **— Lyss. Myst. Lelv um et wadh. Voll, eth lyrness umo.**

*— Small. Grey. Light on the water. A name, and proud of it.*  

Word for word: *— small. grey. light upon(+S) the sea. name, and pride upon-it.*

- *Myst* is "grey" and "the grey" both: the small boat is grey, and of the grey. *Lelv*, "light (not heavy)", comes back unmade in the third line (*nalelv*, heavy).
- **"Proud of it" is the "upon" idiom laid bare**: *Doss lyrness um Y*, "pride is upon Y" (O0487), with the verb dropped, as a fragment drops it: *lyrness umo*, "pride upon it".

*(second ink)*

> **— Garl um et crynireth, ul et farrol tunn. Venth. Tovvol.**

*— A hand on the boards, all the way over. Shallows. Waiting.*  

Word for word: *— hand upon(+S) the planks, in(+N) the voyage whole. shallow-water. waiting.*

- *Crynir* is "a plank; a board" (reserve), plural *crynireth* (slender). **"All the way over" is *ul et farrol tunn*, "in the whole voyage"**: the leaf's own title-word, which Halyna do not know they are saying.
- *Venth*, "shallow water"; *tovvol*, the verbal noun of *tovv* "wait".

*(first ink)*

> **— Pa sestow visk um et tolmath. Driget ulo. Nalelv —**

*— Heels on the stones. Thrown in. Heavy —*  

Word for word: *— two(+S) back S·foot upon(+S) the stones. thrown in-it. heavy —*

- ***Sestow visk*, "a heel", is "the back of the foot"** (W04-02, a set phrase: *sestow* "the back; the stern" + *misk* "foot" softened as possessor), and **"heels" is its dual**, *pa sestow visk*, a body's pair, as *pa yarl* is.
- *Driget*, "thrown", is the participle of *drig* "throw; fling"; *ulo*, "into it", is *ul* with the person ending (the boat is "it" to the readers: this reading never slips into the wood's "our", as IV.4's does). *Nalelv*, "heavy", is *lelv* unmade (*na-*, "un-light", O1557): the first line's lightness turned, and the thought broken across both inks.

*(second ink)*

> **— eth dhaedh. Ul et crynireth. O dhunnoth.**

*— and warm. Into the boards. All of him.*  

Word for word: *— and warm. in(+N) the planks. his S·wholeness.*

- The other ink finishes the first ink's thought (*Nalelv — / — eth dhaedh*), as the reading's form asks.
- **"All of him" is *o dhunnoth*, "his wholeness"** (*tunnoth*, softened after *o*): the word of the Covenant of Completion (*Ryt Dhunnoth*, "the vow of wholeness"). The whole of him came into the boards; the rite that was to join him to the shore was never made whole.

*(first ink)*

> **— Lomm. Um sy doss brodath lona.**

*— Grief. For this we two have words.*  

Word for word: *— grief. upon(+S) this be words at-1DU.*

- **"We two have words for this" is the "at" idiom in the dual**: *Doss X lo Y*, "X is at Y" (§3.7), with *lona*, "at us two". Halyna speak of themselves in the dual, as the formal register requires (§3.9).
- ***Um sy*, "upon this", is fronted, as the English fronts *For this***, so that *this* can only be the grief just named (*sy*, "this", standing alone, O2450). *Um* is the same "upon" as the first line's pride: words laid upon grief, as a feeling is laid upon a person. *(Back-translation fix: the line was* Doss brodath lona umo*, and* umo *is "upon him" as readily as "upon it" (orrowen_v2 §3.6, 3sg m); the blind reader took the Guest, "we two have words about him".)*

*(second ink)*

> **— Vorr. Garlath ul vorrol — nath vorrant. Crynireth. Et crynireth ul vorrol.**

*— Fire. Hands burning — no. Boards. The boards burning.*  

Word for word: *— fire. hands in(+N) burning — NEG(+N) burn-3PL. planks. the planks in(+N) burning.*

- **Orrowen has no word for "no"**: a denial repeats the verb in the negative (orrowen_v2 §3.4). So the wrong image corrected is *Garlath ul vorrol — nath vorrant*, "hands burning — they burn not". It is the one place the reading breaks, as notes_v3 says it should: the readers feel hands burning before they find the boards, and the headnote has already put Rhyna's two hands into the rain-butt.
- *Ul vorrol*, "in burning", is the progressive with its *doss* left out (§3.4).

*(first ink)*

> **— Gannath. Dem neld, dem et lunn, hy —**

*— Poles. Out, to the open, away from —*  

Word for word: *— posts. until(+N) N·far, until(+N) the open, from(+S) —*

- *Gann*, "a post; a pole" (canon), plural *gannath*: the shore's boat-poles carry the same word as the wood's black pillars (*Gannath Myst*, the Mystholders).
- *Dem neld*, "away, far off"; ***dem et lunn*, "to the open"**: *lunn* is "open; room, space" and also "freedom" (the Title's *lunnath*), so the burning boat is shoved out to the open and to its freedom in one word. *Hy —*, "from —", breaks before the word that would end it (*varn*, home).

*(second ink)*

> **— Kylyl. Ul lern et — [ ]**

*— Turning. Against the — [ ]*

Word for word: *— turning. in(+N) face the — [ ]*

- *Kylyl*, "a turning", is the lexicon's *Et Kylyl*, the Turn, the subtitle of Book Six. *Ul lern* is "against, in the face of" (O2631); the line breaks before *kess*, the current, and the gap is marked, not mended.
