## 1 · OF THIS BOOK (*FLENN SEREN*)

### Title

> ## *Flenn Seren*

**OF THIS BOOK**

- *The leaf of Seren*: the lexicon's title for the foreword (O0616). *(reused)*

### Dateline

> *Et syst sullast, ul Hyll Dhumol.*

*The third summer, in the Tide of Remembering.*

- *syst sullast*, "the third summer": the ordinal after the noun, as *et vess hosast*. *Hyll Dhumol* is the lexicon's name for the third Tide (O1046), *ul* before an *h* showing no bite.

### Seren's headnote

> *Re ryt Seren Pa Luth o, ryterd et flennath sy, eth sullast odh {{Halyna}}.*

*By Seren Two-Inks, scribe of this Book, and the third of the hearth of {{Halyna}}.*

Word for word: *Seren Two-Inks wrote it, scribe of this Book, and the third of the hearth of Halyna.*

- *Re ryt Seren Pa Luth o*: the old foreword's active headnote, kept (*ryt*, "cut letters, write": a foreword is written, not laid). *ryterd et flennath sy* is the old I.2 headnote's "scribe of this Book".
- *sullast odh {{Halyna}}*, "the third of the hearth of Halyna": a construct chain, head without article, the pair-name definite at its end. *sullast* is the canon's word for the unbonded third of a hearth (O2421).

### Seren's foreword

> Doss domm nasell et surr. Aedh et hellur um gor lern lom, eth re nadhald Odh Sell dem gesketh. Um et hald veskym et movenn ul memmyl, ul helvhoss sa nath hunnom.

It is the short dark of the year. The hall sleepeth about me, and the Long Hearth is sunk to coals. On the wall I see the banner lift, in a wind I hear not.

Word for word: *There is the short dark of the year. The hall sleeps round about me, and the Long Hearth has sunk down to embers. Upon the wall I see the banner in lifting, in a wind that I do not hear.*

- *Doss domm nasell et surr*: a construct with no article on its head, "the short dark of the year" (*nasell* "short", 'un-long'). The copula would make it *El …*, "the year is a short dark", so the state verb says that it is so.
- *re nadhald Odh Sell dem gesketh*, "the Long Hearth has sunk down to embers": *nadhald*, "sink down" (the un-rising of *tald*), and *kesk*, "an ember", nasalised after *dem*. Not *ul brentheth*: *ul* would bed *brentheth* into *mr-*, which §2.5 forbids.
- *ul memmyl*, "in lifting" (N·*bemmyl*): the progressive of §3.4. *sa nath hunnom*, "that I do not hear": the relative with its gap.

> Doss saed et flennath loskat. Hos hy o wrodhardath, sa re wemm nind thenth um gor helvhoss, doss o ul vimm et stelleth rerddhalden tevar. Vess sy re vymmym dem et flenn hosast. Re luskom et flenn pondul, hy vodh nel hosk ul lern ganna.

The Book is half full. One of its tellers, that held up a wet finger to every wind, is under the east cairns already. Tonight have I turned back to the first leaf. I left it bare, for the lintel is not cut ere the posts are raised.

- *Merge (wf12), H25:* "under the east cairns" is *ul vimm et stelleth rerddhalden*, as V.5 and VI.3 say it (S08, S10). (The unit had *doss o ul vimm stelleth rerddhald tevar*.)

Word for word: *Half of the Book is filled. One of its tellers, who lifted a wet finger upon every wind, he is under the cairns of the east already. Tonight I returned to the first leaf. I left the leaf blank, for there is no lintel before the gate.*

- *Hos hy o wrodhardath*, "one of its tellers": *o* softens *brodhardath*, so the possessive shows (*brodhard*, "a teller", O0179). *sa re wemm nind thenth*, "who lifted a wet finger": *bemm* softened is *wemm*. The dead teller is not named; the hearth knows him by his finger, as the Book wants.
- *stelleth rerddhald*, "the cairns of the east" (*stelleth*, the burial-field plural; *rerddhald*, 'sun-rise').
- *et flenn hosast* is also the lexicon's word for *a foreword* (O0615): she turns back to the first leaf, and the first leaf is this one.
- **Seren's saying**, verbless as the tongue's sayings are: ***nel hosk ul lern ganna***, "no lintel before the gate". *ganna* is a gate and *the two posts* at once, and *hosk* is a lintel and the Title: the leaf at the head of a Book is its lintel, and it is laid last. *re luskom et flenn pondul* is the old foreword's "I left the leaf blank" (the noun, not *o*, which would ask for *vondul*).

> Lo et odh sy re hadhant galdardath hosast et Kethow trenneth. Amm re orrant hy et Kethow, re wrident et trenneth lo et odh. Amm sy resker ul vodh re reskent, amm doss nayalat tul lo et garlath veth so hethyl lo et vorr.

At this hearth the first builders of the Keep laid their tales. When the Keep was left, the tales went cold with the hearth. Now sit we where they sat, when naught is left to do with the hands but hold them to the fire.

Word for word: *At this hearth the first builders of the Keep laid tales. When they went from the Keep, the tales grew cold at the hearth. Now we sit where they sat, when there is nothing still at the hands but their holding at the fire.*

- *galdardath hosast et Kethow*, "the first builders of the Keep" (Tier 3 §5.2, one form in every unit). *trenneth* without *so*: after *et Kethow*, *so dhrenneth* is read as the possessor of *Kethow* ("the Keep of their tales"), which the analyzer reports as the Last Carver's error.
- *re wrident et trenneth lo et odh*: *brid*, "cold; cool", softened; *lo*, "at, by, with": the tales went cold with the hearth and at it.
- *amm doss nayalat tul lo et garlath veth so hethyl lo et vorr* is the old foreword's clause, kept: "nothing still at the hands but their holding at the fire" (*nath … veth* has its positive twin in *nayalat … veth*). *(reused)*

> Lo en sest, ul o valtur, doss et tolm, hosk hemm et ganna ulvenn. Um o lern re sol o ul et ganna, eth et Stonwryt ul lern et trun, dem hedh orl et movenn, amm re hyle {{Halyna}} o. Nath gyl hos o; kyle pana o. Doss Stonwryt et tolm gand tul.

Beside me in its niche is the stone, the old capstone of the inner gate. Face down it lay in the gateway, with its vow to the earth, till {{Halyna}} turned it at dusk on the day of the banner. It is a stone that two must turn. Its vow is sharp yet.

Word for word: *At my shoulder, in its niche, is the stone, the old capstone of the inner gate. Upon its face it lay in the gateway, and the Stonwryt facing the ground, until the dusk of the day of the banner, when Halyna turned it. One does not turn it; both turn it. The stone's Stonwryt is sharp still.*

- *hosk hemm et ganna ulvenn*, Tier 3 §5.2's one form. *ul o valtur* (*paltur*, "a niche", softened after *o*).
- *et Stonwryt ul lern et trun*, "the vow facing the ground": *trun* is the Cry's ground, the one the mortar cries out of (*Carm et lodh hy et trun*). The article, not *o Stonwryt*: before an *s* the possessive cannot show.
- *dem hedh orl et movenn, amm re hyle {{Halyna}} o*: "till … when" is *dem* + a time + *amm*, because *dem amm* would nasalise to the reserve word *mamm*. *orl et movenn*, "the day of the banner", is an addition (S01-05): the Book's clock turns on it.
- ***Nath gyl hos o; kyle pana o.*** "One does not turn it; both turn it": *nath* beds *kyl* into *gyl*, and the dual answers the singular. *pana*, "both, the two", is the word of the shared saying (*grest um vana*). It is Seren's way of saying *a stone that two must turn* without a word for *must*.

**The Stonwryt on the tale-stone's underside** (the `legend_stonwryt_hal` surface; the caption, *The Stonwryt of the first builders, on the tale-stone's underside, in their old hand, the Hal*, and the fold are the reader's furniture, in English). It is drawn as the first builders cut it, in the Hal and in *Garl Hal*, right to left, mirrored, one unbroken course, and signed **ALD | BEN** under one lintel: `orrowen_v2` §11.6 (a), canon, unchanged.

> **ESAN STONOS ETAS XALDOS SIU · RE CADHANA OA ETHAS TESSANA OA SOŊMAS ELIS TOLMOS TOLMOS · STONOS** ⟨gate⟩ ⟨**ALD | BEN** under one lintel⟩

*This wall will stand. We two laid it, and we two answer for it, while stone is stone. It stands.* It is signed under one lintel with the pair-name of {{Aldwena}}, who laid the capstone.

As {{Halyna}} say it in the living tongue (§11.6 (b), canon; the fold's reading in Seren's ink, if the Original gives one):

> *Es ston et hald sy. Re hadhan o, eth tessen o, somm el tolm tolm. Ston.*

- Canon, unchanged; the Hal line passes the analyzer's Hal reading (`--hal`) word for word. As in the old unit, the living-tongue line stands under the Hal's English fold and takes none of its own. *(reused)*

> Hy ull rytym gor yanna lodhat ul vimm hos hosk, hosen hosk um yanna. El hos voll o, eth voll vana.

So write I every bonded pair, under one stroke, as a lintel on two posts. It is one name, and it is the name of two.

Word for word: *So I write every bonded pair under one lintel, as a lintel upon a gate. It is one name, and the name of both.*

- ***gor yanna lodhat***, "every bonded pair": *ganna lodhat* is the lexicon's "a bonded pair ('a bonded gate': a pair is a gate)", softened after *gor*. So the simile in Orrowen is no simile: the stroke over a pair-name **is** called a lintel (`orrowen_v2` §6.8, the sealing lintel), and a pair **is** a gate. *hosen hosk um yanna*, "as a lintel upon a gate", is the word set beside itself.
- ***El hos voll o, eth voll vana***, "it is one name, and the name of both": *vana* is *pana* softened as a possessor, the last word of the old masters' saying *Grest um hos, grest um vana*. A hearer hears the saying in the name.

> El Halvard Tolmvard eth Rhyna Yanna Ulvenn {{Halyna}}. Re ammada {{Idrenna}}, et hemm sost, sona, eth ferre lo et gebb amm cadhat trenn venn. Nath memme {{Orvenna}}, prassyanna et hald, sona harm fedh. Hy {{Enrella}}, et tevel sost, amm cadh hos tolm hosast sodh, cadh ullen et hosk umo.

{{Halyna}} are Halvard Stone-Warden and Rhyna of the Inner Gate. {{Idrenna}}, the eldest, taught them, and nod by the door when a tale is laid true. {{Orvenna}}, our wall-masters, never raise their voices. Of {{Enrella}}, the youngest, the one cannot begin a jest but the other will end it.

Word for word: *Halvard Stone-Warden and Rhyna of the Inner Gate are Halyna. Idrenna, the very old, taught them two, and they two bow at the door when a tale is laid true. Orvenna, the master-pair of the wall, do not ever lift their two voices. Of Enrella, the very young: when one lays the first stone of a game, the other lays the lintel upon it.*

- The copula puts the predicate first: *El Halvard Tolmvard eth Rhyna Yanna Ulvenn {{Halyna}}*. The epithets are the canon's (Tier 3 §5.3).
- Four pairs, four dual verbs (*ammada, ferre, memme*; and *cadh … cadh* for Enrella, one of them and the other). *et hemm sost* / *et tevel sost*, "the eldest" / "the youngest": *sost* makes the most, as in the old foreword's *brodath merr sost*.
- *amm cadhat trenn venn*, "when a tale is laid true": *venn*, true and plumb, the mason's word for a true course.
- *{{Orvenna}}, prassyanna et hald*: *prassyanna*, "a master-pair" (O1979), of the wall (addition S01-06). Not *ol brassyanna*: "our" would bed the *p*, and the wall is the Keep's own.
- **Enrella's jest is laid as a gate is**: *cadh … tolm hosast*, "lay the first stone", and *cadh … et hosk um*, "lay the lintel upon", are the Book's two words for beginning and ending a line two voices share (Tier 3 §5.2 no. 3). *sodh*, "play; a game", is the jest. *(new)*

> Amm doss et trenn tunnat, vollar o: nel brodhat, veth *cadhat*. Trenn sa nath gadhar veth brodhar, doss o sennet dem et ryst. Trenn sa hadhar, el saed et hald o.

When a tale is done we say not that it was told, but *laid*. A tale that is but told is gone by morning. A tale that is laid is part of the wall.

Word for word: *When the tale is finished, we name it: not told, but laid. A tale that we do not lay but only tell, it is gone out by the morning. A tale that we lay, it is part of the wall.*

- *vollar o: nel brodhat, veth cadhat*: a name given is set off by a wedge (old foreword). *nath mysker*, "we say not", is avoided: after *nath*, *mysk* is heard as N·*bysk*, "tie".
- The second and third sentences are the old foreword's, kept: *trenn sa nath gadhar veth brodhar* (*nath … veth*, "only") and *sennet*, gone out as a fire goes out in peace. *(reused)*

> Tess hos brod gor dhrenn lor, eth nath dess ullen fedh: *Tumar.*

Two words answer every tale of ours, and never other two: *We remember.*

Word for word: *One word answers every tale of ours, and another does not ever answer: We remember.*

- **One word, not two.** *Tumar* is one word: the Orrowen says *hos brod*, "one word", where Seren's English, translating it, has to say "two words". The English and the original are each true of their own tongue; this is the one place in the unit where the numbers differ on purpose. **[Jack]** if the original should count as the English does, *pa wrod … pa wrod ullen* goes in and nothing else moves.
- *gor dhrenn lor*, "every tale of ours": the "at" idiom (§3.7), as the old foreword's *Nath noss gor dhrenn … lor*.

> Ul et vess hosast sa re vess et odh sy trenn, nath re yal brod lor. Re vysk Ebba hemm Sedhnell o ul velvyth. Re vess nahos o loy, eth re vysk nahos lor: Fenna. Veth re rik et odh et brod hyoy. Virr gor vess doss ey hosast tul, hos hoss ul lern et ullen, eth mellorar ul sestow Ebba, hosen hyll ul sestow et fodh hosast.

The first night this hearth asked a tale, none of us wist what to say. Old Ebba of Fenholm spake them into the quiet. None bade her, and none bade us follow. But the hearth took the words from her. Most nights she is first yet, a breath before the rest, and in behind her we come, as a tide behind its first wave.

Word for word: *On the first night that this hearth asked a tale, there was no word at us. Old Ebba of Fenholm said it into the silence. No one asked it of her, and no one said to us: Follow. But the hearth took the word from her. Nearly every night she is first still, one breath in front of the others, and we come in behind Ebba, as a tide behind the first wave.*

- *ul et vess hosast sa re vess et odh sy trenn* is pilot I.1's headnote clause, word for word: *vess*, night, and *re vess*, asked (S·*pess*), sound alike, and Seren lets the hearth hear it twice more in two lines (*Re vess nahos o loy*, "no one asked it of her").
- *nath re yal brod lor*, "there was no word at us": "none of us wist what to say" in the "at" idiom. *Re vysk Ebba … o ul velvyth*: the *o* is the one word (*Tumar*), and *velvyth*, silence, is the word Seren will write down after every wood telling.
- *Ebba hemm Sedhnell*, "old Ebba of Fenholm" (*Sedhnell*, the canon's Fenholm, *s* unchanged as possessor). *Ebba* is an addition (S01-04), a name. *hemm* after a name, not *Hemma*, which is the Elderess's title.
- *eth re vysk nahos lor: Fenna* is pilot I.1's "he had not told us to follow", turned to *no one*. *Veth re rik et odh et brod hyoy*: *grik*, "take", softened to nothing before *r*.
- *hosen hyll ul sestow et fodh hosast*, "as a tide behind the first wave": the article, because *o fodh* ("its wave") would show no bite before *f*.

> Re rarr nadhumol um ol nedheth, eth re naelur nahos o. Naelurar ol dumol, hosen naelurant tolmardath et tolmath dem gethyl.

Forgetting came on our fathers, and none chose it. Remembering we choose, as masons choose which stones to keep.

Word for word: *Forgetting came upon our fathers, and none chose it. We choose our remembering, as masons choose the stones for keeping.*

- A forgetting is laid *upon* someone, as a feeling or a burden is (§3.7): *re rarr nadhumol um ol nedheth*. *Naelur* is "choose" (O2980's saying).
- ***Naelurar ol dumol***, "we choose our remembering": *ol* beds *tumol* into ***dumol***, the Title's own second word in the Title's own form (*Ul dumol ol varn …*). What the fathers let fall the hearth picks up in the Captain's word. *(new)*
- ***et tolmath dem gethyl***, "the stones for keeping" (*dem* + N·*kethyl*, as F-27's *dem hunnol*, "for hearing"): *keth*, keep, hold, and know the wood, in one, so the sentence closes on its two halves, *dumol* and *gethyl*, remembering and keeping. *Back-translation fix*: the draft's *et tolmath sa hethent* was heard "the stones that hold" (the relative's gap falls on the subject, as the grammar's own *et tolm sa heth*, "the stone that holds"), and the masons chose sound stones, not which to keep.

> Doss merr hy et trenneth sy lo et mesk, eth et saed strom hy et kaelirvesk. Nadhumant et scaevureleth, eth doss cadhol nycul ulont lo yarl. Nath noss nyculol lo et mesk, veth nath noss veskyl ludal lo.

Some tales are the wood's, most out of the wreck-wood. Our rolls forget, and can be made to lie. The wood cannot lie, but it cannot see fear.

> Cadhom et saedeleth hosen ommant, ul ba luth. Nath mavere sona en rellorol cedh el cedh. Nath noss seskyl los. Galdom et trenn hy et saedeleth. Ul sestow et trenn el velvyth et odh, eth re rytym et velvyth ull.

Where the wood's leaf could not yet be told, I have left it bare, with only the grain showing: the shape of what we understood not yet.

Word for word: *Every night I lay the tale with the speech of the Shore, as a mason lays a course, every joint broken upon the joint below. - *gor wrellir cresket um wrellir ul vimm*, "every joint broken upon the joint below": *brellir*, "a joint between stones", softened after *gor* and *um*. It is also the leaf-hand's own rule, *breaking the joint* (§6.8).

- *eth nath re vess brodhard tul o wrodath sost pawast*: *nath … tul*, "not yet"; *re vess*, asked, the night-word again.

> Virr gor vess doss et brentheth sennet dem vorrdhuss, eth doss et brod dasken tul um en serr. El hosen ull, vess sy.

Most nights the coals are gone to ash, and the last word is yet in my mouth. So it is tonight.

Word for word: *Nearly every night the coals are gone out to ash, and the last word is still upon my tongue. It is like that, tonight.*

- *doss et brentheth sennet dem vorrdhuss*: *senn*, gone out as a fire in peace, as the told tale goes out by morning (F-11). *um en serr*, "upon my tongue" (*serr*, the tongue of the mouth): *ul en brodow*, "in my mouth", would bed *brodow* into *mr-*.
- ***El hosen ull, vess sy***: Kael's *Ew hosen ull*, "It was like that", turned to the present. *(new)*

> Vallom brodhen Orrow.

I love our tongue.

Word for word: *I love the speech of the Shore.*

- *vall*, "love" (O2684). "Our tongue" cannot be *ol brodhen* (N + *br-* gives *mr-*), so Seren names it by its full name, *brodhen Orrow*, as the guild does in formal speech (O0182): three words for her four.

Up the mountain I carried two things, our tongue and a chest; and only the chest was heavy.

Word for word: *I carried two things up the mountain, the speech of the Shore and a chest; and nothing was heavy but the chest.*

- *Re lymmym … dem susk et brunn* is the Book's one form for the chest carried up the mountain (Tier 3 §5.2). *nath re yal nalelv veth et vestul*: "only" of a clause is *nath … veth* (pilot I.1, G8); *nalelv*, "heavy", 'un-light' (O1557).

> El luth et flennath sy. Mavere {{Halyna}} o rytyl ul gyvil et hellur sy, trenn um dhrenn, amm mellor sollan. Dem et fedh ull, sol o ul et vestul ull, ul molt et tolmath nawrodh.

### 5.3 Collisions stepped around, and misreadings recast

- **N + *br-* gives *mr-*** (§2.5): no *ul brentheth* (*dem gesketh* instead), no *ol brodhen* ("our tongue": *brodhen Orrow*, *lo wrodhen Orrow*), no *ul en brodow* ("in my mouth": *um en serr*, "upon my tongue"), no *nath brodh* (*brodh o nayalat*).
- ***nath mysk*** reads N·*bysk* "tie": "we say not that it was told" is *vollar o: nel brodhat, veth cadhat*.
- ***dem amm*** nasalises to the reserve word *mamm*: "till Halyna turned it" is *dem hedh orl et movenn, amm re hyle {{Halyna}} o*.
- **A possessive that cannot show** (§3.5, G9) is said with the article or a noun: *et Stonwryt ul lern et trun*, *Stonwryt et tolm*, *et fodh hosast*, *et odha*, *et scaevureleth*; and no *o* before a word it would wrongly bite (*re luskom et flenn pondul*, not *o pondul*; *re rik brenth*, not *re rik o brenth*; *doss o ul vimm … tevar*, not *doss o tevar*).
- ***et Kethow so dhrenneth*** is read as a construct with an article on its head (the Last Carver's error): *galdardath hosast et Kethow trenneth*.
- **Analyzer readings that were not the meaning**, found by `--gloss` and recast: *hy wemmeth myst* (read S·*bemmeth*: *dem wemmeth myst*), *garl hethyl* (read *heth* "spring": *garl sa heth*), *re vemm* (S·*memm* "friend": *re wemm*, S·*bemm*), *veskyl* after *lo* (read S·*meskyl*: *veskyl ludal lo*, the *lo* last).
- **What the blind back-translation heard otherwise, and the recast** (§BACK-TRANSLATION): a relative's gap falls on the subject (*et tolmath sa hethent* is "the stones that hold", as the grammar's *et tolm sa heth*): "which stones to keep" is *et tolmath dem gethyl*; an ordinal after a noun is its adjective (*Et vennuldath hosast* is "the first True Men"): "First the True Men" is *Hosast re dhessent et vennuldath*; *dem* before a verbal noun is "until" before it is "for" (*dem sennyl* was "until death"): "five things a man might die for" is *lesk sa senner demont*; *merr* with no partitive is a claim about the owner ("the wood has few tales"): *merr hy et trenneth sy*; *ryt … voll hyo* is two acts: *navoll*.
- ***hethow*** (S·*kethow*) reads *heth* + *-ow*: *ul Gethow Dhresk*, *dem gethow*, *ol gethowath*; never *gor hethow*.
- ***en misketh*** is also N·*bisketh* "my heads": *ul vimm misk*, "underfoot".
- ***re vysk*** is "said" and "bound": the half-cloak is bound in the passive, *Re yal et saed ull bysket*.
- **Vowel-initial names** take *n-* or *m-* after a nasalising word (*ul, dem, nath, es, en, ol, va*), which would put *nEbba*, *nIdrenna*, *mOrvenna*, *nEnrella* on the page: here every one stands where nothing nasalises it (after a verb, after *hy*, *lo*, *ul sestow*, or in apposition).
