**Notes on the Orrowen.**

- **✱1 *vodh* used as "what" (a relative).** The grammar gives *vodh* only as a question word ("what?", §3.8); relatives are made with *sa*. I read *myskym vodh …* as an indirect question: "I tell what the wood cannot hold". The alternative is a direct question: "Of the long-lived I say: what can the wood not hold?" *Nath noss* + verbal noun + *lo* Y is §3.7's "Y cannot V"; *kethyl* also means "(of the wood) a knowing". So "what the wood cannot know" is equally possible. "I" is unnamed: the narrator, presumably Seren.
- **✱2 *nath re seskent ew naseskyl*.** The subject of *seskent* (3pl, "they") is not said. I take it as the long-lived of the clause before. *Ew naseskyl* ("[it] was not-knowing") has no word joining it to *seskent*, so the bond is mine:
  - read as "they did not know [that] it was ignorance";
  - or as two clauses: "they did not know; it was not-knowing".

  What "it" is cannot be read from the line. In the grain, the one thing the long-lived "did not know" is why the host drew back (ring 19, runner v9), so "it" is probably that.
- **✱3 *Voll*.** It may be the noun ("a name") or the bare imperative ("Name!"). I read the noun. *Umo* is "upon him / upon it". The idiom *Doss X um Y* means "Y feels X", so the phrase is "it is proud". I take "it" as the boat, because the grain's ring 2 is NAME+GLAD, "proud of the name".
- **✱4.**
  - *Pa sestow visk* is "both heels" (W04.tsv). "On the stones": *um* can also mean "over, across", so the heels may be drawn over the stonework.
  - *Driget ulo* is "thrown in him / in it". I read it as the hull. The grain (ring 4) cuts a single foot, `US:foot`, with no count; the Orrowen has two heels.
  - *Nalelv* ("heavy") has no counterpart in the grain at this point.
- **✱5 *O dhunnoth*.** The mutation shows (t → dh), so *o* is the possessive: "his wholeness" or "its wholeness". "All of him" is my rendering. "The whole of it" is equally possible. Compare the grain's ring 6, `HOLD{all}`.
- **✱6 *Doss brodath lona umo*.** Literally "there are words at us-two, upon him/it". *Doss X lo Y* means "Y has X", and *um* means "about, concerning". So: "we two have words about him", or "we two have words for it".
  - The dual "we two" is the Bonded's register (§3.9), so the speakers are probably the pair: Halvard and Rhyna, Halyna.
  - Who "he" is cannot be read: the Guest, or the hull.
- **✱7.**
  - *Gannath* is "posts; a palisade". Here they may be the poles of the grain's ring 8, but the lexicon gives no "poles" sense.
  - *Dem neld* is "away, far off". *Dem et lunn* is "to the freedom / to the open room": I read it as "the open".
  - *Hy —* ("from —") is broken off. Its object is not given.
- **✱8.** *Ul lern* is "against; in the face of; ahead". The noun after *et* is missing: the line breaks off.
  - **`[ ]` cannot be read.** It is an empty bracket with nothing in it. No rule in the grammar or in §6.7 (punctuation) tells me whether it marks a gap in the source, a word left out, or silence.
- **The voices.** The dashed lines (04–11) have no speaker. They read as short spoken fragments.
  - In order, they follow the grain's rings 2–10. This suggests someone is reading the heart aloud and breaks off at ring 10 ("into the wind / against the current"):
    - *lyss / myst / lelv um et wadh / voll, lyrness* ~ ring 2;
    - *garl um et crynireth … farrol tunn / venth / tovvol* ~ rings 2–3;
    - *sestow visk / driget ulo* ~ ring 4;
    - *ul et crynireth / o dhunnoth* ~ rings 5–6;

    - *vorr … crynireth ul vorrol* ~ rings 7–8;

## B · The grain, line by line

The header reads: `round IV-6-aelvaren {wood: life; pith: war; rings: 23; rules after: [3, 15]; cells: [1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4 …]}`.

- **The kind.** The seal stands in the bark (`bark 0: HOLD`), so this is a telling (A10).
- **Wood:** a shore-man's life: the wood of a Remembering hull (A13). Compare the Orrowen dateline, "in the Tide of Remembering".
- **Pith:** square, the war-pith.
- **Rings and bands:** 23 rings, with band-rules after rings 3 and 15. So the movements are:
  - **I**, rings 1–3;
  - **II**, rings 4–15;
  - **III**, rings 16–23.
- **Cells per slot:** 1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, then 4.

**Files** (§11.2):
- The even files are bearings: 0 is ahead (the shore), 4 the right hand, 8 behind (home), and 12 the left hand, which is the teller, "we", by custom. The diagonals are 2, 6, 10 and 14.
- The odd files are persons and things.
- How each file's referent was introduced is said ring by ring below.

**Bark.** `bark 0: HOLD` is the seal. It opens the telling with "This is held in the grain" and closes it with "…and the grain holds it still."

## C · The two together, in plain English

**The Voyage of the Aelvaren.** The third summer, in the Tide of Remembering.

Halvard lifted this heart in the vault. Rhyna, on the wall, plunged both hands into the rain-butt, and would not say why. Of the long-lived I tell what the wood cannot hold: they did not know it was ignorance.

— Small. Grey. Light on the sea. A name, and pride in it.
— A hand on the planks, the whole voyage. Shallows. Waiting.
— Both heels on the stones. Thrown into it. Heavy —
— and warm. In the planks. All of him.
— Grief. We two have words about him.
— Fire. Hands in the burning, and they do not burn. Planks. The planks are burning.
— Posts. Away, out into the open, from —
— Turning. Against the — [ ]

And no one answered.

*The grain:*

This is held in the grain, and held in every hull of our kind. The hull that brings it to the shore was not there; it knows it because it grew from the Aelvaren.

We are the Aelvaren, proud of our name: a small hull of the old wood, grey, light on the water. We bore Aelrhen across the thin grey to the shore, and all the way his hand lay on our wood.

Aelrhen went up to the door. All that morning we lay in the shallows below it and waited, to bear him home with the Aelthar made whole. The tide came in under us, and turned; and he came not.

The men of the house took him by the foot, and threw him into us, as one casts away a torn net.

That same morning his hand had lain light on our wood; now his blood ran into it. And our wood held all of it.

They set fire to us. The old wood raged in its grief and burned higher than any fire of theirs. We screamed, for wood can scream.

They shoved us off with poles. He lay in us, and was cold. The tide took us and sent us out into the open sea, and we did not go home. The fire was in all our wood.

We turned.

The tide took us as we went burning; but we went, burning, into the wind, and broke its hold. Even charred, and dying, the wood still knew the road home.

Two hulls of the shore-men lay across our way in the thin grey. Heavy they rode, with our kin felled by the iron and stacked in their hulls.

The iron came into us. We went in under them, burning, low, into the iron, where they were heaviest.

Our kin in their hulls took the fire; both hulls broke, and both burned.

Of the shore-men in them, where they went, the wood does not hold.

No keel of the shore has ever gone by the hidden road, among the roots, on the water. We found it where the grey was thinnest; our keel knew its way. The fire went out of us slowly as the grey closed over, the one and then the other, until at the last the grey closed. Then there was only the keel, a few ribs, and Aelrhen's bones among them.

In the night we came up, black and small, onto the pale sand, and ran aground beneath the silverbarks, and were still. The elders came to us. They knew us, for they had sent us.

They laid their hands together on the keel and knew what it held: *a gift; a shore-man's foot;* and *a shore-man's neck taken gently; the blade; blows.*

Wood hides nothing.

Their holiest peace they had sent, and the best they had; and the shore answered it with murder, and with fire. The host drew back from the blade; why, the wood does not hold. The elders looked upon that answer and knew it for a killing, truly. They did not know why the host drew back. And they did not ask.

They saw in it only war.

They laid the keel down among the roots of the council-grove, with Aelrhen's bones in its ribs. The roots drank it.

The knowing went through the roots to every grove, to the far edge of the world, and every young tree that was to be a hull drank it with the rain. So every hull that goes to your wall carries our grief.

…and the grain holds it still.
