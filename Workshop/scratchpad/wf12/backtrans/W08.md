## Part 1 · The Orrowen leaf (`W08.txt`)

**W08-VI2-01** · *Reskowath strom sa orrant um wadh*
- Gloss: place-PL great · REL(+S) · go-3PL · upon(+S) · sea. *reskow strom* is "a throne (a great seat)".
- **English: The Thrones That Walk the Sea** (literally "great seats that go upon the sea").
- *Note:* The lexicon has this exact phrase as a set title. Its entry labels it III.2. Here it heads VI.2.

**W08-VI2-02** · *Lo dhrenn et surr vranast, cumm eth cumm.*
- Gloss: *lo dhrenn* "along, by way of, through" (*trenn* softened after *lo*) · the year sixth · haven and haven.
- **English: Through the sixth year, haven after haven.**
- *Note:* "Haven after haven" is my reading of *cumm eth cumm* ("haven and haven"). The grammar gives no distributive idiom. *trenn* also means "a tale, a legend; a line of writing", so *lo dhrenn* may also be heard as "by the tale of". I give the lexicon's set sense.

**W08-VI2-03** · *Lo gor reskow strom ommat, re vellor hos hy Halyna ul molt et mesketh, eth re ston ullen lo hless, lo sest et hesperdeth, sa re nahebbent orrol virr; eth re heth et hos hyo o hosen re heth et hos ulo. Doss molt reskow strom kethet ul hos hoss, eth et voll dasken, eth duss sost.*
- Gloss:
  - at every throne fallen · PST came-in (*mellor*, S) one from Halyna · in heart-of the trees (*ul molt* "amid, in the heart of") ·
  - and PST stood the-other at (S) bank (*cless*, S) · at shoulder-of the soldiers (*lo sest* "beside") · who PST refused-3PL going near;
  - and PST held (*keth*, S) the one from-him/it · it/him (*o*) · as (*hosen*) · PST held the one in-him/it.
  - Is heart-of throne · held (*kethet*) · in one breath (*ul hos hoss* "at once") · and the name final · and hard self/very.
- **English: At every fallen throne, one of Halyna went in among the trees (into the heart of the trees), and the other stood at the bank, beside the soldiers, who would not go near. And the one outside it held (knew) it as the one inside it held it. A throne's heart is held (known) in one breath; and the name [comes] last, and [it is] very hard.**
- *Notes:*
  - *hos hy Halyna* is "one from Halyna", that is, one of the pair. *ullen* is "the other".
  - *re heth … o hosen re heth …* matches the grammar's own example (§3.5): *o hosen* reads "he/it … as", not "his equal". So *o* is the object "it", and *hosen* introduces the second clause.
  - *hyo* and *ulo* ("from it", "in it") are 3sg masculine/neuter. Their "it" is most naturally the heart of the trees, or the fallen throne. The text does not say which.
  - *keth* is "hold", and of the wood it is "know". I give "held (knew)".
  - *lo hless* could also be "at the edge" (*cless*: a bank, a raised edge).
  - The last clause has no verb. *Doss* ("is") may carry over: "and the name is last". *duss sost* is "hard itself / very hard", as in *et dask sost* "the very end".
  - Grammar observation, not a reading problem: *lo gor* leaves *gor* unsoftened after *lo* (+S). §3.3 gives no exemption for *gor*. The analyzer does not mutate particles, so it passes the phrase.

**W08-VI2-04** · *— Nynth. Lesteth, nalelv umor.*
- Gloss: spring/well/water · houses · heavy · upon-us.
- **English: — Water (a spring). Houses, heavy upon us.**
- *Note:* *nynth* is "a spring, a well; water". The leading dash is not an Orrowen mark (§6.7: direct speech is not marked; the marks are Seren's). I read it as a new turn of speech. The speaker is not named. Line 03 implies the two of Halyna.

**W08-VI2-05** · *— Misketh, ul morrol umor. Lilvyl, ul et sedh.*
- Gloss: feet · in going (*orrol*, N) · upon-us. Rot · in the fen.
- **English: — Feet, walking upon us. Rot, in the fen.**
- *Note:* *ul* + verbal noun is the progressive (§3.4), so "in going upon us" means "walking on us".

**W08-VI2-06** · *— Meskrivullath, ul morrol. Tolm, ul dessyl.*
- Gloss: roots · in going. Stone · in answering (*tessyl*, N).
- **English: — Roots, walking. Stone, answering.**
- *Note:* *tolm* may also be "a dressed stone (of a wall)". *tessyl* may also be "a confession (an answering for a deed)". I give the first sense of each.

**W08-VI2-07** · *— Vorrdhuss. Domm, ul vimm et hal.*
- Gloss: ash ("fire-dust"). Black/dark · under (*ul vimm*) the bedrock.
- **English: — Ash. Dark, beneath the bedrock.**

**W08-VI2-08** · *— Sulv. Fedh sell ul et sulv.*
- Gloss: snow/white. Time/season long · in the snow.
- **English: — Snow. A long time in the snow.**
- *Note:* the first *Sulv* could also be "White".

**W08-VI2-09** · *— Helv, bemmet. Sirr —*
- Gloss: sky/upper air · lifted. Glass —
- **English: — Sky, lifted. Glass —**

**W08-VI2-10** · *— cresket. Vollath, ul vimm gor yalat. Thae — [ ]*
- Gloss: broken. Names · under (*ul vimm*) everything (*gor yalat*). Thae — [ ].
- **English: — broken. Names, beneath everything. Thae— [ ]**
- *Notes:*
  - *Sirr —* / *— cresket* is one phrase, "glass — broken", split across two turns: one voice begins it and the other ends it.
  - **UNREAD (in part) *Thae* —** This is not a whole Orrowen word. I read it only from the unit's lexicon addition W08-01: a Mystaeri loan cut short, the first syllable of the Throne's name *Thaesaen* (Seilrhass *thae* "tide"). The name breaks off at the dash.
  - **UNREAD *[ ]*** This is not Orrowen: it has no letters and the grammar gives no such mark. It may be an editorial gap, a silence where the name should go on. It may also be Seren's `[ ]`, which the grain spec (§16, K3) prints for *the blind* ("we do not know why"). I cannot tell which.
  - Grammar observation: after *ul vimm* ("in the low of"), a possessor would be softened in a construct (*gor* > *yor*?). The lexicon gives *gor yalat* as a set word, and the analyzer passes it.

**W08-VI2-11** · *Eth re dhess nahos.*
- Gloss: and PST answered (*tess*, S) none. This is a lexicon set phrase.
- **English: And no one answered.**

## Part 2 · The grain (`W08_grain.gn2`): ten Throne hearts

**The frame of every round.** Each round is one Throne's heart: eldest wood, a war-pith, read as a telling (a seal in the bark). Rings are paragraphs and bands are movements, read by §11.2:
- band I is rings 1–3;
- band II is rings 4–12 (4–14 in Eirlenth and Neivaere);
- band III is rings 13–14 (15–16).

How I read the files:
- File 12 is **we**, the teller (§11.2), and its thing-signs name us: "a most old heart", "thrones".
- File 0 is ahead, **the shore**, which beyond ring 1 is **you**, the shore-folk (A22, A16b).
- File 4 is the road's right hand, and file 8 is behind (home).
- An empty file that carries a band alone is the band as a thing (A1, A16b(d)): file 11's MIST is **the grey / the sky**, file 14's WATER **the sea**, a DARK file **the night**.

**Shared rings.** Rings 1–11 and the two closing rings are **identical in all ten rounds**, except the root in r1/1. I checked this by diff of every ring line, runner and ray. I translate them once (§2.1–2.3) and then each heart's own rings (§2.4).

## Part 3 · What I could not read, or read only with doubt

**Orrowen**
1. ***[ ]*** (W08-VI2-10): not Orrowen. It is either a gap, or Seren's mark for the blind. **Unread.**
2. ***Thae —*** (W08-VI2-10): a broken-off loan, read only through the unit's lexicon row (the first syllable of *Thaesaen*). It is not a full word.
3. **The leading dashes** (lines 04–10): not Orrowen punctuation. I read them as turns of speech. The speakers are not named.
4. **The referent of *o*, *hyo*, *ulo*** (line 03): "it" is the heart of the trees or the fallen throne. The text does not say which.
5. ***eth et voll dasken, eth duss sost*** (line 03): no verb. The reading "the name [is] last, and very hard" supplies *doss*.
6. **Grammar observations** (not reading problems): *lo gor* and *ul vimm gor* leave *gor* unsoftened, and §3.3 gives no exemption for it. *cumm eth cumm* as "haven after haven" is my idiom, not the grammar's.

**Grain**
1. **Ring 4, d5 and d6:** the cause of the slowness, and the likeness of the judgements to "no word, no sentence, no morrow". I give the structure; the exact sense is uncertain.
2. **Ring 8, `GIFT{last} → @11`:** "gave up the grey", or "gave [ourselves] to the grey". The *to* role does not decide it.
3. **Ring 9, `STRIKE{most}`:** read as the haven's greatest wrong to our kin's timber. "The most-wronged" is also possible.
4. **Ring 2, `DEEP{most}+HEART`:** "the oldest heart(s)", or "the deepest heart of the grey".
5. **Naelthar r12:** the sails "holding at the last" as we fell is uncertain.
6. **Ralensaen r12, `!HOLD →` us:** "did not know us", or "forgot us".
7. **Rhenvael r12:** "answered with the mountain" (by means of it, or together with it).
8. **Eirlenth r13, `DEEP+KNOT`:** the act "waited long", or the name "the Long Wait".
9. **Esthaer's bark (`BURN`) and Naelthar's (`BLOOD[MIST]`):** the grain shows only the signs ("burn", "blood in the grey"). I take the names from the round ids and the soft readings.
10. **The doers on file 4** (rings 3, 12): the felling's doer is only "the right hand", the road's right. No feller is named.
11. **`CARVE#1` in ring 1:** who carved the green hulls is not cut.
