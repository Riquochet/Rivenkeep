**Signs and marks in the source, and how they are read here**
- `{{Halyna}}`, `{{Enrella}}`: a pair-name under its sealing lintel (§3.10, §6.8). Translated as the name; a pair-name takes a dual verb, given here as "they" / "they two".
- `*…*` and `**…**`: Seren's italic and bold, kept. `##`: a heading. `·` in a heading: a separator between the number and the title, kept as "·". Quotation marks are Seren's (§6.7: direct speech is not marked in the hand), kept.
- Names are left as Orrowen spells them. Per the additions, the Book's English spells **Yory** as *Jory* and **Wik** as *Wick*; place- and epithet-names are given in the lexicon's English (Tavow Hemm = Eldhythe, Tavow Vorr = Emberhythe, Corlen Dhavow = Corlen of the Harbour, and so on).
- "[ ]" encloses words supplied in English that the Orrowen leaves to the reader; "(lit. …)" gives the literal sense where the English departs from it.

## Part V.2 · The Night of the Naming

**S08-V2-00** · ## *The Night of the Naming*
- *Vess Vollol*: lexicon phrase O2738.

**S08-V2-01** · *Late in the second summer.*

**S08-V2-02** · *I held the lamp that night, and I laid this at the hearth on the evening of the next day (lit. in the dusk of the morrow). The planks I speak of (lit. that I tell upon them) rest in the lowest vault still; one goes down when one wishes, and lays one's hand upon them.*
- *ul hedh et ryst*: "in the dusk of the morning/morrow"; read as the following evening.
- *orr hos … cadh hos*: present, general "one" (anyone).

**S08-V2-03** · Hold the stone, Seren.

**S08-V2-04** · All through that first winter, this fire laughed at the grey sails. Kael said: "They chose the wrong keep"; and I laughed like the others.
- *navenn*: "false, untrue; mistaken, wrong". Read here as "wrong".
- *um et wemmeth myst*: "upon / about the grey sails", read as "laughed at".

**S08-V2-05** · On a late night in the Second Tide, Halyna went down, down to the oldest vault (lit. the very old vault) beneath this hall, where the rite had been found. I followed them, and I had the ink and the lamps (lit. the ink and the lamps at me). It is as cold there in summer as in winter. The courses lay still, grey upon grey, like a turned field that has given up stonework.
- **Uncertain:** *trunow kylet sa re hebb tolmath*. *kylet* is glossed only "changed"; I read "turned" from the senses of *kyl* (change; curl, turn), so "a turned (ploughed?) field that gave stonework". It could equally be "a changed field". *tolmath* is "stonework, masonry".

**S08-V2-06** · There were fifty-three planks of the first winter there, and every one was carved. The runners had brought them home, two runners to a plank, from wherever the sea had given them, with the salt running down their arms (lit. the salt in pouring upon their arms). The others were at the soldiers' fires, and in the tide. The wall called them "the tablets". They were only planks.
- *Re yal et ullen lo vorrath et hesperdeth, eth lo et hyll*: literally "the others were at the fires of the soldiers, and at the tide". That is, the rest of the planks had gone to the soldiers' fires or stayed in the tide. That reading is mine.
- *pa vurr eth sullnoth*: fifty-three (lexicon O1899).

**S08-V2-07** · Every one of them but one gave Halyna one word; and I wrote the words, one word to a leaf. The one gave nothing, though it was carved as well as any (lit. but it was carved good like every one). Weeks after the first, the sea brought it.

**S08-V2-08** · The planks of the Second Tide bore two signs each, cut close together, the way names are cut close on an old lintel. On the shingle Halyna had learned to hold both together (lit. the holding of both together), and every time there was a cleft in the telling. I did not mend them with guesses (lit. with thoughts). Until that night, no one had gone down to put the question to the fifty-three: is there more?
- **Uncertain:** *ho ross mest?* is literally "is [it/there] much?"; *mest* is "much, many". I read it as "is there more?". It could also be "are there many?".
- **Ambiguous:** *dem bessyl et pa vurr eth sullnoth*, "for the asking of the fifty-three". This could mean asking the fifty-three or the fifty-three's question.

**S08-V2-09** · The night was short, and the courses (the night's tales) were long; so they began (lit. laid the first stone) at both ends, Rhyna at the stair and Halvard at the far wall. I had never seen Halyna holding apart from each other (lit. in holding, from the other) before that night. And they were not far apart: the length of the vault.
- *re hadha*: the analyzer offers "IMP.PL", but after *re* it is the past 3du, "they two laid". *cadh et tolm hosast* is "begin" (O2859).
- *hy ullen*: the analyzer's word gloss "S·of rivers" (from *rhullen*) is an artifact, because softened *rh-* gives *h-*, not nothing. The phrase is *hy ullen*, "apart" (O1036).
- *trenneth*: both "the courses of a wall" and "the tales of a night". The line plays on both.
- **Uncertain tone:** "not far apart: the length of the vault" reads as wry understatement. The Orrowen has no "only".

**S08-V2-10** · Rhyna took up the very first plank, the wave that said *shore*, and it had one long riven edge (lit. and one long riven bank at it). Halvard took up the mute one, and it too had a long riven edge, as the first had.
- *et fodh sa re vysk orrow*: "the wave that said 'shore'". I take "the wave" as the plank's sign, and *orrow* (italic) as the word it gave.

**S08-V2-11** · I stood between Halyna (lit. in the mortar of Halyna), with the lamp. No wind comes down into this deep, and the flame stood straight (lit. rose true) in my hand.

**S08-V2-12** · Rhyna lifted her plank. At the far wall Halvard lifted his. So the far knots and the near knots rise together, because it is one whole net.
- *Re wemm*: past S of *bemm* "lift" (the noun *wemm* "sail" cannot follow *re*).

**S08-V2-13** · In one moment, both their faces changed (lit. the two faces changed).
- **Ambiguous:** *et pa lern*, "the two faces". I read these as Rhyna's and Halvard's faces. *lern* can also be the face (front) of a thing, so it could mean the two faces of the planks.

**S08-V2-14** · Then Rhyna spoke, and it was not a word. It was a whole order.
- *haral*: "an order; a command; a law". I read it as a command, because the planks' text that follows is in the imperative.

**S08-V2-15** · *To the shore. Go as a root goes to water (lit. like a root to the spring, go). Do not wait for the branch.*
- *Nath dovv um yend*: negative imperative; *tovv um* is "wait on, wait for" (O2883); *yend* = S·*gend* "a shoot from a stump; a branch".

**S08-V2-16** · From the far end, with the mute plank in his hands (lit. and the mute plank at him), Halvard said it with her (lit. together), word for word.
- *brod um wrod*: "word for word" (O2971).

**S08-V2-17** · Then every plank spoke. We laid them in order, as the sea had given them; and that night each one gave its own line. All night Halyna went along the courses, from one to the next; and I went between them, and wrote. In the middle of the courses my hand hurt to the joint, and I did not stop. The others are here, as I wrote them:
- **Uncertain:** *re yal grest um en narl dem et brellir*, "harm was upon my hand as far as the joint". *brellir* is "a joint between stones; a hinge". Said of a hand it may mean a knuckle or the wrist. The lexicon gives no body sense.

**S08-V2-18** · *Break a wall. The first stone, the only stone. Strike until it opens, or until it is ash (lit. hammer to opening, or to ash).* That was the whole Square: this is how they write the mute thing, stone. Those ships hammered a wall until they drowned, and we named them: Wild.
- *Cresk hald*: the bare stem, so either an imperative ("break a wall") or a 3sg present ("a wall breaks"). I read it as an imperative because the planks' lines are orders (V2-14).
- *et gemmyorn*: "the square ('four-corner')". I take it as the name of the grain-sign (cf. O2998 *Gemmyorn Nawrodh*, "the Mute Square"). *rytent so* has an emphatic pronoun after a 3pl verb: "*they* write".
- *Re runnant … dem felmyl*: past 3pl, S of *drunn* (mallet; strike, knock); *dem felmyl* is "until drowning".

**S08-V2-19** · *Go in to the blow (lit. go until the stroke). Where the hide splits, swerve aside.* That one turned, and none followed it. We named it: Craven (lit. Un-brave).
- *dem drunn*: *dem* nasalises, but a nasalised *d-* before *r* stays *d-*, so this is *drunn* "a blow, a stroke".
- *nawerd*: "not brave, un-brave".

**S08-V2-20** · *Before the bearer, thin wood. The thin wood falls; the bearer goes on.* The ships at the head of the line were ordered (lit. it was commanded at them): Die first! And they died first.
- *Ul lern lymmerd, mesk bynt*: verbless; *ul lern* "in front of"; *lymmerd* "a bearer"; *mesk bynt* "narrow / thin wood".
- *Senna*: imperative plural.

**S08-V2-21** · *Where it sparked, strike. The last spark, the only spark.* The gun that spoke last, they turned on it. We named it: Want.
- *maver*: "wish, want; a need". I give it as "Want"; "Need" or "Wish" are equally possible.
- *re hylent dem o*: "they turned to it".

**S08-V2-22** · *Wait in the grey, as seed waits in frost. When stone rests, come.* They came in when we stopped watching.

**S08-V2-23** · *Home, when wounded. One branch falls; every one turns, water after water.* At the first loss they scattered, and the wall shouted for joy.
- **Uncertain:** *nynth eth nynth*. *nynth* is "a spring, a well; water", and the X *eth* X pattern means "X after X" (*grem eth grem*, *fedh eth fedh*), so "water after water". The sense (each hull turning back across the water?) is my guess.
- *re rigent*: past 3pl, S of *drig* "throw; fling; scatter"; read as "scattered".

**S08-V2-24** · The soldiers took the head of each order for a name, one by one, in plain words: *To the shore. Break the wall. Wait in the grey.*
- *bisk et haral*: "the head of the order", that is, its first words. The three names are those heads turned into plural imperatives with the article (*Dem et orrow*, *Creska*, *Tovva*). Compare V2-15, V2-18 and V2-22.
- *ul et brodath venn*: "in the true / plain words".

**S08-V2-25** · When Halyna met in the middle, the grey of morning was coming down the stair (lit. was in the going-down upon the stair). One other plank lay at the end of the last course, alone. A mason of Emberhythe had brought it up the mountain after the Fall, burnt black at one end. He could not bear to bring nothing out of his town, and he never knew what it was.
- *Tavow Vorr* (here *Dhavow Vorr*, softened as possessor): Emberhythe. *et Ommol*: the Fall.
- *nath re hydull o lymmyl nayalat hy o hinnar*: "he did not endure the bringing of nothing from his walled town".

**S08-V2-26** · At last Halyna held it, hand by hand upon the grain (lit. hand and hand), while the lamps paled.
- **Uncertain:** *garl eth garl*. By the X *eth* X pattern it is "hand after hand"; it may mean the two hands (his and hers) side by side.

**S08-V2-27** · It said: *Fire*.

**S08-V2-28** · No one spoke, and for a long time I did not write. Then Halvard laid it in its place, at the end of the last course, alone.

**S08-V2-29** · That was all that the young wood knew: one word to a ship, and in it one order. Every ship said its word to our wall, and said it again, and said it until it drowned.
- *et mesk tevel*: "the young wood (the Hasty Tide's hulls)" (O1381). *re vysk pawast*: "said again" (O1457).

**S08-V2-30** · They chose nothing.
- *Nath re naelurant galat*: "they did not choose anything". This answers Kael's line in V2-04.

**S08-V2-31** · They were sent (lit. loosed).
- *luskat*: "loosed; adrift, cast off; an envoy, one sent".

**S08-V2-32** · When the day rose, I took up the first plank and the mute one. All night my eyes had kept going back to their edges. I laid them side by side, torn edge to torn edge.
- *sest lo sest*: "side by side" (O3004).

**S08-V2-33** · The broken grain met.
- *Re hemm*: past S of *cemm* "meet" (not *hemm* "old").

**S08-V2-34** · It met the way two halves of a riven tree meet. I did not ask Halyna what it meant, and they did not tell me.
- *nath re wrodha*: the analyzer offers "IMP.PL"; after *nath re* it is the 3du past.

**S08-V2-35** · Then we sat among the planks, we three. The lamps were still burning, and no one put them out. At last I said this, and Halyna let it stand:
- *re luska Halyna o ul stonol*: *lusk* X *ul* VN is "let X do V" (O3003), so "let it stand".

**S08-V2-36** · "I think: those ships were the very youngest wood, sent first, because the young grows fastest. The wood at our backs is not so wholly evil (lit. is not evil-itself). It is still growing."
- **Uncertain:** *Nath noss dath sost et mesk ul ol sestow*. *sost* is "self, very; so (very)", so this is "the wood behind us is not very evil", or "is not evil itself". *ul ol sestow* is "in our back", that is, behind us.
- *Doss o ul devyl tul*: progressive, *doss* + *ul* + VN (*tevyl* "a growing", nasalised).

**S08-V2-37** · This I lay as it was laid for me.
- The hearth's closing formula (O0238).

**S08-V2-38** · *And the hearth said:* **We remember.**

## Part V.5 · The Tides That the Wall Counted

**S08-V5-00** · ## *The Tides That the Wall Counted*
- Lexicon O1054 gives "The Tides, as the Wall Counted Them". Literally: "Tides that the Wall counted".

**S08-V5-01** · *At the end of tide after tide, through six winters.*
- **Ambiguous:** *Lo rask hyll eth hyll*. It can be read as "at the end of tide after tide" (construct) or as "at last, tide after tide" (*lo rask* alone is "at last" in V2-26 and V2-35).

**S08-V5-02** · *Kael the Counter laid this, who laid no tide until it was whole (lit. did not lay a tide but when it was whole). In the sixth winter he bade me make the six new, as I had made his first telling new; I made them smaller than the first.*
- *nath … veth* is "only" (§3.8). *re haral o lom clennyl et vran* is "he ordered at me the remaking of the six". *o dhrenn hosast* is "his first course (telling)". *lyss hy et hosast* is "small from the first", that is, smaller than the first.

**S08-V5-03** · Give me the stone. I will not sit; I shall stand.
- *Nath reskym* is a present negative ("I do not sit"). *es stonom* is the future.

**S08-V5-04** · **One · The Hasty Tide**

**S08-V5-05** · I count. I am a counter.

**S08-V5-06** · Eight sails on the first morning, and none kept station beside another (lit. none held a place at another's shoulder). Every ship rammed the first wall it touched, until it drowned.
- *Re runn*: past, S of *drunn* "strike, knock".

**S08-V5-07** · No one stood on the decks. Out of the transports came the root-men, like poured kindling. To the wall they are treelings, as if a name could make them small.
- *uldath veskrivull*: "the root-men" (O2974); *meskeleth*: "treelings" (O1385). *hosen amm es lyss voll so* is "as though a name will reduce them".

**S08-V5-08** · By noon the fleet's fire was dwindling (lit. was in reducing), and we learned a saying: "The fleet has spent its fire."
- The saying is O2979.

**S08-V5-09** · A breach fell open in the southern face of the wall, out of reach (lit. to far), as the slates of the black chest had said; and every one lived through it (lit. breathed after that). I swear upon that. It stands.
- **Uncertain:** *Re omm vathur um lern bithlernen et hald dem neld*. This is literally "a breach fell upon the southern face of the wall, away/far off". *dem neld* is "away, far off" (O0393), and its exact force here (out of reach, at a distance, falling away?) is unclear.
- *re hoss gor hos ul sestow ull*: *hoss* is "breathe; live; outlive", so "everyone lived after that".
- *Rytom um ull. Ston.*: an oath and its close. *Rytom* is the canon's broad form.

**S08-V5-10** · All through that winter I said at this fire: "The grey sails chose the wrong keep." Every night it went along the benches together with the cup, and every man drank to it. We laughed. Forgive us, God: we laughed.
- *Vardh*: *Mardh* softened in the vocative.

**S08-V5-11** · Yory of Eldhythe [the lexicon's English: Jory], who whistled on the east wall, laughed at it loudest. Yory's name is first beneath the eastern cairns.
- *re dhyvull o bont sost*: "he laughed it very loud". *stelleth* is "cairns; a burial-field".

**S08-V5-12** · A fire-ship took him. That night the Captain tore a hand's breadth from the half of the cloak he wears, and he bound it on a pike, on Yory's cairn.
- *sa hovv o*: relative + S·*covv* "wear". It has no past particle, so "that he wears" (present).

**S08-V5-13** · It was the first piece.

**S08-V5-14** · ***Laid in Seren's hand:*** *It was not the last.*

**S08-V5-15** · **Two · The Second Tide**

**S08-V5-16** · The second count, and harder than the first.

**S08-V5-17** · They came in two by two. The one in front took the shot; and the one behind took the ground.
- *trun*: "ground", and on the wall "the water a battery watches". The second ship took the battery's water (its ground).

**S08-V5-18** · At the northern spire a ship took a shot. A mile to the south, the sail of another ship went pale, from grey-green to white, like a man's face; and it turned.
- *bost lusul*: "a mile" (O0117).

**S08-V5-19** · It had not seen. It knew.
- *Nath re vesk o. Re heth o.*: *vesk* "see"; *keth* "hold; know (as the wood knows)". The pronoun *o* could also be the object ("it did not see it; it knew it").

**S08-V5-20** · Then the crown-hulls came in, and the winged seed, where our guns were silent (lit. where the guns at us were quiet).

**S08-V5-21** · When we sank a Standard-Bearer, another took up the banner, and the fleet took the new Standard-Bearer's heart. I still say it to the young gunners: *Choose which heart you leave them.*
- **Uncertain:** *re rik et scethan molt et movennerd clenn*. This is literally "the fleet took the heart of the new Standard-Bearer". I read it as "took heart from / followed the heart of" the new one.
- The saying is O2980. *luskos* is 2pl.

**S08-V5-22** · Corlen of the Harbour, eldest of the Twelve, held a wet finger to the wind before the Holding, every time. He said: "Wait. The sea has not finished yet."
- *et kethyl*: "the Holding", the siege hour when the grey sails come in (O1124). The saying is the addition S08-08.

**S08-V5-23** · In the seed-fall (lit. in the falling of the seed) a slow ship came in onto his water (lit. his ground). On his own judgement he held his battery silent until the ship came close (lit. until the coming-in of the ship [was] near). It watched the water, and nothing behind it.
- **Ambiguous:** *Re vesk o um et nynth, eth um nayalat ul o sestow*. *o* can be the ship ("it looked at the water and at nothing behind it") or Corlen ("he watched the water, and nothing behind him"). I read it as the ship, following V5-19.

**S08-V5-24** · The seed came in upon the wall where the guns were silent.

**S08-V5-25** · His guns.
- *O wrammath*: possessive *o* + S·*brammath*. A verbless phrase.

**S08-V5-26** · He died at the parapet, in sight of the water, and three of his gun-crew with him. That night the Captain tore the second piece.
- *hosk hald*: "a parapet" (O0995).

**S08-V5-27** · Corlen built what the Captain builds. That is the whole of a True Man. And that killed him.
- **Ambiguous:** *Re yald Corlen galat sa yald et Crenn*. The relative has no past particle, so "what the Captain builds". Literally it is "Corlen built a thing that the Captain builds".
- **Ambiguous:** *El gor yalat vennuld ull*. This can be read as "that is everything of a True Man" (predicate *gor yalat vennuld*, subject *ull*). It can also be read as "that True Man is everything".

**S08-V5-28** · Voss took his battery, and he rose a True Man. At the next Holding he held a wet finger to the wind, as Corlen had. The Captain, beside him, looked up, and not to the sea.
- *nel dem et wadh*: the negative copula used as "not".

**S08-V5-29** · "Change your shot."
- The second call (O1128).

**S08-V5-30** · **Three · The Tide of Remembering**

**S08-V5-31** · You knew Wik [the lexicon's English: Wick]. He ran from place to place, even to this very hearth, and he sat at the front where the children sit, his knee up to his mouth. He could run the wall-walk while someone said the Title twice. He wanted only to run a word to the Captain.
- *Re seskys*: 2pl, addressed to the hearth.
- *Re yal haskol et haldorrol lo*: "the running of the wall-walk was at him", the "can" idiom (§3.7).
- *nath … veth*: "only".

**S08-V5-32** · By night the Captain moved the southern gun forty paces. In the grey of morning Wik stood at the gun's old place, to watch the water. On the northern wall, Hesk's lamp still burned.
- **Grammar point:** *lo et reskow hemm et bramm* puts an article on the head of a construct, which the grammar (§3.2) forbids. I read it anyway as "the gun's old place".

**S08-V5-33** · One ship came in, alone and slow, sounding the water. Not toward the wall. Toward the place where the gun had been. It fired there, to see: would the gun answer?
- *ul nydhyl nynth*: *nydhyl* is "a sounding (a reckoning of the water)".
- *ho dhess et bramm?*: a present question, "does the gun answer?".

**S08-V5-34** · The shot took the parapet, and Wik fell with it.

**S08-V5-35** · I did not see it. Hale saw. Wik gave him three words.

**S08-V5-36** · "They are watching."
- *Dossant ul veskyl*: progressive, "they are in seeing". These are the three words.

**S08-V5-37** · His word reached the Captain, as he had wished all through the war (lit. every day of the war).

**S08-V5-38** · Hale ran it.

**S08-V5-39** · That night the Captain tore Wik's piece. So a youth died of a gun that was not there.

**S08-V5-40** · We thought: they are beasts, that learn by the stick. They look first, and then they trust. They knew our walls; our Captain, never. After that we moved the guns by night, and from then on it was a quiet war, and I loved it less than the loud war.
- *Re hethent ol haldath*: *keth*, "held / knew (by touch)".
- *re vallom o lyss hy et gorrol bont*: "I loved it small from the loud war", that is, less than the loud war.

**S08-V5-41** · **Four · The Joined Tide**

**S08-V5-42** · All night the drums were knocking in the grey, from ship to ship. At dawn the sails came in on three roads at once, north and east and south. Three roads, one hour.
- *et drunnatath*: "the drums", the soldiers' word for the thunder-tongue knocking out of the grey.

**S08-V5-43** · Lanner of the Crags whistled for the wind, the way crag men whistle. The Captain stood beside him, and was still. Lanner stopped whistling.
- *Lanner Gynten*: Lanner of the Crags (O2879). *hossvell* is "whistle" (addition S08-06).
- **Ambiguous:** *re sol o* ("he was still"). The subject could be the Captain or Lanner.

**S08-V5-44** · "Loose."

**S08-V5-45** · One hour after dawn, Lanner's battery sank their Standard-Bearer.

**S08-V5-46** · The fleet did not falter. At the hour the dead heart had set down (lit. cut), they turned onto the eastern road, into empty water: one sail paled, and then the next, a breath apart. They kept a dead mind's hour. They were not Wild ones, only the Standard-Bearer's own ships.
- **Uncertain:** *Ul et clem sa re ryt et molt sennet*. *ryt* is "cut letters, write; swear". So "the hour the dead heart had written (or sworn)". I read it as the hour the drowned Standard-Bearer had appointed. This links to V5-21 and the next sentence.
- *hos hoss ul lodh*: "a breath apart" (O2976).
- *dend*: I read it as the name given in V2-18 ("Wild"). *nath … veth* is "only".

**S08-V5-47** · Before dusk I went along the wall to count. I found Enno of the guild in the eastern breach; and a mile from him, in the north, Della.
- *Enno Stonwrytan*: "Enno [of] the Stonewrights".

**S08-V5-48** · **Five · The Tide That Learned Deceit**

**S08-V5-49** · The fifth count, and I do not love it.

**S08-V5-50** · They came in as the young wood had come in, loud and alone and plain; and we laughed as we had laughed in the first winter. Every gun spoke, and the hunters came in on the guns that had spoken, every one. Every day they loosed straw onto a new road, ships that were not ships; and on the road where no gun woke, the root-men.
- *venn*: "true; plain, straight, honest". I give it as "plain".

**S08-V5-51** · But look close, as Pellow said. The straw floated high, and left no wreck, and did not flinch at a shot. The feigned rout kept its courses, and a green hull that went pale was not a green hull.
- *Vesk* is an imperative; everything after it is past (*re haemer*, *nath re lusk*, *nath re ferr*, *re heth*, *new*). It reads like Pellow's lesson, but it is told as what happened.
- *keth o dhrenneth*: "keep its courses, keep formation" (O2981). *navenn*: "false, feigned".
