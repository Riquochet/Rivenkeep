### Notes, line by line

**01.** *Hebbet* is "a gift". *sa re yal kethet* is REL + PST + *yal* (the suppletive past of *doss*) + *kethet*, "held": "that was held". *hos clem* is "one hour". The lexicon lists the whole line as the title of IV.4 (O0900). A plainer rendering is "The Gift Held an Hour". No uncertainty.

**02.**
- *heth* is "spring (the season, the lengthening)", and *sullast* (*sull* + *-Ast*) is the ordinal "third", set after the noun as *et tolm hosast* is.
- *Hyll Dhumol* is a construct, *tumol* softened as the possessor: "the Tide of Remembering" (a name in the lexicon, O1046).

**03.** This line holds three sentences.
- **(a) *Ul vess veskyl Brenn re vess tev vodh re hebb et arra.***
  - The words are clear. *ul* is "in, on" (+N; *v* does not change). *vess* is "night". *veskyl* (*vesk* + *-Ol*) is "seeing, sight". *Brenn* is a name. *re vess* is PST + S·*pess*, "asked". *tev* is "a child". *vodh* is "what". *re hebb et arra* is "the guest gave".
  - **The syntax is not certain.**
    - By VSO (§3.1), *re vess tev* is "a child asked". *Brenn* then belongs to the phrase before it, "the night of Brenn's seeing". But a possessor is softened (§3.2), names included (*lest Vardh*), and *Brenn* would then be *Wrenn*.
    - The other way to read it, "On the night of the seeing, Brenn asked a child what the guest had given", keeps the mutation rule and breaks VSO.
    - I give the VSO reading first. Either way, Brenn and a child stand at the asking, and the question is *what the guest had given*.
  - *vess veskyl* may be a fixed name for a night, as *Vess Vollol* is "the Night of the Naming". The lexicon has no row for it.
- **(b) *Heth sy re heth Rhyna o hos clem, eth re rarr o uloy niss, hosen nynth um dholm;***
  - "This spring (*heth sy*), Rhyna held (*re heth*, PST + S·*keth*) it (*o*, the object pronoun after the subject) one hour, and it came (*re rarr*, S·*darr*) into her (*uloy*) slowly (*niss*), like (*hosen*) a spring of water (*nynth*) upon (*um*, +S) stone (*dholm*, S·*tolm*)."
  - The two *heth* are a play: "spring" and "held".
  - Note that *keth* is the verb for knowing the wood by touch (§3.7). "Held" here is also "read, knew".
- **(c) *nath re femm Halvard o, lo et lodh um et parow, eth ew o sa re hadh et tolm hosast ul et brodhol.***
  - *nath re femm Halvard o* is "Halvard did not touch it". *nath re* is the negative past (O2861).
  - *lo et lodh um et parow* is literally "at (by, with) the mortar (the mortar-line; the bond) upon the courtyard (the ward)". **The words are read, but the sense is UNCERTAIN.**
    - The most literal sense is a place: he was at the mortar-line of the courtyard's paving.
    - It could also be "by the bond upon the ward", a reason he did not touch it. The lexicon has no phrase for it.
  - *eth ew o sa re hadh et tolm hosast ul et brodhol* is a cleft, "and it was he who laid the first stone in the telling". The idiom *cadh et tolm hosast* means "begin a telling" (O2859; **LEAN**: that row quotes "it was he who began the telling"). "He" can only be Halvard.

**04.** *Hemm* is "old" (it can also be the noun "an elder"). *Domm* is "black, dark". *Tolm — nel* is "stone — is not". *Nel tolm* is "is not stone". *Ew mesk* is "was a tree / was wood", with the copula in the past. "It was a tree" fits the shift from present *nel* to past *ew*.

**05.**
- *Garlath* is the plural of *garl*, "hands". *dhaedh* is "warm". *fedh sell* is "a long time".
- *Flenneth* is the plural of *flenn*. The base lexicon gives *flenn* only as "a leaf of a book or of slate; a page". The W03 addition gives "a leaf of a tree", plural *flenneth*. Both senses fit. I take "leaves", because of the simile that follows.
- *Nel —* is "is not —", used as a correction: "No —". *brodath* is "words; a speech". *hosen flenneth* is "like leaves".
- *Hos, eth hos, eth hos* is "one, and one, and one".

**06–07.** One sentence split by the dash between two voices: one voice to the dash, the other finishing it, the pair's antiphony (§3.9 item 5).
- *Sull* is "three", and *sull brod* is "three words" (a numeral with a singular noun).
- *ulo* is "in him / in it". **UNCERTAIN:** the third person *o* does not tell "him" from "it".
- *nayalat ul so lodh* is "nothing between them" (*ul so lodh*, "in their mortar", O2884).
- *Doss o lona* is "it is at us two", which means "we two have it" (or "him"). The speaker is a pair speaking as "we two", the dual (§3.9 item 1). What "it" is, the line does not say.

**08.**
- *Gebb* is "a door".
- *Ol —* breaks off. **UNCERTAIN:** before *l* the bedding cannot show (§3.5), so it is "we" or "our". With *lonn et mesk*, "kin of the tree" (a construct with no article on the head, as the grammar requires), "Our — kin of the tree" reads best, the speaker voicing the wood.
- *ul et gannath* is "in the posts / in the palisade". *Dannat* is "hewn".
- *Nath dessent* is NEG + N·*tess* + 3pl: "they do not answer". The tense is present.

**09.**
- *Lummol* is "kneeling". *Cadhat* is "laid".
- *O yell sost* is *o* (+S, so possessive) + S·*gell* "good" + *sost* "very". Adjective + *sost* is the superlative (*hemm sost* "eldest", *hyvenn sost* "outermost"), so this is "his best" (or "its best").
- *Misk* is "a foot". It is also the verb "kick", and as a one-word line it could be either. **UNCERTAIN**, between two senses.

**10.** *Tuss* is "dust". *Dhyvullol* is the verbal noun of *dhyvull*, "laughing". *lo dhrenn et tuss* is "along / through the dust" (*lo dhrenn*, "by the course of", O1217, as *lo dhrenn et grem* is "all winter").

**11.** *Biskyann* is "a neck" ("head-post"). *kethet* is "held". *Pa wisklern* is the dual, two + S·*bisklern*: "two foreheads". *hos —* is "one —", broken off.

**12.** *et preller* is "the knife (the short blade)".
- **UNREAD: `[ ]`.** It is an empty bracket with no letter in it. It is no mark of the course-hand punctuation (§6.7) and no word, so I cannot read it.
- *Conjecture, not a reading:* in the grain the knife (ring 11) is followed by ring 12's blind, the empty cup, "why, the wood does not hold". The empty bracket may stand for that silence.

**13.** *Eth re dhess nahos* is "and answered no one", that is, "And no one answered" (O0563). *dhess* is also the noun "dawn". After *re* it must be the verb, S·*tess*.
