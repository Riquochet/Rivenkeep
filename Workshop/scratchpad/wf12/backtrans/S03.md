# S03 · blind back-translation (Orrowen → English)

**Reader:** blind. Sources used, and only these: the grammar `wf7/orrowen_v2.md`; the lexicon `wf12/base/lexicon_orrowen.tsv` merged with the unit's additions `wf12/additions/S03.tsv`; the analyzer `orr_analyze.py --gloss`, run on copies in `wf12/tmp/S03bt/`. I opened no Book, plain text, old unit or earlier back-translation.

**Analyzer result.** All 98 lines and every token came back **OK**. No word was left without a lexicon match. So nothing below is marked *unreadable* in the strict sense. What *is* marked is every place where the reading is uncertain: a word or construction with two possible senses, a referent I could not fix, a possessive the mutation cannot show (§3.5), or a place where I overrode the analyzer's short gloss.

**Conventions.**
- `[ ]` marks words English needs and Orrowen leaves out: the object of *cadh* "lay", copulas, "it".
- `{ }?` marks a reading I am not sure of. Each one is explained under its line, marked **◆**.
- *re* is the one past particle. I chose the English simple past, perfect or pluperfect from context.
- Direct speech is never marked in the text (§3.11 D10, and the leaf-hand follows suit). Where a line reads as someone's words, I say so in a mark.
- The `/` in I4-05 is the blind text's verse break, not an Orrowen sign. I kept it.

## I.4

**S03-I4-00** · The Rite of the Cornerstone

**S03-I4-01** · The first spring of the siege.

**S03-I4-02** · Seren Two-Inks laid [this tale], the third of Halyna's hearth. In the fourteen nights I was a child of the guild, and they trusted me with nothing but the water.
- ◆ *sullast odh Halyna*: *sullast* is "a third; the unbonded third of a hearth". So the line says "the [unbonded] third of Halyna's hearth".
- ◆ *tev et Stonwrytan* is a construct: "the guild's child, a child of the guild". It could also be heard as "a child [among] the Stonewrights".
- ◆ *gemmnoth vess* is "fourteen nights" (a cardinal before a singular noun), not "the fourteenth night", which would be *gemmnothast*. I read it as the fourteen nights of the Rite (compare I4-27, *orl gemmnothast*, "the fourteenth day").
- ◆ *nynth* is "a spring, a well; water". I chose "water" here because I4-22 (*new nynth o*) and I5-15 (*sa re lymm et nynth*) both point to a child's water-carrying task.

**S03-I4-03** · "Hold the stone, Seren." I say it to myself every time.
- ◆ *Keth et tolm, Seren* reads as remembered speech. *Myskym o lom* is literally "I say it at me".

**S03-I4-04** · On the third night, after the call, a light moved, and I woke. Rhyna was awake, and in the same breath Halvard took up the lamp. I did not cry out. The light went from me, down, turn upon turn, to a spark in a well.
- ◆ *ul et hoss sost* is literally "in the breath itself". I read it as "in the same breath".
- ◆ *kylyl um hylyl* is literally "turning upon turning": a winding stair.

**S03-I4-05** · Over their one cradle, Rhyna's mother had sung a song of the old line of Aldwena, and its meaning no one asked:
- ◆ *Um sona hos tevow* is literally "upon their-two one cradle". *sona* is the dual possessive, but it cannot show on *hos* (h is unchanged by softening, §3.5). The reading "over the single cradle the two of them shared" is my best guess.
- ◆ *Hyna* is *Rhyna* softened as a possessor (rh → h): "Rhyna's mother". *dhesketh* is *tesketh* softened: "the generations, the old line".

**S03-I4-06** · Below the hall, below the floor, / below the stair that stops, / down, where the rope let go the first stone, / the first two hold a thing that the first two know.
- ◆ *pa hosast* is literally "two first(s)". I read it as "the first two", that is the first pair (Aldwena). The verbs *kethe* and *seske* are dual, which agrees.
- ◆ *galat* is "a thing; anything; something".

**S03-I4-07** · Below the stair where it stopped, Rhyna knew where to look, and Halvard went down into the shaft. She paid out the rope, and the two of them said not a single word.
- ◆ *ul vodh dem veskyl* is literally "where, for seeing", so "where to look".
- ◆ *sona hos brod*: *sona* here is the emphatic subject "they two", not "their". The h of *hos* cannot show the possessive.

**S03-I4-08** · At the foot, as Halyna told it to me, there stood a chest of black stone, and on its lid dust as deep as a finger. Halvard took the dust away with his sleeve.
- ◆ *Lo visk* is *lo* + S *misk*, "at [the] foot". It has no article and no possessor, so "the foot [of the shaft]" is inferred.
- ◆ *hy dholm domm* is literally "from black stone": the material. *gunn hosen nind* is "deep as a finger".

**S03-I4-09** · It was the mark of Aldwena.

**S03-I4-10** · In it lay the Rite, on slates: slates of building {for} battle, an annal of the first builders, and a staff along the floor [of the chest]. In the corner stood three flasks of stone. Halvard {shook} each of them by the lamp.
- ◆ *halflenneth yaldol dem stemir* is literally "slates of building until/for battle". *dem* means "for" elsewhere in this text (*dem dolm eth crest*, *voll dem mymmyl*). So I read "slates on building for war". "Until battle" is also possible.
- ◆ *ryt surr et galdardath hosast*: *ryt surr* is "an annal (the inscription of a year)". So "the first builders' annal".
- ◆ *re varr*: *marr* is "shift, move; shake; tremble". I read "shook" because of the next line. "Moved each of them to the lamp" is also possible.

**S03-I4-11** · Nothing moved in them.

**S03-I4-12** · By the fire Halyna read the slates through to their meaning, line, and breath, and line. I set down the cup, and I forgot it.
- ◆ *trenn eth hoss eth trenn* is literally "course and breath and course". The lexicon has *trenn eth trenn*, "paragraph by paragraph", with *hoss* "breath" set in between.
- ◆ *re hadhom et nunth* is literally "I laid the cup". *nunth* means "a cup", not *nynth* "water".

**S03-I4-13a** · This is the Rite of the Cornerstone,
- ◆ The copula puts the predicate first. It could equally be "The Rite of the Cornerstone is this".

**S03-I4-13b** · which we lay [as] a holder {at it}, upon a great work, for its rising and for its holding, for its standing.
- ◆ **The least certain line in the unit.** *sa hadhar ketherd lo um yaldat strom* puts two prepositions side by side, *lo um*. The lexicon has no *lo um* phrase. I read *lo* as the conjugated 3sg "at it/him" (§3.6), so "a holder at it", and *um yaldat strom* as "upon a great work". *ketherd* (lower case) is "one who holds or keeps; a commander". The capitalised *Ketherd* is the Commander. Here I took the common noun.
- ◆ *o stonol*: the softening cannot show on s, so "its standing" rests on the parallel with *o dhaldol* and *o hethyl* (§3.5).

**S03-I4-14** · Below it stood the {Order} of the Staff.
- ◆ *haral* is "an order; a command; a law". The line is *Re ston haral et hulter vimm hyo*, literally "stood [the] order of the staff lower than it". I read it as a second section on the slates. I4-15 gives its words.

**S03-I4-15a** · A staff is cut for its work, and does not let go of its work,
- ◆ *Doss hulter rytet* is literally "is staff carved". *dem o yaldat* is "for its building/work" (*o* + S *galdat*). *nath lusk o yaldat* has no subject stone, so the staff is supplied: "[it] does not loose / leave its work".

**S03-I4-15b** · and when the work is done, the staff is laid in it.
- ◆ *doss et galdat galdat* is literally "the building is built". *galdat* is both the noun "a work" and the participle "built, done".

**S03-I4-16** · On the slates below those, the breath grew long between the lines. A bent wall, for turning shot. A bent gate, against the ram. A course laid so that a breach in it falls outward. The first builders knew war. Against what, is not written on the slates.
- ◆ *re sell et hoss ul lodh et trenneth* is literally "the breath stretched/was long in the mortar of the courses". It may mean the reading slowed. It may also mean the spacing between the lines (*lodh* "the mortar line, spacing") widened. I kept it literal.
- ◆ The three building lines have no verb: they are namings. *ferret* is "bent, crooked; bowed". *gylyl vedh* is "the turning of shot" (N *kylyl*, S *pedh*).
- ◆ *Ul lern vodh* is "against what". *vodh* is "what", not *cedh* "who".

**S03-I4-17** · The Rite needs three oils, which no one living had made.
- ◆ *Doss maver … um et Ternil* is the "upon" idiom, "need is upon the Rite".

**S03-I4-18** · For the cedar, Stannard led Halyna through the snow to the grove where the first staff had been cut. They felled no living tree. On a fallen tree Stannard knocked with his finger, and he listened to it.
- ◆ *rytet* is "carved, cut; written". Of a staff, "carved".
- ◆ *re yostant* is 3pl, not dual: Stannard and the pair, three people.
- ◆ *re runn* is S *drunn*, "a mallet; knock; strike". *re rern o* uses *rern*, "an ear; to listen".

**S03-I4-19** · "I know which of them will hold."
- ◆ The speaker is unmarked. It is a 1sg present, and reads as Stannard's words after knocking on the trees. It could be Seren's own present-tense aside (she has those in I4-03 and I4-37), but "which of them will hold" fits the man who listened to the wood.

**S03-I4-20** · Halyna drew oil from the heartwood at this hearth, and on a wet night the stonework of the hearth still breathes it.
- ◆ *hossant tolmath … o tul* is literally "breathe the stonework of the hearth it yet". The verb is 3pl, agreeing with *tolmath*.

**S03-I4-21** · "Bloom," the masons call the pale crust on polished stone that has lain untended generation after generation, because it is a flower where no one comes.
- ◆ *sast* is "skin, a hide; a crust". *dholm dasorat* is "polished / dressed stone".
- ◆ *el sinth o* is literally "is flower it": "it is a flower".

**S03-I4-22** · Then we came, the children, with old chisels, and we bent along the forgotten stonework like reapers through a reaping-field. That was the first toil that was given to me, and it was not the water.
- ◆ *tolmath nadhumat* is "unremembered stonework". *ferrer* is "we bowed, bent low".

**S03-I4-23** · I had raw fingers from it.
- ◆ This is the "at" idiom, literally "raw fingers were at me from it".

**S03-I4-24** · The mountain could give no myrrh. Idrenna brought one flask the whole road, for the dead men of the guild. On the pass the ground did not take {them}. When Rhyna warmed the myrrh in the cedar, the hall was quiet, as the road is quiet for a dead man. With the myrrh of the dead men of the road the Captain would be anointed.
- ◆ *Nath re yal hebbyl styvess lo et brunn* is the "able" idiom, literally "giving of myrrh was not at the mountain".
- ◆ *nath re rik et trun so*: *so* is plural "them", not the dual *sona*. So it most likely refers to the guild's dead men: the frozen pass would not receive them, so they lay unburied. It is less likely to mean Idrenna, whom the dual would name. *rik* is "catch; take; seize".
- ◆ *Lo styvess uldath sennet et orrol* is a construct chain in an unusual order: "myrrh, dead men, the road". It could be "the myrrh of the road's dead" or "the myrrh of the dead men's road". The sense is the same either way.
- ◆ *es noss* is a future ("will be"). I gave it as "would be" in the past narrative.

**S03-I4-25** · I knew whom it had been brought for.

**S03-I4-26** · All through the night Idrenna's flask stood empty on the hearthstone, and none of us touched it. Late, the Captain came in from the wall, mortar to the arm, and he looked at it, and went out again.
- ◆ *pawast re orr o* is literally "again he went".

**S03-I4-27** · On the fourteenth day a thin sun came in, the first since the banner. In the courtyard lay the new lintel that Stannard had quarried and Orvenna had polished. At its shoulder sat Idrenna, and the flasks in their laps. The Captain stood where the slate bade the overseer stand, his hand on the stone and his face to the people.
- ◆ *et hosast hy et movenn* is literally "the first from the banner". I read "since the banner [was raised]".
- ◆ *Lo o sest* is literally "at it, shoulder". The softening cannot show on s. Read as "at its shoulder, beside it".
- ◆ *o lern*: the l is unchanged, so "his face" rests on the parallel with *o yarl* (§3.5).
- ◆ *nenderd* is "an overseer, a watcher".

**S03-I4-28** · He did not like that. He stood there.

**S03-I4-29** · On the gate the Torn Cloak lifted once, and it fell. I heard the meltwater running in the old channels.
- ◆ *re wemm* is S *bemm*, "lift". It is not *wemm* "a sail".

**S03-I4-30** · Then Idrenna rose, each bowing to the other, and with four old hands they tipped every flask over his bowed head. That winter their hands had shaken on everything else. On the flasks they did not shake.
- ◆ *hos ul ferryl um ullen* is literally "one in bowing upon another". **Override:** the analyzer's short gloss read *ullen* after *um* as S *rhullen*, "of rivers". After a consonant, softened rh is lost (§2.5, §3.3), so the form really is ambiguous. I read *ullen* "another", following the lexicon phrase *hos um ullen*.
- ◆ *o wisk ferret* is "his bowed head" (S *bisk*). The referent "his" is the Captain.

**S03-I4-31** · One named each oil, and the other its meaning:
- ◆ *hos … ullen* is "one … the other": the two of Idrenna, speaking in antiphony.

**S03-I4-32** · Cedar, for patience. Granite-bloom, for strength. Myrrh, for the burden of captaincy.

**S03-I4-33** · The oil ran into his cloak, and he let it be. We children in front lifted our raw hands to one another, and we were glad, for the first time since the passes.
- ◆ *hos um ullen*: the same override as in I4-30. After a verb of lifting rather than seeing, it could also mean "one upon another".
- ◆ *re yal hylenn umor* is the "upon" idiom, "gladness was upon us".

**S03-I4-34** · Idrenna held out the staff of the guild. Thus the slate names it: a staff of polished wood, capped with polished stone, {the very good one}, which every overseer of a great work of the guild has carried since the founding. It was the first staff itself. The Captain looked at it a long time, and then he stretched out his hands.
- ◆ *et gell sost* is literally "the good itself/very". The tongue gives no superlative that I know of. It may mean "the best" or "the selfsame good [staff]".
- ◆ *hoskat lo dholm* is literally "lintel-laid with stone": capped.
- ◆ *hy et rivull* is literally "from the foundation, the bottom course".

**S03-I4-35** · Four hands laid it, and two hands took it.

**S03-I4-36** · By the staff he was named Commander of Rivenkeep, and he stood still in the noise.
- ◆ *Ketherd Hethow Dhresk* is a lexicon phrase, "the name the Rite gave the Captain". *bontoth* is "loudness".

**S03-I4-37** · That night I held the lamp while Halyna cut their sign and their Stonwryt under the new stone. One set the chisel, and the other struck. I cannot write their sign. Beside the Stonwryt they cut words that are not a vow, cut from the cloak, stroke by stroke.
- ◆ *Nath noss rytyl sona rellor lom* is present tense, "the cutting/writing of their sign is not at me". *ryt* means both "cut" and "write", so Seren may be saying she cannot reproduce it on the leaf.
- ◆ *re runn ullen* is literally "the other malleted".

**S03-I4-38** · In memory of our home, our families, our God, our freedoms, our peace.

**S03-I4-39** · It was the Captain's hand, large and slow.

**S03-I4-40** · They did not mend a single stroke.
- ◆ *hlenne* is S *clenn*, 3du: "make new, mend".

**S03-I4-41** · The True Men lifted the stone into the gate, the sign downward. In the corner of the gatehouse the Captain leaned the staff, because the wall and the war were his work. He went to the wall again, half a cloak on his back.
- ◆ *ew o yaldat et hald eth et gorrol* puts the predicate first: "his work was the wall and the war".
- ◆ *o sestow*: the s is unchanged, so "his back" is read from context.

**S03-I4-42** · No one moved the staff. When you doubt the tale, go, and look at the dust on it.
- ◆ *bethirys* is 2pl. *orra* and *veska* are imperative plurals. The reader is addressed.

**S03-I4-43** · When the stone was laid and the tools cleaned, Halyna let me go down alone with the lamp, to lay the slates back again. I went down, turn upon turn, to where I had watched their light going. I brought up the empty flasks.
- ◆ *re wemmym* is literally "I lifted". *ul morrol* is N *orrol*, "in going": the light going down.

**S03-I4-44** · There was one leaf more.

**S03-I4-45** · Its letters were large and careful, as a child's letters are, and as my letters still are. I carried it up under my cloak, and it was cold against me. By the fire Halyna read it to me: a leaf of the {youngest} prentice, in the first days.
- ◆ *dholmath*: *tolmath* is "stonework", but it is also the plural of *tolm*, "a letter of the course-hand" (§1). "Letters" suits a leaf.
- ◆ *prassel tevel sost* is literally "the prentice young itself/very": "the very young prentice".
- ◆ *orlath* is "days; a lifetime".

**S03-I4-46a** · When the stone is laid, and the tools cleaned, we sit by the hearth, and one of us lays a tale,

**S03-I4-46b** · as a course is laid upon a wall, one tale every night; and so the wall and the telling rise together.
- ◆ This is the leaf's text, in the present. *talda* could be imperative plural "rise!" or 3du "they two rise" (§3.4). It has a dual subject (*et hald eth et brodhol*), so I read 3du.

**S03-I4-47** · So this hearth is older than the havens. We did not make it. We forgot it, and found it again at the bottom of a chest.

**S03-I4-48** · The next night Kael stood, {because he had not sat}, and he laid the first tale. It was about the Captain.
- ◆ *hy vodh nath re resk o* is literally "because he did not sit". The logic is unclear: he would not sit, or he alone had not sat down. **Override:** the analyzer glossed *Kael* as "tally-notch". It is the name.
- ◆ *Re yal o um et Crenn* is literally "it was upon the Captain": "about him".

**S03-I4-49** · The people called him: Commander. In his own heart, he was still only the Captain.
- ◆ *ew tul hosel et Crenn o* is literally "was yet alone the Captain he".

**S03-I4-50** · I lay it as it was laid for me.

**S03-I4-51** · And the hearth said: We remember.

## I.5

**S03-I5-00** · The Naming of the Rivenmen

**S03-I5-01** · The sixth winter, the eve of the Going-Down.

**S03-I5-02** · Voss Who Asked laid [this], of Eldhythe, True Man of the harbour battery, who grew up in his grandmother's harbour-house on the harbour.
- ◆ *o scaelur*: the s is unchanged, so "his grandmother" is read from context (§3.5). *re dhev* is S *tev*, verb "grow".

**S03-I5-03** · "Give me the stone." I have owed this hearth an answer a long time.
- ◆ *Tessym tessyl lo* is a lexicon idiom, "owe an answer".

**S03-I5-04** · At the {head} of the first winter I buried a friend on the eastern face, under a cairn, because no spade goes into the ground there. When we were children, we turned the rope together on the harbour while the little children skipped. The last stone on him, I laid it, I myself.
- ◆ *Lo hosk et grem hosast* is literally "at the lintel of the first winter". *hosk* is "a lintel; a capstone; a title". I read "the head, the onset", but the figure is uncertain.
- ◆ *re hyler et bunth* is S *kyl*, "change; turn": turning a skipping rope.

**S03-I5-05** · Jory was his name.
- ◆ *Yory* is the S03 addition: Jory, written with y for /j/.

**S03-I5-06** · That night I asked a question at this fire. I was cold, and I {meant} it as a hard question.
- ◆ *re haeverym* is S *kaever*, "think; mean, intend". It could be "I thought it a hard question".

**S03-I5-07** · "Why do we fight for stone and ice?"

**S03-I5-08** · Once Stannard knocked on the hearthstone, and answered me.
- ◆ *re dhess o lom* is literally "answered it/he at me". *o* could be the object "it" or the emphatic "he".

**S03-I5-09** · "It is not the ice that I fight for."

**S03-I5-10** · That was a good answer, but it was his answer. My answer, I have {cut} it all through the war.
- ◆ *re rytym o lo dhrenn et gorrol tunn*: *ryt* is "cut letters, write; swear; carve". It could be "written", "sworn" or "cut". *lo dhrenn … tunn* is "along the whole course of".

**S03-I5-11** · A slow answer is a slow tide, as my grandmother told it to me: it comes in, up to the very stones.
- ◆ *El hyll niss tessyl niss* is a copula proverb with the predicate first, "[a] slow tide is [a] slow answer". *mellor o um et tolmath sost* is literally "it comes in upon the stonework itself".

**S03-I5-12** · {It} rose in the week after the Rite: the whole Keep in the courtyard under the Torn Cloak. I stood among the companies in the snowy mud, and Jory at my shoulder. For once in his life he was not whistling. The Captain stood before us, and the staff in its corner.
- ◆ *Re dhald o*: the referent of *o* ("it/he") is unclear. I read it as the answer or the name that this leaf tells of. Compare I5-19, *Ul et parow ull re dhald o*.
- ◆ *Re ston et Crenn lor* is literally "the Captain stood at us".
- ◆ *o yorn* is S *gorn*: "its corner".

**S03-I5-13** · Kael the Counter climbed the fallen stones at the gate. He had one thing [to say], and he said it once.

**S03-I5-14** · "The Commander fought."

**S03-I5-15** · And the whole guild finished it, in one cry, from Idrenna to the very smallest child, who carried the water:
- ◆ *re dhunn* is S *tunn*, verb "complete, finish".

**S03-I5-16** · "The Stonewrights built."

**S03-I5-17** · That was the whole speech. It was enough. We looked at one another, soldier and mason. We were not two peoples who shared a ruin. We were one, as two courses become one wall when the mortar sets.
- ◆ *hos um ullen*: the same override as in I4-30 (not "of rivers").
- ◆ *sa re saede kaelir*: *saede* is *saed* "halve, share" + 3du. Its subject is the dual *pa lunt* (the gap). It is not *kaelir*, "a ruin", which is the object.
- ◆ *kyle* is 3du of *kyl*, "change, turn": "two courses turn into one wall".

**S03-I5-18** · On that day we laid down the name of the Keep on dry land. Rivenkeep is the havens that hold the grey sails, and the edge of the sea is the edge of the Keep. It is a vow.
- ◆ *re hadhar voll et Kethow um et gaed* is literally "we laid the Keep's name upon the dry land". I am unsure of the sense.
- ◆ *sa hethent et wemmeth myst*: the relative's gap could be the subject ("that hold the grey sails") or the object ("which the grey sails hold"). Both nouns are plural. I chose the subject reading.
- ◆ *El ryt o*: *ryt* is also "an inscription". "It is an inscription" is possible.

**S03-I5-19** · We named ourselves Treskan. Our fathers we name Orrowan, because they were Orrowan; and ourselves we name: {us}. In that courtyard it rose.
- ◆ The final *ol* stands alone after a colon as a quoted name. It could be "we", "us" or "ours", and I am not sure which is meant.
- ◆ *Treskan* is "the Rivenmen, the cleft-folk". *Orrowan* is "the Shorelanders".

**S03-I5-20** · All through that day we went up, one by one, to the stone under the pike. Each laid a hand on it and swore it, as men tie their ropes on the harbour at dusk. Jory went up in front, and he came down whistling. When the night came, the Keep was held.
- ◆ *re ryt o* is "cut-vowed it": "swore it".

**S03-I5-21** · We swore: "We will take back every stone. Not one stone that was not ours. But every one that was."
- ◆ *Nel hos tolm sa nath re yal lor* is literally "is not one stone that was not at us". Read with the "at" idiom of having: "not a stone we did not have".

**S03-I5-22** · The old ones themselves never loved the new name, and they said nothing. They still whisper in their blankets: Orrow.
- ◆ *et hemmeth sost* could be "the old ones themselves" or "the very oldest". *hemm* is also "Elder".
- ◆ *Orrow* is "the Shore".

**S03-I5-23** · This winter, when the ten great hulls sat in the havens like birds in the shallows, I asked old Ebba of Fenholm why she keeps it. She did not look up from the fire, but she held her hands out to it.
- ◆ *ey garlath*: *ey* causes no mutation, so "her hands" is read from context.

**S03-I5-24** · "It is a name for the homecoming, when the last wall stands, and the last Mystarch lies broken."

**S03-I5-25** · To the fire, not to me, she said one more word.
- ◆ *nel lom* is literally "not at me".

**S03-I5-26** · "Perhaps."

**S03-I5-27** · I did not understand it then. I think: I understand now.
