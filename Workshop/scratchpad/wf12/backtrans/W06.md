## Part 1 · The Orrowen text (`blind/W06.txt`)

### W06-V4-01
> Redul ul vimm et Helv Bynt

- **literal:** council / in(+N) the-low [of] / the / Sky / Narrow
- **English:** **The Council Under the Thin Sky**
- *ul vimm* is "below, beneath" (the lexicon's phrase), and *Bynt* means "narrow; fine, thin". The lexicon also carries the whole line as a name.

### W06-V4-02
> Et grem gemmest, ul Hyll Lodhat.

- **literal:** the winter fourth, in(+N) Tide Bonded.
- **English:** **The fourth winter, in the Joined Tide.**
- *Hyll Lodhat* is a name in the lexicon, "the Joined Tide". Literally it is "the bonded (mortared) tide".

### W06-V4-03
> Re hadh Rhyna garl um et molt sy, eth re nahadh ey o ul hos hoss; re rarr Halvard dem susk hy et nelter, eth re yal lern thenth lo. Ew clemel et kethyl; ullen et hyll ull, et brodhol; hy vodh doss prenereth mest ul redul, eth re yal hos ganna wrodow lonta.

| clause | literal | English |
|---|---|---|
| Re hadh Rhyna garl um et molt sy, | PST S·lay(*cadh*) Rhyna hand upon(+S) the heart this | **Rhyna laid a hand upon this heart,** |
| eth re nahadh ey o ul hos hoss; | and PST un-lay(*na-* + S·*cadh*) she it in one breath | **and she took it off again in a single breath;** |
| re rarr Halvard dem susk hy et nelter, | PST S·come(*darr*) Halvard up(*dem susk*) from(+S) the vault | **Halvard came up out of the vault,** |
| eth re yal lern thenth lo. | and PST was(*re yal*) face wet at-him | **and his face was wet** (lit. "a wet face was at him") |
| Ew clemel et kethyl; | was a-moment the holding | **The holding was a moment;** |
| ullen et hyll ull, et brodhol; | the-rest [of] the tide that, the telling | **the telling, the rest of that tide;** |
| hy vodh doss prenereth mest ul redul, | because(*hy vodh*) are minds many in(+N) council | **for there are many minds in a council,** |
| eth re yal hos ganna wrodow lonta. | and PST was one gate [of] S·mouth(*brodow*) at-them-two | **and the two of them had one gate of a mouth** (one mouth between them) |

- ⚠ *et molt sy*, "this heart": the text does not say whose heart this is. *molt* is "a heart; the middle". Since the leaf is a wood leaf, it is probably the heart of the wood in front of them, but that is my inference.
- ⚠ *ullen et hyll ull*: I read *ullen* ("another; the rest, the others") as the head of a construct, "the rest of that tide". The verbless clause can also be read "the telling was another [matter], that tide". The sense (the telling took far longer than the holding) is the same either way.
- *kethyl* is "holding; (of the wood) a knowing" (§3.7: to know the wood is to hold it). The holding is the reading of the wood by touch.
- ⚠ *hos ganna wrodow lonta*: the literal sense is clear ("one gate of mouth was at the two of them": they two had it). The idiom is not in the lexicon. *ganna* is "a gate; a pair (a pair is a gate)", so the phrase is either "the pair had a single mouth to speak with" (one voice for the many minds) or "one pair of mouths". I take the first, because the clause answers "many minds in a council".
- *lonta* (3du) and *lona* (1du, line 11) make the speakers a pair, Rhyna and Halvard.

### W06-V4-04
> — Gendeth hosen crisur, feth ul vimm. Hellur — nel. Meskan.

- **literal:** boughs as roof, moss in-the-low. Hall — is-not. Grove.
- **English:** **"Boughs for a roof, moss below. A hall — it is not. A grove."**
- *gend* is "a shoot from a stump; a branch, a bough", and the plural is *gendeth*.
- *Hellur — nel*: the negative copula *nel* has no subject, so "[it] is not a hall" (or "A hall? No.").
- *Meskan* is "the folk of trees", a grove.
- Lines 04 to 11 open with the speech dash. No speaker is named. Line 11's *lona* (we two) points to the pair telling what they held.

### W06-V4-05
> — Et helv bynt. Tresketh ulo, hosen desteth orrat.

- **literal:** the sky narrow. Clefts in-it, as teeth gone.
- **English:** **"The thin sky. Clefts in it, like teeth that are gone."**
- The first sentence has no verb. It is either a naming ("the narrow sky") or "the sky [is] thin".
- *orrat* is "gone, walked". "Like teeth gone" means gaps like missing teeth.

### W06-V4-06
> — Hos lyss, brid ul ryst. Nath rynn.

- **literal:** one small, cold in(+N) morning. NEG(+N) wake.
- **English:** **"A small one, cold in the morning. It does not wake."**
- ⚠ *Hos lyss*, "a small one": no noun is given, so the text does not say what the small one is.
- *ryst* is "morning; the morrow".

### W06-V4-07
> — Carmath hemm. Tovva. Tovva lont.

- **literal:** voices old. Wait(IMP.PL). Wait(IMP.PL) at-them.
- **English:** **"Old voices. Wait. Wait by them."**
- *hemm* may be the adjective "old" or the noun "an elder" as a softened possessor. *h* does not change, so the mutation cannot show which (§3.5). "Old voices" and "an elder's voices" are both possible.
- *Tovva* is the imperative plural. It sounds like the 3du, but a 3du verb always has a subject (§3.4), and this one has none.
- ⚠ *lont*, "at/by them": the "at" idiom of having (*Doss X lont*) needs *doss*, which is absent. So I read "wait by (beside, with) them". The text does not say who "they" are. The grain's ring 5 has "wait, with the pillars", which fits "wait by them".

### W06-V4-08
> — Breller, domm, ul et meskrivullath. Derdeth ul et crestalath.

- **literal:** keel, black, in(+N) the roots. Bones in(+N) the ribs.
- **English:** **"A keel, black, in the roots. Bones in the ribs."**
- *meskrivull* is "a root ('tree-foundation')". *crestal* is "a rib of a hull or a body", so the ribs may be the hull's or a body's. I read them as the hull's, because a keel has just been named.

### W06-V4-09
> — Tovva —

- **English:** **"Wait —"**
- The line breaks off at the dash, and the next line answers it.

### W06-V4-10
> — nath dovvar. Nath dovva. Carmath tevel, um et hemman. Mest —

- **literal:** — NEG(+N) N·wait-1PL. NEG(+N) N·wait-IMP.PL. Voices young, upon(+S) the elders. Many —
- **English:** **"— we do not wait. Do not wait. Young voices, upon the elders. Many —"**
- *nath dovvar* is the present 1pl ("we do not wait"; the future would be *es*).
- ⚠ *Nath dovva*: the grammar gives no negative imperative. I read *nath* + the imperative plural as "do not wait!" The 3du ("they two do not wait") needs a subject and has none.
- ⚠ *um et hemman*, "upon the elders": the feeling idiom *Doss X um Y* would need *doss*. As it stands, the young voices are laid upon (pressed on, raised against) the elders. The exact force is my guess.

### W06-V4-11
> — mest sost. Nath noss kethyl gor harm lona — [ ]

- **literal:** — many very. NEG(+N) N·be holding every S·voice at-us-two — [ ]
- **English:** **"— very many. We two cannot hold every voice — [ ]"**
- *sost* is "self, very; even", so "very many, many indeed".
- *Nath noss* VN *lo Y* is "Y cannot V" (§3.7). *harm* is *carm* softened after *gor*. *kethyl* is also "knowing", so the line can be read "we two cannot hold (know) every voice".
- ⚠ **`[ ]`, unread.** These are two empty brackets with nothing between them. No word or letter can be read. The brackets may mark a gap or something withheld, since the line breaks off at the dash before them, but nothing in the grammar or the lexicon defines the mark.

### W06-V4-12
> Eth re dhess nahos.

- **English:** **And no one answered.**
- Word by word, *dhess* glosses "dawn". The lexicon's phrase (O0563) settles it as *tess* "answer" softened after *re*: "and answered no one", that is, no one answered.

### W06-V4-13
> Et flenn ul lern, ul narl Seren:

- **literal:** the leaf in(+N) face, in(+N) N·hand Seren:
- **English:** **The facing leaf, in Seren's hand:**
- *ul lern* is "before, in front, facing". *narl* is *garl* "a hand; a hand of writing" nasalised after *ul*, so this is Seren's handwriting.

### W06-V4-14
> Nath noss ol drenn lo sy. Nath re yalar lo ull, eth nath re sesker galat um naelurol. Re luskom et flenn sy pondul, eth pessym gor sa heth et Flennath sy ul en sestow: Lusk o hosen sy.

| clause | literal | English |
|---|---|---|
| Nath noss ol drenn lo sy. | NEG N·is our(+N) N·tale(*trenn*) at this(*lo sy*: here) | **Our tale is not here.** |
| Nath re yalar lo ull, | NEG PST were-1PL at that(*lo ull*: there) | **We were not there,** |
| eth nath re sesker galat um naelurol. | and NEG PST knew(a fact)-1PL anything upon choosing | **and we knew nothing of the vote.** |
| Re luskom et flenn sy pondul, | PST loose/leave-1SG the leaf this empty | **I have left this leaf empty,** |
| eth pessym gor sa heth et Flennath sy ul en sestow: | and ask-1SG every(+S) who(+S) S·hold(*keth*) the Book this in(+N) my(+N) back | **and I ask everyone who holds this Book after me:** |
| Lusk o hosen sy. | leave(IMP.SG) it as this | **Leave it so.** |

- ⚠ *ol drenn lo sy*: *trenn* means "a course of stones; a line of writing; a tale; a story". The line can be read "our tale (our line) is not here", or by the having idiom, "this [leaf] does not hold our tale". The two say the same thing.
- *naelurol* ("a vote; choosing") has no article, so it is "of the vote" or "of any choosing".
- *ul en sestow* is the lexicon's *ul sestow* "after, behind" with "my": "after me".
- *Lusk* is the bare imperative singular, said to each one who holds the Book. *o* after the verb is the object "it" (§3.5).
- The "we" of the first three clauses is 1pl, not the dual. So Seren speaks for a plain we, not for the pair, and she speaks for herself as I (*luskom*, *pessym*).
