# S09 back-translation (blind reader)

**Source:** `wf12/blind/S09.txt` (141 lines: V.7 parts I to III, then VI.1).
**What I read it with:** only the grammar `wf7/orrowen_v2.md`, the lexicon `wf12/base/lexicon_orrowen.tsv` with the unit's additions `wf12/additions/S09.tsv`, and `orr_analyze.py --gloss`. I ran the analyzer from `wf12/tmp/S09bt/` on the merged lexicon, then looked up every doubtful word by hand. I did not open any Book, plain text, old unit or earlier back-translation.

**Conventions**
- *[square brackets]* mark English words I had to add because the tongue leaves them out (the copula, "it", "so").
- *(lit. ...)* gives the literal word-for-word sense wherever my English moves away from it.
- **UNREAD** marks a sign I could not read at all. **UNCERTAIN** marks a reading where the grammar allows more than one sense. **NOTE** is a remark on the form.
- A proper name stays a name. Names with no sense (Enno, Della, Ormund, Penna, Ruan, Ebba, Hale, Voss and the others) are not translated. Pair-names and Throne-names are given as the lexicon renders them.

## V.7 The Mirror in the Grain

**S09-V7-01** · The Mirror in the Grain.
- NOTE: *vesksirr* means "mirror" (lit. "see-glass"), and *meskdhrenn* means "the grain of wood".

**S09-V7-02** · The fourth summer, the fifth spring, and the sixth winter.
- NOTE: lit. "summer fourth, spring fifth, and winter sixth". It has no verb, so it is a naming. The three seasons match the openings of the three courses: I happens in summer (no season word), II opens "In spring", and III opens "In snow". The text does not say what the ordinals count (years of the war?).

**S09-V7-03** · I laid it, Seren Two-Inks, in three courses, each course on the night a carving was held. On all three [nights] I held the lamp upon the shingle, and this is that lamp.
- NOTE: *ul et sull* is lit. "in the three"; the noun (nights or courses) is left to the reader. *el et greller sy o* is lit. "[it] is this lamp, it".
- UNCERTAIN: *sa re yal et rytet kethet* is lit. "that the carving was held". I read it as "the night a carving was held (read)". *keth* means both "hold" and "know" (of the wood).

**S09-V7-04** · The First Course: Three Goings, One Hour.
- NOTE: *orrol* means "a going; a manner; a road". Line 08 shows it means the fleet's waves or onsets here.

**S09-V7-05** · Hold the stone, Seren.

**S09-V7-06** · Through that short night the drums were beaten in the grey, like mallets in a far quarry. Through all of it Enno and Della sat in their house of one door. Enrella's sign lay beneath its first stone. Enno set the chisel, and Della struck. It stood one course high; higher than that, never.
- UNCERTAIN / NOTE: in *re runn et drunnatath* the verb (S·*drunn* "strike") has no person ending, but *et drunnatath* "the drums" is plural. A plural subject elsewhere in this text takes *-ant*, so I read the drums as the object of an unnamed striker ("[someone] beat the drums"). The lexicon says *et drunnatath* is the soldiers' word for the knocking out of the grey.
- NOTE: *lest hos gebb* is lit. "house [of] one door". *o dholm hosast* means "its first stone"; nothing shows whose (the house's, I take it). *Re hadh Enno et bresk* is lit. "Enno laid the chisel". *susk hy ull* is lit. "high from that", that is, higher than that.

**S09-V7-07** · Hand in hand the two of them came into that courtyard, where I came in alone.

**S09-V7-08** · At dawn the fleet came in three goings within one hour, and it broke the wall in the east and in the north. Every gate was in breach, and there were breaches still. Orvenna, the master-pair of the wall, who never cry out: the two of them built, speechless, a thing that masters had never built before.
- UNCERTAIN: *sa nath garma fedh* is NEG + N·*carm* "call, cry out" (3du) + *fedh* "time; ever". I read it as "who never cry out". It can also be read "who do not call time" (who never ask for a halt). It is present tense (no particle), so it states a habit.
- NOTE: *nawrodh* means "mute, speechless" (*na-* + S·*brodh*). *galat* means "a thing".

**S09-V7-09** · Orvenna parted a pair. (lit. "Orvenna loosed a gate apart, [one] from the other".)
- UNCERTAIN: *ganna* is literally "a gate". The tongue's figure is that a pair is a gate (the pair-name is the lintel), and the next line sends Enno and Della to different breaches. So I read it as Orvenna sending a bonded pair apart. The bare reading, "loosed a gate apart", would mean taking a gate's posts apart, which fits nothing here.

**S09-V7-10** · Ormund gave the eastern breach to Enno, and Penna gave the north to Della. At the head of the stair Enno called over his shoulder: "[We] are as one man;" and Della threw the other [half] back to him, running: "with four hands, and no other."
- NOTE: *El hosen hos uld* is a copula with no subject: "[it] is like one man". *re rig* is S·*drig* "throw". *Rella* is *Della* softened after *lo*.
- UNCERTAIN: *et ullen lo* is lit. "the other at him". I read it as the other half of a saying split between the pair (the wedge antiphony of §3.9). *lo yemm garl* is lit. "at four hands" (S·*gemm*), and I read *lo* as "with", as in line 22. *eth nahos ullen* is "and none other".

**S09-V7-11** · The Bonded are seldom apart, and never far apart. A mile of wall is far.
- NOTE: the last sentence is lit. "is far, four-hundred-paces wall": the copula puts the predicate first, and *bost lusul* means "a mile".

**S09-V7-12** · In the east a shot took the wall, and Enno with it. I did not see it. Hale saw it.
- NOTE: *rik* is S·*grik* "catch, seize, take"; the softened *g* drops before a consonant. *eth Enno lo* is lit. "and Enno at it".

**S09-V7-13** · In the north I was at Della's side, and I had the mortar. She turned the last stone over, to cut the sign beneath it. She herself set the chisel, and she struck; and the hand did not shift.
- NOTE: *et lodh lom* is lit. "the mortar at me". *re hyl* is S·*kyl* "change; turn". *dem rytyl* is lit. "to the cutting of", that is, in order to cut. *ul o vimm* is lit. "in its low": under it, on its underside. *nath re varr et garl* uses S·*marr* "shift", with *et garl* "the hand" as subject (her hand).

**S09-V7-14** · Then the beating in the sky stopped.
- UNCERTAIN: *et drunn* is "the mallet; a blow". I read it as the drum-beat of line 06, which is the only beating in the scene.

**S09-V7-15** · She looked toward the east. Then she bowed a second time, and she cut the last stroke, and she laid the chisel beside the sign, and she lay down, face upon stone.
- NOTE: *ferr* means "bow, bend low; flinch". *pawast* means "second; again". The text tells of no first bow.

**S09-V7-16** · I laid my thumb on the mortar, as masons do. It had not set yet.
- NOTE: *hosen cadhant tolmardath* is lit. "as masons lay". *hyn* is S·*rhyn* "set fast".

**S09-V7-17** · She died.

**S09-V7-18** · Hale came running, and the word from the east with him; and he did not say it. No runner ever came [so] late.
- UNCERTAIN: *Nath re rarr haskard desir fedh* is lit. "not came a runner late ever". *desir* "late" carries no mark of degree. I read it as "never did a runner come so late". "No runner had ever come late" is also possible.

**S09-V7-19** · In the annals there is no other sign that a single hand finished. At evening I searched a long time for the stroke that Della cut alone.
- NOTE: *ryteth surr* means "the annals". *dhunn* is S·*tunn* "complete, finish". *hedh* means "the last light, evening".

**S09-V7-20** · Enrella's sign, whole, on the underside of the northern stone, as Seren saw it at the end of the light
- NOTE: this is a caption. It has no main verb and no closing mark, and Seren is named in the third person. *lo rask et baegyth* is lit. "at the end of the light" (S·*dask*).

**S09-V7-21** · You cannot know.
- NOTE: lit. "knowing (a fact) is not at you (pl.)", the negated "at" idiom. *los* is 2nd plural (the listeners). No object is given.

**S09-V7-22** · At nightfall Stannard carried Enno the whole mile and laid him at her side. With their own four hands Orvenna laid the northern stone over them, the sign downward. Idrenna, who had sealed Enrella, prayed over it in two halves:
- NOTE: *re hoska* is "lintel-ed (3du)", that is, sealed (wedded). *ul ba saed* is "in two halves" (*pa* nasalised after *ul*): the prayer is split between the pair.
- UNCERTAIN: *umo* means "upon him/it": over Enno or over the stone. It is not "over them two", which would be *umonta*.

**S09-V7-23** · God, you bonded the two into one, while neither had yet outlived the other.
- NOTE: *Vardh* is the vocative of *Mardh* (God), softened; the analyzer flags it as a probable vocative.
- UNCERTAIN: lit. "while not yet did one from-them-two live on". *hoss* is "breathe; live; outlive, live on", and *hos hyonta* is "one from the two of them". A weaker reading is "while not one breath had yet [gone] from them".

**S09-V7-24** · They had one cradle from you, and one stone from us; and you do not part them.
- NOTE: *dreskith* is N·*tresk* "cleave, separate" with the 2sg ending: a statement, "you do not cleave them two", which can be heard as a plea.

**S09-V7-25** · Over the stone Ormund began the old jest, and Penna finished it. Then the Captain spread a piece of his cloak, on a pike, over the two of them: the first for the Stonewrights. And they were his company. Kael stood by, and he counted them.
- NOTE: *re hadh ... et tolm hosast ul* is lit. "laid the first stone in", which is the idiom "began". *re hadh ... et hosk umo* is lit. "laid the capstone upon it", which is "finished". *dhyvullat* means "a jest" (unit addition).
- NOTE: *re esk ... saedel hy o hovv um hagess umonta* is lit. "covered a piece from his cloak upon a pike upon them two". *ew o hest sona* is a copula, "[it] was his company, they two", with S·*kest*.
- UNCERTAIN: *et hosast lo Stonwrytan* is lit. "the first at the Stonewrights". I take it as the first such honour given to Stonewrights; the text does not say what it is the first of.

**S09-V7-26** · "One," he said.

**S09-V7-27** · At the turning of the tide a plank of the Standard-Bearer came in. Rhyna went down to it, and I went at her side, with the lamp. On the wall above, where Enno had knelt, Halvard mended the breach while the masons sang *Two at the Gate*. No one took up the first call, before the comma or after it. At the second course the other [voice] came in, as though the first had been sung.
- NOTE: *hylyl* is S·*kylyl* "a turning". *crynir et movennerd* is a construct, "plank of the standard-bearer"; whether the Standard-Bearer is a man or a ship is not said here, and line 61 treats it as a hull. *hlenn* is S·*clenn* "mend". *hessel* means "a comma", the pause where the song's two voices change. *et trenn pawast* is "the second course" (verse). *re vellor* is S·*mellor* "come in".

**S09-V7-28** · Rhyna laid a hand on the grain, and she went pale. Above, Halvard went pale in the same breath, the trowel in his hand. He came down, and the two of them spoke it: the order of the whole fleet, ship bonded to ship.
- UNCERTAIN: *haral et scethan tunn* is best read as a construct, "the order (command) of the whole fleet": the head has no article and the possessor is definite. A reading as an imperative, "command the whole fleet", is grammatical but fits less well.

**S09-V7-29** · For a long time the two of them were silent. I asked what harm was upon them, but I think I knew it.
- NOTE: *re vessym* is S·*pess* "ask". *kaeverym* means "I think" (present).

**S09-V7-30** · So we build a wall.
- NOTE: *hosen sy* is lit. "like this".

**S09-V7-31** · The one waits for the other's stroke, and needs no telling.
- NOTE: lit. "and need of telling is not upon him" (*doss maver um*, negated).

**S09-V7-32** · The carving named that tide: Aelthae. That night I knew the first half. *Ael* I had written once, in the Ael'thar. I wrote the name, and I do not write what I thought.
- NOTE: these are Mystaeri loans, read from the lexicon: *Aelthae* is "the Joined Tide"; *ael* is "the joining" (a Seilrhass word, written by sound); *Ael'thar* is "the rite of the binding", with the knock.
- UNCERTAIN: in *Re voll et rytet et hyll ull* I read *et hyll ull* as the object ("named that tide"). It may instead be an adverb of time, like *vess ull* "that night" in the next sentence: "that tide, the carving gave a name".
- NOTE: *nath rytym* is present: "I do not (will not) write".

**S09-V7-33** · On Halvard's trowel the mortar was setting.
- NOTE: *wirnenn* is S·*birnenn* "trowel". *ul rhynyl* is the progressive, "in setting".

**S09-V7-34** · This I lay as it was laid for me.

**S09-V7-35** · And the hearth said: We remember.

**S09-V7-36** · The Second Course: What We Taught Them.

**S09-V7-37** · In spring, on a grey morning, a fleet ran upon the wall in the old way, every ship alone, and loud, and true. Then the whole wall laughed, and every gun spoke upon it, Pellow's battery among the rest.
- NOTE: *ul et orrol hemm* is lit. "in the old going": the old manner. *venn* means "true; plumb", so "straight on its course". *umo* is "upon it" (the fleet). *Vellow* is S·*Pellow* as possessor; the analyzer's "singing-place" is a misparse.

**S09-V7-38** · Pellow of the Spire, the youngest: he had two words for the men of the wall, for each one.
- NOTE: *Vell* is S·*pell* "spire". The lexicon warns that it also sounds like *vell* "song". *et tevel sost* is "the very young [one]", the youngest. *re yal pa wrod lo* is lit. "there were two words at him".

**S09-V7-39** · Look close.
- NOTE: lit. "see near". This is the two words of line 38.

**S09-V7-40** · What came of that I have from Hale. When his guns spoke, Pellow raised the glass, and a sail went pale in it. Then the second. Then the third.
- NOTE: *re lymm ... et sirr dem susk* is lit. "brought the glass upward". *re stacorn wemm ulo* is "a sail paled in it".

**S09-V7-41** · They are not green.
- UNCERTAIN: this is a negative copula. Whether "green" means young or unseasoned wood (compare *mesk tevel*, "the young wood", in the lexicon) the line does not say.

**S09-V7-42** · Hale ran it. Behind him, Pellow turned his guns toward the grey, unbidden, and stood on the parapet with the glass.
- NOTE: *Re hask Hale o* is lit. "Hale ran it": he carried the word at a run. *ul o sestow* is "in his back", behind him. *hosk hald* means "parapet".

**S09-V7-43** · The Captain went along the wall, and at the side of each True Man, every one, he called: Shift your ground. He did not hurry, as a kindler does not hurry, or the fire dies.
- NOTE: *vynt* / *bynt* are S· and N·*pynt* "quick; hurry". "He" is the Captain (line 45 says so outright); *o* itself cannot show which man.

**S09-V7-44** · Out of the grey came the hunters, to the guns that had spoken, each one, and they did not leave them. Every battery he reached lived. One by one is slow.
- NOTE: *re hoss* is from *hoss* "breathe; live; live on". *sa re vellor o demo* is "that he came to". *El niss hos eth hos* is lit. "slow [is] one by one".

**S09-V7-45** · Pellow's battery was the farthest from him, and the nearest to the grey. They came to it first, and he stood in their face, alone. The Captain did not hurry.

**S09-V7-46** · Pellow lay dead at the parapet.

**S09-V7-47** · The glass lay at his hand, and it was whole.

**S09-V7-48** · At the turning of the tide Halyna laid a hand on a plank of that fleet.

**S09-V7-49** · Beneath it there was a carving: **⟦grain⟧**
- **UNREAD: ⟦grain⟧.** It is not an Orrowen word. It is a sign or placeholder standing for the drawn carving of the wood's grain, and it is outside the tongue, the grammar and the lexicon, so I cannot read it. The analyzer reports it as unknown. The rest of the line reads normally: "in its low there was a carving".

**S09-V7-50** · "The wood cannot lie," I said; and then I saw it. The wood does not lie. It had been bidden to lay a lie in our minds, and it obeyed.
- NOTE: *dem gadhol nycul* uses *cadh nycul* "deceive" (lit. "lay a lie"). *ul ol brener* is "in our minds" (N·*prener*). *lynir* means "obey".

**S09-V7-51** · They learned it from us.

**S09-V7-52** · We taught it to them with the quiet guns, each one.
- UNCERTAIN: *gor hos* means "each one, apiece". It may refer to every gun or to every one of the Mystaeri.

**S09-V7-53** · They had no word for "lie". We gave them one. In that carving was their word for us, cut small: Rhenear, the deaf.
- NOTE: *Rhenear* is a Mystaeri loan, "the stone-deaf" (lexicon). *et nahunn* is "the unhearing". *rytet lyss* is "cut small" or "a small carving".

**S09-V7-54** · At evening we built Pellow's cairn, and the Captain laid a piece over it. Then he gave the glass to Hale, and the battery with it.
- NOTE: *re esk ... saedel umo* is lit. "covered a piece upon it". Here "a piece" of what is not said; in line 25 it was of his cloak. *eth et bramman lo* is lit. "and the battery at him", so it may also read "and the battery to him".

**S09-V7-55** · "I am not a True Man," said Hale. "I run."

**S09-V7-56** · Then they went to Ruan of the Spire, his corporal, and the Captain made a True Man of him. He set the glass to his eye, and he looked beyond the cairn.
- NOTE: *re yald ... vennuld hyo* is lit. "built a True Man from him". *o hesterd* is "his corporal" (S·*kesterd*); "his" is most likely Pellow's. *Ruan Vell* is "Ruan of the Spire" (unit addition). *lo et veskow* is "at the eye".

**S09-V7-57** · "Look close," he said.

**S09-V7-58** · This I lay as it was laid for me.

**S09-V7-59** · And the hearth said: We remember.

**S09-V7-60** · The Third Course: The Plan That Carved Defeat upon Itself.
- NOTE: lit. "a plan that cut defeat upon itself".

**S09-V7-61** · In snow to the knee a standard-bearer came, with a whole morning carved in its heart. We sank it before noon, and the tide brought the heart to the shingle.
- UNCERTAIN: *eth ryst tunn rytet ul o volt* has no verb. I read it as "and a whole morning carved (*rytet*, the participle) in its heart", that is, a whole morning's plan cut into it. *volt* is S·*molt*. Since it is sunk (*felm* "drown; sink"), the standard-bearer here is a hull.

**S09-V7-62** · It was the longest holding Halyna ever held. Twice I filled the lamp before they had told it whole; and at its end there was this:
- NOTE: *kethyl* means "holding; (of the wood) a knowing". *sell sost* is "longest". *somm nath ... tul* is lit. "while not yet".

**S09-V7-63** · They carved defeat upon themselves in the plan.

**S09-V7-64** · There is one hand in all that carving, and it is not the hand of a council.

**S09-V7-65** · And the hand is young.

**S09-V7-66** · But it was the last two words that held us. The young wood knew home only as the place a wounded thing runs to. In this it was carved on purpose.
- NOTE: *rasken* is *dasken* "last" softened after the dual *pa wrod*. *sa re heth ol* is "that held us" (3sg verb, *ol* object). *nath ... veth* means "only". *hosen re yal kaeveret* is "on purpose" (lit. "as it was meant").

**S09-V7-67** · We three looked at one another over the lamp, as a wife looks up when a husband in a long fever asks for his boots.
- NOTE: *verr* means "wife", *humm* "husband", *suress* "fever". *viskhovvath* is *o* + S·*miskhovv* "boot" (unit addition), plural.

**S09-V7-68** · They carve the morning now, as we carve.
- UNCERTAIN: *ryst* is "the morning; the morrow". I read it as: they cut a plan for a morning (compare line 61), as we do.

**S09-V7-69** · So we will build a morning they did not carve toward.
- NOTE: *es naldar* is FUT + N·*galdar* "we will build". *sa nath re rytent demo* is lit. "that they did not cut to it".

**S09-V7-70** · The lamp went out on the shingle, and I did not fill it a third time.
- NOTE: *senn* means "die; go out (of a fire)".

**S09-V7-71** · This I lay as it was laid for me.

**S09-V7-72** · And the hearth said: We remember.

## VI.1 The Book of the Homecomings

**S09-VI1-01** · The Book of the Homecomings.
- NOTE: the lexicon gives this as Book Six.

**S09-VI1-02** · How the havens came home, and the war went home.
- NOTE: *re vymment* is S·*mymm* "return, come back" (3pl).

**S09-VI1-03** · Homecomings.

**S09-VI1-04** · The year of the homecomings, at ten fires.

**S09-VI1-05** · The True Men laid it at the fire in every one of the havens, on the night that haven's Throne fell; I laid each one beside it. Kael laid the first words and the last words at this hearth, when the tenth was home; and he counted the leaves I had written, before his first stone.
- NOTE: *ul et vess sa re omm et reskow strom ulo* is lit. "in the night that the great seat fell in it"; *ulo* refers back to the haven. *reskow strom* means "a throne".
- UNCERTAIN: *re hadhom gor hos lo o sest* is lit. "I laid every one at its/his side". Whose side (each True Man's telling? each man's?) is not shown.
- UNCERTAIN: *ul lern o dholm hosast* is lit. "in the face of his/its first stone". It can be read "in front of" or "before" his first stone, and the stone's owner is not shown. Line VI1-64 speaks of a second stone for Kael.

**S09-VI1-06** · Give me the stone. I do not sit; I will stand.
- NOTE: *Hebb et tolm lom* is lit. "give the stone at me".

**S09-VI1-07** · At the end of winter we went down by the old roads over the mountains: the True Men and their troops in front, the masons behind. All along the way the snow-water ran beside us. In every one of the havens a throne sat amid the fallen walls, and it waited.
- NOTE: *hryrullath* is *so* + S·*cryrull* "troop", plural. *nynth et sulv* is lit. "the spring/water of the snow", that is, meltwater. *re resk reskow strom* is lit. "a great seat sat".

**S09-VI1-08** · At this hearth each one of us swore upon the haven that was his. I am the oath-keeper, and I held all ten. Where a man had fallen, another spoke in his place.
- NOTE: *sa re yal lo* is lit. "that was at him". *re hethym et noth tunn* is "I held the whole ten". *lo o reskow* is lit. "at his seat", which means in his stead (unit addition).

**S09-VI1-09** · Now hear the names of the havens, and keep them, for we have them a second time.
- NOTE: *dossant lor pawast* is lit. "they are at us a second time".

**S09-VI1-10** · Eldhythe.
- NOTE: *Tavow Hemm* is lit. "the old landing".

**S09-VI1-11** · Voss, for Corlen.

**S09-VI1-12** · Here the first walls of the havens were laid, and here they stand a second time. The Tide-Heart drowned in the sea it came from, and it did not rise. Who will bring the first boat home? Corlen will bring it. He lies beneath the eastern cairns. So the boat came into my hand.
- NOTE: the question and answer are not marked as speech, and who asked is not said. *Es lymm Corlen* is the future, "Corlen will bring [it]". *Molt Hyll* is "the Tide-Heart", a Throne. *sulter* means "a small boat".

**S09-VI1-13** · I held a wet finger up, as he used to hold it, but the wind told me nothing. I brought it to the harbour on the evening tide, slowly. Then I waited, as he would wait, until the sea was full.
- NOTE: *hosen es dovv o* is a future, "as he will wait", which I give as "would".
- UNCERTAIN: *dem dunnol et wadh* is lit. "until the completing of the sea". I read it as high water.

**S09-VI1-14** · On the harbour stood my grandmother's house, and it had no roof. Above the cold hearth the three wide grey stones were still in the wall, and the lowest stone [was] marked where the ship-masters knocked out their pipes. They are in my pack tonight. They hung long enough above a dead fire.
- NOTE: *rellorat* is "marked", with no verb, so I supply the copula. *re runnant et higileth mygurath* is S·*drunn* "strike" (3pl): "where the ship-masters struck pipes". *higil* means "one who knows the water, a ship-master". *re haemerent fedh loskat* is lit. "they floated/hung time enough".
- UNCERTAIN: in *Dossant* ("they are") and *haemerent* ("they hung"), "they" is most likely the three stones; the pipes are grammatically possible.

**S09-VI1-15** · And the hearth said: We remember.

**S09-VI1-16** · Tidesmeet.
- NOTE: *Cemm Hyll* is lit. "the meeting of the tide".

**S09-VI1-17** · Tamm of the Three Spans.

**S09-VI1-18** · It had three heads, and we struck the three in the one hold, and none came back. Where the bridge stood in the midst of the three, the Bearer of Branches went down. ## Summary of marks

**Unread (1 sign).**

**Unknown words: none.** Every other word parsed against the lexicon plus the S09 additions. The analyzer flagged only *grain* as UNKNOWN.

**Possible text issue for the author:**
- S09-V7-06, *re runn et drunnatath*: if the drums are meant as the subject, the verb lacks the 3pl *-ant* that every other plural subject in this unit takes (*re rarrant et ganurardath*, *re vellent et tolmardath*). As written, it reads as an object with no named striker.
