**Analyzer result.** Every one of the 2,083 word tokens parses `OK`. No word is UNKNOWN or ILL-FORMED, and no mutation lacks its trigger. So nothing below is unreadable as a *form*. Every mark is about **sense**: an idiom I can parse but cannot pin down, or an ending with two possible readings.

**Conventions in this file**
- `[ ]` marks English I supplied that has no word in the Orrowen (articles, possessives, a dropped subject).
- `⟨?⟩` marks a reading I am not sure of. The note under the line says why.
- `{{Name}}` in the source is read as the **sealing lintel** over a pair-name (§3.10, §6.8). It is a sign, not a word, and I read it as "this is a pair-name". It appears on *Halyna* five times and on *Wendhessa* once.
- ` / ` in the source is read as a line break (in verse or a caption).
- Names are given in the Book's English where the additions list one: *Merrik* = Merrick, *Yory* = Jory. *Higil* (pl. *higileth*), "one who knows the water", is translated **ship-master**.
- Direct speech is not marked in the source. A colon is the only cue, and I keep the colon.

## IV.5

**S07-IV5-01** · *Sulter sa vorr*
The Boat that Burns *(title: "The Burning Boat")*.

**S07-IV5-02** · *Ul et syst pawast, ul Hyll Pawast.*
In the second summer, in the Second Tide.

**S07-IV5-03** · *Re hadh Voss sa re vess, hosen re wrodh scaelur Voss, Elspet, o lo, lo ey vorr um dhavow Dhavow Hemm. El trenn o sa nath gadhant et higileth, eth vell sa nath hessent et tevath vellyl; re dhyvull et Orrow umo, eth nath re dhyvull et wadh.*
Voss Who Asked laid [this tale], as Voss's grandmother, Elspet, told it to him by her fire on the harbour of Eldhythe. It is a tale that the ship-masters do not lay, and a song that the children do not stop singing. The Shore laughed at it, and the sea did not laugh.
- *o lo* means "it at him", so "told it to him". *um dhavow Dhavow Hemm* is "upon [the] harbour of Eldhythe" (*tavow* softened after *um*, and *Tavow Hemm* softened as the possessor).
- *gadhant*, "they lay", has the tale-sense: a tale is laid like a course. *umo* is "upon it", that is, "at it / about it".

**S07-IV5-04** · *Hebb et tolm lom. Nel en drenn sy. El trenn en scaelur; eth re yal o loy hy ey vost, eth re yal o lo hy et wadh.*
Give me the stone. This is not my tale. It is my grandmother's tale; she had it from her brother, and he had it from the sea.
- *re yal o loy* is "it was at her", so "she had it" (the "at" idiom).
- *Hebb et tolm lom* is a lexicon set phrase. Compare V1-04, *Keth et tolm, Seren* ("Hold the stone, Seren"): the teller seems to take the stone before speaking.

**S07-IV5-05** · *Ul gethyl hosast et gorrol sy, um et dhysal, re hunnom scethel ul vorrol. Re scyndal o. Lo dhrenn et hald re hylent et farrardath hemm lerneth hy et wadh, eth Merrik hosast. Um et hald rerddhalden re inthvell Yory.*
In the first Holding of this war, on the shingle, I heard a ship burning. It screamed. Along the wall the old sailors turned [their] faces from the sea, and Merrick first. On the eastern wall Jory whistled.
- *gethyl* is *kethyl* after *ul*. The lexicon gives "(et kethyl) the holding: the hour of the siege day when the grey sails come in", so I read it as the Holding. The plain sense "the first holding of this war" is also possible.
- *scethel*: "one hull, a single ship".

**S07-IV5-06** · *Re hyl tevvell ulom hosen bunth hunneryl. Amm re yal dhom surr lom, re hunnerym lo. Amm re yal pa vurr surr lom, re hyrrilym o.*
A children's rhyme turned in me like a skipping-rope. When I was seven, I skipped to it. When I was forty, I understood it.

**S07-IV5-07** · *Amm ew luld en lo ey genth, re vessym en scaelur hy vodh nath hunnant et higileth o. Re dhess ey lom; ew trenn o. Nydha o.*
When I was a boy at her table, I asked my grandmother why the ship-masters do not hear it. She answered me: it was a tale. Count it. ⟨?⟩
- ⟨?⟩ The *-a* of ***Nydha*** is either the imperative to many or the third-person dual. To one child the imperative would be bare *Nydh*, so I read it as a plural command, "count it [all of you]". If so, it may be addressed to the hall and not to the boy. *nydh* also means "reckon, weigh", so it could be "Weigh it."
- ⟨?⟩ Where her words begin is unmarked. *ew trenn o* could be her answer ("It was a tale") or the narrator's report.

**S07-IV5-08** · *Amm re yal delv surr loy, re heth ey et vorr ul davowlest ey odha. Re vellorant et higileth hy et helvnynth, eth et helv ul so vern. Ul susk et vorr re haemer sull tolm ryss myst, eth tolmath umont, bynt hosen miskryteth nasath um rull thenth. Re sesk nahos vodh re vyskent. Nath re vessent veth et tevath. Re runnant et higileth mygurath um et tolm vimm sost.*
When she was twelve, she kept the fire in her mother's harbour-house. The ship-masters came in out of the rain, with the sky in their hair. Above the fire hung three wide grey stones, with letters on them, fine as the tracks of birds on wet sand. No one knew what they said. Only the children asked. The ship-masters knocked [out their] pipes on the lowest stone.
- *tolmath* means "stones" or "letters". I read it as letters because of the birds' tracks. *haemer* means "float, hang". *nath … veth* is "only".
- *re runnant* is *drunn* softened ("knock, strike"). The line says only "knocked pipes upon the very low stone"; "out their" is mine.

**S07-IV5-09** · *Amm re vymm berderd hy et myst, eth trenn lo, re dhyvull ey, eth re losk ey et nunth. Kyl Mystow uldath venn dem nyculardath, re vysk ey.*
When a bold one came back from the grey with a tale, she laughed and filled the cup. The Mystlands turn true men into liars, she said.
- *eth trenn lo* is "and a tale at him", so "with a tale". *dem nyculardath* is "as far as liars", so "into liars".

**S07-IV5-10** · *Re vysk ey o um ey vost.*
She said it of her brother.
- *um* "upon" here means "about, concerning".

**S07-IV5-11** · *Ew gostard Daveth ul et gostol. Ul et syst ull re yal o ul mymmyl dem varn hy lern et myst, ul hos hy et sultereth dasken et mesk domm, vimm ul et wadh; eth sulter pawast ul o sestow.*
Daveth was an axeman in the felling. That summer he was coming home from the face of the grey, in one of the last boats of the black wood, low in the water, and a second boat behind it.
- *ul mymmyl* is "in homecoming", the progressive. *mesk domm* is "the black wood" (the lexicon adds: the timber of the black pillars). *ul o sestow* means "behind it", or "at its stern".

**S07-IV5-12** · *Re rarr sulter umonta hy et myst, ul vorr, ul lern et helvhoss eth ul lern et kess; nath noss darrol hosen sy lo sulter. Ul scyndalol re rarr o, hosen darrant wadhnasath um luldath sa rikent hy nasow. Vimm re runn o umonta, ul vodh re haemer et havul nalelv sost, eth re rik et mesk domm sa re lymme et vorr hyo. Ul sestow re rarr et helvnynth, um wisketh pa hryrull.*
A boat came upon the two of them out of the grey, on fire, against the wind and against the current. No boat can come like that. Screaming it came, as seabirds come down on boys who take from a rookery. Low it struck upon the two, where the heaviest cargo rode, and the black wood that the two were carrying caught the fire from it. Behind came the rain, onto the heads of two crews.
- *umonta* is "upon them two" (the two boats). *nath noss darrol hosen sy lo sulter* is "a coming like this is not at a boat", so "a boat cannot come so".
- *re runn* is *drunn* softened ("a blow, strike"). *re haemer* means "rode, floated".
- *re rik et mesk domm sa re lymme et vorr hyo* follows verb–subject–object order: "the black wood that the two bore caught the fire from it". *pa hryrull* is "two" with *cryrull* ("troop, crew") softened.

**S07-IV5-13** · *Re vymment sull dem varn.*
Three came home.

**S07-IV5-14** · *Re vymm Daveth dem varn um vivenn cresket, eth et wadh ul vidhyl hy o hovv um et pressir sa re vidh en scaelur. Hosen ull re wrodh ey o lom. Ul et bremm ull tunn re wrodhant et sull o, eth nafedh pawast re hadhant misk ul sulter.*
Daveth came home on a broken mast, with the sea pouring from his cloak onto the floor that my grandmother had scrubbed ⟨?⟩. That is how she told it to me. All that autumn the three told it, and never a second time did they set foot in a boat.
- ⟨?⟩ ***re vidh*** has two readings: softened *midh* "clean", or unmutated *vidh* "pour". I take "clean", because "the floor that she poured" makes no sense.
- *Hosen ull* is "like that". *re hadhant misk* is "they laid foot".

**S07-IV5-15** · *Hy ull re rytent uldath et deldvar lo so nunthath um sulter hastat ul vorr, eth uld sennet ulo. Ul neld, re rytent, re hyl o. Um et scyndal re rytent mest hy gor yalat, eth um sull brod orrowen sa re vysk et uld myst; veth um vodh sull, nath re hemme fedh pa hyont.*
After that, the men of the outpost swore over their cups to a boat pushed out on fire, with a dead man in it. Far out, they swore, it turned. To the scream they swore more than to anything, and to three Orrowen words that the grey man said. But as to which three, no two of them ever agreed.
- *lo so nunthath* is "at their cups". *ryt … um* is "swear upon, swear to".
- *nath re hemme fedh pa hyont* is "two of them did not once meet". *cemm* means "meet; agree" (the lexicon: "the tales do not meet").

**S07-IV5-16** · *Re nydh ey et sultereth lom um ey nindeth, hosen re nydh Daveth so loy. Hos nind, eth ullen. Hy ull re hadh ey garl ryss um et genth.*
She counted the boats for me on her fingers, as Daveth had counted them for her. One finger, and another. Then she laid her hand flat on the table.
- *garl ryss* is "a wide hand", that is, spread flat. *ey* never mutates the next word, so this is "her hand" or "she [laid] a hand".

**S07-IV5-17** · *Re yal pa sulter.*
There were two boats.

**S07-IV5-18** · *Amm sy myskent et higileth: murr.*
Now the ship-masters say: twenty.

**S07-IV5-19** · *Re dhyvull et Orrow um et sull hy et mivenn. Ul morl re dhyvull ey lo, eth re losk ey et nunthath ul vimm et tolm vimm sost.*
The Shore laughed at the three from the mast. By day she laughed at it, and filled the cups beneath the lowest stone.
- *et sull hy et mivenn*, "the three from the mast", seems to mean the three who came home on it (IV5-13, 14). *ul vimm et tolm vimm sost* is "under the very low stone", probably the lowest of the three stones over the fire (IV5-08).

**S07-IV5-20** · *Ul vess nath re dhyvull ey.*
At night she did not laugh.

**S07-IV5-21** · *Re fimm Daveth um o dhrenn, eth re felm et tavow o, hos drigyl dholm hy lest o odha.*
Daveth drank over his tale ⟨?⟩, and the harbour drowned him a stone's throw from his mother's house.
- ⟨?⟩ ***um o dhrenn*** is literally "upon his tale (or course)". *um* can mean "about, because of", so "he drank on account of his tale" is possible. The exact sense of drinking "upon" a tale is not given anywhere I can read.
- *hos drigyl dholm* is "one throwing of a stone", a stone's throw.

**S07-IV5-22** · *Hy vodh nath hunnant et higileth o? Re veskym higil sa re hadh et nunth, eth re orr ul helvnynth, hy vodh nath re vaver o hunnol et trenn. Hy ull re hunnom sull hyont, ul et hiskenneth, gor wrod cadhat hosen nunth.*
Why do the ship-masters not hear it? I saw a ship-master set down the cup and go out into the rain, because he did not want to hear the tale. After that I heard three of them in the passes ⟨?⟩, every word set down like a cup.
- ⟨?⟩ ***hiskenneth***: the lexicon's only sense is "a pass through mountains". "In the mountain passes" sits oddly with ship-masters in a haven. The form is certain; the place is not.
- *sull hyont*, "three of them", are presumably ship-masters. They might be the three from the mast (IV5-13); the next line gives their three versions.
- *gor wrod cadhat hosen nunth* is "every word laid like a cup", that is, set down with care.

**S07-IV5-23** · *Sulter lyss ul vorr, ul neld ul et myst, re vysk et hosast. Et pawast: Re hyl o ul lern et helvhoss, eth ul sestow, helv. Et sullast: El et sulter o sa rarr demos amm sennys. Eth et hosast, pawast: Luska et Orrow ul dhyvullol, hy vodh nafedh es narr o dem et Orrow.*
A small boat on fire, far out in the grey, said the first. The second: It turned into the wind, and behind it, sky. The third: It is the boat that comes to you when you die. And the first, a second time: Leave the Shore to its laughing, for it will never come to the Shore.
- *ul sestow, helv* is "in the back, sky", meaning nothing behind it but sky. *Luska* is the plural imperative of *lusk*, "loose, let be"; literally "loose the Shore in laughing".

**S07-IV5-24** · *Re yal o rytet lo et hinnarath: Besk hespet, hurrat. Sulter vorrat, luskat. Eth cedh brodyorr ul lern ryt?*
The walled towns had it written ⟨?⟩: A stranger with a sword, killed. A boat burned, set loose. And who argues against what is cut?
- ⟨?⟩ *re yal o rytet lo et hinnarath* is literally "it was [a] thing cut at the walled towns". With the "at" idiom it means "the towns had it inscribed". It could also mean "it was cut at (by) the walled towns".
- The two rulings are bare participles, terse like an inscription. *ryt* is "a cut vow, an inscription".

**S07-IV5-25** · *Veth re yal tevvell umo lo et tevath, eth brodyorrant tevath ul lern gor yalat. Re hunnerer lo um et tavow, pa dhev ul gylyl eth hos ul hunneryl. Re hylen et bunth, Yory eth en, hy vodh ew et strom sost eth et niss sost olna; eth lo et lyss sost hyont re hyl Yory o mest niss.*
But the children had a rhyme about it, and children argue against everything. We skipped to it on the harbour, two children turning and one skipping. Jory and I turned the rope, because we two were the biggest and the slowest; and for the smallest of them Jory turned it slower still.
- *re hylen* is first-person dual. *lo et lyss sost hyont* is "at the very small one of them", so "for the smallest". *mest niss* is "much slow", so "slower".

**S07-IV5-26** · *Hy ull re vellent et tevath, hosen vellent o tul:*
Then the children sang, as they sing it still:

**S07-IV5-27** · *Sulter myst, sulter myst, hy et myst, / re lumm lo yebb et pard hos ryst; / stinel dem ninth, tolm dem hebbet, / eth cemmyl hosen sy, nahunnat! / Vorra o, hasta o, hebba o dem hyll; / veth re harm sulter myst, eth re hyl.*
Grey boat, grey boat, out of the grey, / knelt at the Warden's door one morning; / a stroke to the lip ⟨?⟩, a stone for a gift ⟨?⟩, / and a greeting like this, unheard of! / Burn it, push it, give it to the tide; / but the grey boat called, and turned.
- *re lumm* ("knelt") has no subject but the grey boat. *yebb et pard* is "the warden's door" (the lexicon lists *Gebb et Pard* as the title of IV.3).
- ⟨?⟩ ***stinel dem ninth*** is literally "a stroke as far as [the] lip". It looks like a gesture, perhaps a finger laid to the lips, but nothing I have defines it.
- ⟨?⟩ ***tolm dem hebbet*** is "a stone to/for [a] gift (given)"; *dem* means "until, to, for". I read "a stone for a gift" (or "a stone held out").
- *Vorra, hasta, hebba* are plural imperatives. *re harm* is *carm* softened, "called, cried".

**S07-IV5-28** · *Re yal pa dhrenn ullen, sa nath re veller, hy ludal hunnol higil.*
There were two more lines, which we did not sing, for fear of a ship-master hearing [them].

**S07-IV5-29** · *Sulter myst ul vorr, eth helvnynth ul sestow; / eth nath mymm dem varn et hos sa rarr o demo.*
Grey boat on fire, and rain behind; / and the one it comes to does not come home.

**S07-IV5-30** · *Ul hos hedh re hunn Merrik o hyor. Re rik o ol munth, eth re hyl o um o sestyarl, hosen kylent uldath bunth sa haeverent kethyl. Nel ull, re vysk o.*
One evening Merrick heard it from us. He took our rope and coiled it on his arm, the way men coil a rope they mean to keep. Not that one, he said.
- *re hyl o um o sestyarl* is "turned it upon his arm". *sa haeverent kethyl* is "which they think/intend [for] keeping". *Nel ull* is "[it] is not that"; I take "that" to be the rhyme.

**S07-IV5-31** · *Ew Daveth o yedh.*
Daveth was his father.

**S07-IV5-32** · *Resk Merrik, et higil hemm sost, lo et gebb vess sy. Nath re orr o.*
Merrick, the oldest ship-master, sits by the door tonight. He has not gone out.
- *Nath re orr o* is "he did not go". It seems set against the ship-master of IV5-22 who went out into the rain.

**S07-IV5-33** · *Ul et grem hosast tunn re inthvell Yory o um et hald rerddhalden, et hunnat eth nafedh et brodath. Re haever o fimmyl pawast lo vorr en scaelur, amm es mymm et cummath dem varn. Gor ryst re vysk Merrik lo: Hess; eth gor ryst re dhyvull Yory: El hosel tevvell o.*
All the first winter Jory whistled it on the eastern wall, the tune and never the words. He meant it for a second drinking ⟨?⟩ at my grandmother's fire, when the havens come home. Every morning Merrick said to him: Stop. And every morning Jory laughed: It's only a rhyme.
- ⟨?⟩ ***Re haever o fimmyl pawast*** is literally "he thought/intended a second drinking". It could mean that Jory meant to drink again at her fire when the havens returned, or that he meant the tune as a second toast there. *kaever* means "think; mean, intend".

**S07-IV5-34** · *Ul et ryst sa re senn o, re yalom lo o sest, eth re orr et hunnat ul o lern lo dhrenn et haldorrol. Niss re inthvell o, hosen re hyl o et bunth lo et lyss sost hyor. Hy ull re dhald et tevvell ulom, duss hosen bunth bysket ul hyll losk. Re hunnom et pa dhrenn sa nafedh re veller.*
On the morning he died, I was at his shoulder, and the tune went before him along the wall-walk. He whistled slowly, the way he had turned the rope for the smallest of us. Then the rhyme rose in me, hard as a rope knotted in a full tide. I heard the two lines that we never sang.
- *ul o lern* is "in his face", so "ahead of him". *bunth bysket* is "a knotted rope", or "a rope's knot".

**S07-IV5-35** · *Re vysk Merrik lo: Hess.*
Merrick said to him: Stop.

**S07-IV5-36** · *Nath re vyskym lo: Hess.*
I did not say to him: Stop.

**S07-IV5-37** · *Ul vimm et haldorrol re vellor sceth ul vorr um et tolmath. Re scyndal o somm re rarr o.*
Below the wall-walk a ship on fire came in onto the stones. It screamed as it came.

**S07-IV5-38** · *Re hess et hunnat.*
The tune stopped.

**S07-IV5-39** · *Sol o ul et stell hosast um et lern rerddhalden.*
He rests in the first cairn on the eastern face.
- The text has only *o*, "he". From IV5-34 I take it to be Jory.

**S07-IV5-40** · *Amm ston lest en scaelur tul, haemer et sull tolm ryss myst ul susk odh sennet, eth nath noss crisur lont veth et helvnynth.*
Where ⟨?⟩ my grandmother's house still stands, the three wide grey stones hang over a dead hearth, and they have no roof but the rain.
- ⟨?⟩ ***Amm*** means "when; if". Literally the line says "When (if) my grandmother's house still stands". "Where" is my English. *nath noss crisur lont veth* is "no roof is at them but", so "they have no roof but".

**S07-IV5-41** · *Nadhumar. Gor fedh re nadhumar gell. Veth tum Mesk Myst.*
We forget. We have always forgotten well. But the Mystwood remembers.
- *Gor fedh* is "every time", so "always".

**S07-IV5-42** · *Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom.*
This I lay as it was laid for me. *(literally: "I lay it as it was laid at me")*

**S07-IV5-43** · *Eth re vysk et odh: Tumar.*
And the hearth said: We remember.

## Book Five, head

**S07-B5-01** · *Flennath et Hyll*
The Book of the Tide.
- The lexicon's set phrase reads "Book Five: the Book of the Tides". The line itself has no "five", and *Hyll* is singular (the plural would be *hylleth*).

**S07-B5-02** · *Et gorrol: trenn sa re luskant et sennetath, sa yarlant et hossenath, ul lern lunt sa nafedh re sesk: El et bynd ol.*
The War: a tale that the dead set loose, which the living have in hand, against a people that never knew: We are the foe. ⟨?⟩
- ***El et bynd ol***: the copula puts the predicate first, and *ol* stands **after** the noun. That makes it the subject pronoun "we", so the clause reads "the foe [is] we", **We are the foe**. It is not the possessive "our foe", which would be *ol mynd* (*ol* before the noun, nasalising it). I am confident of the grammar.
- ⟨?⟩ How the clause attaches is not clear. It may be what the people "never knew", or the tale's own verdict after the colon. *ul lern* may mean "against" or "before, in the face of".
- *yarlant* is *garl* ("use, handle") softened after *sa*, third-person plural. *hossenath* is "those of breath", the living.
- **Form note:** *sennetath* and *hossenath* take the broad plural *-ath* on slender stems (*sennet*, *hossen*), where §3.2 harmony predicts *senneteth* and *hosseneth*. The meaning is unaffected and the analyzer accepts both forms, but this may be a slip or an unlisted irregular.

## V.1

**S07-V1-01** · *Rellor Hosast*
The First Sign *(the lexicon has "The First Glyph")*.

**S07-V1-02** · *Ul et grem hosast, lo rask wress hosast.*
In the first winter, at the end of the first month.

**S07-V1-03** · *Re hadh Seren Pa Luth, sa re heth et greller. Doss grull et dhysal ull ul lodh et flennath sy tul.*
Seren Two-Inks, who held the lamp, laid [this tale]. The sand of that shingle is still in the mortar of this Book.
- *ul lodh* is "in the mortar of", that is, "between", so the sand is still between [the leaves of] this Book.

**S07-V1-04** · *Keth et tolm, Seren.*
Hold the stone, Seren.

**S07-V1-05** · *Um et dhysal re hethe {{Halyna}} et mesk, eth re hethym et greller: gemm garl lonta um et meskdhrenn, eth hos lom. Hosen ull re hadhar et tolm hosast; eth hosen ull ser o tul.*
On the shingle Halyna held the wood, and I held the lamp: four hands of theirs on the grain, and one of mine. So we laid the first stone; and so it goes on still.
- *re hethe* is third-person dual of *keth*, "hold; (of the wood) know". *gemm garl lonta* is "four hands at them two". *cadh et tolm hosast*, "lay the first stone", means "begin".

**S07-V1-06** · *Hos bress, ell virr, re solant et wemmeth myst ul lern et hald sy, eth re sesk nahos hyor vodh ew so. Ul et vess sa re yal et kiser hosast cresket, re lymm et hyll et kaelir ul vimm et hald: crynireth myst, crestalath cresket, breller sell hosen ganna. Re scaevur o ul et venth fedh eth fedh, hosen amm re hyl et wadh gor saedel dem veskyl et vimmlern.*
For a month or near it the grey sails lay still before this wall, and none of us knew what they were. On the night the first assault was broken, the tide brought the wreck under the wall: grey planks, broken ribs, a keel as long as a gate. It rolled in the shallows time after time, as though the sea were turning every piece over to show its underside.
- *dem veskyl et vimmlern* is "to the sight of the underside".

**S07-V1-07** · *Nath re orrant et farrardath hemm hy et hald. Re orrant et hesperdeth, eth re hadhant vorrath hyo um et hylldhrenn, hy vodh ew merr et mesk um et sorth. Vorra o, re vysk Harl Orrow sa Vorr. Re hresk o hos crestal hyo um o yerd, eth re hebb o dem et vorr. Hos eth hos re dhald et vorrath, hosen taldant grellereth lo dhrenn dhavow ul hedh. Re lusk et Crenn so ul vorrol et saed strom hyo, eth re ston o ul so lodh, sol.*
The old sailors did not leave the wall. The soldiers went, and laid fires of it along the tide-line, because wood was scarce on the ridge. Burn it, said Harl of the Burning Shore. He broke a rib off it over his knee, and gave it to the fire. One by one the fires rose, as lamps rise along a harbour's line at dusk. The Captain let them burn the greater half of it, and it ⟨?⟩ stood among them, still.
- *orr hy* means "leave". *vorrath hyo* is "fires from it" (from the wreck).
- *et saed strom hyo* is "the half great from it". I read it as "the greater half".
- ⟨?⟩ In ***re ston o ul so lodh, sol*** the *o* may be "it" (the unburned half, standing among the fires) or "he" (the Captain, standing among the soldiers). *sol* may be an adjective ("still, at rest") or a verb ("rest"). I read "and it stood among them, still".

**S07-V1-08** · *Somm re hyn et vorrath hosast, re rarr Hale Haskard dem susk hy et hylldhrenn. Lo sest et vorrath re orr o, eth lo sest et Crenn, dem {{Halyna}}.*
While the first fires were catching, Hale the Runner came up from the tide-line. He went past the fires, and past the Captain, to Halyna.
- *re hyn* is *rhyn* softened, "set fast, take hold". *lo sest* is "at the shoulder of", so "beside, past".

**S07-V1-09** · *Rellorath ul et mesk.*
Signs in the wood.

**S07-V1-10** · *Lo hylyl et hyll re orra {{Halyna}} dem vimm, garl Halvard ul vimm sestyarl Hyna um et tolmath thenth. Re rarrom ul sestow, eth et greller lom; hy vodh ew sullast {{Halyna}} en, eth re yal lymmyl et baegyth lom. Re wammant et uldath hos crynir dem susk hy et spant, eth re hadhom et greller lo.*
At the turn of the tide Halyna went down, Halvard's hand under Rhyna's arm on the wet stones. I came behind with the lamp, for I was Halyna's third, and the bringing of the light was mine. The men pulled a plank up out of the surf, and I set the lamp by it.
- *sullast* is "the unbonded third of a hearth". *lymmyl et baegyth lom* is "the bringing of the light [was] at me".

**S07-V1-11** · *Lo dhrenn o veskdhrenn re orr hos rellor, bynt eth venthen hosen stinel hosast dholmard. Ew clenn hos cless sell, treskat lo dhrenn et meskdhrenn, hosen amm re yal et ullen treskat hyo.*
Along its grain ran a sign, fine and shallow, like a mason's first stroke. One long edge was new, split along the grain, as though the other part had been torn away from it.
- *o veskdhrenn* is *meskdhrenn* softened after *o*, "its grain".

**S07-V1-12** · *Hy yalatath sa ross brodhol lont, re yal lor et tolmath nawrodh ul vestul rytow, eth havul ul vimm hosk {{Wendhessa}}, sa nath re nahosk hos ganna. Amm sy re yal et crynir lor. Re rytent gostardath et gostol lo so nunthath: Hebb et mesk domm kaever ul narl uld, amm sol o yarl umo loskat. Re dhyvull et Orrow umont. Re yal nayalat ullen lor dem raevulol.*
Of things that could speak ⟨?⟩, we had the mute stones in the archive chest, and a bundle under the seal of Wendhessa, which no pair had unsealed. Now we had the plank. The axemen of the felling swore over their cups: The black wood puts a thought into a man's hand, when his hand rests on it long enough. The Shore laughed at them. We had nothing else to go on.
- ⟨?⟩ ***Hy yalatath sa ross brodhol lont*** is literally "from the things which telling is at them", that is, things able to tell. *hy* "from, out of" I read as "of". I take the line as wry: the things that might have spoken were mute.
- *hosk* is "lintel", here "seal". *hos ganna*, "one gate", means one pair. *loskat* is "enough" ("filled"). *dem raevulol* is "toward seeking", so nothing else to search by.

**S07-V1-13** · *Hy ull re resk Rhyna lo et crynir, eth re hadh ey pa yarlremull ryss um et meskdhrenn. Re lumm Halvard ul sestow, eth re hadh o pa yarl umont. Nath re wrodha. Re rarr et hyll, eth re orr. Re vorr et greller dem vimm ryssyth nind.*
Then Rhyna sat by the plank and laid her two palms flat on the grain. Halvard knelt behind and laid his two hands on them. They did not speak. The tide came in and went out. The lamp burned down a finger's breadth.

**S07-V1-14** · *Hy ull re dhald et kethyl ul narlremullath Hyna. Re vorr o, re vysk ey, nel hosen vorr, veth hosen dusstyvil brid ul molt et grem. Ul et vorrol re yal et lern: hos stinel ul gylyl dem lern, hosen fodh sa es gresk. Nath re wrodh ey tul, eth re wemm Halvard o wisk.*
Then the knowing rose in Rhyna's palms. It burned, she said, not like fire but like cold iron in the heart of winter. In the burning was the shape: one stroke turning forward, like a wave about to break. She had not yet spoken when Halvard lifted his head.
- *kethyl* here is "(of the wood) a knowing". *lern* means "face; shape". The last sentence is literally "she had not yet spoken, and Halvard lifted his head".

**S07-V1-15** · *Orrow, re vysk o.*
Shore, he said.

**S07-V1-16** · *Re vysk Rhyna: El venn o.*
Rhyna said: It is true.
- *el venn o* is a set phrase: "it means it, it is in earnest".

**S07-V1-17** · *Nath re yal myskyl lo Halvard hosen vodh re heth o o. Ew hosen amm re hadh hos et brod ul o wrodow, re vysk o, eth re orr dem neld. Re hethe mest hy et brod; veth ew kaever lurr et ullen, eth nath re yal veth kaever et orrow lonta. Re hethe o somm re vorr et greller dem vimm, eth nath re yal brodhol lonta veth hos brod.*
Halvard could not say how he knew it. It was as though someone had laid the word in his mouth, he said, and gone away. They knew more than the word, but the rest was a forest's thought ⟨?⟩, and they had no thought but the shore's ⟨?⟩. They held it while the lamp burned down, and they could tell only one word.
- ⟨?⟩ ***kaever lurr*** is "a thought of forest", and ***kaever et orrow*** is "the thought of the shore". Read literally, the rest of what they knew was in the forest's (the wood's) kind of thought, and they themselves had only the shore's. *orrow* is lower-case and could also stand for the word "shore" itself. Nothing I have says which.

**S07-V1-18** · *Re dhalda niss hy et tolmath, eth re rarrant ullen dem vimm dem raevulol. Hosast re rarr Yory Dhavow Hemm, sa resk vess sy ul sestow et hellur sy, lo sest Voss. Re hadh o yarl umo, ul dhyvullol. Crynireth thenth, re vysk o, eth re orr o dem susk et dhysal ul ninthvellyl, et hunnat eth nafedh et brodath.*
They rose slowly from the stones, and others came down to try. First came Jory of Eldhythe, who sits tonight at the back of this hall, beside Voss. He laid his hand on it, laughing. Wet planks, he said, and went up the shingle whistling, the tune and never the words.
- *re dhalda* is third-person dual (Halyna), past. *dem raevulol* is "to seek, to try".
- **Reader's flag (continuity, not language):** here Jory "sits tonight" in the hall beside Voss. In IV.5 he whistles on the morning he dies (IV5-34) and rests in the first cairn (IV5-39). Both lines read cleanly. Whether one telling night comes before the other is for the authors.

**S07-V1-19** · *Hy ull re rarra Enno eth Della, sa re orra dem susk et brunn garl ul narl. Re hadha gemm garl um et crynir olen. Re vysk o: El brid et mesk; eth re vysk ey: Nel; eth ew venn pana.*
Then came Enno and Della, who had gone up the mountain hand in hand. They laid four hands on the plank together. He said: The wood is cold. She said: It is not. And both were true.

**S07-V1-20** · *Amm re orra, re reskym ul vodh re resk Rhyna. Hosel re hadhom en narlremull sost um et meskdhrenn. Re dhald galat ulo hy et mesk, strom hy hethyl. Ew rhull losk o, eth nemm en, eth nunth lom.*
When they had gone, I sat where Rhyna had sat. Alone, I laid my own palm on the grain. Something rose into it ⟨?⟩ from the wood, too great to hold. It was a river in flood, and I was a girl with a cup.
- ⟨?⟩ ***ulo*** is "in him / in it". I read it as "into it (my palm)". "In me" would be *ulom*.
- *strom hy hethyl* is "greater than holding". *eth nunth lom* is "and a cup [was] at me".

**S07-V1-21** · *Re luskom.*
I let go.

**S07-V1-22** · *Re hadh Halvard et greller ul en narl pawast.*
Halvard put the lamp in my other hand. *(literally "my second hand")*

**S07-V1-23** · *Nath re orrar dem susk tul; re lunnom et flennath sy um et gerdeth, lo flenn midh, veth ew merr et flenneth amm ull. Ul molt et flenn re hadhom hos brod, orrow, eth nayalat ullen. Nath re femm et luth o tul, eth re vyskym o sull fedh, hosen myskym gor wrod, dem hunnol ho heth o. Re heth o. Ew nasell o, eth gemmyorn; eth re resk o ul et brodow hosen tolm ul et lodh.*
We had not yet gone up. I opened this Book on my knees at a clean leaf; but there were few leaves then. In the middle of the leaf I laid one word, shore, and nothing else. The ink had not yet touched it, and I said it three times, as I say every word, to hear whether it holds. It held. It was short, and square, and it sat in the mouth like a stone in mortar.
- *lunn* here is the verb "open". *flenneth* is the ordinary plural "leaves", distinct from *Flennath*, "the Book". *ho heth o* is "whether it holds".
- In the sentence order, the word is "laid" before the ink touches it. I read this as: she set the word down and sounded it before she wrote it.

**S07-V1-24** · *Um et flenn ul lern re rytym et rellor. Ul helv et wadh nath re wroc et luth.*
On the facing leaf I drew the sign. In the sea air the ink did not dry.
- *ul lern* is "in front, facing". *helv et wadh* is "the sky of the sea". *re wroc* is *broc* ("dry") softened.

**S07-V1-25** · *Ew lyss sost et brod um o flenn.*
The word was very small on its leaf.

**S07-V1-26** · *Re rarrar dem susk, eth et vorrath vimm ul sestow. Lo et ganna re hesse {{Halyna}}.*
We came up, with the fires low behind us. At the gate Halyna stopped.

**S07-V1-27** · *Re rarr uld myst et trenn hemm dem nebb, eth re yal sull brod lo, eth nath re yal trenn.*
The grey man of the old tale came to a door, and he had three words, and he had no tale.
- *uld myst et trenn hemm* is a construct, "the grey man of the old tale". *dem nebb* is *gebb* nasalised after *dem*, "to a door".

**S07-V1-28** · *Eth doss hos brod lona.*
And we two have one word.
- *lona* is "at us two". The line does not say which two (perhaps Halyna, with their one word, *Shore*).

**S07-V1-29** · *Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom.*
This I lay as it was laid for me.

**S07-V1-30** · *Eth re vysk et odh: Tumar.*
And the hearth said: We remember.

**S07-V1-31** · *Doss et rellor um et flenn ul lern, ul narl Seren: hos stinel, ul gylyl dem lern, hosen fodh sa es gresk.*
The sign is on the facing leaf, in Seren's hand: one stroke, turning forward, like a wave that will break.

**S07-V1-32** · *Fodhrellor, hosen re ryt Seren o. / Orrow.*
The wave-sign, as Seren drew it. / Shore.

## The marks, gathered

**Words or signs I could not read as forms: none.** Every token parses against the lexicon and additions, and every mutation has its trigger.

**Signs read by convention, not by the lexicon**
- `{{ }}` is read as the sealing lintel over a pair-name: *Halyna* (V1-05, 08, 10 ×2, 26) and *Wendhessa* (V1-12).
- ` / ` is read as a line break (IV5-27, IV5-29, V1-32).

**Senses I read only doubtfully (⟨?⟩)**

| Line | Word or phrase | Why |
|---|---|---|
| IV5-07 | *Nydha o* | *-a* is the plural imperative or the third-person dual; it is unclear who is told to "count it" and where the grandmother's words begin |
| IV5-14 | *re vidh* | two parses: *midh* "clean" softened, or *vidh* "pour"; I take "clean" |
| IV5-21 | *um o dhrenn* | "drank upon his tale": the idiom is not defined |
| IV5-22 | *hiskenneth* | the only sense given, "mountain passes", fits a haven setting poorly |
| IV5-24 | *re yal o rytet lo et hinnarath* | "the towns had it inscribed" or "it was cut by the towns" |
| IV5-27 | *stinel dem ninth*, *tolm dem hebbet* | gestures ("a stroke to the lip", "a stone for a gift") that nothing defines |
| IV5-33 | *re haever o fimmyl pawast* | "he meant a second drinking": a plan to drink again, or a toast |
| IV5-40 | *Amm* | "when / if"; my "where" is a free rendering |
| B5-02 | *El et bynd ol* | the grammar gives "We are the foe" (confident); how it attaches after the colon is unclear |
| B5-02 | *sennetath*, *hossenath* | broad plural on slender stems, against §3.2 harmony (a form note) |
| V1-07 | *re ston o ul so lodh, sol* | whether *o* is "it" or "he", and whether *sol* is the adjective or the verb |
| V1-12 | *Hy yalatath sa ross brodhol lont* | "of things that could speak": *hy* stretched to "of" |
| V1-17 | *kaever lurr*, *kaever et orrow* | "a forest's thought" against "the shore's thought": the sense is not pinned down |

| V1-28 | *lona* | "we two" are not named |
