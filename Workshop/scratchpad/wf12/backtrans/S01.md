**S01-FRONT-02** The tales laid at the Long Hearth in Rivenkeep, as Seren Two-Inks laid them.

## Seren's leaf

**S01-F-00** Seren's Leaf.
- *Lit.* "the leaf of Seren". The lexicon lists this heading as Seren's foreword ("Of This Book").

**S01-F-01** The third summer, in the Tide of Remembering.

**S01-F-02** Seren Two-Inks wrote it, scribe of this Book, and the third of the hearth of Halyna.
- *sullast* means "a third; the unbonded third of a hearth". So she is the unbonded third of Halyna's hearth.

**S01-F-03** It is the short dark of the year **(?1)**. The hall sleeps all around me, and the Long Hearth has sunk down to embers. Upon the wall I see the banner lifting, in a wind that I do not hear.
- (?1) *Doss domm nasell et surr* is, *lit.*, "is dark short the year". I read *domm* as the noun "the dark", *nasell* as "short", and *et surr* as its possessor: "the year's short dark". That fits the short summer nights of F-01. The other reading is "the year is dark [and] short", which fits summer less well. The word order, with an adjective between the head and the possessor, is unusual either way.
- *um gor lern lom* means "on every side of me".
- *ul memmyl* is *lit.* "in lifting" (ul + N *bemmyl*), the progressive.

**S01-F-04** Half the Book is filled. One of its tellers **(?2)**, who lifted a wet finger to every wind, is already under the cairns of the east. Tonight I went back to the first leaf. I had left the leaf empty, because a lintel does not come before the gate **(?3)**.
- (?2) *hos hy o wrodhardath* is *lit.* "one from his/its tellers". The *o* could be "his" or "its". I take it as "its", meaning the Book's.
- *doss o ul vimm stelleth rerddhald tevar* is *lit.* "he is under the cairns (burial-field) of the east already".
- (?3) *nel hosk ul lern ganna* is *lit.* "is not lintel before gate".
  - *ul lern* can mean "in front of" or "ahead, before". I read it in time: you do not lay the lintel before the gate stands, so the first leaf is written last, which is what she is doing tonight.
  - A spatial reading, "no lintel stands in front of a gate", is possible.
  - *ganna* is unsoftened after *lern*, so *ul lern* is working as a compound preposition here, not as a construct.
  - The lexicon also has *Hosk ul Lern*, "the Bar Before", as Seren's name for a root of the grain. I do not think that is meant, because no grain is being described.

**S01-F-05** At this hearth the first builders of the Keep laid tales. When they went from the Keep, the tales went cold at the hearth. Now we sit where they sat, when the hands still have nothing but their holding at the fire **(?4)**.
- *trenneth* again means both tales and courses: "laid courses".
- (?4) *amm doss nayalat tul lo et garlath veth so hethyl lo et vorr* is *lit.* "when is nothing yet at the hands but their holding at the fire". It uses *Doss X lo Y*, "Y has X". I take it to mean the hour when the hands are empty except for warming at the fire.

**S01-F-06** At my shoulder, in its niche, is the stone, the old lintel of the inner gate. Face down it lay in the gate, and the Stonwryt [was turned] to the ground, until dusk on the day of the banner, when Halyna turned it. One does not turn it; both turn it. The Stonwryt of the stone is sharp still.
- *um o lern* is *lit.* "upon its face".
- *et Stonwryt ul lern et trun* has no verb: "the Stonwryt against (facing) the ground".
- *dem hedh orl et movenn* means "until the dusk of the day of the banner" (from the additions).
- *nath gyl hos o; kyle pana o* is habitual. *hos* is "one [person]" and *pana* is "both", with a dual verb.
- *ul o valtur* means "in its niche". The bedding after *ul* does not show on the pronoun *o*; there is no *m-*. The same happens in I1-11 (*ul o wrodow*).

**S01-F-07** *(the Hal)* THIS WALL WILL STAND · WE TWO LAID IT AND WE TWO ANSWER [FOR] IT, WHILE STONE IS STONE · IT STANDS.

**S01-F-08** This wall will stand. We two laid it, and we two answer for it, while stone is stone. It stands.

**S01-F-09** So I write every bonded pair under one lintel, as a lintel [lies] upon a gate. It is one name, and the name of both.
- *gor yanna lodhat* is *lit.* "every bonded gate". A pair is a gate.
- *voll vana* is "the name of both".

**S01-F-10** Halyna is Halvard Stone-Warden and Rhyna of the Inner Gate. Idrenna, the eldest of all **(?5)**, taught them, and they bow at the door when a course is laid true **(?6)**. Orvenna, the master-pair of the wall, never raise their voice. Of Enrella **(?7)**, the youngest of all: when one lays the first stone in play **(?8)**, the other lays the capstone upon it.
- (?5) *et hemm sost* is *lit.* "the old, very" (*sost* means "self, very"). It stands in apposition to Idrenna and could mean "the very Elders" or "the eldest". The object "them" is *sona*, "them two".
- (?6) *ferre* is the dual of *ferr*, "bow".
  - *f* does not show the softening, so the verb may continue the past ("and bowed") or be present ("and bow"). I chose the present.
  - *amm cadhat trenn venn* is *lit.* "when laid [a] course true".
- *Nath memme … sona harm fedh* is *lit.* "do not lift … their-two call ever".
- *prassyanna et hald* is *lit.* "the master-pair of the wall".
- (?7) *Hy Enrella* is *lit.* "from Enrella". I read it as a fronted topic: "of / as for Enrella".
- (?8) *cadh hos tolm hosast sodh* is *lit.* "lays one stone first play".
  - I read *hos* as the subject ("one of them"), *tolm hosast* as "the first stone", and *sodh* ("play, a game") as a possessor or manner: "of a game" or "in play".
  - *cadh ullen et hosk umo* is "the other lays the capstone upon it". *cadh et hosk um* is also the idiom for finishing a work.

**S01-F-11** When the tale is whole, we name it: not told, but laid. A tale that we only tell, and do not lay, is dead by morning. A tale that we lay is part of the wall.
- *nath gadhar veth brodhar* is *lit.* "we do not lay but tell". *nath … veth* is "only".
- *saed* means "half" or "a part". *el saed et hald o* is *lit.* "it is half (a part) of the wall".

**S01-F-12** Among us one word answers every tale, and no other ever answers: *Tumar*, "We remember."
- *lor* is *lit.* "at us".

**S01-F-13** On the first night that this hearth asked for a tale, we had no word. Old Ebba of Fenholm said it into the silence. No one asked it of her, and no one said to us: Follow. But the hearth caught the word from her. Nearly every night she is still first, one breath ahead of the others, and we come in behind Ebba, as a tide behind the first wave.
- *Sedhnell* is Fenholm, "fen-islet".
- *nath re yal brod lor* is *lit.* "a word was not at us".
- *Fenna* is an imperative plural.
- *hos hoss ul lern et ullen* is *lit.* "one breath before the other(s)".

**S01-F-14** Forgetting came upon our fathers, and no one chose it. We choose our remembering, as masons choose the stones that hold.

**S01-F-15** The wood has few tales, and the greater part [of them come] from the wreck-wood. The rolls forget, and a hand can lay a lie in them. The wood cannot lie; but it cannot see fear.
- *doss cadhol nycul ulont lo yarl* uses the "can" idiom: *lit.* "laying a lie in them is at a hand".
- *veth nath noss veskyl ludal lo* is *lit.* "but seeing fear is not at it".
- *kaelirvesk* is "wreck-wood": *kaelir* "a ruin" plus softened *mesk*.

**S01-F-16** One came from a black stone **(?9)**. It lay in the chest underfoot, under the seal of Wendhessa, who brought the chest from Eldhythe in the Fall. Every pair kept that seal, until the time when the wood gave Halyna the knowing of the mute stones that rest at its shoulder **(?10)**. Then Idrenna gave leave.
- (?9) *Re rarr hos hy dholm domm* is *lit.* "came one from S·stone black". I take *hos* as "one [of the wood's tales]", following on from F-15. The line does not say what the black stone is.
- *hosk* means "lintel; seal". *ul vimm hosk Wendhessa* is "under the seal of Wendhessa".
- *Dhavow Hemm* is *Tavow Hemm*, Eldhythe ("the old landing"), softened after *hy*.
- *et Ommol* is "the Fall".
- (?10) I parse this as follows:
  - the verb is *re hebb* and the subject *et mesk*;
  - *lo Halyna* means "to Halyna";
  - the heavy object is set last: *kethyl et tolmath nawrodh*. Of the wood, *kethyl* means "a knowing", but it also means "holding, keeping".
  - An equally good rendering is "…gave Halyna the keeping of the mute stones".
  - In *lo o sest*, "at its/his shoulder", the owner is unclear. It could be the wood's or the chest's.
- *re hebbe Idrenna lunn* is *lit.* "Idrenna gave freedom", meaning they gave leave.

**S01-F-17** We do not read the wood, but we hold it, whole, as we hold memory. So Halyna give it in pieces, and one catches where the other's words fall.
- *hebbe* and *grik* are present tense.
- *brodath ullen* is "the words of the other".

**S01-F-18** I lay the pieces as they fall, in two inks. They do not want me to mark which of them is which. You cannot know it. I build the tale from the pieces. After the tale is the silence of the hearth, and I have written that silence.
- *Nath mavere sona en rellorol cedh el cedh* is *lit.* "they-two do not want my marking [of] who is who".
- *Nath noss seskyl los* is *lit.* "knowing is not at you (pl.)".
- *ul sestow* means "behind, after".

**S01-F-19** Halyna want the wood's leaf at the right hand, facing the stone leaf.

**S01-F-20** A tale told from one side of a wall

**S01-F-21** [is] half a tale.
- F-20 and F-21 are one verbless saying over two lines. *lern* means "face, side".

**S01-F-22** Where there was not yet a telling of the wood's leaf, I left the leaf empty, and only the grain of the wood is upon it: the face of something that we have not yet understood.
- *lern yalat* is "the face of a thing".

**S01-F-23** I am not one of the Bonded, but Halyna brought me into their hearth, as the third of the hearth. When they hold the wood, I hold the lamp. Once I laid my own hand upon the grain, and I let go. My hand is a hand that writes, and not a hand that holds.

**S01-F-24** Every scribe before me was half of a gate. I write alone.
- *Ew saed yanna gor ryterd demom* is *lit.* "was half [of a] gate every scribe until me".

**S01-F-25** The Captain hears everything, and says nothing, and his hood [is] up.

**S01-F-26** Where a carving was broken, I did not mend it with guesses. Where the guild cut a name from it **(?11)**, I laid ink. Where the wood speaks, I have written the wood's own names for things, and not Shoreland names.
- *lo haevereth* is *lit.* "by thoughts/guesses".
- (?11) *re ryt et Stonwrytan voll hyo* is *lit.* "the guild cut a name from it". It could mean they cut a name away (struck it out), or cut a name taken from it. *re hadhom luth* is *lit.* "I laid ink".
- *vollath sost et mesk um yalatath* is *lit.* "the wood's very names upon things".
- *nel vollath orrowen* is "not Orrowen names".

**S01-F-27** Every night I lay the tale in the speech of the Shore, as a mason lays a course, every joint broken over a joint below. Every word I say aloud three times before I write it, to hear: Does it hold? The tale is the teller's; the words are mine. I read every leaf aloud to the one who laid it, and no teller has yet asked for his own words back **(?12)**.
- *dem hunnol* is *lit.* "until/for hearing".
- *Doss et trenn lo et brodhard* is *lit.* "the tale is at the teller".
- (?12) *nath re vess brodhard tul o wrodath sost pawast* is *lit.* "a teller has not yet asked his own words a second time". *pawast* can be "back" or "again".

**S01-F-28** Nearly every night the coals are dead to ash, and the last word is still upon my tongue. So it is tonight.
- *El hosen ull, vess sy* is *lit.* "it is like that, this night".

**S01-F-29** I love the speech of the Shore.

**S01-F-30** I carried two things up the mountain, the speech of the Shore and a chest; and only the chest was heavy.
- *nath re yal nalelv veth et vestul* is *lit.* "was not heavy but the chest".

**S01-F-31** This Book is ink. Halyna want it cut into the granite of this hall, course upon course, when peace comes. Until that time it rests in that chest, among the mute stones.
- *El luth et flennath sy* puts the predicate first: "is ink, this Book".
- *o rytyl* is "its cutting".

- *re hresk o* is S·*cresk*, "it broke".
- *so yedheth* is "their fathers", meaning the fathers' fathers.

**S01-INV-06** A few of these tales we have [as] tales cut true, as the stones of the wall are cut. The wood has a few, and their telling is hard, and they do not hurry. Where the two do not agree, we have kept both.

**S01-INV-07** Hold the stone. Lay the tale. When it is laid, the hearth will answer.

## I.1 · The Torn Cloak

**S01-I1-00** The Torn Cloak.
- *lit.* "the riven cloak".

**S01-I1-01** The first winter, the night after the Rite.

**S01-I1-02** Kael the Counter laid [this], on the first night that this hearth asked for a tale. He did not let me change one word of it; I changed many, and when I read it to him, he swore: It was word for word, as I told it.
- *ryt* means "swear" as well as "cut letters".

**S01-I1-03** Give me the stone. I do not sit; I will stand. I have sat enough this winter.

**S01-I1-04** I swear upon the lintel of the western stair, on which the old wardens cut their names. It stands. I am a counter, not a singer. I count heads, and stones, and days. What I did not count, I do not lay.

**S01-I1-05** The column came in at dusk, under the outer gate. I heard it before I saw it: many feet, and no voice among them. From the gate I counted it, head by head.

**S01-I1-06** Old men came in, and women with packs, and children asleep on their feet, and the soldiers of six broken companies, who had lost their captains. The Captain came in last, who had held the rear every day of the march. Two thousand and some hundreds, counted whole.
- *um so visketh* is *lit.* "upon their feet": S·*misketh*.

**S01-I1-07** We walked a month from the ash of Glasspire to the Keep. We came into a heap of rubble. One hinge held the gate, and in the courtyard the grass stood to the knee. Where the songs had laid a great hall, the roof was fallen, and snow lay upon the hearth you sit at now.
- *dem gethow* has no article: "to [the] keep".
- *tolmstell* means "a heap of stones".
- *brellir* means "joint between stones; hinge".
- *re hadhant et velleth hellur strom* is *lit.* "the songs laid a great hall".

**S01-I1-08** Some shook. Some wept. Some fell, and did not rise.
- *varrant* is from *marr*, "shift; shake; tremble".

**S01-I1-09** Then the crying-out rose, and it was not one cry. Some cried: Ask the grey sails for a truce! But no one living has ever understood one word that they said. Some cried: To the high passes, and the snow beyond! Some cried: Go down into the cellars, and seal the doors, and wait! As though the sea would forget us.
- *Pessa haethil dem wemmeth myst* is *lit.* "ask truce to the grey sails".
- *hoska et gebbeth* is *lit.* "lintel the doors".
- *Hosen amm es nadhum et wadh ol* is *lit.* "as when the sea will forget us".

**S01-I1-10** I heard men breaking in the long retreat. I had never heard a people breaking, until that dusk. It is a sound like river-ice going out.

**S01-I1-11** The wind came down from the riven ridge, and the whole winter in its mouth.

**S01-I1-12** The people looked to the general, and the generals were killed. It looked to the lords, and the lords were scattered. It looked to the guild, and the guild sat in the grass, with their chisels in their laps.
- *dem grenn hesteth* is singular ("a general"), but then *et crenneth hesteth* is plural ("the generals"). I kept the text as it stands.
- *re vesk o* has *o* = it, the people (*lunt*, singular).
- *driget* means "thrown, flung; scattered".

**S01-I1-13** None of them looked to the Captain. He was the captain of a small company, and we were his company. Every day of the long march he waited for one who had the leading; and no one came, because they were dead. Do not mistake me: there was no one greater than he, and I do not think a man like the Captain ever lived. But he did not want the leading. I swear upon that. It stands.
- *ew o hest ol* puts the predicate first: *lit.* "was his company, we".
- *sa re yal et neskyl lo* is *lit.* "at whom the leading was", meaning who held the command.
- *Nath hyrrila en navenn* is *lit.* "do not (pl.) understand me false".

**S01-I1-14** The True Men looked to their Captain. The Captain looked upon the wall.

**S01-I1-15** Then he climbed. In the hall he took coal from the dead hearth, this hearth, and none of us knew why. He went up a broken face, alone, by the course of the stones that had risen from the mortar. We stood at the foot, and we let him go, because he had not said to us: Follow. I have counted those stones since that time: they are thirty-one, and four of them shift.
- *re rik brenth* names no subject; it is "he", carried from the sentence before.
- *re dhaldant hy et lodh* is from *tald*, "rise, stand up": the stones had stood up out of the mortar.
- *doss hosnoth eth murr* is *lit.* "are eleven and twenty".

**S01-I1-16** At the top he took off his cloak. It was crimson once, before the march had changed it to the colour of brick. In that wind, he had nothing but it.
- *nath re yal galat lo veth o* is *lit.* "a thing was not at him but it".

**S01-I1-17** No one spoke in the courtyard. Among the women a small child's breath broke, and the mother laid silence upon it. Then there was nothing but the wind.

**S01-I1-18** He tore it along the line of its back.
- *lit.* "he rived it by the course of its back".

**S01-I1-19** It was a small sound, the sound of old cloth giving way. I heard it over the wind.
- *ul villurol* is *lit.* "in yielding".

**S01-I1-20** He laid one half upon the capstones, and he knelt upon it with the coal, and he wrote. He wrote slowly, as men write who have not written much. That half was tied to a pike from the rubble. He laid the other half back upon his shoulder, nothing written upon it.

**S01-I1-21** I was the first to follow him up, and the first to read it. His hand did not shake once. I turned to the courtyard, and I read it aloud, and my voice did not hold, and I do not lie about it.
- *nath re ston en garm* is *lit.* "my voice did not stand".
- *nath nyculom umo* is *lit.* "I do not lie upon it".

**S01-I1-22** In memory of our home, our families, our God, our freedoms, our peace.

- *dem va seskyl* is *lit.* "until your knowing".

**S01-I1-26** Stannard of the Ridge. Corlen of the Harbour. Tamm of the Three Spans. Marl the Fen-born. Aske of the Canopy. Garvel of the Smithies. Harl of the Burning Shore. Ulden of the Galleries. Hesk of the Mere. Lanner of the Crags. Pellow of the Spire **(?14)**. And Kael the Counter, oath-keeper of the True Men; I have no haven, because the Captain found me on the road.
- The names are names. Each epithet is a construct or an apposition:

  | Epithet | Reading |
  |---|---|
  | *Sorth* | "ridge" |
  | *Dhavow* | S·*tavow*, "harbour" |
  | *Sull Heskal* | "three span(s)" |
  | *Sedhen* | "fen-born" |
  | *Hrisur Lurr* | S·*crisur lurr*, "roof of the forest", the canopy |
  | *Runnowath* | S·*drunnowath*, "smithies" |
  | *Orrow sa Vorr* | "the shore that burns" |
  | *Salereth* | "galleries" |
  | *Lynth* | "mere" |
  | *Gynten* | "of crags" |

- (?14) *Vell* is either *vell*, "song", or S·*pell*, "spire". I follow the lexicon's set phrase, "Pellow of the Spire", but the letters alone cannot rule out "Pellow Song".
- *nath noss cumm lom* is *lit.* "a haven is not at me".
- *lo orrol* is *lit.* "at going", meaning on the road or on the march.

**S01-I1-27** Twelve. I counted us twice, and it was not long.

**S01-I1-28** Then the six companies answered, every True Man one troop, and they trust us as we trust him. Then the people of the column answered, who had carried their children on their backs through the passes. One cry. Twelve **(?15)**. Then a hundred and a hundred, until the time came that I could not count.
- *oskant* and *oskar* have no *re*, so they are present: "they trust" and "we trust".
- (?15) *Hos carm. Delv.* is two verbless lines: "One call. Twelve." The people's one cry may have been "Twelve!" (that is, the Twelve, without the article), or it may be Kael's tally.
- *nath re yal nydhyl lom* is *lit.* "counting was not at me".

**S01-I1-29** I stopped. It is the only time that I have stopped.

**S01-I1-30** He gave no order. He wrote only what we still had, and it was enough.

**S01-I1-31** Crimson is the pennant of this Keep, and the pennant of the keeps we build. It is the colour of that cloak.
- *gever ol gethowath sa yaldar* is *lit.* "the pennant of our keeps that we build".

**S01-I1-32** Look at the end of the bench, children at the front. He is sitting there, and his hood [is] up.
- *dhevath* is a vocative.
- *ul reskyl lo ull* is *lit.* "in sitting, at that".

**S01-I1-33** The other half is upon his back.

**S01-I1-34** He says to you: It was not like that. It was like that.
- *los* means "to you (pl.)".

**S01-I1-35** I lay it as it was laid for me.

**S01-I1-36** Then the True Men sang, as they sing it still:

**S01-I1-37**
> Gate broken, lords scattered,
> meeting [us] at the shore, the grey sails;
> he climbed alone upon loosed stones,
> and tore the cloak upon his shoulder.
> He wrote five things [to hold] until death,
> and none of us wanted a sixth.

- *ul gemmyl lo orrow* is *lit.* "in meeting at shore".
- *lesk galat dem sennyl* is *lit.* "five things until dying".

**S01-I1-38** And the hearth said: We remember.
