### Notes, line by line

**W02-IV2-01**
- *Meskdhrenn* is *mesk* "wood" + S *trenn* "course": "the wood's course", the grain.
- There is no article: it is a heading.

**W02-IV2-02**
- *Hyll Dhumol* is a construct: *tumol* "remembering" is softened as the possessor.
- I take it as the name of a Tide, which the lexicon's set phrase confirms.
- The line is a dating: "the third winter, in the Tide of Remembering".

**W02-IV2-03**
- *rik* is *grik*, softened. A softened *g* before a consonant drops.
- *o tunn* is not "its whole": *o* would soften the next word to *dhunn*, so *o* is the subject "it", and *tunn* is "whole".
- *ul molt et tevath* is the set phrase "among, amid" ("in the heart of the children"). The literal "in the heart of the children" is also possible, but the set phrase reads better.
- *ew sull vess et brodhol*: the copula puts its predicate first, so this is "the telling was three nights [long]".
- *trenn* has, among its senses, "a tale laid in one night". The line plays on it: the telling took three nights because it was not a *trenn*, a one-night tale. English cannot keep the pun.
- *ulonta* (in-3DU) and the dual pair-name: the "two of them" are Halyna, the pair.

**W02-IV2-04**
- *Trenneth* is the plural with a sense of its own: "the courses of a wall; the tales of a night". *trenn* alone also gives "a rank, a row".
- **⟦?⟧ Which sense is meant cannot be fixed from the line.** I chose "ranks" because the grain's r1 holds the long ranks.
- *sast* is "skin, hide; crust". The tongue uses it here for the bark.
- *kethyl* is "holding", and "(of the wood) a knowing". Both readings are possible: "a holding of the breath, downward".

**W02-IV2-04 to -11** are fragments in direct speech.
- Each line that ends on "—" is taken up by a line that opens with "—", so they read as two voices finishing each other's lines.
- **⟦?⟧ Who speaks is not written.** The frame (-03, -12) speaks of Halyna, so the voices are presumably Halvard and Rhyna. That is an inference, not something the text states.

**W02-IV2-06 and -11: the empty brackets `[ ]` are not words.**
- In the grain spec's convention (§16) they are Seren's brackets, marking what is not (yet) told.
- **⟦?⟧ Nothing can be read in them.** In -11 the bracket stands exactly where the softened verb after *nath re* would stand: "and it did not ___".
- From -13's *Nath re dhess o* ("it did not answer") and the grain's r18, the missing verb is probably "answer". I have left it untranslated.
- In -06 the bracket follows a count ("One, and —"). -07's strokes suggest the counting of strokes, but nothing is written.

**W02-IV2-08**
- *gor hos* is the set phrase "each one". *Hos* also gives "anyone, someone".
- *lunn* as a verb is "open, make room": "the sky made room".

**W02-IV2-09**
- *Gendeth* is the plural of *gend*, "a shoot from a stump".
- *pawast* as an adverb is "a second time, again".

**W02-IV2-10**
- *Rellorath* is "signs; a code": a plural with a sense of its own.
- *galat nawrodh* is "the mute (made) thing". The grain calls the stone "the mute thing" too.

**W02-IV2-12**
- *Pa rellor* is the dual, "two signs".
- *sa re yal* VN *lo Halyna* is the past of the "can" idiom (Doss VN lo Y), so "which Halyna could see".
- *nath … tul* is "not yet". *lonta* (at-3DU) is "they two could not yet tell".
- **⟦lex⟧ *lern et mesk*:** *lern* alone is first "the face", and the phrase could be read "the face of the tree". The W02 lexicon addition fixes it as "the wood's shape", a knowing's shape, and I follow it.

**W02-IV2-13**
- This is a caption to two drawn marks, which match the grain:
  - **the first** is the grain's r5: the ranks all, the memory ray to the pith, and the split runner "what one pillar held";
  - **the second** is r15's plea (CARVE → the said-pocket, in the stone), tied by runner u1 across the rings to r18's `!MOUTH`.
- *lo so dhrenn* is "at/by their course".
  - ⟦?⟧ *trenn* here could be "rank", "line" or "course". I chose "rank", to match *gor dhrenn* ("every rank") in the next clause.
  - Read as "line", the phrase describes the memory ray running down the file to the heart.
- *re heth gor dhrenn o*: the verb comes first, then the subject (*gor dhrenn*), then the object pronoun (*o*), so this is "every rank held it".
- *keth* is "hold", and of the wood "know"; "held" covers both.
- *scaevurath* (wheels, rings) is the grain's rings.
- **⟦lex⟧ *brodow kylet*:** "the turned mouth" is literal. Its sense, the grain's MOUTH mirrored (not speaking), comes from the W02 lexicon addition.
- *bessyl* is *pessyl* nasalised after *dem*.

**W02-IV2-14**
- **⟦?⟧ Two readings:** VSO with *nahos* as subject gives "and no one answered". A null 3sg subject with *nahos* as object gives "and it answered no one".
- The lexicon's set phrase gives the first, so I chose it.

## 2 · The grain round (`blind/W02_grain.gn2`)

### 2.1 The round as a whole

**Header.** `round IV-2 {wood: long; pith: war; rings: 24; rules after: [3, 20]}`.
- **A telling, in wood of a long life.**
- **The pith is square (war):** the heart grew after the vote, *Grow until the shore is silent*, which lies beneath every carving.
- **24 rings in three movements:**
  - band I is r1–r3;
  - band II is r4–r20;
  - band III is r21–r24.

**Root.** `0: AXE*`: **this is a telling of the axe** (the iron; the felling).

**Bark.** `bark 0: HOLD`, the seal: **"This is held in the grain … and the grain holds it still."**

**Files, as I read them.** Even files are bearings, odd files placeless (§11.2, A16b).
