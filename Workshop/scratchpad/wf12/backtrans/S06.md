# S06 back-translation (blind reader)

**Source:** `wf12/blind/S06.txt` (104 lines, IV.1 and IV.3).
**What I used:** only the grammar `wf7/orrowen_v2.md`, the lexicon `wf12/base/lexicon_orrowen.tsv` with the unit's additions `wf12/additions/S06.tsv` merged in, and the analyzer `orr_analyze.py --gloss`. All three were copied to `wf12/tmp/S06bt/`, along with the gloss (`gloss.txt`) and the check report (`report.txt`). I opened no Book, plain text, old unit or earlier back-translation.

**Conventions.** Words in [square brackets] are not in the Orrowen; I supplied them for English sense. Words in (round brackets, italic) are a literal sense where it differs from the English. **⟦blot⟧** marks a sign I could not read (see the list just below). A note under a line says where I chose between readings.

**Signs I could not read.** I marked them in every line where they occur.
- **▒▒▒▒, 12 times** (IV1-33 once; IV3-08, -10, -15, -17, -19, -22, -24, -30, -35, -38, -45). This is the blot. The lexicon (S06-12) calls it "the place on a leaf where a name has been cut out." The name under it cannot be read: it is gone from the leaf, not just unfamiliar. In IV.3 every blot fills the slot of one person, the warden of the outpost (*et pard*). IV3-49 says outright that his name was cut away (*Re stin o voll et pard dem neld*). In IV1-33 the blot is a name cut at the foot of the outpost's letter, and Rhyna lays her finger on it. I take that to be the same name.
- **No other word was unreadable.** The analyzer found every token in the lexicon (report: no UNKNOWN or ILL-FORMED word). The doubts left are about meaning, not about reading, and they are noted under their lines.

## IV.1

**S06-IV1-01** The Felling of the Black Pillars
- *Gostol et Gannath Domm*, "felling [of] the posts black". Lexicon O0783 gives the title.

**S06-IV1-02** The second spring, in the Second Tide.
- A dating line. *heth* means "spring, the lengthening", and *Hyll Pawast* is the Second Tide (O1048).

**S06-IV1-03** Seren Two-Inks laid [this] from the rolls of the guild, upon the Harvest. Halyna read them to me in the nights, and I wrote from them, very quickly; my letters bowed like grass in the wind.
- *um et Derryl*, "upon the Harvest", means "concerning the Harvest" (S06-15: *et Derryl*, the felling of the black pillars).
- *re veske Halyna so lom* uses a dual verb: "Halyna (the two) read them at me".
- *hyonta* is "from them two" (taken down from their reading).
- *en dolmath* is "my N·stones". The analyzer glosses *tolmath* as "stonework", but *tolm* is also "a letter of the course-hand", so I read "my letters".

**S06-IV1-04** Hold the stone, Seren.

**S06-IV1-05** We had read [for] one year, when, in the night that the ice went soft, we came down to the bottom of the archive chest. The rope of the very old roll was rotted, and it broke in Rhyna's fingers. Halyna had not yet read me one line in it; first I wrote this:
- *re rarrar dem vimmow vestul rytow* is "we came to the low-place of the chest of the archive", that is, to the bottom of the chest.
- *nindeth Hyna* is "the fingers of S·Rhyna" (a construct).
- *Re vesker hos surr* is literally "we saw/read one year".

**S06-IV1-06** "We two did not live it,
- *hoss* means "breath; breathe; live; outlive, live on". I chose "live" here. "Breathe" is possible.

**S06-IV1-07** but the fathers of our fathers lived it. They wrote it, and they never read anything that they wrote."
- *gedheth ol nedheth* is "our forefathers" (the gloss gives the phrase).
- *nath re veskent fedh galat* is "did not read at any time anything", that is, "never read anything".

**S06-IV1-08** In the very old roll the grey is written as a darkish veil on the edge of the sea, and nothing else. So it is a question, one they asked in the night-watches and no one ever answered: what it was, and who breathed it. When the answer came, it was not written in our hand.
- *cedh re hoss o* could be "who breathed it" or "who lived [through] it". With the grey being sea-fog, I chose "breathed".
- *ul gless* is "in N·bank/edge".
- *nath re yal o rytet ul ol narl* is "it was not written in our N·hand".

**S06-IV1-09** Then came high summer, when pride was upon the havens. The ships went far out, where their fathers had not dared, and at the edge of the grey they found the black posts in their courses, true as a plumb-line. The ship-masters named them: the Mystholders, because they held the grey up as they saw it, as posts hold up a roof. The black wood was harder than oak, and lighter than iron, and it was proof against rot.
- *molt syst* is "the heart of summer" (high summer).
- *werdent* is S·*berd* "dare" in the 3PL.
- *higileth* means "ones who know the water, ship-masters".
- *Gannath Myst* is "posts of the grey" (Mystholders).
- *russvesk* is S·*dussvesk* "oak", and *russtyvil* is S·*dusstyvil* "iron", both after *hy* "than".
- *re vemess o lilvyl* is "it warded off rot" (*pemess*, "be proof against").

**S06-IV1-10** For two generations the Trade Council sent the axes far out. The rolls keep the count, cargo by cargo. Hear it now, and hold it, because it is ours.
- *lo dhrenn pa dhesk* is "by the course of two S·generations".
- *re lusk ... dem neld* is "loosed ... away" (sent off).
- *Hunna*, *ketha* are imperatives plural, addressed to the hearers.
- *doss o lor* is "it is at us", that is, "we have it" or "it is ours".

**S06-IV1-11** Black wood, into buttresses and bridges. Black wood, into three bridges at Tidesmeet, and into the posts of Fenholm. Black wood, into the roof-trees of Holtward, and into the cranes of Highreach. Black wood, into the galleries of Sandreach, and into a hall upon the ice at Rimewatch. Black wood, burned slowly in the forges of Emberhythe. And at Carnhold the axes were hammered, and they went back to the edge for more cutting.
- *dem* (until, as far as) is rendered "into" throughout the list.
- Place-names: *Gemm Hyll* is N·*Cemm Hyll* (Tidesmeet), *Sedhnell* is Fenholm, *Lurrvard* is Holtward, *Dhaldow Helv* is S·*Taldow Helv* (Highreach), *Rullsorth* is S·*Grullsorth* (Sandreach), *Frennvar* is Rimewatch, *Dhavow Vorr* is S·*Tavow Vorr* (Emberhythe), and *Gethow Stellath* is N·*Kethow Stellath* (Carnhold).
- *drunnat* is "hammered, struck" (forged).
- *dem stinyl mest* is "to much cutting".

**S06-IV1-12** After that, the things that had no need of such a thing.
- Literally "the things that had not needed a thing like that". *vaverent* is S·*maver* "wish, want, need" in the 3PL. The next lines list luxuries.

**S06-IV1-13** Black wood, into the floor of the dancing-hall of the lord of Eldhythe.
- *bressir* is N·*pressir* "floor", *vithul* is S·*pithul* "lord", and *Dhavow Hemm* is Eldhythe.

**S06-IV1-14** Black wood, into the trade-tables of the harbour-houses, and their dice-boards.

**S06-IV1-15** [Here the roll is stained, and three lines are lost.]
- The brackets are in the source.

**S06-IV1-16** At the very end, a trader at Tidesmeet hewed one post into one bed.
- *Lo rask sost* is "at the S·end itself" (*dask* softened to *rask*).
- *re rann* is S·*dann* "hew".

**S06-IV1-17** He slept in it, and he was proud of it.
- *re yal lyrness umo* is "pride was upon him" (the feeling idiom).

**S06-IV1-18** Halvard turned the leaf, and turned it a second time.
- *re hyl* is S·*kyl* "change, turn".
- *pawast* is "again, a second time".

**S06-IV1-19** The rolls keep another list beside that one, stranger than it. Where a post had been felled, a coppice rose from the stump, true and black. The axemen came back to cut it, because those were better posts than it; and the guild bought them for scaffolding.
- *dyss hyo* is "strange from it", that is, "stranger than it".
- *gannath gell hyo* could also be "good posts out of it" (*hy* is both "from" and "than"). I chose the comparison.
- *re lenull et Stonwrytan so dem brynur* is "the guild bought them to N·scaffold".

**S06-IV1-20** They cut it, and it grew back. They cut it, and it grew back. They cut it.
- *re dhev o* is S·*tev*, here the verb "grow, sprout". "Back" is my word.

**S06-IV1-21** Under the list of posts, Rhyna's finger stopped. A clerk had written one more line, very small: twice I tipped the lamp to it, and then she read it.
- *saevul* means "one who keeps the rolls, a clerk".
- *re ferrym et greller dem o* is "I bent/tipped the lamp to it". The "I" is the narrator and "she" (*ey*) is Rhyna.

**S06-IV1-22** Along the whole edge, nothing rose from the stumps.

**S06-IV1-23** No one wrote why, and no one asked. The roll did not say whose the hand was. I will keep the hand's name.
- *cedh ew et garl* is "who was the hand", here "whose hand it was".
- *Es gethym voll et garl* reads plainly word for word: "I will hold/keep [the] name [of] the hand". The intent is unclear, because the hand's name is unknown. It may be a vow to keep its memory. It may also be read as "I will keep 'the Hand' as its name".

**S06-IV1-24** Time after time the grey closed in, and the Council named it: Luck. I sought that word under my tongue, and it would not hold, like sand out of old mortar.
- *re hyl et myst dem myntyth* is literally "the grey changed/turned to N·narrowness". I read this as narrowing or closing in.
- *Pussal* means "a lot cast; one's lot; fortune; luck".

**S06-IV1-25** The guild warned the Council. It is in our own hand, on a leaf that was never blackened by fingers:
- *ul ol narl sost* is "in our N·hand itself".
- *nath re yal domm hy nindeth fedh* is "was not ever black from fingers" (never thumbed).

**S06-IV1-26** "These trees grow in strange ground. The carvings that we find beside them carry a speech that we cannot read. Where wisdom is lacking, restraint is our guide."
- *tevent* is *tev* "grow" in the 3PL.
- *veskyl lor* is "seeing/reading is not at us" (cannot).
- The last sentence is verbless. Lexicon gives the phrase: "Where wisdom is absent, let restraint be our guide."

**S06-IV1-27** But already trade sat on a throne, and our counsel was like something grandmothers say by the fire. Once the warning was read aloud, in the council-hall of Eldhythe. Then they laid it on the shelf.
- *tevar* means "early, already".
- *reskow strom* is a throne.
- *niltenn* is "plan, counsel".
- *vesket ul garm* is "read aloud".
- *um yenth* is "upon S·table/shelf", here "shelved, tabled".

**S06-IV1-28** For there had been carvings. At the feet of the posts the axemen had found stonework laid true, carved in strange signs, lovely as frost on a window. No one could read it.
- *rellorath kylet* is "changed/turned signs", rendered "strange signs".
- *risker* is S·*disker* "window".
- *Re yal veskyl lo nahos* is "reading was at no one".

**S06-IV1-29** They hung a few in the harbour-houses, turned into ornaments. Men drank beneath the stones, and counted. The guild locked the rest in archives, and the warning itself with them.
- *haemer* means "raft; float; hang". I chose "hang", and IV1-38 supports it.
- *re nydhent* is "they counted/reckoned", with no object given.
- The last clause is verbless: "and the warning itself [is] at them".

**S06-IV1-30** A few of them rest in the chest that I carried up the mountain, on my knees. Halyna laid four hands upon them, and they gave nothing to the two of them. Stone cannot translate.
- *lo yerd* is literally "at S·knee". "On my knees" is a guess at an idiom the lexicon does not list.
- *gemm garl*: four hands, the pair's two each.
- *Nath noss brodlymmyl lo dholm* is a lexicon phrase (O2967).

**S06-IV1-31** Among the lists, one late summer, Halvard found a letter from the outermost outpost. A clerk had written it between the list of posts and the list of oars, in a single [act of] tending. Halvard read it as he read the oars.
- *flenn* is "letter" (new sense, S06-13).
- *ul hos vothol*, "in one tending", I take to mean as part of the same routine bookkeeping. The exact sense is uncertain.

**S06-IV1-32** Stranger unknown. Intent hostile. Stopped. Ship burned and cast adrift.
- The letter is in telegraphic noun-participle style.
- *dask* is "end; a cause, a purpose; intent", here "intent".
- *luskat* means "loosed; adrift, cast off".

**S06-IV1-33** At its foot a name is cut: ⟦blot⟧. Rhyna laid her finger upon the blot, and drew it back from it.
- **⟦blot⟧ unreadable**: a name cut out of the leaf (see the top).
- *re rik ey o hyo* is "she took it from it" (*grik*, "catch, take"), meaning her finger from the blot.

**S06-IV1-34** On the next leaf two boats of the black wood are struck from a cargo-list, and beside them, in one hand: lost at the edge.
- *stinet hy nydhyl havul* is "cut from a list of cargo".
- *ul hos garl* is "in one hand", probably the same hand as the other entries.
- *mammat ul gless* is "lost in N·edge".

**S06-IV1-35** I wrote it as the clerk wrote it, between the posts and the oars.

**S06-IV1-36** I did not know what I held.

**S06-IV1-37** It was not wisdom that stopped the Harvest, but the fickleness of men: the near posts had grown few, and the hills of the north gave much cheap wood. From the year of the Burning Boat, not one crew would row within sight of the grey, and the rolls did not say why. So the axes stopped. No one decided it.
- *New ... veth* is "was not ... but".
- *re yalant et gannath virr merr* is "the posts near were few".
- *Sulter sa vorr* is a name, the Burning Boat (O2425).
- *re naelur nahos o* is "no one chose/ordained it".

**S06-IV1-38** Then the havens forgot. Above the ale and the dice the stones still hung, and children asked what they said, and nothing was said to them. Those children were some of you who sit at this fire.
- *re haemerent et tolmath tul* is "the stones still hung".
- *myskent so* is "they say" (present), rendered "said".
- *merr hyos* is "few/some from you". It can also be read "fewer than you". I chose the partitive, "some of you".

**S06-IV1-39** Late, very late, the wheel of pride turned. The temples were made new, and men climbed our worn steps a second time to ask counsel, glad as men who mend a sea-wall in a calm. No one asked about the stones, and we said nothing. It was we who turned the key. That half is ours.
- *re hyl scaevur lyrness* is "turned [the] wheel [of] pride" (a construct).
- *lesteth Vardh* is temples.
- *dem bessyl niltenn* is "to N·asking [of] counsel".
- *hlennent hald wadh* is "[who] S·mend a wall of sea".
- *El ol saed ull* may also be read "We are that half". Copula order (predicate first) allows both.

**S06-IV1-40** But humility came late.

**S06-IV1-41** The black sails were upon the sea first, and early.
- *hosast tevar* is "first, already/before the time". The sense seems to be that the black sails came before humility did.
- *wemmeth domm* is "the dark sails" (O2950).

**S06-IV1-42** This I lay as it was laid for me.

**S06-IV1-43** And the hearth said: We remember.

**S06-IV3-04** The sign of Wendhessa, the two archivists of Eldhythe, upon the seal of Brenn's bundle, as it broke:

- *hosen re hresk o* could also be "as he broke it". I chose "as it broke".

**S06-IV3-05** "We two hold the stone,

**S06-IV3-06** but the words are not ours. As they were written, so we two read them."
- *nath noss et brodath lona* is "the words are not at us two".
- *hosen sy vesken so* is "like this we two read them".

**S06-IV3-07** I was nineteen. We were twelve, and the warden, at the last outpost of dry land, where the bedrock runs into the grey. Old Tobe had been netman at the bank thirty years. I was the runner. I had sworn, when I was fifteen, my hand flat upon the runners' stone: "What I saw, I carry. I carry nothing else."
- *Re yal rostnoth surr lom* is "nineteen years were at me".
- *noth eth murr* is "ten and twenty", thirty.
- *Ew et haskard en* is "was the runner, I".
- The oath is a lexicon phrase (S06-07).

**S06-IV3-08** ⟦blot⟧ had laid the black wood through the whole outpost: in the posts of the door, in his table, in the frame of the bed. He held the outpost by the name of his father in the Trade Council, and he was not much older than the runner. They sent him off to the edge, out of its heart, where he fell on no one but us, the Twelve. He had never known battle. He had never known patience.
- **⟦blot⟧ unreadable**: a cut-out name. It is the subject of *re hadh* (*cadh*, "lay, put"). By the next sentence it is the warden.
- *Re luskant o dem gless hy o volt*: "out of its heart" is literal (*o* is "its/his" and *volt* is S·*molt*). "Its" may be the Council's ("out of the Council's mind, out of the way") or "his". This is the least certain phrase in the unit.
- *nath re omm o veth umor* is "he did not fall except upon us" (a burden only to us).

**S06-IV3-09** One morning at the end of summer, a small grey boat came in out of the grey. There was no wind, but the boat came in.
- *re vellor* is S·*mellor* "come in, arrive".

**S06-IV3-10** The stranger beached it, and went up to the warden's door. ⟦blot⟧ stood in the house, and he came no nearer. The stranger was tall, and pale, and clad in mist-cloth that moved when he did not move. I saw no sword upon him.
- **⟦blot⟧ unreadable**: a cut-out name, the warden standing inside his own house.
- *nath re rarr o mest virr* is "he did not come much nearer". The "he" is most likely the stranger, but it could be the warden.
- *re varr* is S·*marr* "shift, move".

**S06-IV3-11** He spoke first in his own tongue, loud, like a storm-wind in an old forest; and we were afraid. We did not know: that is how they speak, and nothing more. Then he said three words of Orrowen, carefully, as a man lays down a thing that might break.
- *re yal ludal umor* is "fear was upon us".
- *Nath re sesker: hosen sy brodhant so, eth nayalat ullen* means "we did not know [that] they speak like this, and [it was] nothing else".
- *nayess* means "perhaps, might".

**S06-IV3-12** "Stop… Sky… Dying…"

**S06-IV3-13** They were true words, truly told. I know it now. But words that have no course laid under them are only sounds, and we heard nothing but sounds.
- *Ew brodath venn so* is "were true words, they". *so* here is the subject "they", after the predicate.
- *el hunnatath hosel brodath sa nath noss trenn lont* means "sounds alone are words at which there is no course/line/tale".
- *nath ... veth* means "only".

**S06-IV3-14** Then he knelt upon the threshold, and laid before the warden a black stone, as big as two hands. He laid it as you would lay bread before a guest. Not one of us moved. Behind him a seabird came down, and walked among the stones.
- *ul lern* is "in the face of", that is, "before".
- *es gadhos* is "you (pl) will lay", a habitual future.
- *ul o sestow* is "at his back".

**S06-IV3-15** ⟦blot⟧ kicked it aside.
- **⟦blot⟧ unreadable**: a cut-out name, the warden.
- *re visk* is S·*misk* "foot; kick".
- *lo sest* means "aside".

**S06-IV3-16** It went into the dust by the wall. Then the warden's men laughed. I stood beside them, and I hear that laughing still.

**S06-IV3-17** The stranger rose. He laid his right hand on the back of the warden's neck, gently, as a mother did for me when I had a fever. He drew their heads together until the foreheads touched, and he stayed so, in silence. ⟦blot⟧ stood still, and he let him go. I think: if he had stopped there, our life would have gone otherwise.
- **⟦blot⟧ unreadable**: a cut-out name, the warden.
- *o yarl venn* is "his right hand" (*garl venn*).
- *hosen re yald odha lom* is "as a mother did (*gald*, 'do, make') at me".
- *re yal o ul velvyth* may also be read "it was in silence".
- *Re ston ⟦blot⟧* uses *ston* "stand firm, hold steady".
- *re lusk o o* is "he loosed him".
- *es morr ol hossol ullen* is "our life will N·go other". The future serves as the conditional here.

**S06-IV3-18** Then the stranger took a small knife in his left hand, and touched it to the warden's right arm.
- *o yarl ullen* is "his other hand", the left.

**S06-IV3-19** ⟦blot⟧ tore away from him. Blood had never come from him in his life. In the tearing, the cut, which had been meant to be shallow, went deep, and the blood came.
- **⟦blot⟧ unreadable**: a cut-out name, the warden.
- *re dhresk* is S·*tresk* "tear, separate".
- "From him" in the second sentence could be the warden or the stranger. The structure suggests the warden: he had never bled.
- *sa re yal kaeveret venthen* is "that was meant shallow".

**S06-IV3-20** "Seize him," he screamed.
- *Grika* is the imperative plural of *grik*.

**S06-IV3-21** They seized him. He did not fight. The knife was still in his hand, turned toward his own arm. Between their hands he knelt a second time, where he had knelt by his gift. I did not understand him.
- *re hyrrilym o* is "I understood him/it".

**S06-IV3-22** "Kill him," ⟦blot⟧ screamed.
- **⟦blot⟧ unreadable**: a cut-out name, the warden.

**S06-IV3-23** They took whatever was at hand: staves, and stones of the shingle, and the poles of the boats. One of them took my pole, the pole I pushed the boats off with.
- *gann* means "post, pole".
- *lo* at the end is a stranded "with": "that I pushed the boats with".

**S06-IV3-24** He did not lift his hand. When he cried out, it was in his own tongue, and I heard it as wind. I counted the blows. I count them still. When he lay still, ⟦blot⟧ went on screaming, and a few went on. They broke both the stranger's arms: the one that had held the warden's neck so gently, and the one that had held the knife.
- **⟦blot⟧ unreadable**: a cut-out name, the warden.
- *drunnath* is "mallets; blows, strokes".
- *re serent merr* is "a few went on [with it]".

**S06-IV3-25** The warden's men obeyed.

**S06-IV3-26** We obeyed.

**S06-IV3-27** I say "we", because the outpost was one thing that day, and I was in it. I did not lay a hand on him. I did not stop them. I was a boy among men.

**S06-IV3-28** Then there was silence, and the seabird went.

**S06-IV3-29** They dragged him by his feet over the shingle, to the sea, and his head touched every stone. They threw him into his own boat, as a fisherman throws a torn net into the bottom of a boat.
- *o wisk* is "his S·head".

**S06-IV3-30** ⟦blot⟧ held his arm, and did not look at the boat.
- **⟦blot⟧ unreadable**: a cut-out name, the warden.

**S06-IV3-31** "Burn it," he said, "and push it far out, and give it to the tide. Let nothing go that could show."

**S06-IV3-32** They set it alight, and it did not burn as wood burns. There was screaming in it. The stranger was dead. It was not a man that screamed. It was the wood itself.

**S06-IV3-33** They pushed it far out with the posts themselves. Old Tobe went into the sea up to his knees, and washed the posts. The rest of us stood on the shingle, and we did not want to look, and we looked.

**S06-IV3-34** Out beyond, the boat turned. It went into the face of the wind, and into the face of the current, and into the grey, burning.
- *ul neld* means "beyond".
- *ul lern* is "in the face of", here "against".
- *ul vorr* means "on fire".

**S06-IV3-35** That same day ⟦blot⟧ called for a clerk, and the clerk wrote to the Council. I was beside [them], and I have its wording by heart:
- **⟦blot⟧ unreadable**: a cut-out name, the warden.
- *doss o vyskyl lom hy volt* is "its saying is at me from the heart".

**S06-IV3-36** Stranger unknown. Intent hostile. Stopped. Ship burned and cast adrift.
- This is the same as IV1-32.

**S06-IV3-37** There is no word about the gift. There is no word about the kneeling.

**S06-IV3-38** The next morning, while everyone still slept, I took the stone from the dust. I do not know why I did it. At his door ⟦blot⟧ gave me the letter. So the gift and the lie went in one bundle, and I ran it.
- **⟦blot⟧ unreadable**: a cut-out name, the warden.
- *gor hos* is "each one".
- *re yaldom o* is "I did (*gald*) it".

**S06-IV3-39** I ran one day and one night, and mile by mile I said the oath a second time.
- *bost lusul eth bost lusul* means "mile by mile" (S06-10).
- *et ryt pawast* could be "the oath again" or "the second oath". The same ambiguity is in IV3-61.

**S06-IV3-40** At Eldhythe an old clerk of the Council read it, ink upon his fingers, and he looked up at me.

**S06-IV3-41** "Is it so?"
- *Ho ross* is Q + S·*doss*.

**S06-IV3-42** I did not say: It is so. I said: "It is written." Forty years I have weighed that small cleverness.
- *Doss o rytet* is "it is written" (S06-17).
- *pa vurr* is forty.
- *nydh* means "count, weigh".
- *kylyl* is "a turning aside, a small cleverness" (S06-16).

**S06-IV3-43** I carried the lie for one day and one night. I carried the truth forty years, and gave it to no one.

**S06-IV3-44** That autumn the Shore laughed at three axemen who came back home on a mast, and who swore to a boat burning out of the grey. I did not laugh, because I had seen it turn. But I said nothing. The Council had it in writing.
- *um vivenn* is "upon S·mast".
- *re rytent um* is "swore upon/about".
- *ul gylyl* is "in N·turning".
- *Re yal o rytet lo et Redul* is "it was written at the Council", that is, "the Council had it written".

**S06-IV3-45** ⟦blot⟧ wore long sleeves until his death, through the hot summers at Eldhythe; and I know why. Last winter he died in a bed of the black wood. I alone of the Twelve am still [living].
- **⟦blot⟧ unreadable**: a cut-out name, the warden.
- *re hovv* is S·*covv* "wear".
- *grem sa re orr* is "last winter".
- *Dossom hosel tul hy et delv* is "I am alone yet from the Twelve".

**S06-IV3-46** Forty years the stone has lain at the bottom of my pack, and I have not held it once. I give it now with these words, and I ask forgiveness of God, because I have no one to ask but God.
- *nath noss hos lom dem bessyl veth Mardh* is "there is not one at me to ask, but God".

**S06-IV3-47** Thus far, the words of Brenn. At every blot, Halyna were silent.
- *Dem sy* is "until this".
- *luth eth luth* is "blot by blot" (S06-11).
- *re yala Halyna nawrodh* is a dual: "Halyna were mute".

**S06-IV3-48** The warden's arm bore one mark: half a thing, torn through the middle. The Council kept the letter.
- *saed yalat* is "half [of] S·a thing".
- *ul molt* is "in the heart/middle".

**S06-IV3-49** Brenn died, as a man lays down a bundle. The guild buried him under his name. It cut the warden's name away.
- *Re lamm et Stonwrytan o* has a 3SG verb with the collective guild.
- I read the next *Re stin o* as the same subject, "it [the guild] cut", because the pronoun *o* is "he/it". "He [Brenn] cut" is grammatical but makes less sense. This line accounts for the blots.

**S06-IV3-50** Wendhessa sealed his words, and told them to no one. In the Fall the two of them carried the chest from the haven, his hand on one handle and hers on the other. On the Going their arms gave out. The masons said: There is nothing of value in it. And they gave it to two very young ones: to Tarnel, and to me. Tarnel tied his net to his left hand. I saw the knots.
- *re wrodha*: the analyzer glosses it as an imperative plural, but with Wendhessa as subject it must be the 3DU of broad *brodh* (the two forms sound the same, grammar §3.4).
- *et Ommol* is the Fall (O1869).
- *et Orrol* is capitalised. The lexicon has only *orrol*, "a going; a road". I read it as a name, "the Going" (the journey out after the Fall), on the pattern of *et Ommol*. That is my inference.
- *re ommant* is *omm*, "fall; fail".
- *galat dyll* is "a rich thing".
- *pa dhevel sost* is "two S·young-very".
- *Re wysk Tarnel o viskur* is "Tarnel S·tied his S·net".

**S06-IV3-51** In the night of the pass Wendhessa lay down to rest, and did not rise. By the chest Tarnel and I slept, his hand on one handle and my hand on the other. In the morning there was frost upon both.
- *re sola* and *re dhalda* are both 3DU (Wendhessa).
- *re aedhen Tarnel eth en* is 1DU, "we two slept".

**S06-IV3-52** Tarnel did not wake.

**S06-IV3-53** There I stopped, this night, my hands upon the stone. Then Halyna laid their two hands beside them.
- *lo so sest* is "at their shoulder", that is, beside my hands.

**S06-IV3-54** "She carried it every step,

**S06-IV3-55** and from the passes she carried it alone."

**S06-IV3-56** There is no law older than this among the peoples: a man who kneels at your door, under your roof. We were a people of doors.
- *haral* means "an order; a law".
- *Ew lunt yebbeth ol* is "was a people of S·doors, we".

**S06-IV3-57** This we two lay as it was laid for us two.

**S06-IV3-58** And the hearth said: We remember.

**S06-IV3-59** This hearth had never said it over a killing that our own people did. Old Ebba laid the first stone, and the others were slow to follow. But they followed.
- *Re hadh Ebba hemm et tolm hosast* is the idiom "lay the first stone" (begin, O2859): Ebba began the answer.

**S06-IV3-60** And the Captain put his hood back.
- Literally "laid the hood to the back".

**S06-IV3-61** In the morning the runners swore the oath again, and laid four words upon it: Not as Brenn carried. Wik, the youngest, swore it loudest.
- *et ryt pawast* is "the oath a second time", or "the second oath" (see IV3-39).
- *Nel hosen lymmyl Wrenn* is a lexicon phrase (S06-08).
- *et tevel sost* is "the young-very", the youngest.
- *o bont sost* is "it loud-very".
