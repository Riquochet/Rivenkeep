# S11 back-translation (blind reader)

**Source:** `wf12/blind/S11.txt` (150 lines). **Read with:** the grammar `wf7/orrowen_v2.md`, the lexicon `wf12/base/lexicon_orrowen.tsv` plus the unit's additions `wf12/additions/S11.tsv` (21 rows, O-S11-01 to O-S11-21), and the analyzer `orr_analyze.py --gloss`. All of these were run from copies in `wf12/tmp/S11bt/`, with the additions appended to the copied lexicon. I opened no Book, plain text, old unit or earlier back-translation.

**Analyzer result:** every one of the 435 distinct word-forms parsed (check mode: 1920 OK, no failures). Where the analyzer's top parse was wrong in context, I say so in the notes (for example *ullen*, *kaevera*, *hedh*).

**Marks used below**
- *(lit. …)*: the literal reading, where the English smooths it.
- **[?]**: a word I read, but with more than one live sense. The note gives the alternatives.
- **[unread]**: a sign I could not read as Orrowen. There are only two kinds, listed next.

**Signs I could not read**
1. **`{{Halyna}}`** (CO-14, 15, 23, 24, 25, 27, 32, 33, 35, 40, KN-05). The name inside is the pair-name *Halyna*, and it takes dual verbs (*re hethe*, 3du) as a pair-name should. The double braces are not Orrowen writing. My guess is that they stand for the **sealing lintel** that a pair-name carries in writing (orrowen_v2 §3.10, §6.8). Each time, I translate the name and leave the braces unread.
2. **`·`** (the middle dot), in every Contents and table line. It is not one of the marks in §6.7 (perpend, wedge, gate, coping), and the text writes its wedges as commas. I read it as a typographic field separator (title · teller · companion piece · facing leaf) and render it as " · ". It has no Orrowen reading.

The other punctuation (`,` `;` `:` `?` `…`) I take to be the romanisation's own. The grammar says the questions and pauses in the Book's punctuation are Seren's.

## The Last Note

**S11-LN-01** The Last Note · Three Stones over the Hearth.
*(Rytet Dasken, lit. 'the last thing cut/written', also heard as 'the last carving')*

**S11-LN-02** Laid last of all the leaves.
*(lit. "laid last from the leaves")*

**S11-LN-03** Hold the stone, Seren.

**S11-LN-04** On the first night of this winter a child asked, before us all, what they say: the three flat grey stones above this hearth. In the havens the answer was always: They say nothing; and so they answered me, when I was small. I did not say that. I said: I do not know.
*(**[?]** ul lern has no clear object here. Lit. 'a child asked in the face [of] what they say'. I read it as adverbial: 'in front, openly, to (our) face'. It is not "against". The subject of myskent comes after it: et sull tolm ryss myst … 'the three flat grey stones'. hosen ull 'like that'. amm ew lyss en, lit. 'when was small I'. Nath re vyskym o, lit. 'I did not say it'.)*

**S11-LN-05** Every night after that, when the hall was empty, I held the lamp to the lowest stone. I did not look for a meaning in it, only for the sounds, because stone does not translate. The Mystaeri cut the Shore's words among their own signs, by their sound, as near as their signs would go. And I had three Shore words, from Brenn.
*(nath … veth 'not … but' = only. Nath noss brodlymmyl lo dholm 'stone does not translate' (O2967). virr hosen es morr so rellorath, lit. 'near as their signs will go' (es FUT in a past frame = 'would'). re yal X lom 'I had X'.)*

**S11-LN-06** Last night the hall was empty, and the fire was low, and the lamp burned down a finger's breadth while I read.
*(gordal ul vess 'last night'. vesk is 'see; read'.)*

**S11-LN-07** Then I had them, the three, all at once, on the lowest stone: the sounds close, and the signs wrong.
*(re yal so lom 'they were at me' = I had them. ul hos fedh 'all at once'.)*

**S11-LN-08** The lowest stone of the three flat grey stones above the Long Hearth: the turned signs of the bank, and among the signs, three Shore words, cut by their sound.
*(**[?]** rellorath kylet et cless 'the changed/turned signs of the bank'. cless is 'a bank, a raised edge; an edge', so this is either "the turned signs along the edge (rim)" or "of the [fog-]bank" (cless vyst 'a fog-bank', O0275). I cannot tell which.)*

**S11-LN-09** Stop… Sky… Dying…

**S11-LN-10** At the feet of the black pillars they were cut, and taken away from beside the wood, and sold. For three generations they hung above the fire of a harbour-house at Eldhythe. Under them the Shore laughed at three axemen who floated home on a single beam. On the lowest stone the ship-masters knocked out their pipes. And no one read them.
*(lo visketh = lo + S·misk-PL 'at the feet'. nalymmet 'taken away'. lo sest et mesk 'beside the wood'. hynalat 'sold'. re haemerent 'they hung / floated': 'hung' for the stones, 'floated' for the axemen. lo Dhavow Hemm 'at Eldhythe'. um 'about, concerning'. stomornel 'a single beam'. re runnant … mygurath 'they knocked pipes'. higileth 'ship-masters'. **[?]** re vesk nahos so: vesk is 'see' or 'read', so "no one read them" or "no one saw them".)*

**S11-LN-11** Here no one knocks a pipe on them. I think: I have read them. I say: I think.
*(lo sy 'here'. nath drunn: d before r stays d under nasalising (§2.5), so this is a regular form.)*

**S11-LN-12** This I lay as it was laid for me.
*(the tale's close (O0238))*

**S11-LN-13** And the hearth said: We remember.
*(Tumar 'we remember', which is also Orrowen's 'yes')*
