# S02 · blind back-translation (I.2 and I.3)

Read blind. Sources used: the grammar `wf7/orrowen_v2.md` (§2, §3, §5 and the samples of §11), the lexicon `wf12/base/lexicon_orrowen.tsv` with this unit's additions `wf12/additions/S02.tsv`, and `orr_analyze.py --gloss`. All three were copied to `wf12/tmp/S02bt/`, and the additions were merged into the lexicon as `lex_merged.tsv`. I opened no Book, unit or earlier back-translation.

**Conventions.**
- `[ ]` marks a word I supplied. The grammar leaves it unsaid: a dropped subject or object, the copula, an "of" in a construct.
- `(lit. …)` gives the word-for-word reading where the English moves away from it.
- `{?}` marks a word or phrase I could read but whose sense I could not settle. Each one is explained in the note under its line and listed again at the end.
- Names are kept as written. *Kael* is capitalised, so I read it as the name. The analyzer glosses it "tally-notch", which is the lexicon's sense of the name.

**What could not be read.** Nothing failed to parse. The analyzer returned OK on every word of all 86 lines, with no UNKNOWN or ILL-FORMED word and no mutation-trigger error. The only non-letter signs are punctuation and the ` / ` dividers inside the two songs (I2-36, I2-40) and the three calls (I3-10). I read those dividers as verse-line breaks and kept them. All the doubts below are about sense, not about reading the words.

## I.2

**S02-I2-00** · The Cry of the Bonded.
- *Carm et Lodhan*: a construct, "[the] cry [of] the Bonded". The lexicon also lists it as the set title "I.2 The Cry of the Bonded".

**S02-I2-01** · The first winter, after the first assault.
- *ul sestow*: "in the back of", that is, after.

**S02-I2-02** · My own tale, and the first that I ever laid; the second that this hearth has heard. On the day I tell of (lit. "the day that I tell upon it"), I was twelve winters old, and I was a child of the guild.
- *en drenn sost*: "my very course". *trenn* means a course of stones, a line, or a tale; here it is the tale.
- *ew tev et Stonwrytan en*: the copula with its predicate first. *en* ends the clause with no word after it, so I read it as the subject "I" and not as "my". *tev et Stonwrytan* is a construct, "a child of the guild (of the Stonewrights)".
- *re yal delv grem lom*: the additions' age idiom, "twelve winters were at me".

**S02-I2-03** · Tonight I was afraid of the telling (lit. "this night fear of telling was upon me"), and Halyna laid both my hands upon the stone.
- *hadha* is a 3du verb, as the pair-name takes. *narlath* is *garlath* nasalised after *en*.

**S02-I2-04** · Hold the stone.

**S02-I2-05** · Hold it, and it will hold you.
- No subject is stated for *es geth*. Read as "it [will hold]".

**S02-I2-06** · And so I hold it (lit. "from that I hold it"; present tense).

**S02-I2-07** · At the evening of the day of the banner, the guild sat in the grass of the old courtyard, a few hundred of us {?}, and the chisels in our laps. The last light lay on the snow, thin as ash. On the wall the soldiers cried out to the banner (lit. "until, as far as the banner"). In the grass we said nothing.
- *Lo hedh*: after *lo*, *hedh* could be softened *cedh* "who?" or plain *hedh* "evening, eve". The sense needs "evening".
- *orl et movenn*, "the day of the banner", is not a set phrase in the lexicon. It may be a named day (the Banner Day), but I cannot tell.
- *merr lesk murr hyor* {?}: *merr* is "few; some, a few", *lesk murr* is a hundred, and *hyor* is "from us". So "a few hundred of us", or possibly "some hundred of us".
- *dem et movenn*: *dem* means "until, as far as". Whether the soldiers were cheering the banner or crying until it rose or fell is not said.

**S02-I2-08** · There I sat, and I had as yet no craft but carrying water. I had walked the high passes at the shoulder of a boy of the guild, who talked of nets {?} (lit. "said a net") as he walked. Every night I slept at his shoulder.
- *nath … veth*, "not … but", is "only". *lymmyl nynth* is "the carrying of water".
- *vysk piskur* {?}: *vysk* can only be softened *mysk* "say". There is no *pysk*, and *bysk* "tie, knot" would soften to *wysk*, not *vysk*. *piskur* is "a net". "Said a net" is odd. I read it as "talked of nets". If the writer softened *bysk* wrongly, the line would mean "who knotted nets as he walked". I cannot decide.

**S02-I2-09** · I came into that courtyard alone.

**S02-I2-10** · A short while after me (lit. "in my back") two children came, their hands joined (lit. "their two hands met"): Enno and Della, who were fifteen winters old. Of the children of the mountain, no whole pair had climbed but those two (lit. "not climbed a whole gate from the children of the mountain, but they two"). They two sat together, and did not let go.
- *rarra* is read as 3du, with *pa dhev* as its subject. The form is the same as the imperative plural.
- *ganna* is "gate", and also "a pair (a pair is a gate)". *ganna tunn* is "a whole pair".
- *luska* is 3du. The object of "let go" is not stated; from "their hands met" it is their hands.

**S02-I2-11** · Ten havens we watched die (lit. "ten havens, we saw them in dying") in twenty months. We came away from them (lit. "went far from them"), one by one, and the chisels in our packs. Not one of us lifted a hand, because the creed forbade it: Build; do not destroy. Guard; do not attack. We had no arms but mortar and chisel, and they were not enough.
- *noth cumm* stands first and is taken up by the object pronoun *so*. "Them" in *hyont* I take to be the havens.
- In *re nalusk et daevarat o*, the *o* stands after the subject, so it is the object "it".

**S02-I2-12** · The faith of twelve generations still sat upon us, like a wet cloak. Each one of the elders there was turning over one hard thing: walls built for peace must in the end be held with blood (lit. "in the end there is need of their holding by blood"). And when it came to that (lit. "when it was like that"), who gives the blood? Who lifts the hands?
- *ul gylyl* is *kylyl* nasalised, "in changing, turning". "Turning over [in the mind]" is my reading of the progressive.
- The two questions are present tense (no *re*), so "who is to give…, who is to lift…".

**S02-I2-13** · Halyna: I knew them then only as the Elder and the Elderess (lit. "I did not know them two then, but as …") who sat apart beneath the broken inner gate, and spoke to no one, for they had no need (or: no wish) to. Her fathers laid that gate. His fathers quarried its stone from the mountain. They two were husband and wife, and one from the cradle.
- *wrodha* is 3du.
- *maver* is "wish, want; a need". Either sense fits.
- *o dholm*: "its stone", the gate's. "His stone" is also possible.

**S02-I2-14** · Then the Captain came down from the wall. I saw the cloak rising {?} (lit. "in rising"), and I did not know what it was to the guild. He was not tall (lit. "was not high, he/it"). There was coal-black still upon the Captain's fingers, and half a cloak upon his back. He looked at the elders, and from them at the broken gate, and he asked a single question (or: made one plea). This is the one speech of his that this hearth may say a second time {?}:
- *ul daldol* {?}: a progressive, "in rising". Whose rising is unclear: the cloak lifting (in the wind?), or something coming up.
- *vodh ew o*: *o* is "he" or "it", so this is the cloak or the Captain. *New susk o*: "it/he was not high". Since the next clause turns to the Captain, I read "he was not tall".
- *brenthromm* "coal-black" stands as a noun here, so: coal.
- *pessyl* means "a plea; a question".
- *sa ross myskyl pawast lo et odh sy* {?}: *doss* + verbal noun + *lo Y* means "Y can V", which gives "that this hearth may say again". A plainer reading is "that is said a second time at this hearth".

**S02-I2-15** · Your walls are falling. Will you build, or will you watch and die?
- *va* is 2pl, which is used for many people or as the respect form. *veskys* means "see"; here, watch.

**S02-I2-16** · No one answered him. I remember the wind in the grass.

**S02-I2-17** · Then Halyna rose.

**S02-I2-18** · At the Torn Cloak they two looked up (lit. "upon the torn cloak they looked up"), and they bowed to him in one breath. Their two chisels rose as one.
- *covv treskat* is the lexicon's set name, "the Torn Cloak". *ul hos hoss* means "at the same instant".

**S02-I2-19** · The two chisels caught the last light.

**S02-I2-20** · She laid the first stone of the Cry, and he laid the capstone upon it:
- That is, she began it and he finished it (the idiom *cadh et hosk um*).

**S02-I2-21** · The mortar cries out from the ground. Rise, Stonewrights! Lay stone upon stone. Stand every wall to the very end,

**S02-I2-22** · Rise! Rise! Rise!

**S02-I2-23** · It was one cry, in two throats. She was first only because a cry must have a first stone (lit. "she was not first, but because there is need of a first stone upon a cry"). A few tell it so (lit. "few tell it"): She called, and he answered, and a silence between them.
- *doss maver … um harm* is the "upon" idiom: "a cry needs …".
- *merr* is "few", or "some".

**S02-I2-24** · There was no silence.

**S02-I2-25** · Then the guild rose, like an arch when they knock the beam out (lit. "when they mallet the beam out of it"): every stone upon another. I rose among them, and I do not remember the rising. We caught the cry, and the soldiers on the wall caught it from us, and the course {?} caught it from them.
- *stomorn* is "a beam; a frame", the centring. *hyo* is "out (of it)".
- *et trenn* {?}: "the course", that is, a course of stones (or a line, or a tale). What "caught" the cry, whether the wall's own courses or a line of people, is not clear.

**S02-I2-26** · Old stone does not shift. Underfoot I felt it shifting.
- *marrol* is the verbal noun of *marr*, which stays unchanged after *ul*. The analyzer also offers nasalised *arr-Ol* "guest", which the sense rules out.

**S02-I2-27** · That night there was a fire at this hearth (or: this hearth had a fire), the first in more years than counting. At the fire Idrenna made (lit. "built") the truce with the Captain, and I carried the water. Slowly they two spoke, as the old speak, and the Captain did not hurry them.
- *yalda* and *wrodha* are 3du, as the pair-name takes.

**S02-I2-28** · The guild does not bear arms, and the Captain did not ask it [of them]. But the guild would build in between the volleys, and would mend breaches beneath the guns. So the truce still stands, on the guild's own words: with the speed and precision that only the Aetherbonded had. Then Idrenna gave it to him in two halves:
- The two *es* verbs are future, "the guild will build … will mend", as terms laid down.
- *glenn vathur* has no article, so "mend breach(es)".
- *Lodhan Helv* is the Aetherbonded.

**S02-I2-29** · Not the sword, but the stone.

**S02-I2-30** · Not the killing, but the holding.

**S02-I2-31** · The Captain bowed. It is the guild's word now.

**S02-I2-32** · While that fire had not yet died, Orvenna split the siege-day in three, as quarrymen split a block of granite along its own cleft. To the Captain they two said:
- *Orvenna* is a pair-name (the additions: Ormund and Penna, the wall-masters), so its verbs are 3du.

**S02-I2-33** · Every gun ye haul is a course we lay not (lit. "every gun hauled at you, a course unlaid at us"),

**S02-I2-34** · so spend the gun as a careful man spends silver.
- *vyssora* is the imperative plural, addressed to the Captain in the plural, as *los* is.

**S02-I2-35** · Then the masons sang, as they still sing it:

**S02-I2-36** · Lay the stone while the sea lies empty, / haul the gun while the gun is cold, / hold the wall when the grey sails come in, / and the wall will hold.

**S02-I2-37** · And when you see one Stonewright on the wall in the holding, look again. You will see two.

**S02-I2-38** · Late in that night, by torches that whispered in the falling frost, we laid the first course of the war in the cleft of the inner gate. We broke every joint over the joint below. To the old song of the gate, which the havens sang, Orvenna asked {?} the calls, very bright (lit. "asked the calls bright self"). Enno and Della rose, and caught the first call between them, one to the comma, and the other the rest (lit. "the other the other").
- *dresk* is *tresk* "cleft" nasalised after *ul*.
- *gor wrellir um et brellir ul vimm*: I read this as the mason's rule of breaking joint (no joint set over the joint below).
- *vell hemm et ganna*: "the old song of the gate". It is probably the song *Pa lo Yanna* (Two at the Gate) in I2-40.
- *re vesse Orvenna et carmath baeg sost* {?}: *vesse* is softened *pess*, "ask", 3du. "Asked the calls" may mean they led them (gave the call that others answer). *baeg sost* "very bright, clear" may describe the calls or the voices. The sense is not settled.

**S02-I2-39** · Then they two sang, and the guild behind them:

**S02-I2-40** · Stone upon stone, and the stone keeps faith; / hand upon hand, and the true hand holds; / two at the gate, when the grey sails meet [them], / two at the gate, and the gate stands whole.
- *keth et tolm vennoth*: read as verb, subject, object, "the stone holds faith". The imperative "hold the stone [in] faith" is also possible.
- *keth et garl venn*: "the true hand holds". The lexicon's *garl venn* is "the right hand", so it could also be "the right hand holds".
- *amm cemment wemmeth myst*: "when the grey sails meet". The object is unstated.

**S02-I2-41** · I sang to the comma, and after that I sang the other [half].

**S02-I2-42** · When the torches were low (lit. "small"), Enno and Della slept by the new course, their hands still joined, as they had climbed the mountain. I lay apart, by the water-butts (lit. "the cups/butts of water"), and I slept.

**S02-I2-43** · By morning (lit. "until morning") the mortar had set.

**S02-I2-44** · I lay it as it was laid for me.

**S02-I2-45** · And the hearth said: We remember.

## I.3

**S02-I3-00** · The Captain's Long Breath (lit. "the long breath of the Captain").

**S02-I3-01** · The first winter, in its second month.

**S02-I3-02** · Hale the Runner laid [this], who was seventeen years old, and in his first weeks on the wall. He told the tale standing, and as quick as he ever ran; I have laid it slowly.
- *re hadh* has no object stated, so "laid [this tale]".
- The "I" of *re hadhom* is the one who lays the telling down, not Hale.

**S02-I3-03** · Give me the stone, then. I am no teller. I run.

**S02-I3-04** · Each haven had a runners' stone, haven by haven. Ours we built in the first week, from a fallen stone by the inner gate; and on it we swore the old vow:

**S02-I3-05** · What I saw, I carry. I carry nothing else. It stands.
- Literally: "a thing that I saw, I carry it. I carry not another thing."

**S02-I3-06** · Last, Wick swore it, the youngest of us and the quickest (lit. "the very young from us and the very quick"). Wick loved nothing better than carrying word to the Captain.

**S02-I3-07** · In the fog of the morning of the first assault I stood by the guns that Kael commands (lit. "leads"), all that we still have (lit. "everything that is at us yet"). Kael heard the Captain's thought: where he will watch, what shot he will lay, and when he will hold. Then the Captain went off, away to the end of the wall. Kael laid his hand upon my shoulder.
- *nesk* and *ross* are present tense in the Orrowen, and I kept them so.
- *kaever* is "think; a thought", so this could also be "heard the Captain think".

**S02-I3-08** · Nothing changes that thought but his own call at my shoulder, boy {?}; and a man can stand in only one place (lit. "standing is not at a man but in one place").
- *Nath gyl kaever ull veth o harm sost lo en sest* {?} has two readings:
  - (a) "nothing changes that thought but his own call at my shoulder", with *o harm sost* as the subject held back by *veth*;
  - (b) "that thought does not change; only his own call [comes] at my shoulder".
- I lean to (a).

**S02-I3-09** · Then the crews said the three calls, over and over, as they still say them:

**S02-I3-10** · Shift your ground, and watch the new water; / change your shot to the sea, the shore, the sky; / hold, or loose, when the Captain is at [your] shoulder.
- *nynth clenn*: *nynth* is "a spring, a well; water", so "new water".

**S02-I3-11** · When they had finished, Jory of Eldhythe leaned on the east parapet (lit. "the parapet of the east"), and whistled a tune that the old ship-masters hate. At the foot of the stair was Merrick, coiling rope (lit. "in rolling of rope"), the oldest of them. He said to him: Stop. Jory laughed down at him.
- *ferr* is "bow, bend; lean". *visk* is *misk* "foot", softened after *lo*.
- In *Re vysk o lo*, "he" is Merrick and "him" is Jory. That comes from the sense; nothing in the grammar marks it.

**S02-I3-12** · It is only a children's rhyme.

**S02-I3-13** · Merrick did not laugh.

**S02-I3-14** · Before half the morning was gone (lit. "while half the morning had not yet gone"), the sails of my first holding came out of the fog, faster than I could count (lit. "quick from my counting"). In the laying the hours go slowly, because stone is heavy; and in the hauling, slower than that. In the holding an hour goes by in a heartbeat.

**S02-I3-15** · Late in the holding, when the rest was spent and the masons were upon the breach, the battery's lookout saw a hull in the far-glass that Pellow had polished. It had waited behind, in the fog, where none had once waited {?}. Then it came in, late and alone.
- *et ullen vyssorat*: "the other/the rest, spent". The text does not say what was spent (the shot? the other hulls?).
- *ul vodh nath re dhovv hos fedh* {?} has no subject: either "where it had never once waited [before]" or "where none had ever waited". *ul sestow* could also be "astern".

**S02-I3-16** · Between the Captain and that hull lay the smoke. Kael gave me three words.

**S02-I3-17** · A hull behind (or: hull astern).

**S02-I3-18** · I ran it.

**S02-I3-19** · I run. Under the smoke, head down, past the masons, past the ropes. I speak to no one, and I do not stop. Up, to the light, above the gate.

**S02-I3-20** · The Captain stands still.

**S02-I3-21** · That I remember first. Everything on that wall was moving; he was not. He held the parapet (lit. "the lintel of the wall") with one hand, and his face [was set] to the sea. He did not look at me. But he heard every word, to the last word.
- *o lern*: before *l* the softening cannot show, so "his face" or "he [held his] face". Both give the same sense.

**S02-I3-22** · Then he went to the guns that Kael commands, at his own pace, and I at his shoulder. While he walked, the hour was stretching.

**S02-I3-23** · He did not stop quickly {?}. He went slowly, like a cart of stones up a long hill, so that (lit. "until the time that") a boy behind could count the spokes of the wheel. I cannot lay it better than this.
- *Nath re hess o pynt* {?}: literally "he did not stop quick(ly)". *hess* can only be "stop". The sense may be "he was in no hurry to stop", or the writer may have meant "he did not hurry", which would be *nath re vynt o*, as in I2-27. I cannot settle it.

**S02-I3-24** · I saw the water pouring from the bow of the late hull. I counted the ripples running down along the grey sail, as you count the rings on a cut tree: one, and then another. Each one was a minute, I think. The gulls lay still. The smoke stood. He walked on still.
- *lern* is "the face; the bow of a hull". *scaevurath* is "wheels; rings".

**S02-I3-25** · For a time I was afraid (lit. "a time of fearing was at me"). Then for a time I was ashamed of that. Then for a time I stopped {?} (lit. "a time of stopping was at me").
- *fedh hessyl* {?}: "a time of stopping". Stopping what (the fear, the shame, or my own movement) is not said.

**S02-I3-26** · He stopped, and Kael was at his shoulder. Kael was counting under his breath. Then the Captain gave the call, in his own voice (lit. "at his own call").
- *carm* is also "a voice" in the lexicon.

**S02-I3-27** · Hold.

**S02-I3-28** · The battery held. Below, the masons laid on still, two by two, and did not look up. The late hull came on still, through the smoke, and still, until I could see the water on its planks (lit. "until the time that seeing the water upon its planks was at me").

**S02-I3-29** · Loose.

**S02-I3-30** · Slowly the hour let us go, as a held breath goes out. The guns fired. In the smoke the hull broke, in the one place in that water where there was no harm from it to anyone, where the Captain had thought it to its breaking {?}.
- *et clem ol*: *ol* comes after the noun, so I read it as the object "us". A possessive "our" would come before its noun.
- *re wrammant* is *bramm* "fire (a gun)", softened.
- *re haever et Crenn o dem greskyl* {?}: "the Captain thought it to breaking", that is, he had reckoned where it would break. The exact sense is uncertain.

**S02-I3-31** · I do not know what the Captain thought. I know what he built.

**S02-I3-32** · That evening I walked the tide-line, as we runners do (lit. "as we go, our runners") after every holding. In the shallows I saw marks cut in a plank, and I carried them up past the fires, to Halyna:

**S02-I3-33** · Marks on the wood.

**S02-I3-34** · I ran it.

**S02-I3-35** · That night I came back by Hesk's lamp, his mother's lamp, on the north wall. From it I knew: the north is awake.
- *gern* is a verb, "wake, be awake", in verb-subject order: "wakes the north".

**S02-I3-36** · Now the men say it all along the wall: With the Captain, an hour is as long as a day. I think I laid its first stone, but Voss says: The saying is older than you (lit. "the told [thing] is old from you"). They say it as though the Rite had given it to him {?}. I said it like that myself; and now I know better than that. Kael mended it, and I carry it as he said it, and I carry nothing else:
- *o lo* {?}: "it to him". Who "him" is (the Captain? the first to say it?) is not stated.

**S02-I3-37** · It is not the ground that goes slow, boy. It is the Captain who does not hurry. The minute that he chose was the minute that came.

**S02-I3-38** · I lay it as it was laid for me.

**S02-I3-39** · And the hearth said: We remember.

## Doubts, in one list

Smaller points:
- I read *en* at a clause end (I2-02) as "I".
- Three forms are both the imperative plural and the 3du: *rarra*, *wrodha*, *luska*. I read them as 3du.
- I read *ol* after a noun (I3-30) as the object "us".
- The present tenses inside the past narration (I3-07 *nesk*, *ross*; I2-12 *hebb*, *wemm*) are kept as present.
