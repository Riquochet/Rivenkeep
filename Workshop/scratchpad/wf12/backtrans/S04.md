# S04 blind back-translation (II.1 and II.3)

Translated without seeing the Book or any earlier unit, back-translation or plain text. I used only the grammar (`wf7/orrowen_v2.md`), the base lexicon with the S04 additions merged in (`wf12/tmp/S04bt/lexicon_orrowen.tsv`, 3016 + 8 rows), and the analyzer (`orr_analyze.py --gloss`, run on every line; working files in `wf12/tmp/S04bt/`: `gloss_all.txt`, `dict.txt`, `report.txt`).

**How to read this file.**
- Each id gives the Orrowen line, then the English, kept as close to the Orrowen as English allows.
- `[square brackets]` hold words I supplied that the Orrowen does not say: an object left unstated, a copula, or a "that" or "of" the grammar implies.
- `{?}` marks a word or sign I could read but not settle. The note under the line says why and gives the other reading.
- **Every word in the text was read.** The analyzer reported no unknown word and no mutation it could not undo. Nothing is marked unreadable. What is marked is ambiguity: the grammar or the lexicon allows two readings, or the referent is unclear.
- **Line S04-II3-16 is missing from the blind text.** The ids run straight from II3-15 to II3-17. I translated nothing for it.

**Where I overrode the analyzer's gloss.**
- After *re*, a broad verb in *-a* or a slender verb in *-e* is the **3rd-person dual**, never the imperative plural the analyzer offers first: *re lumma, re hadha, re harma, re vessa, re ammada, re volla, re firre, re hyle, re ferre, re reske*. The imperative has no tense particle (§3.4).
- *aedhen* (II3-10) is glossed first as the adjective "sleepy" (O0002). After *re* it can only be *aedh* + 1du *-en*, "we two slept".
- *ryten, kethen, firren, hebben, sennen, cadhan, hadhan, naldan* are all 1du verbs. They are not adjectives in *-en*.
- *hosen* is "as, like" (O0991), not *hos* + *-en*. *ullen* is "other" (O2649). *olen* is "together" (O1864). *dasken* is "last" (O0368).

## II.1

**S04-II1-01**
> Flennath et Galdard

The Book of the Builder.
- The lexicon's set phrase gives "Book Two: the Book of the Builders", in the plural. The text has the singular *galdard*, with no *-ath*. Read strictly, it is "of the Builder" {?}.

**S04-II1-02**
> Cedh ew ol, eth cedh ew so.

Who we were, and who they were.

**S04-II1-03**
> Kethow sa re yal nadhumat

[The] Keep that was unremembered (forgotten).
- This is a heading, so it has no article. The lexicon's set phrase titles it "II.1 Of the Keep That Was Forgotten". The text has no word for "of".

**S04-II1-04**
> Syst hosast et tynur.

The first summer of the siege.

**S04-II1-05**
> Re hadh Seren Pa Luth. Doss ulo flenn et galdardath hosast; doss o lont, eth nath noss o lom: re veske Halyna o lom hy et Hal, eth nath re werdym clennyl wrodath ull.

Seren Two-Inks laid [this]. In it is a leaf of the first builders. It is theirs (it is at them), and it is not mine (not at me). Halyna read it to me out of the Hal, and I did not dare to make those words new.
- *Re hadh* "laid" has no object. I supply "[this]": the tale is laid like a course.
- *werdym*: S·*berd* + 1sg. *berd* is "brave, bold; dare" (adj, v). It is read as the verb "dared".
- *clennyl wrodath ull*: "the making-new of those words". *wrodath* is the softened possessor, which the analyzer confirms. This could also be "to mend those words". *clenn* means both "make new" and "mend".

**S04-II1-06**
> Keth et tolm, Seren.

Hold the stone, Seren.

**S04-II1-07**
> El ryt surr et Kethow sy, hosen re hethent et galdardath hosast o, eth hosen nath re hether o.

This is the annal (the year-cutting) of the Keep, as the first builders kept it, and as we did not keep it.
- *sy* could close the copula ("this is…") or belong to *Kethow* ("…of this Keep") {?}. I chose the copula, because the text uses *El X sy* "this is X" again (*El Lodh Helv sy*, *El olna rellor sy*).
- *keth* is "hold, keep" and also "know (by touch)". "Kept" fits here.

**S04-II1-08**
> Et flenn hosast, re ryte Aldwena o um halflenn ul et orlath hosast, ul dolmath lyss hosen fymmetath.

The first leaf: Aldwena cut it upon slate in the first days, in letters (stones) as small as sown seeds.
- *Et flenn hosast* stands first as a topic, followed by a comma. The lexicon calls *flenn hosast* "a foreword".
- *dolmath*: N·*tolmath*. *tolm* is "a stone; a letter of the course-hand". The lexicon lists *tolmath* as "stonework, masonry". "Letters" fits "small as seeds" better {?}.

**S04-II1-09**
> Cadhan sy, olna sa re hadhan et ganna.

This we two lay, we two who laid the gate.

**S04-II1-10**
> Dem susk um et sorth re vellorar, eth re wesker et sorth treskat hy o wisk dem o rivull, hosen amm re omm hesp strom umo. Ul et tresk re yaldar kethow hy dholm et sorth sost. Re stannant et stannowvardath et tolm; eth re ryt et hosast hyont voll ul hosk et bystir rerdsennen, eth re lusk o et tolm ul vimm o lo o hassan.

Up onto the ridge we came, and we found the ridge riven from its head to its foundation, as if a great sword had fallen upon it. In the cleft we built a keep out of the stone of the ridge itself. The quarry-wardens quarried the stone. The first of them cut [his] name in the lintel of the western stair, and he left the stone below it to his sons.
- *voll* has no possessive ("cut a name"), so "[his]" is supplied.
- *re lusk o et tolm*: "loosed he the stone". *lusk* is "loose, let go; leave, let be; release". I read "left [free] for", that is, the space under his name kept for his sons' names.
- *lo o hassan* "to his sons": *h* does not soften, so "his" is not marked by mutation (§3.5). Context makes it "his".

**S04-II1-11**
> Lo olna yemm garl re hadhan hosk et ganna ulvenn. Ul vimm o re ryten olna rellor, eth olna dhragul lo o sest, ul vodh es vesk nahos pawast o, somm ston et hald.

With our four hands we two laid the lintel of the inner gate. Beneath it we two cut our sign, and our promise at its shoulder, where no one will see it again (a second time) while the wall stands.
- *olna rellor*: *r* does not soften, so "our sign" is unmarked. It could also be "we two [emphatic] … a sign" {?}. *olna dhragul* is marked (*t* > *dh*), "our promise", so "our sign" is the parallel reading.

**S04-II1-12**
> Es ston et hald sy. Re hadhan o, eth tessen o, somm el tolm tolm. Ston.

This wall will stand. We two laid it, and we two answer for it, while stone is stone. It stands.
- This is the Stonwryt, word for word as in the grammar, §11.6(b).

**S04-II1-13**
> Ul vimm et tolm sy doss rellor ull eth tragul ull. Nath noss o hylyl lom, hy vodh el tolm o sa vaver pa. Desir, amm doss nahos lo et vorr, re hadhom en narl ul vimm o, eth re fennym sona rellor lo hos nind.

Beneath this stone are that sign and that promise. I cannot change it ("its changing is not at me"), because it is a stone that needs two {?}. Late, when there is (was) no one at the fire, I laid my hand beneath it, and I followed their sign with one finger.
- *sa vaver pa*: REL + S·*maver* "wish, want; need, require" + *pa* "two". The relative gap may be the subject, "a stone that needs two", or the object, "a stone that two wish for" {?}. I chose "needs two": she is one, and cannot change it alone.
- *amm doss nahos* is present tense inside a past telling. I keep it as "there is (was)".
- *sona rellor* "their sign": *r* does not soften, so the possessive is unmarked (§3.5). The sense is clear.

**S04-II1-14**
> Stonwryt, vollant et prassath o: ryt hoskat ul hal, nethilet lo vyskorn eth prassoth, eth nath lenull styvil o. Veth re runnant prassyannath et cummath, um serull, so rellorath, eth re rikent uldath et serull hosen re rikent et rellor: osk, hoskat ul dolm. Nath noss rellor lom. Es rytym hos nafedh.

Stonwryt, the masters call it: a vow lintelled (sealed) in bedrock, paid for with sweat and craft, and gold does not buy it. But the master-pairs of the havens struck their signs on a coin, and men took the coin as they took the sign: trust, sealed in stone. I have no sign. I will never cut one.
- *runnant*: S·*drunn* + 3pl. *drunn* is "a mallet; knock; mint, strike (a coin)".
- *rikent*: S·*grik* "catch; pick up; take".
- *so rellorath* "their signs": unmarked possessive (*r*). The sense is clear.

**S04-II1-15**
> Ul vimm voll et pard hosast re rytent o hassan so vollath, hos ul vimm ullen; eth ul vimm et dasken doss et tolm dasorat. Doss Halvard Tolmvard hy dhrenn ull, eth doss Rhyna Yanna Ulvenn hy dhrenn Aldwena. Ew hos pa dhrenn, sell tevar hy sona hos tevow.

Beneath the name of the first warden his sons cut their names, one beneath another; and beneath the last, the stone is polished (rubbed smooth). Halvard Stone-Warden is of that line (course), and Rhyna of the Inner Gate is of the line of Aldwena. The two lines were one long before the one cradle of the two of them.
- *Ew hos pa dhrenn*: the copula puts the predicate first: "one [were] the two lines".
- *sona hos tevow* "their one cradle". The lexicon has a set phrase *cadhat ul hos tevow*, "laid in one cradle (of a bonded pair)".

**S04-II1-16**
> Hy ull re nayyst et lunt dem et tavowath dhaedh, eth et Stonwrytan ul o sestow. Tevar hy so orrol, re hadhant et galdardath hosast halflenneth et Kethow eth o hulter hosast ul et vestul domm, hosen cadh galdard o yarlelath ul hedh. Um et orrow re yaldant Tavow Hemm, sa vollant uldath et cumm hemm sost. Nel et hemm sost o veth ul molt et cummath.

After that the people climbed down to the warm harbours, and the guild behind them (behind it). Before their going, the first builders laid the slates of the Keep and its {?} first staff in the black chest, as a builder lays down his tools at evening. On the shore they built Eldhythe (the Old Landing), which men call the very oldest haven. It is not the very oldest, but [one] among the havens {?}.
- *ul o sestow* "in its back": *o* is singular and refers back to *et lunt*, the people (a collective). So it is "behind them".
- *o hulter hosast*: *h* does not soften, so the possessive cannot show. It may be "its first staff" (the Keep's) or "his first staff" {?}. No single builder is named here.
- *Nel … veth ul molt et cummath*: *ul molt* is "among, amid; in the heart of". It reads either "it is not the eldest itself, only one among the havens" or "not the eldest, but [it is] at the heart of the havens" {?}.
- *orrow* is in lower case here, "the shore" as a common noun.

**S04-II1-17**
> Ul sestow et dasken hyont, sa nath noss so vollath um halflenn, re vellor et helvhoss ul vimm et ganna sa re heth nahos.

After the last of them, whose names are not on slate, the wind came in under the gate that no one kept.

**S04-II1-18**
> Re omm o nafedh. Nath re yal o veth luskat.

It never fell. It was only let go (it was nothing but loosed).
- *luskat* is "loosed; adrift, cast off": "abandoned". *nath … veth* means "only" (§3.8).

**S04-II1-19**
> Hy dhumol re orr o, eth nath re heth hos o veth et velleth. Nath re yyst ganna dem susk, dem glennyl et brellirlodh, eth re wesk et helvnynth gor wrellir. Vallom et brod ull. Ul sestow et helvnynth re vellor et frenn, eth ul sestow et frenn, et crest.

It went out of memory, and no one kept it but the songs. No pair climbed up to mend (renew) the pointing, and the rain found every joint. I love that word. After the rain came the frost, and after the frost, the ice.
- *ganna* is "a gate; a pair (a pair is a gate)". With "climbed" it must be "a pair", a bonded pair of masons {?}.
- *dem glennyl*: *dem* + N·*clennyl*. *dem* here is "for, toward", as in the lexicon's *dem dolm eth crest* "for stone and ice".
- **Vallom et brod ull** "I love that word": the text does not say which word {?}. The nearest candidates are *wesk* "found" (the rain *found* every joint), *helvnynth* "rain" ("sky-water") or *brellirlodh* "pointing".

**S04-II1-20**
> Ul et grem sa re nydh nahos, re orr hosk et ganna ulvenn dem vimm hy et pa yann.

In the winter that no one counted, the lintel of the inner gate went down from the two posts.

**S04-II1-21**
> Re hunn nahos o ul mommol.

No one heard it in [its] falling.

**S04-II1-22**
> Ommol et cummath, re hunn Orrow tunn o. Ul sestow Sirrvell re yyster dem et reskow hosel sa es geth, hosen re vyskent et velleth; eth re wesker dhenn.

The fall of the havens: the whole Shore heard it. After Glasspire we climbed to the one place that would (will) hold, as the songs said; and we found a shadow {?}.
- *dhenn* is "a shadow; a ghost; a phantom". It may mean a ruin, or a ghost of the Keep {?}.
- *Ul sestow Sirrvell* "after Glasspire": a bare name, read as "after [the fall of] Glasspire".

**S04-II1-23**
> Re yald et nadhumol galat sa nath re yal o yaldol lo wynd fedh.

The forgetting did (built) a thing that no foe was ever able to do (build).
- *gald* is "build, raise; do, make". The line plays on the verb: the forgetting "built" what no foe could. *nath re yal o yaldol lo wynd* is the "cannot" idiom: "its doing was not at a foe".

**S04-II1-24**
> Ul hedh orl et movenn, re vellora Halvard eth Rhyna ul vimm et ganna ulvenn, eth et trenn ul sona sestow. Virr ul sona sestow re vellorom, lo et nynthsast. Nath re seskym sona vollath.

On the evening of the day of the banner, Halvard and Rhyna came in under the inner gate, and the column behind them. Close behind them I came, with the water-skin. I did not know their names.
- *trenn* is "a course; a column (a line of people)".

**S04-II1-25**
> Ul et ganna re yal et hosk ul firryl. Re rasor Halvard et sulv hyo lo o hovvel. Um o wisk re dhev et feth, stacorn hosen frenn um yann.

In the gateway the lintel was lying down. Halvard rubbed the snow off it with his sleeve. On its head the moss had grown, pale as frost on a post.
- *re dhev*: S·*tev* "a child; grow, sprout", used as the verb "grew".

**S04-II1-26**
> Re lumma sona ul sulv, eth re hadha sona yemm garl ul vimm o. Re hyle sona o, hosen kylent sugornath ul Gemm Hyll sulter sa re yal mammat sell.

They two knelt in the snow, and they two laid four hands beneath it. They two turned it, as fishers in Tidesmeet turn a boat that was long lost.

**S04-II1-27**
> Ew sona dholm o.

It was their stone.

**S04-II1-28**
> Re heth et vimmlern rellor Aldwena, eth et tragul. Ew gand so, hosen ul et orl amm re yalant rytet. Re yal dest wresketh et pardath lo so sest. Re hadh Rhyna ey nindeth um rellor ey vadullath. Re hadh Halvard nind strom ul nest o yedheth.

The underside held Aldwena's sign, and the promise. They were sharp, as on the day when they were cut. The bite (tooth) of the wardens' chisels was at their shoulder. Rhyna laid her fingers on the sign of her forebears. Halvard laid [his] thumb in the bite of his fathers.
- *dest* is "a tooth; bite". I read "the tooth of the chisels" as the chisel-marks, beside the sign and the promise {?}.
- *nind strom* "thumb" comes from the S04 additions. It has no possessive, so "[his]" is supplied.

**S04-II1-29**
> Ew tunn o. Ul et parow re vellor et lunt tul. Nath re yaldant et misketh hunnat um et sulv.

It was whole. Into the courtyard the people were still coming. The feet made no sound on the snow.

**S04-II1-30**
> Re heth et tolm sost o dhragul. Ew ol sa nath re hether ol dragul.

The stone itself kept its promise. It was we who did not keep our promise.

**S04-II1-31**
> Somm re yal et hedh lo et sulv tul, re harma sona et Stonwrytan dem susk.

While the last light was still on the snow, they two called the Stonewrights up.

**S04-II1-32**
> Amm re yal et Ternil tunnat, re dhald et tolm clenn dem et ganna. Et tolm hemm, re lymment et vennuldath o ul et hellur sy, eth re hadhant o ul baltur hald odh.

When the Rite was completed, the new stone rose up to the gate. The old stone: the True Men carried it into this hall, and they laid it in a niche of the hearth-wall.

**S04-II1-33**
> Gor sa heth o, keth tragul et galdardath hosast, eth kaemul so dhevath. Vess sy cadhom pa yarl umo.

Everyone who holds it holds the promise of the first builders, and the shame of their children. Tonight I lay two hands upon it.

**S04-II1-34**
> Cadhom o hosen re yal cadhat lom.

I lay it as it was laid for me (at me).

**S04-II1-35**
> Eth re vysk et odh: Tumar.

And the hearth said: We remember.

## II.3

**S04-II3-01**
> Lodhan eth Tolmath Vardh

[The] Bonded and the Theoliths (stones of God).
- This is a heading, with no article. The lexicon's set phrase is "II.3 Of the Bonded and the Theoliths".

**S04-II3-02**
> Molt syst hosast, vess hoskol Enrella.

The first high summer, the night of the sealing of Enrella.
- *molt syst* "high summer" ("heart of summer") + *hosast*. It could also be read "the heart of the first summer" {?}.

**S04-II3-03**
> Ryst ull re hoska Idrenna Enno eth Della ul vimm et ganna ulvenn, et hoskol hosast ul et Kethow hy et galdardath hosast; eth re hethym et greller. Vess ull re hadha Halyna sy lo et tevath, eth re vessa sona lom rellorol cedh re vysk vodh, hy vodh vallant tevath seskyl.

That morning Idrenna sealed Enno and Della beneath the inner gate: the first sealing in the Keep since (from) the first builders. I held the lamp. That night Halyna laid this for the children, and they two asked me to mark who said what, because children love to know.

**S04-II3-04**
> Lo sona yarl um et tolm, re hadha sona et tolm hosast:

With their hand upon the stone, they two laid the first stone (they began):
- *sona yarl* is "their hand", singular, not the dual *pa yarl*. I take it as one hand each, or as the pair's single hand {?}. *cadh et tolm hosast* is a set phrase: "begin (a telling)".

**S04-II3-05**
> Kethen et tolm olen,

We two hold the stone together,

**S04-II3-06**
> hosen kethen gor yalat.

as we two hold everything.

**S04-II3-07**
> Rhyna. Darra virr, gor hos, ul vodh doss weskyl va lerneth lo et vorr. Es noss et lern lo et tevath, eth doss lunn rernyl lo et hemman, amm dossant velv. Ryst sy re veskys Enno eth Della: re lumma pa, eth re dhald hos. Amm sy el Enrella sona.

Rhyna. Come near, every one, where the fire can find your faces. The children shall have the front (the face), and the elders may listen, if (when) they are quiet. This morning you saw Enno and Della: two knelt, and one rose. Now they two are Enrella.
- *doss weskyl va lerneth lo et vorr* is the "can" idiom: "the finding of your faces is at the fire".
- *doss lunn rernyl lo et hemman* is the "may" idiom: "freedom of listening is at the elders".

**S04-II3-08**
> Re vess nemmel lonta: Vodh doss umos? Re vysk Enno: El hosen hos uld; eth re dhunn Della o: lo yemm garl, eth nahos ullen. Es naldan lest hos gebb, re vyske sona, eth es ryten olna rellor ul vimm o dholm hosast, hy vodh nath noss tovvol et dasken lona. Re dhyvull et odh. Hy ull re vess et nemmel lom: Amm vodh es noss hoskol loth? Nath re yal tessyl lom, eth re serym.

A little girl asked them (the two): What do you feel ("what is upon you")? Enno said: It is like [being] one person; and Della finished it: with four hands, and no one else. "We two will build a house of one door," they two said, "and we two will cut our sign beneath its first stone, because we two cannot wait for the last {?}." The hearth laughed. Then the little girl asked me: When will you have a sealing? I had no answer, and I went on.
- *re dhunn Della o* "Della finished it". The lexicon's *tunn ullen* is "the other finishes (a line)".
- *tovvol et dasken*: "the waiting of the last". *dasken* "last, final" is used here as a noun. I read "the last [stone]", set against "its first stone" {?}. "We cannot wait for the end" is the other reading.

**S04-II3-09**
> Halvard. New Stonwrytan gor Orrowan, veth ew Orrowan gor Stonwrytan. Carm et Stonwrytan tev ul et tevow, hy odh sy ell hy odh ull; eth hos fedh, myskent et scaevureleth, hy lest vernul ul Gethow Stellath. Re nahebb odha ell gedh o nafedh, hy vodh re heth et Stonwrytan vennoth Orrow hosen re heth haldath Orrow.

Halvard. Not every Shorelander was of the guild, but every Stonewright was a Shorelander. The guild calls a child in the cradle, from this hearth or from that one; and once, the rolls say, from the house of a gaoler in Carnhold. No mother or father ever refused it (him), because the guild kept the faith of the Shore as it kept the walls of the Shore.
- *o* in *re nahebb … o* is "it" (the call) or "him" (the child) {?}.
- *Stellath* is the canon's harmony slip, which the analyzer records (by the rule it would be *stelleth*).

**S04-II3-10**
> Rhyna. Kethen: re lodh Mardh gor yanna ul hos, amm nath re hossa sona tul. Orrant veskerdeth ul molt et tevowath lo reller, eth ferrent, eth veskent galat sa re yal rytet sell tevar hy ol devyl. Nath naelurant. El Lodh Helv sy. Um o dhevow eth um en devow re ferr veskerd hosen ull, eth hy vess ull re firren ul hos, ul lest o odha eth ul lest en modha, hos eth ullen. Re aedhen hos vess ul ba lest nafedh, ul olna hossol tunn. Rellora sy, hy vodh es maverys o: doss et lodh galdat tevar hy et tevow; vesk veskerd o ul et tevow; eth nath noss o tunn veth lo et hoskol.

Rhyna. We two hold [this] {?}: God bonded every pair into one when they two had not yet breathed. Seers walk among the cradles with a lamp, and bow, and read a thing that was cut long before our birth. They do not choose. This is the Aetherbond. Over his cradle and over my cradle a seer bowed like that, and from that night we two lay as one, in his mother's house and in my mother's house, by turns. We two never slept one night in two houses in our whole life. Mark this, because you will need it: the bond is made before the cradle; a seer reads it in the cradle; and it is whole only at the sealing.
- *Kethen:* "we two hold / know". *keth* is "hold, keep; know (by touch)", and *sesk* is the word for knowing a fact. I read it as "we hold [this to be so]" {?}.
- *aedhen* is the verb, 1du (see the overrides above). It is not the adjective "sleepy".
- *ul ba lest* "in two houses": *ul* nasalises *pa* to *ba*.

**S04-II3-11**
> Halvard. Ew Idrenna olna Dholmath Vardh, sa reske lo et gebb vess sy. Re ammada sona olna stinyl, eth cadhol, eth rhynyl, eth ammelyl, eth kethyl nydhyl ul et Hal. Et ryt, re volla sona o lona: Ryt Dhunnoth, eth nayalat ullen; eth nath hebben galat ullen. Es hyrrilys o, amm doss o los. Es ferre Idrenna amm cadhan sy venn.

Halvard. Idrenna, who sit at the door tonight, were our Theoliths. They two taught us two cutting, and laying, and setting (of mortar), and the invocation, and keeping the reckoning in the Hal. The vow: they two named it to us two, the Covenant of Completion, and nothing else; and we two give nothing else [of it]. You will understand it when you have it. Idrenna will nod if (when) we two lay this true.
- *olna stinyl*: *s* does not soften, so *olna* may be the object "us two" or the possessive "our cutting" {?}. Object pronouns follow the subject (§3.5), so I read "taught us two".
- *kethyl nydhyl* "the keeping of the reckoning (list)". Compare II3-24, where the guild *heth et nydhyl*, "keeps the reckoning".

**S04-II3-12**
> Lo et gebb re reske Idrenna, bisk sulv lo wisk sulv. Nath re ferre sona.

At the door sat Idrenna, white head by white head. They two did not nod.

**S04-II3-13**
> Rhyna. Lo et hoskol mysk et Stonwrytan ul garm galat sa re vysk et tevow hosast. Cadhant et prassath hos voll um vana, galdat hy vana, hosen doss ganna galdat hy pa yann eth hy dholm cadhat umonta. Eth hebbent lo voll et rellor sa re veskent et veskerdeth: voll lo wrodow, eth rellor lo dholm. Nath ryt hos o veth sona yemm garl, eth es gresk ganna ullen o nafedh. Hosen ull re yalan hoskat, eth vollat Halyna. El humm Hyna o, eth el verr Halvard en; eth ew hos ganna olna sell tevar hy hummoth ell verryth.

Rhyna. At the sealing the guild says aloud a thing that the first cradle said {?}. The masters lay one name upon both, built from both, as a gate is built from two posts and from a stone laid upon them. And they give the name the sign that the seers read: a name for the mouth, and a sign for the stone. No one cuts it but their own four hands, and no other pair will ever break it {?}. So we two were sealed, and named Halyna. He is Rhyna's husband, and I am Halvard's wife; and we two were one gate long before husbandhood or wifehood.
- *galat sa re vysk et tevow hosast*: the relative gap may be the object ("a thing that the first cradle said") or the subject (less likely) {?}. My sense: the guild speaks aloud what was read at the first cradle.
- *es gresk ganna ullen o nafedh*: "another gate (pair) will never break it". *ganna* is "gate" or "pair" {?}.

**S04-II3-14**
> Re ferr Halvard dem et odh, eth re ferrent et tevath hosen o.

Halvard bowed as low as the hearth, and the children bowed like him.
- *dem* is "until, as far as". It could also be "bowed to the hearth" {?}.

**S04-II3-15**
> Halvard. Doss et vorrdhuss lilv vess sy. Nenda et nind sy, hy vodh es rytym o hos fedh hosel. El olna rellor sy. Ul vess et Ternil re ryten o ul vimm et hosk clenn, ul vodh es vesk nahos pawast o, somm ston et hald. Veska hos fedh.

Halvard. The ash is soft tonight. Watch this finger, because I will cut it once only. This is our sign. On the night of the Rite we two cut it beneath the new lintel, where no one will see it again while the wall stands. See it once.

**S04-II3-16**
(Not in the blind text. Nothing to translate.)

**S04-II3-17**
> Nath re varr hos tev. Ul et vorr re sol brenth.

Not one child moved. In the fire a coal lay still (settled).

**S04-II3-18**
> Rhyna. Amm sy dasorom o lo yarlremull, eth nath noss o. Doss vorrdhuss lo et helvhoss. Doss rellor lo dholm, eth es geth tolm.

Rhyna. Now I rub it smooth with [my] palm, and it is not [there]. Ash belongs to the wind ("ash is at the wind"). The sign belongs to stone ("a sign is at stone"), and stone will hold.

**S04-II3-19**
> Halvard. Um et hald ul gethyl re veskys olna: hos galdat, eth brod nafedh. El galdat relv galdat yanna, hosen re nydhent prassath hemm o. El Hemm Halvard en, eth el Hemma Rhyna ey; eth nel hemm hy et ullen en ell ey. Nath re hadh ey tolm pawast fedh.

Halvard. On the wall, at the holding, you have seen us two: one work, and never a word. A pair's work is the work of twelve, as the old masters counted it. I am Elder Halvard, and she is Elderess Rhyna; and neither I nor she is elder than the other. She has never laid a stone a second time {?}.
- *ul gethyl*: N·*kethyl*. *et kethyl* is "the holding: the hour of the siege day when the grey sails come in".
- *tolm pawast fedh*: *pawast fedh* is "a second time" (compare *hos fedh* "once", *pa fedh* "twice"). So she never had to lay a stone twice. Another reading is "she never laid a stone second [after me]", which follows on from "neither is elder" {?}.

**S04-II3-20**
> Re nydh luld ul lern delv lo nindeth, eth re raskant et nindeth.

A boy in front counted twelve on [his] fingers, and the fingers ran out (came to an end).
- *raskant*: S·*dask* "the end" (n, v) + 3pl.

**S04-II3-21**
> Rhyna. Amm stinym en narl ul et taldow withlern, ledh Halvard o um et hald wridlern, eth es gadh o los vodh garl. Re yal myskyl umo lo vrassath hemm. Grest um hos, grest um vana.

Rhyna. When I cut my hand in the south tower, Halvard feels it on the north wall, and he will tell (lay out) to you which hand. The old masters had a saying about it: Harm to one is harm to both.
- The verbs are present and habitual (*stinym*, *ledh*) with a future (*es gadh*). The English keeps that.
- *gadh*: N·*cadh* "lay". To lay a tale is to tell it.
- *et taldow withlern*, *et hald wridlern*: an article stands on the head before a softened, bare noun of direction. I take these as compound-like ("the south-tower"). They do not break the construct rule, because the possessor has no article.

**S04-II3-22**
> Halvard. Doss et Lodhan hy ullen merr fedheth, eth deld nafedh. Nath re yal lodh cresket fedh, ul ryteth surr et Stonwrytan. Treskol, nath re lusk Mardh o.

Halvard. The Bonded are seldom apart, and never far. No bond was ever broken, in the annals of the guild. Parting: God has not allowed it.

**S04-II3-23**
> Re senn et vorr hos nind. Re reske Enno eth Della, garl ul narl; eth re yal et nemmel ul naedh lo ey gerd.

The fire went down one finger. Enno and Della sat hand in hand, and the little girl was asleep at her knee.
- *ey gerd* "her knee": Della's, the nearest "she".

**S04-II3-24**
> Rhyna. Hy ull senn et Lodhan olen, eth nath noss ludal umona. Amm doss hos hy yanna hurrat, nath noss grestet lo et ullen, eth nath noss cathal umo. Veth el cresket et molt, hy vodh sesk o. El solat et derdsast, hy vodh doss o yaldat tunn. Thell orl, myskent uldath; nath re hoss hos hosel fedh sell hy ull. Clemeth, mysk et Stonwrytan, sa heth et nydhyl. Re hossant mest so hoss dasken ul hos clemel, bost lusul hy ullen; eth re sesk gor hos orrol ullen. Eth re fenn gor hos. Amm senn et garl ullen, ho nath senn et garl venn?

Rhyna. So (therefore) the Bonded die together, and we two are not afraid. When one of a pair is killed, the other has no wound, and there is no sickness upon him. But the heart is broken, because it knows. The body is at rest, because its work is whole. Eight days, men say; no one ever lived alone longer than that. Hours, says the guild, which keeps the reckoning. Many breathed their last breath in one moment, a mile apart; and each knew the other's going. And each followed. When the left hand dies, does the right hand not die?
- *Hy ull* is "so, therefore" or "after that" {?}. Coming after a narrated pause, "So" reads best.

**S04-II3-25**
> Halvard. Amm sy et galat dasken, eth hy ull dem lodull. Fedheth re senn hos hy dhevow tevar hy et hoskol, eth re hoss ullen, hy vodh nath re yal et lodh tunn tul. El nalodhat tev hosen sy, eth doss o hebbet lo odh lodhat, hosen sullast et odh. Keth et nalodhat hos voll, eth ul molt et Stonwrytan el lommol hos voll. Veth re vyskent prassath hemm: nath lymm Mardh et hos, veth amm lusk galdat lo et ullen.

Halvard. Now the last thing, and after that to bed. Sometimes one of a cradle died before the sealing, and the other lived on, because the bond was not yet whole. Such a child is unbonded, and he is given to a bonded hearth as the third of the hearth. The unbonded keeps one name, and among the guild one name is a mourning. But the old masters said: God takes (brings) the one only when he leaves a work for the other.
- *hos hy dhevow* "one from a cradle": one of a pair read in the cradle. It runs parallel to *hos hy yanna* "one of a pair" (II3-24).
- *nath lymm … veth amm lusk*: *nath … veth* means "only" (§3.8). *lymm* is "bring; bear, carry". I read "take [him]".

**S04-II3-26**
> Rhyna. El olna sullast Seren, sa re heth et greller ryst sy, eth sa ross ul rytyl sy. Re firre ey eth Tarnel ul hos tevow: Tarnel, hass vynull ul Gemm Hyll. Gor orl et orrol sell re yald Tarnel piskur, bysket um wysket, somm re orr o, dem lyntheth et brunnath, amm es mellorar so. Re ammad o ey et bysket. Ul vess et hiskenn re rik et bridyth tevath mest, eth merr hy et hemman. Re firre Tarnel eth ey ul vimm hos covv. Re yern ey lo o sest.

Rhyna. Our third is Seren, who held the lamp this morning, and who is writing this. She and Tarnel lay in one cradle: Tarnel, son of a boatwright in Tidesmeet. Every day of the long going, Tarnel made a net, knot upon knot, as he walked, for the lakes of the mountains, when we should reach them {?}. He taught her the knot. On the night of the pass the cold took many children, and few of the elders. Tarnel and she lay under one cloak. She woke at his shoulder.
- *sa ross ul rytyl sy*: this is the progressive, "who is in the writing of this".
- *dem lyntheth et brunnath, amm es mellorar so*: *dem* is "for" (purpose), or "until, as far as" {?}. *amm es mellorar so* is "when we will reach them", with *so* "them" as object. The clause order leaves it open whether the net is *for* the lakes or he walks *as far as* the lakes. I took "for".

**S04-II3-27**
> Halvard. Hy ull re hoss ey, eth el hos ey. Re vysk Tarnel loy: Piskur, el bysketeth hosel o, garl ul narl. Eth lo o wysket doss et flennath sy bysket. Nath noss kethyl et mesk loy, hy vodh doss kethyl kethet hosen kethe pa yarl tolm sa nath noss o yaldol lo hos. Veth nath senn ey hy sennyl ullen; eth doss orrol loy, hosel, ul vodh nath noss orrol lona; eth doss seryl et flennath sy loy amm sennen. Re yal gor ryterd demoy hy yanna; eth amm re orr ganna, re orr sona flennath dem nelter ul sona sestow. El luth hosel flennath sa wrodh nahos. Veska: nath hess ey dhenth. Doss maver hossol sell hy o wrodhardath um et flennath sy. Hy ull el Seren sa ryt o.

Halvard. So she lived on, and she is one. Tarnel said to her: A net is only knots, hand in hand. And with his knot this Book is knotted fast. She cannot know (hold) the wood, because the holding is held as two hands hold a stone that one cannot raise. But she does not die of another's death; and she can go, alone, where we two cannot go; and she can carry this Book on when we two die. Every scribe before her was of a pair; and when a pair went, their book went to the vault behind them. A book that no one tells is only ink {?}. Look: her pen does not stop. A wish for a long life lies upon this Book from its tellers. So it is Seren who writes it.
- *lo o wysket … bysket*: *lo* is "with" (instrument), as in *lo hos nind* "with one finger". *bysket* is "a knot" or "knotted, tied fast" (participle). After *doss* I read the participle.
- *Nath noss kethyl et mesk loy*: the wood is "known by holding" (§3.7). *yaldol* is S·*galdol* "building, raising": "a stone that one cannot raise".
- *flennath sa wrodh nahos*: "a book that no one tells" or "a book that tells no one" {?}. Both mean a book with no living teller.
- *dhenth* "pen" is the S04 addition (a feather cut for ink).
- *Doss maver hossol sell hy o wrodhardath um et flennath sy*: "is a wish of long life from its tellers upon this Book". It is not the "upon" idiom for feeling, because the "upon" is a book.

**S04-II3-28**
> Lo et covv re hess en dhenth. Re hastom o ul et luth, eth re serym. Lo et gebb, re ferre Idrenna.

At the cloak my pen stopped. I pushed it into the ink, and went on. At the door, Idrenna nodded.

**S04-II3-29**
> Olen re hadha sona et hosk umo, lo vyskyl hemm sost et Stonwrytan:

Together they two laid the capstone upon it (they ended it), with the very oldest saying of the guild:
- The set phrase *cadh et hosk um* is "end, finish (a telling)".

**S04-II3-30**
> Re lodh tevyl sona; nath re yal sona dhreskol lo sennyl.

Birth bonded them; death did not have their parting ("their parting was not at death").

**S04-II3-31**
> Cadhan o hosen re yal cadhat lona.

We two lay it as it was laid for us two.

**S04-II3-32**
> Eth re vysk et odh: Tumar.

And the hearth said: We remember.
