### Line notes (word by word, where it matters)

**W07-V6-01.** *Tevyl* is *tev* "grow, sprout" + *-Ol*, the verbal noun: "growing; growth". The lexicon gives "a birth" as its first sense. As a title, "Growing" or "Of the Growing".

**W07-V6-02.** *et heth gemmest*: "the spring fourth". *Gemmest* is *gemm* "four" + *-Ast*, the ordinal. The ordinal stands after the noun, as a quality does. It reads as a dateline, but the text does not say what it is the fourth spring of.

**W07-V6-03**, clause by clause:
- *Re lymm Halvard et molt sy*: PST bring/bear · Halvard · the heart · this. So "Halvard bore this heart". *Molt* is "a heart; the middle; a temper".
- *dem susk*: "up, upward (to the height)", a lexicon phrase. *Um et sest*: "upon the shoulder". The text says "the" shoulder; "his" is implied.
- *eth amm re yal o umo tul*: "and when · was (*re yal*, the past of *doss*) · it · upon-him · still". So "and while it was still on him".
- *re rarr o ulonta pana*: "came (*darr*, softened after *re*) · it · in-them-two · both". So "it came into the two of them, both". *O* stands before a vowel, so it is the subject ("it"), not a possessive (§3.5).
- *veth Rhyna lo et odh*: "but/though · Rhyna · at · the hearth". The clause has no verb: Rhyna was at the hearth, away from Halvard and the heart.
- *Ew strom o hyonta*: the past copula, predicate first: "was great · it · from/than-them-two". After a copula, *hy* is "than" (§3.6), so "It was greater than the two of them."
- *eth re sol o ul et nelter dem heth*: "and · rested/lay still · it · in · the vault · until · spring".
- *re hadhom vell et molt ul vell*: "laid-I (*cadh*, softened after *re*, 1sg *-om*) · song (of) the heart · in · song". *Vell et molt* is a construct, "the song of the heart", with no article on its head, as the grammar requires. *Cadh* is "lay (a stone, a course, a tale); set down". *Vell* is "a song; a rhyme; verse". So: "I laid the heart's song in song (in verse)".
- *virr hosen es morr et luth*: "near · as · will go (*orr*, nasalised after *es*) · the ink". So "as near as the ink will go".

**W07-V6-04.**
- *Vellyl*: "singing".
- *Fedh sell*: "a time, long", that is, a long time or a long season.
- *Ferryl*: "bowing; bending low".
- *Demo*: *dem*, 3sg masculine/neuter, "to him / toward it / as far as it". The text does not say what "it" is. Nearest is the singing: "a bowing toward it".

**W07-V6-05.**
- *Gann* is "a post, an upright; a pole; a trunk; a pillar (of the wood)". I cannot tell which: a trunk and a mast are both possible.
- *Meskrivull*: "a root" ("tree-foundation").
- *Meskel* is "a treeling: a small tree; also the wall's word for a root-man". In a list with "trunk" and "root", "a small tree, a sapling" fits better (?).
- *Nesket*: "led" (the participle of *nesk*).
- *Dem neld*: "away, far off".

**W07-V6-06.**
- *Sceth*: "a ship, a hull (a trunk hewn out)".
- *Nahos ulo*: "none · in-it/him". Read as "No one in it."
- *Nahos sost*: "none · self/very/even".

**W07-V6-07.**
- *Meskrivullath*: "roots" (the plural of *meskrivull*).
- *Ul vimm*: "below, beneath".
- *Gor yalat*: "everything".
- *Ul lodhol*: "in the caulking" (*lodh* "mortar; caulk" + *-Ol*).
- *Lonn*: "kin by blood; a kind, a sort". Three times, joined by *eth* "and".

**W07-V6-08 / -09.** One voice speaks to the dash, and the other takes the rest. So *Et vellyl — / — hess* reads as one broken sentence, "The singing — stops." *Hess* (3sg present) is "stops"; on its own it could also be the bare imperative "Stop!". *Preller, ul et molt*: "a knife (a short blade), in the heart".

**W07-V6-10.**
- *Stinet* is the participle "cut" (*stin* + *-At*). *Ulor* is *ul* 1pl, "in us". So "Cut, in us" or "cut into us".
- *Ul et stinel*: "in the stroke" (*stinel*, "a single cut").
- The line breaks off at the dash, and then comes **[ ]**.

**W07-V6-11.** *Eth re dhess nahos*: "and · answered (*tess*, softened after *re*) · none". This is a lexicon phrase: "And no one answered." *Dhess* is also a noun, "dawn", but after *re* it can only be the verb.

### Unread / doubtful (Orrowen)

1. **`[ ]`** (W07-V6-10) is not a word or a mark of the tongue. The course-hand's marks (§6.7) are the perpend, the wedge, the gate and the coping, and none of them is a bracket. I read it as a **blank**: a gap where nothing is written, or a word or sign the copyist could not or would not set down. It cannot be read.
2. **"I"** in *hadhom* (W07-V6-03). The 1sg is certain. The text never names its writer; it is the hand that "laid the song … as near as the ink will go".
3. ***Nahos sost*** (W07-V6-06). After a noun, *sost* is "very; self; even" (*et dask sost*, "the very end"). So I read "the very none": no one at all. Two other readings are possible, "not even one" or "no one, itself" (the ship alone). The grammar has no *veth* here, so "no one but itself" is not written.
4. ***Demo*** (W07-V6-04) has no stated referent: "toward it, or him".
5. ***Gann*** and ***meskel*** (W07-V6-05) each have more than one sense in the lexicon (see the line note). I chose "post/trunk" and "treeling (a small tree)".
6. ***Hess*** (W07-V6-09): "stops" (3sg) or "Stop!" (imperative).
7. **The dashes** (W07-V6-04 to -10) mark speech. Direct speech is not marked in Orrowen (§6.7), so these are the copyist's marks. The text does not name the speakers. By the antiphony rule (§3.9.5), one voice speaks to the dash and the other takes the rest.

## 4 · The two texts side by side (a reader's note, not a translation)

The Orrowen leaf is a frame and a fragment. Halvard carries a heart; it comes into him and Rhyna both; it is greater than they; it rests in the vault until spring; the writer lays its song down "as near as the ink will go". Then come the broken words heard from it.

Those words follow the grain's own story, ring by ring:

| Orrowen | Grain |
|---|---|
| "Singing. A long time, singing. A bowing, toward it." | r6, sung through many summers; the tree bent to the singing |
| "A post, and a root, and a treeling." | r3, trunks, roots and saplings into hulls, keels and masts |
| "Led — led far away." | r2, guided by the long road |
| "A ship. No one in it." | r5, none of us sails in it |
| "Roots beneath everything … Kin, and kin, and kin." | r8, the bond of roots through all the hulls |
| "The singing — stops." | r6, f5, sang, then stopped |
| "A knife, in the heart." | r7, the iron came into the heart |
| "Cut, in us. In the stroke —" | r8, carved in the wound |

The fragment then breaks off (`[ ]`), and *no one answered*. I note this agreement only as a check on my two readings. Neither was translated from the other.
