## Book Three

**S05-B3-0** *The Book of the Haven.*
- *Flennath et Cumm*: *cumm* is singular here. The lexicon's phrase row reads it as "Book Three: the Book of the Havens", so the singular is probably meant as a collective. [?]

**S05-B3-1** *Ten havens, and ten men who lay them: what we had, and how we left it.*
- lit. "ten haven, and ten man that lay(3pl) them: what was at us, and like what we went from it". *Vodh re yal lor* = "what was at us" (the 'at' idiom for having). *Hosen vodh* = "how". *Orr hy* = "leave".
- *sa hadhant so*: there is no tense particle after *sa*, so this is present or habitual: "who lay them". *Cadh* means to lay a stone, a course or a tale, so this could mean the men who built the havens or the men who tell them. [?]

## III.1

**S05-III1-1** *Their Building.*
- *So Yaldol*: "their building / raising" (*galdol* softened after *so*). The lexicon's phrase row gives "Of Their Raising".

**S05-III1-2** *The first autumn of the siege.*

**S05-III1-3** *The True Men laid [their tellings], one to each haven, when Kael the Counter named them; and every man stood up to answer, as under the banner. {{Halyna}} found the rolls in the chest that I brought. Whatever was in them and in no man's memory, I read aloud.*
- *Re hadhant et vennuldath*: the verb has no object. Something was laid (a tale, a course) but the text does not say what. [?]
- *hos ul hos cumm*: "one in one haven", that is, each man for his own haven.
- *Kael*: the lexicon also gives "a tally-notch". With *Nydherd* it is plainly the name, Kael the Counter.
- *dem dessyl*: lit. "until answering", "to answer".
- *re weske Halyna*: a dual verb, as a pair-name takes.
- *ul dumol nahos*: lit. "in the remembering of none".
- *re veskym o ul garm*: *vesk* means "see; read (letters)"; *ul garm* means "aloud".

**S05-III1-4** Give me the stone. No, I will stand. At a naming, men stand.
- *Hebb et tolm lom* = "give the stone at me". *Nath reskym; es stonom* = "I do not sit; I will stand"; the additions give it as "No, I will stand". *Lo vollol stonant uldath* = "at naming stand men".

**S05-III1-5** At first light I went up the eastern tower. From that tower you can still see the Shore, as far as the grey. Not one hearth on it sends up smoke.
- *dem susk et taldow*: lit. "to the height of the tower".
- *doss veskyl Orrow tul*: lit. "there is sight of the Shore still".
- *Nath lusk hos odh umo dhidh dem susk*: lit. "not one hearth upon it lets smoke go up". This is present tense.

**S05-III1-6** Now hear the names of the havens, and hold them, for they are ours.
- *hunna, ketha*: imperative plural. *Hy vodh dossant lor* = "because they are at us".

**S05-III1-7** Another man's town I do not lay. I name the haven, and the haven's own man will answer, and he will swear what he will build when he comes home. I am the oath-keeper. I hold them.
- *Hinnar uld ullen, nath gadhom o*: lit. "town of another man, I do not lay it".
- *uld et cumm sost*: lit. "man of the haven itself".
- *es ryt vodh es nald o*: lit. "will cut-a-vow what will build he". *Ryt* is to swear by cutting.
- *El rytvard en*: lit. "is oath-keeper, I".

**S05-III1-8** *First I read from the rolls, as it was bidden. Our fathers were builders, not seafarers. They feared the open sea, and they built where the mountains fall to the sea in long falls; and they laid roads over the mountains like veins. Every roll begins with one line:* This day the first course was laid, and a staff cut for the work. *The clerks' hand is small and careful, as though they hated to spend a leaf. Every wall in the ten havens was the guild's work. Ten havens, and ten staves.*
- *hosen re haral o*: lit. "as he/it ordered". *O* may be a person (Kael?) or the custom. [?]
- *Re yal ludal et wadh lunnat umont*: lit. "fear of the open sea was upon them".
- *lo ommolath sell*: lit. "by long fallings", that is, long slopes or drops.
- *Cadh … et tolm hosast lo hos trenn*: *cadh et tolm hosast* is "begin" (lit. "lay the first stone"), here "with one line".
- *hosen amm nath re vallant vyssorol et flenn*: lit. "as when they did not love the spending of the leaf". *Flenn* here is a leaf of a book.
- *Ew galdat Stonwrytan gor hald…*: a copula sentence with the predicate first: "the guild's work was every wall".

**S05-III1-9** **Eldhythe (*Tavow Hemm*, "the old landing"), the oldest haven.**
- *et cumm hemm sost*: lit. "the very old haven"; the lexicon gives "the Eldest Haven".

**S05-III1-10** Corlen of the Harbour. For fifty years the evening tide brought the boats home past my harbour's shoulder, slowly, to the warmest harbour-houses on the Shore. The first of our boats to come home, I will beach it. It stands.
- *Pa vurr eth noth surr*: "two twenties and ten year", fifty years.
- *hyll et hedh*: lit. "tide of the last light".
- *lo sest en davow*: lit. "at the shoulder of my harbour", that is, beside it.
- *dhaedh sost*: lit. "very warm".
- *es davom o*: *tav* is "come ashore, land a boat", so "I will land / beach it".

**S05-III1-11** *Then Corlen sat, the eldest of the Twelve, and held up a wet finger, as he always did at the Holding on the wall. There is no wind in this hall. The young men at the back laughed. He told them what he tells his battery when they want to fire: Wait. The sea is not done yet.*
- *re heth o nind thenth*: the softening cannot show on *n*, so *o* reads "he": "he held a wet finger".
- *et kethyl*: the lexicon gives "the Holding: the hour of the siege day when the grey sails come in and the wall must stand".
- *Nath noss helvhoss…*: this is present tense inside the narration. It may be Corlen's own words.
- *Tovva*: imperative plural, spoken to many.
- *Nath dunn et wadh tul*: lit. "the sea does not finish yet". The additions give "The sea hath not finished".

**S05-III1-12** **Tidesmeet (*Cemm Hyll*), where the river meets the tide.**

**S05-III1-13** Tamm of the Three Bridges. It stood on the two banks, and three great bridges bound it together across the river.
- *Um et pa hless*: *cless* "bank", softened after *pa*.
- *re lodhant sull heskal strom o*: *lodh* means "mortar; join, bind". By verb-subject-object order the subject is "three great bridges" and the object is *o* "it". The other reading, "they mortared three great bridges to it", would leave *o* without a role. [?]

**S05-III1-14** *Tamm laid three stones by the fire, in their row, as he does by his blanket every night.*
- *ul so dhrenn*: lit. "in their course".
- *hosen lo o naeval gor vess*: lit. "as at his blanket every night". The verb ("he lays them") is supplied.

**S05-III1-15** One bridge is a plank. Three is a town. On the bank where they stood, I will lay them. It stands.
- *El crynir hos heskal*, *El hinnar sull*: copula sentences with the predicate first. Read lit.: "[is] plank, one bridge" and "[is] town, three". "A plank is one bridge" would break the copula's order. [?] (the sense is plain enough)

**S05-III1-16** **Fenholm (*Sedhnell*), the town that was forever drowning.**
- lit. "that was every time in drowning", the progressive.

**S05-III1-17** Marl the Fen-born. Our haven stood on posts, and it sank a hand's breadth every lifetime, and every generation laid a new course on the old one. A fen-born man who does not laugh at that is a hill-man.
- *um yannath*: *gannath* "posts; a palisade", softened after *um*.
- *el uld wrunnel o*: lit. "[is] man of hill, he".

**S05-III1-18** Old Ebba, my grandmother, laid the last course of our house. I have a stone from it in my pack, and I will bring her too, when she lets it go to me. She is there, by the fire, bent over. I will lay Ebba's stone on the old course. It stands.
- *Doss tolm hyo ul en havul*: lit. "there is a stone from it in my pack".
- *es lymmym ey sost*: lit. "I will bring her herself".
- *amm es lusk ey o lom*: lit. "when she will loose it at me". *Lusk* covers "loose, let go; allow, let", so this could also be "when she lets me (allows it)". [?]
- *ul ferryl*: lit. "in bowing", that is, bowed or bent low.

**S05-III1-19** **Holtward (*Lurrvard*), the keep beneath the canopy.**
- *ul vimm et crisur lurr*: lit. "under the roof of the forest".

**S05-III1-20** Aske of the Canopy. It stood so deep among the trees that a traveller could pass beneath and not see its wall. We never cut a single shoot; and every autumn we swept the leaves off the wall-walk by hand.
- *Re yal o sost virr ul molt et mesketh*: *sost* means "so, very" or "itself". Since *hy ull* "so (that)" follows, I read "so near in the heart of the trees". [?]
- *re orr orrard ul vimm*: lit. "a traveller went below".
- *nath re vesk o hald*: "he did not see [the/its] wall". The softening cannot show on *h*.
- *re lunnar et haldorrol hy et flenneth*: this is the additions' new sense *lunn X hy Y*, "clear X of Y". *Flenneth* is "leaves" of a tree (additions). *lo yarl* = "by hand".

**S05-III1-21** *That night wreck-wood lay by the hearth. Aske laid his hand on every plank of it before it went into the fire.*
- *Re firr kaelirvesk et vess*: I read *et vess* "the night" as a time phrase. A construct, "the night's wreck-wood", is possible. [?]
- *somm nath … tul*: "before" (additions).

**S05-III1-22** They were trees once. No axe comes into Holtward while I breathe. It stands.
- *Ew mesketh so*: lit. "[were] trees, they".
- *narr*: *darr* "come", nasalised after *nath*.

**S05-III1-23** **Carnhold (*Kethow Stellath*), the keep of the cairns.**
- The canon name keeps broad *-ath* (*Stellath*). The common noun has the regular *stelleth*, "cairns; a burial-field".

**S05-III1-24** Garvel of the Forges. We quarried it out of the middle of the first quarrymen's cairns, and a man could tell from the ringing which forge was alight. Every gun on this wall, I listen to it as a mother's ear listens to a cough. Guns that ring true speak true. But on the anvil in Carnhold I will make a thing that is not a gun, and I will hear it ring. It stands.
- *ul molt stelleth et stannardath hosast*: lit. "in the heart of the cairns of the first quarrymen".
- *re yal seskyl lo uld*: the 'at' idiom of ability, "knowing a fact was at a man".
- *hosen rern odha hoss gask*: lit. "as a mother's ear [to] a cough (broken breath)".
- *Myskent venn brammath sa wontant venn*: lit. "say truly guns that ring truly".
- *um runndholm ul Gethow Stellath*: *drunndholm* "anvil" (additions), softened. *Kethow* is nasalised after *ul*.
- *es hunnom o ul montol*: lit. "I will hear it in ringing".

**S05-III1-25** **Emberhythe (*Tavow Vorr*), the forge on the burning shore.**
- *et orrow sa vorr*: lit. "the shore that burns".

**S05-III1-26** Harl of the Burning Shore. It stood where the mountain's smoke runs down to the sea, and the very ground was warm, so that our children ran barefoot on the snow. I will win back the burning shore, and I will burn the grey, and the sea itself, if it will catch fire. It stands.
- *lo visketh naesket*: lit. "with uncovered feet".
- *es gethym pawast*: *keth pawast* is "take back, win back".
- *amm grik o vorr*: *amm* means "when; if". Lit. "when/if it catches fire".

**S05-III1-27** **Sandreach (*Grullsorth*), the keep carved into the wall of the gorge.**

**S05-III1-28** Ulden of the Galleries. It had no harbour. But we called it a haven, because it guarded us. It was not built but dug, gallery under gallery in the red bedrock. Every night until the Fall I slept under the whole mountain as under my own roof, with my ear on the floor; and I heard it hold. Under that roof I will sleep again. It stands.
- *Nath re yal tavow lo*: lit. "no harbour was at it".
- *re var o ol*: *par* "guard", softened after *re*. Lit. "it guarded us".
- *galdat … paskat*: both are participles: "built … dug". *Paskat* also means "a ditch". [?] (I take the participles)
- *ul gethyl*: lit. "in holding".

**S05-III1-29** **Rimewatch (*Frennvar*), the watch on the frozen mere.**

**S05-III1-30** Hesk of the Mere. All winter the great hall stood on the ice, and the town ate under its roof in gladness, and was warm. Evening after evening a small torch went from window to window, and the lamps guided the fishers home across the ice. My mother tended those lamps. One of them burns on the north wall tonight. I will light it again on the ice. It stands.
- *Lo dhrenn et grem*: "all winter" (lit. "by the course of the winter").
- *re lestirent et grellereth et sugornath*: verb, subject (the lamps), object (the fishers).
- *Doss hos hyont ul vorr*: lit. "one from them is in fire".

**S05-III1-31** **Highreach (*Taldow Helv*), the tower of the floating stones.**
- *taldow et tolmath sa haemerent*: lit. "tower of the stonework that floats". *Haemer* also means "hang". The lexicon row says this phrase is the floating stones.

**S05-III1-32** Lanner of the Crags. The crags were so high that every course went up on cranes and floated on the wind, as hawks float over the field. I can smell wind a day before it comes. A boy of those crags whistles the wind up before he can walk. I will climb the very highest crag again, and I will whistle to the wind. It stands.
- *Lanner Gynten*: lit. "Lanner crag-born / of crags".
- *Re yal et gynteth sost susk, hy ull…*: lit. "the crags were very high, so…". I read "so high that".
- *re haemer o um et helvhoss*: "it (the course) floated on the wind".
- *Doss mummol helvhoss lom hos orl tevar*: lit. "smelling of wind is at me one day early".
- *somm nath noss orrol lo tul*: lit. "while walking is not yet at him".
- *et gynt susk sost*: lit. "the very high crag".

**S05-III1-33** **And Glasspire (*Sirrvell*), the last and the proudest.**
- *et dasken eth et deth sost*: lit. "the last and the very proud".

**S05-III1-34** Pellow of the Spire. Seven generations built it, and it caught the sun like a knife of ice. At the top stood the great glass, which threw the sun a day's sail out into the grey. My father polished it. A piece of it that I carried up the mountain in straw, I ground into our far-glasses. When Glasspire comes home, I will be the first to see it. It stands.
- *Pellow Vell*: *vell* is "song", or *pell* "spire" softened as an epithet possessor. Pellow means "of the spire", so I take "of the Spire". [?]
- *Lo wisk*: *bisk* "head, top", softened after *lo*.
- *re rig*: *drig* "throw", softened after *re* (*dr-* becomes *r-*).
- *Galat hyo*: lit. "a thing from it". *lo wroclinth* = "with/in straw".
- *re rasorom o ul ol sirreth dem neld*: lit. "I polished it into our far-glasses" (additions).
- *hosast es veskym o*: lit. "first I will see it".

**S05-III1-35** *Then Pellow, the youngest of the Twelve, held his glass up by the fire, as he always holds it up, even at his meals. He said to the children in front: Look close.*
- *tevel sost et Delv*: lit. "very young of the Twelve".
- *keth o o*: "he holds it".
- *lo o naskol sost*: lit. "at his very eating".
- *lo et tevath ul lern*: *ul lern* means "in front". It could also go with the verb ("said before the children"). [?]
- *Veska virr*: imperative plural, spoken to many (additions).

**S05-III1-36** Ten towns, ten harbours, ten kindreds that called themselves one people.

**S05-III1-37** Under the banner, twelve of us answered. Ten swore tonight. Stannard of the Ridge swore nothing, because the ridge was never a haven. He knocked once on the hearth-stone with his knuckle. He does not trust a stone until he has knocked on it.
- *Re dhesser delv hyor*: lit. "we answered, twelve from us".
- *Re rytent noth*: lit. "ten swore".
- *new cumm et sorth fedh*: lit. "the ridge was not a haven ever".
- *re runn o*: *drunn* "knock", softened.
- *Nath hebb o osk lo dholm dem drunnol umo*: lit. "he does not give trust to a stone until knocking upon it". Present habitual.

**S05-III1-38** I have laid ten havens tonight, and ten vows; I hold them. I have no haven.
- *Nath noss cumm lom*: lit. "no haven is at me".

**S05-III1-39** The Captain found me on the road.
- *lo orrol*: lit. "at a going", that is, on the road or as I walked. There is no article, so this is not the additions' *et Orrol* "the Road". [?]

**S05-III1-40** I lay it as it was laid for me.
- lit. "I lay it as it was laid at me" (lexicon phrase).

**S05-III1-41** *And the hearth said:* **We remember.**
- *Tumar*: the hearth's yes.

## III.2

**S05-III2-1** *Their Fall.*
- *So Ommol*: lit. "their falling". The lexicon gives "Of Their Fall".

**S05-III2-2** *The second winter, in the Second Tide.*

**S05-III2-3** *The True Men laid [it] a second time, each man the fall of his own town, on a night of hard frost, when the guns had gone cold and the mortar had not yet set. Kael the Counter named them, and I spoke to the guild.*
- *gor uld um ommol o hinnar sost*: lit. "every man upon the falling of his very town". *Um* means "about, concerning" (what a tale is told upon).
- *re wrident et brammath*: *brid* "cold" used as a verb, 3pl: "the guns were / grew cold".
- *nath re hyn et lodh tul*: *rhyn* "set fast (of mortar)", softened after *re*.
- *re vyskym dem et Stonwrytan*: *mysk* "say", softened, 1sg. Lit. "I said to the guild", maybe "I recited [it]". There is no object. [?]

**S05-III2-4** Give me the stone. No, I will stand. This tale is not one for sitting.
- *Nel dem reskyl et trenn sy*: lit. "[is] not for sitting, this course/tale".

**S05-III2-5** The names of the havens: you held them in the autumn. Hold them now through the Fall, and do not look away, for we did not look away in the telling.
- *lo dhrenn et ommol*: lit. "by the course of the falling".
- *nath veska dem neld*: *nath* with the imperative plural, a prohibition. *Vesk* is slender, so *-a* cannot be the 3du here.

**S05-III2-6** In the last spring of the peace, twenty months before the banner, the swallows built their nests in the arch of every gate. Not one of the havens ever broke a nest. The havens had no war-fleet. The havens' walls were built for ceremony and for weather. Every gate was wide, and stood open at night.
- *murr bress tevar hy et movenn*: lit. "twenty month early from the banner".
- *lodhnasath nasowath*: "swallows" and "nests", both from the additions.
- *ul grernil gor yanna*: *crernil* "arch", nasalised; *ganna* "gate", softened after *gor*.
- *Nath re hresk hos hy et cummath nasow fedh*: lit. "not one from the havens ever broke a nest".
- *galdat dem dernil eth dem helv*: *ternil* "rite"; *helv* "sky; weather".

**S05-III2-7** Out of the grey came the black sails, and there was no warning. No envoy came that anyone could understand, only a knocking out of the grey, like thunder; and it did not stop. The soldiers on this wall call it the drums. We looked for a captain on those ships, and a crew, and a man to each rope. No one stood on the decks. I swear to that. It stands.
- *Nath re vellor luskat sa re hyrril hos, veth drunnol…*: lit. "there came in no envoy whom anyone understood, but knocking…".
- *brammol helv*: "thunder" (lit. "the firing of the sky").
- *Vollant et hesperdeth um et hald sy o*: present tense, "the soldiers on this wall name it".
- *et drunnatath*: "the drums", the soldiers' word (lexicon).
- *hos uld lo hos bunth*: lit. "one man at one rope".
- *Rytom um ull. Ston*: the oath form (additions). *Rytom* keeps the canon's broad ending.

**S05-III2-8** So we named it the ghost fleet; and its senders, the Mystaeri, out of the grey. A man wants a name for his death.
- *scethan dhenn*: lit. "fleet of shadow".
- *et luskardath*: lit. "the senders / those who loose".
- *Maver uld voll dem o sennyl*: lit. "wants a man a name for his dying". *Maver* also means "need". Present, gnomic.

**S05-III2-9** Those sails were humbler than the sails that come against us now. Each sail came alone, and needed no other. No haven sent to another for help, because no one had help to send.
- *nestorn … hy*: "humbler than" (*hy* "than").
- *sa orrant ul ol lern amm sy*: lit. "that go in our face now". *Ul lern* means "against; ahead, in front". "That go before us now" is also possible. [?]
- *nath re yal maver ullen umo*: the 'upon' idiom, "no need of another was upon it".
- *dem mullen dem renn*: *ullen* "another", nasalised (*m-*), then "for help".
- *renn lo hos dem luskol*: lit. "help at anyone for sending".

**S05-III2-10** *Then every man laid his hand on the stone, one by one, Corlen first.*

**S05-III2-11** **Eldhythe.**

**S05-III2-12** On the first morning of that spring the black sails came in at the harbour mouth, on the rising tide. From first light to last light the harbour burned: the moored boats first, and after them the harbour-houses, where the ship-masters of the whole Shore used to sit by the fire.
- *lo wrodow et tavow*: *brodow* "mouth", softened after *lo*.
- *um et hyll sa dhald*: lit. "on the tide that rises".
- *et sultereth bysket*: lit. "the boats knotted fast", that is, tied up or moored.
- *higileth Orrow tunn*: lit. "ship-masters of the whole Shore".

**S05-III2-13** *Then he waited, as he had at his guns, and the hall waited at his shoulder.*
- *o*: "he". I take this to be Corlen, the teller. The text does not name him.

**S05-III2-14** At dusk the wind laid the smoke over the town, so the town did not see the harbour go. Three months it held, and on the last evening the tide came in over the ash, slowly, as it always came in; and the sea had not finished yet.
- *nath re vesk et hinnar et tavow ul morrol*: lit. "the town did not see the harbour in going".
- *Sull bress re heth o*: "it held three months". *O* is "it", the haven.
- *nath re dhunn et wadh tul*: this matches the additions' past form of Corlen's saying.

**S05-III2-15** *Corlen took his hand from the stone, and Tamm held it. It was the last time Corlen held it.*
- *re rik*: *grik* "catch, take", with the softened *g* dropped before *r*.
- *re heth Tamm o*: *o* is "it": his hand, or the stone. [?]
- *Ew et fedh dasken sa re heth Corlen o*: lit. "[it] was the last time that Corlen held it". By word order Corlen is the subject. Whether "it" is the stone (most likely, since he gives it up) or something else is not said. [?]

**S05-III2-16** **Tidesmeet.**

**S05-III2-17** For three days it fell. From the high road we watched the three bridges falling down into the river, and we heard them go. The high bridge went, and after it the low bridge. The middle bridge stood a very long time, and we waited on it, and it went. That night I climbed down into the dark, and took a stone from each of them.
- *ul mommol dem vimm*: lit. "in falling down".
- *ul morrol*: lit. "in going".
- *et heskal anth*: *anth* is listed only as a verb, "lower". Here it stands as an adjective, "the lower / low bridge". [?] (the form is a verb used as a modifier)
- *et heskal ul molt*: lit. "the bridge in the middle". *sell sost* = "very long".
- *re dhovvar umo*: *tovv um* "wait on".
- *re yystym*: *gyst* "climb", softened (*y-*), 1sg.
- *tolm hyont gor hos*: lit. "a stone from them, each one".

**S05-III2-18** **Fenholm.**

**S05-III2-19** In one night it sank, as though the fen had only been waiting. "A town: nothing but a last course on the old course." We said it all the time, and laughed. That night no one laughed. My grandmother carried her course-stone out of it on her own back, and she did not give it to me until the fen had closed over the last course.
- *hosen amm nath re yal et sedh veth ul dovvol*: *hosen amm* "as if"; *nath … veth* "only". Lit. "as when the fen was not but in waiting".
- *Hinnar, nayalat veth trenn dasken…*: the saying they repeated. The quotation marks are mine.
- *ey trenndholm hyo*: "course-stone" (additions; also "the tale-stone"). *Hyo* = "from it".
- *nath re hebb ey o lom, somm nath re esk et sedh … tul*: lit. "she did not give it at me while the fen had not yet covered…". *Somm nath … tul* is "before". The sense "not until" is my reading. [?]

**S05-III2-20** **Holtward.**

**S05-III2-21** It burned in its trees, and the leaves fell burning on the wall-walk we had swept. I laid my hand on the last tree still standing. Its bark was warm, like a living hand.
- *ul o vesketh*: *mesketh* "trees", softened after *o*.
- *ul vorr*: "alight, on fire".
- *sa re lunnar*: "that we cleared" (additions' sense of *lunn*).
- *sast et mesk*: lit. "the skin of the tree".

**S05-III2-22** **Carnhold.**

**S05-III2-23** It burned in its forges. As the smiths left, I heard the hammers stop, forge after forge. The last anvil rang a little while, alone.
- *Um orrol et drunnardath*: lit. "upon the going of the smiths".
- *ul hessyl*: lit. "in stopping".
- *re wont*: *bont* "ring", softened.
- *fedh lyss*: "a short while".

**S05-III2-24** Then it did not ring.
- The tongue has no word for "no longer" (grammar §3.8). This is plain "then it did not ring", which means it rang no more.

**S05-III2-25** **Emberhythe.**

**S05-III2-26** Five months it held on the burning shore, and all winter the line stood at our shoulder, warm on the bare ground. When it fell, one of our masons carried a charred plank from it in his pack. I said to him: Burn it.
- *re yal et trenn lo ol sest*: *trenn* can be "a course of stones", "a column / line of people", "a rank" or "a tale". I read the defenders' line, shoulder to shoulder. Any of the other senses is possible. [?]
- *dhaedh um et trun naesket*: lit. "warm upon the uncovered ground". The ground was warm (see III1-26).
- *tolmard hyor*: lit. "a mason from us".
- *Vorr o*: either the imperative singular "burn it!" or the present "it burns". I chose the imperative, as speech to one man. [?]

**S05-III2-27** **Sandreach.**

**S05-III2-28** Gallery after gallery was taken from us. Every night I lay down with my ear on the floor, and I heard the gallery above go before any runner came. In the end we came out of it in the dark, at the foot of the gorge. Every night since then I have slept on the open ground, with my ear to it.
- *griket hyor*: lit. "caught from us".
- *et saler ul susk ul morrol*: lit. "the gallery above in going".
- *somm nath re rarr hos haskard tul*: "before a single runner came".
- *re rarrar hyo*: *darr* "come", softened, 1pl, "from it".
- *en rern umo*: lit. "my ear upon it".

**S05-III2-29** **Rimewatch.**

**S05-III2-30** It was taken at the first ice. The great hall burned on the mere, with the lamps still lit. Window by window the fire went, as my mother's little torch went at dusk. It burned as bright as it had always been.
- *Ul et crest hosast re yal griket*: lit. "in the first ice [it] was caught". There is no subject; I supply the haven.
- *eth et grellereth ul vorr tul*: lit. "and the lamps in fire still".
- *tymornel en modha*: a construct, "small torch of my mother" (*odha*, nasalised after *en*).
- *Re vorr o hosen re yal baeg gor fedh*: lit. "it burned as it was bright every time".

**S05-III2-31** Into the smoke I went, to her window, and took down her lamp. It was alight.

**S05-III2-32** Then I turned toward the shore. The ice was new, one night old. All across the mere it spoke underfoot, far off, and then near. Once the wind took the flame down to the wick, and I put my hand around the flame, and it stood up again. Behind me the roof fell. Ahead there was no light but the one in my hand.
- *re hylym*: *kyl* "turn", softened, 1sg.
- *hos vess lo*: lit. "one night at it", the 'at' idiom of having.
- *re wrodh o ul vimm misk*: *brodh* "speak", softened. Lit. "it spoke under foot".
- *dem et prelor*: lit. "to the thread".
- *um gor lern et vem*: "round the flame" (lit. "on every face of").

**S05-III2-33** The ice held.

**S05-III2-34** I brought the lamp out of it still alight. Evening after evening since then I have lit it on the north wall, and it guides the night-watch.
- *hyo ul vorr*: lit. "from it in fire".
- *lestir o et vessvar*: present tense, "it guides the night-watch".

**S05-III2-35** Someone must keep a lamp.
- lit. "need of tending a lamp is upon someone". The additions give "Someone must keep a light".

**S05-III2-36** **Highreach.**

**S05-III2-37** Before the sails comes the wind. A whole day ahead of them I smelled the grey on it, and that day there was nothing for us but to go. We went down the stairs of the crags in the dark, with the children on our backs. Behind us the cranes stood burning on the crags.
- *Ul lern et wemmeth darr et helvhoss*: a present, gnomic sentence.
- *Hos orl tunn tevar hyont*: lit. "one whole day early from them".
- *re vummom et myst umo*: *mumm* "to smell", softened, 1sg.
- *nath re yal galat lor veth orrol*: lit. "nothing was at us but going".

**S05-III2-38** **Glasspire.**

**S05-III2-39** At the first snow it fell in one night into its own cellars, spire and glass together. The dark lies under it, still open. A few who fled say: a shadow stood far out in the grey that night, greater than any ship. I was on the last stair of the spire, and the glass came down on me in pieces, and the snow with it.
- *ul o nyrulath sost*: lit. "into its very cellars".
- *Firr et domm ul o vimm, lunnat tul*: present tense, "the black lies in its low (place), open still".
- *ul neld ul et myst*: "beyond, in the grey".
- *strom hy gor sceth*: "greater than every ship".
- *re yalom um wystir dasken et pell*: *bystir* "stair", softened.
- *et sirr ul mommol umom ul saedeleth*: lit. "the glass in falling upon me in pieces".

**S05-III2-40** I looked close. I saw nothing.
- *Re veskym virr*: the 1sg past of *Veska virr* (additions).

**S05-III2-41** *Then Pellow sat, with his glass in his lap, and he did not hold it up. His place at the stone, he gave to me.*
- *O reskow lo et tolm, re hebb o o lom*: lit. "his seat at the stone, he gave it at me".

**S05-III2-42** On the last day of the haven, when a mason's two hands had no other work left, the guild went out of it by the landward gate. They carried chisels in their packs, and nothing else. The masters went last of all. They washed their trowels, as after every day's toil, and wrapped them in cloth for the Road.
- *Lo orl dasken et cumm*: *cumm* is singular, "the haven", perhaps standing for the havens as a whole. [?]
- *amm nath re yal galdol ullen lo pa yarl dholmard*: lit. "when there was no other building at two hands of a mason".
- *et ganna gaeden*: lit. "the gate of dry land".
- *Ul sestow gor uld*: lit. "behind every man / everyone".
- *ul sestow pyskorn gor orl*: lit. "after the toil of every day".
- *dem et orrol*: "for the going", which I read as the additions' "the Road". The article fits the name. [?]

**S05-III2-43** The guild did not fight. It went, and did nothing else; and I was in their midst.
- *Re orr, eth nayalat ullen*: lit. "it went, and nothing other".
- *ul so volt*: lit. "in their heart", "among them".

**S05-III2-44** The staves the guild did not carry. The masters bowed each haven's staff against its falling wall, as a woman banks the fire on her last night in her mother's house, as on every night. A staff is cut for its work, and does not leave it.
- *Et hultereth, nath re lymm et Stonwrytan so*: the object is put first and picked up again by *so*.
- *Re ferrent et prassath hulter et cumm um o hald ul mommol*: *ferr* means "bow, bend low; lean; tip". "Leaned" is possible. *um o hald*: *o* after a preposition can only be possessive.
- *esk essa et vorr*: lit. "a woman covers the fire".
- *Doss hulter stinet dem o yaldat, eth nath morr o hyo*: the additions' "law of the staff". *Orr hy* is "leave".

**S05-III2-45** There were ten staves, and not one came up the mountain.

**S05-III2-46** *Kael laid both hands on it again, and he laid the Road.*
- *pa yarl umo*: lit. "two hands upon it" (the stone).
- *re hadh o et Orrol*: *cadh* "lay (a tale)". So "he laid (began telling) the Road". [?] (the figure is plain; the exact English verb is not)

**S05-III2-47** When Glasspire went, the column turned up the mountain. Haven after haven swelled it, and in the end there was no haven behind it. For a month we were in the passes.
- *re hyl et trenn*: *kyl* "turn", softened. *Trenn* is "a column (a line of people)".
- *Cumm eth cumm re vest o*: *mest* "multiply, grow many", softened. Lit. "haven and haven multiplied it".
- *ul o sestow*: lit. "in its back".

**S05-III2-48** On the night of the pass, the coldest night of the Road, many children died. A few of the old died beside them. They had given their cloaks to the children.
- *vess brid sost et orrol*: lit. "the very cold night of the going / of the Road".
- *merr hy et hemmeth*: lit. "few from the old ones".
- *lo so sest*: lit. "at their shoulder".
- *So hovvath, re hebbent so lo et tevath*: lit. "their cloaks, they gave them to the children". The past particle does not show order in time, so "had given" is my choice.

**S05-III2-49** In the morning the snow was still falling. We rose out of it, every man who could rise, and we went on.
- *Re dhaldar hyo*: lit. "we rose from it". *It* is the snow, or the night. [?]
- *sa re yal taldol lo*: lit. "at whom rising was".

**S05-III2-50** At the foot of the passes I counted us, and at the top I counted us again. What lay between, I do not lay before you.
- *Lo visk … lo wisk*: *misk* "foot" and *bisk* "head, top", both softened after *lo*.
- *re nydhym ol*: lit. "I counted us". There is no "again"; the second count is simply said a second time.
- *Nath gadhom los vodh re yal ul lodh*: lit. "I do not lay at you what was in the mortar", that is, between.

**S05-III2-51** I lay it as it was laid for me.

**S05-III2-52** *And the hearth said:* **We remember.**

## Words and signs I could not read, or read only in part

No word was unreadable. Every form parsed, and the additions covered the new words (*Ebba, lodhnas, ganurnas, hossvell, drunndholm, nindyorn, hoss gask, nasow* 'nest', *trenndholm* 'course-stone', *lunn* 'clear', *Orrol* 'the Road', *somm nath*, and the phrase rows).
