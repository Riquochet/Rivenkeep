**Tools used, and nothing else:** the grammar `wf7/orrowen_v2.md`; the lexicon `wf12/base/lexicon_orrowen.tsv` merged with `wf12/additions/S10.tsv` (8 rows); and the analyzer `orr_analyze.py --gloss`, run from copies in `wf12/tmp/S10bt/`. **Conventions:**
- [square brackets] hold words English needs that the Orrowen leaves to the reader: a dropped object, a copula, or a pronoun carried by the verb ending.
- (parentheses) give a literal rendering.
- † marks a reading I could not settle. The note under the line says why.
- Mystaeri loan-names are spelled by sound. Their glosses come from the lexicon, not from Orrowen roots.

## VI.3

**S10-VI3-01** The Knowing of the Green Wood. *(lit. "Holding the Green Wood"; keth "hold" is the tongue's verb for knowing wood.)*

**S10-VI3-02** The sixth autumn, when the boat went away.

**S10-VI3-03** Seren Two-Inks laid [this], who held the lamp. That night Halyna were with me at the stone, hand and hand beside my hand; and we knew it so, long before the telling.
- *re hadh* (S·cadh "lay") has no object. I supply "[this tale]" (cadh = lay a tale).
- *en narl* is "my hand" (N·garl after *en*).
- *re hether* is 1pl of keth: "we held / knew".

**S10-VI3-04** Hold the stone, Seren.

**S10-VI3-05** All through that day the tenth great seat lay where it had fallen: black its grain, and riven open, a hill of ship-timber higher than the harbour-house. It was Thaesaen ("the Tide-Heart"), in the harbour of Eldhythe. The soldiers did not go near, because they feared it, even dead.
- *reskow strom* is a throne ("great seat") in the lexicon, so "the tenth throne" is the other reading. † I cannot tell whether "seat" here means a throne or only a great sitting-place. The lexicon has no tree sense for it, but the context is a fallen tree.
- *veskdhrenn* is read as S·*meskdhrenn* "grain of wood" after *o* "its".
- *Thaesaen* is a Mystaeri loan. The gloss "the Tide-Heart" is the lexicon's.
- *Dhavow Hemm* is S·*Tavow Hemm* (Eldhythe) as a possessor.

**S10-VI3-06** Since the spring Halyna had known nine hearts. Every one was dead wood.
- *rost molt*: "nine hearts" (heart = heartwood). The nine are presumably the nine named in TOK-01..09.

**S10-VI3-07** Toward dusk the Captain himself came down, to it, and he stood a long while near, with his hood up. Then he looked toward Halyna.

**S10-VI3-08** At dusk they two arrived, and I behind them with the lamp. Overhead the water fell, drop by drop *(lit. "tear and tear")*. In the very old grain lay the heart, grey and hard and still.
- *re vellora sona* is read as S·*mellor* "come in, arrive" + 3du *-a*, with an emphatic *sona*. The analyzer offered "IMP.PL", but after *re* the form can only be 3du past.
- *nynth*: "a spring, a well; water". *semm*: "a tear", here a drop.

**S10-VI3-09** Beside the heart, in a cleft of the heartwood, lay a sliver that was no longer than a hand.

**S10-VI3-10** It was green.

**S10-VI3-11** I brought the lamp down to it, because I did not trust the light.

**S10-VI3-12** For six winters we had searched the wrecks for orders, and the hearts for names, and the grey for sails. Not one of us had looked for anything that breathes. But this was breathing in the black of a dead heart, as seedlings breathe in the crack of a wall.
- *dem* is read here as "for, toward" (its directional use, as in *dem bana* "for both").
- *ul nomm volt sennet* = in + N·*domm* "the dark/black" + S·*molt* (possessor) + *sennet* "dead".

**S10-VI3-13** From the first winter, a hand on the wood alone had been enough for them two. But they two stretched out hand and hand, and touched it in the same moment.
- † *hosel garl* has *hosel* before its noun, so I read it as an adverb, "only a hand".
- *loskat lonta* = "enough at them two".
- † *re selle*: *sell* is "long; stretch". "Lingered" is also possible.
- *ul hos clemel* is lit. "in one minute", which can mean "at one moment" or "for a moment".

**S10-VI3-14** A drop *(tear)* fell from the trees into the flame, and it whispered.
- *re vedh* = S·*medh* "whisper".

**S10-VI3-15** Halyna's two faces changed as one.

**S10-VI3-16** It was not a command, nor a wound. Rhyna spoke, as if someone had laid the word in her mouth and gone away.
- *haral* "an order, a command". *grestet* "a wound; hurt".
- *eth re orr dem neld*: I give the going-away to the "someone" (*hos*). It could also mean the word went far off.

**S10-VI3-17** Home.

**S10-VI3-18** Halvard said: It is true (= it means it). But the green wood did not give the word alone, and it did not stop where we stop. They two said it together, in one breath:
- *El venn o* has the lexicon sense "it means it, it is in earnest".

**S10-VI3-19** We remember our home too.
- The final *eth* is read in its lexicon sense "too, also".

**S10-VI3-20** Rhyna wept, and Halvard did not weep, because she was weeping for both. I held the lamp until Rhyna had finished.
- *dem dunnol Hyna* = until + N·*tunnol* "completing" + S·*Rhyna* as possessor ("the finishing of Rhyna").

**S10-VI3-21** Then they two gave me the other thing, veiled in the answer as a seed is veiled in the fruit: the name that the people of the wood give themselves. Naelear, the people of the breath. Three times I said it before I wrote it, and it stood. Mystaeri is a word of the Shore alone.
- *Naelear* is a Mystaeri loan, "those of the breath" (lexicon).
- *El brod et orrow hosel, Mystaeri*: the copula comes first, then the predicate ("a word of the Shore only"), then the subject.

**S10-VI3-22** Since the coming of the iron, not one leaf has grown upon the trunks. They have waited until the sky is as it was.
- † *flenn*: the lexicon gives only "a leaf of a book or of slate; a sheet; a page". Here it is used of a leaf that grows. The tongue has no other leaf-word in the lexicon.
- *gannath*: "posts; a palisade", and *gann* "a pillar (of the wood); a trunk". So "the posts" may be the standing trees.
- *dusstyvil* "iron".
- † *re dhovvant dem et helv hosen re yal o*: literally "they waited until the sky as it was". "Sky" could also be "weather" (*helv*).

**S10-VI3-23** So it was a seed.
- *hy ull*: "so, therefore", or "after that".

**S10-VI3-24** Rhyna carried it up to the Keep, on moss, with Halvard's hand beneath her arm. Into the courtyard ran a fisher-girl from the eastern keep, she who had held the northern lamp, and she asked: Where will it go?
- *Gethow* = N·*Kethow*.
- *nemm sugornan* = "a girl of the fisher-folk".

**S10-VI3-25** Home.

**S10-VI3-26** And we two wish to find a boat for it.
- *doss maver X umona* is lit. "a wish of X is upon us two". It is the feeling idiom, 1du.

**S10-VI3-27** There is no Stonewright's boat in the annals of the guild, none. Always the sea was a thing we built a wall against, and it was never a road.
- *ul o lern*: "in its face, against it".
- *new orrol o fedh*: "it was not a road at any time".

**S10-VI3-28** But the boatwrights of the havens carried the adzes up the mountain, as masons carry chisels. In the morning the boatwrights went down, to the shingle. The oldest of them was Tarnard, father of Tarnel. He laid the keel himself.

**S10-VI3-29** From the vault Harl of the Burning Shore carried the first plank on his shoulder. "It burns," he said, "plank after plank, through the war." He laid this plank at Tarnard's feet, and he said nothing more. Tarnard laid a flat palm upon it.
- *Harl Orrow sa Vorr* is the lexicon's "Harl of the Burning Shore".
- † *Vorr o* has no past particle. As speech it is present, "it burns", or imperative, "burn it!". "It burned" fits the context better but is not what is written.
- *lo visketh Dharnard* = at the S·feet (*misk*) of S·Tarnard.
- *garlremull ryss*: "a palm wide/flat".

**S10-VI3-30** Wood that held the sea, wood that holds the road home.

**S10-VI3-31** From the planks of the vault they built it. Each one had spoken to us once; and now they will answer for us.
- † *Re vysk gor hos lor hos fedh*: lit. "each one said at-us one time". I read *lor* as the hearer. Whether "each one" means the planks or the builders is unclear.
- *es dessent demor*: *tess* "answer; stand surety for", so "answer to us" or "stand surety for us".

**S10-VI3-32** For the stem Tarnard took the two of which I have told, riven edge to riven edge: the one that said "Shore", and the quiet one. Orvenna keyed them as a stone is keyed. Penna held the last key, and Ormund struck it, once.
- † *pana sa re hadhom*: lit. "both which I laid", where *cadh* = lay a tale. I read it as "the two I have laid [in this telling]". Which two is not said.
- † *et hos sa re vysk Orrow*: either "the one that said 'Shore'" (*Orrow* as the word spoken) or "the one the Shore spoke" (*Orrow* as subject). Both are grammatical.
- *cless* "a bank, a raised edge". *lo hless* is S·*cless*.
- *re runn* = S·*drunn* "mallet, strike".

**S10-VI3-33** The broken grain met, and it held.

**S10-VI3-34** That evening Halyna cut the sign upon the joint: one laid the chisel, and the other struck. Before that, no sign of ours had ever gone into the wood. I held the lamp, and it burned down a finger's breadth.
- *brellir* "a joint between stones; a hinge".

**S10-VI3-35** The wood held it.

**S10-VI3-36** Rhyna laid two palms on the carving, and Halvard two hands upon them.

**S10-VI3-37** Tarnard left the binding of the bow to me. I made Tarnel's knot upon it, which he had taught me on the long road, one knot to each mile. Tarnard looked at it a long time. Then he said, quietly, his son's saying:
- *lusk ... lom byskyl et lern* = "loosed to me the tying of the bow".
- *hos bysket ul hos bost lusul* = "one knot in one mile (four hundred paces)".
- *o dhev* = his S·*tev* "child". "Son" follows from Tarnel being "Tarnard's son" above.

**S10-VI3-38** A net is nothing but knots that hands hold.
- † *sa heth garlath*: the verb is 3sg. Elsewhere in this text a plural subject takes 3pl (e.g. *re orrant et hesperdeth*), so neither "knots that hold hands" nor "knots that hands hold" fits the agreement exactly. *Nel piskur veth ...* can also read "It is not a net, but knots ...".

**S10-VI3-39** One day it rode out past the spire, in hard waves, and it did not take water. It was small, and it was whole.
- *haemer*: "a raft; to float, ride on the water".

**S10-VI3-40** On the evening tide the whole hearth went down, the elders leaning on the young. Halyna laid the green sliver at the bow of the boat, on the moss, by the keyed stem. Beside the sliver they two laid a Stonwryt, half gold and half black stone: trust, sealed in stone. That was our dearest gift. Then they two laid the two chisels beside the Stonwryt, and at the bow of the boat the two chisels caught the last light.
- *styvil* "gold, the metal".
- *vallat sost* = "loved, very / own", so "dearest".
- *re rike* = S·*grik* "catch" + 3du (subject *et pa wresk*, a dual).

**S10-VI3-41** Let them go with it,
- *Luska* is imperative plural. *lo* = "at / with it". † *so* "them" is plural, while the two chisels would be a dual (*sona*). The referent is probably the chisels or the gifts.

**S10-VI3-42** for we two have cut our last sign.

**S10-VI3-43** No one spoke on the shingle. Halyna knelt on the wet shingle, before the boat, and behind them the whole hearth knelt. The soldiers laid down their pikes. Kael the Counter, who does not sit when a tale is laid, knelt with the rest. At the end of the line, his hood up, the Captain knelt.
- *ul lern* "before, in front of". *ul sona sestow* "behind them two". *lo et ullen* "with the rest". *lo rask et trenn* "at the end of the line/course".

**S10-VI3-44** He is sitting there now, at the end of the bench. The wet of the shingle is still dark on his knees.
- *yerdeth* = S·*gerdeth* "knees".

**S10-VI3-45** From the bow Halyna said:

**S10-VI3-46** This far can one people go alone: the gift, and the kneeling.
- *Doss* VN *lo Y* = "Y can V". *dem ull* = "as far as that".

**S10-VI3-47** The rest lies on the wish of the other people.
- † This line has two parses. I take *maver et lunt ullen* as a construct ("the wish of the other people") and *um et ullen* as "upon the rest" (*ullen* n. "the rest"). The feeling-idiom parse is "the other [one] wishes for the other people". The words allow both.

**S10-VI3-48** A wave came up the shingle and washed the keel. Then Harl rose, and he took the bow in his two arms, and he went down with it, into the tide. The fisher-children pushed it away. First, up to her heart in the cold sea, was the girl who had asked where it would go.
- *re dhald* = S·*tald* "rise".

**S10-VI3-49** Out beyond, on the tide that goes out from it, the boat turned. It went against the wind, and against the current, and into the grey.
- † *lo hyll sa orr hyo*: "at the tide that leaves it". "It" may be the shore or the spire, and the phrase probably means the ebb.
- *re hyl* = S·*kyl* "change; turn".

**S10-VI3-50** Along the shingle stood the old ship-masters, Merrick first. For six winters they had turned their faces from the sea, since the ships burned.
- *higil* "one who knows the water, a ship-master".
- *amm re vorr sceth*: *sceth* has no plural ending, so it could be "a ship burned".

**S10-VI3-51** They did not look away.

**S10-VI3-52** This I lay as it was laid for me.

**S10-VI3-53** And no one answered.

**S10-VI3-54** When three hands had gone from the stone, the hearth was long silent. The hearth-fire burned low, and no one rose to lay wood on it. We waited, I think, the space of one old call. Old Ebba of Fenholm lies beneath the eastern cairns.
- † *amm re orr sull garl hy et tolm*: lit. "when three hands went from the stone". It could be a measure of time or three tellers' hands leaving the stone. I cannot settle it.
- † *ryssyth hos carm hemm*: lit. "the breadth of one call, old". *hemm* is also "an Elder", so "an Elder's call" is possible. It seems to be a measure of time.
- *Ebba Sedhnell* = "Ebba [of] Fenholm" (the S10 addition: Old Ebba of Fenholm).

**S10-VI3-55** Then a child spoke, near the front, not loudly. It was the girl who had gone first into the sea. Old Ebba was her great-grandmother.
- *nawont* = *na-* + S·*bont* "un-loud".
- *scaelur hemm* is the S10 addition, "great-grandmother".

**S10-VI3-56** We remember.

**S10-VI3-57** And the hearth said: We remember.

**S10-VI3-58** Then, for the first time, the hearth sang like the wood. We call it The Two Homes:

**S10-VI3-59** Our home was hewn from the bedrock; / we forgot it, and we found it, and we held it in blood. / Their home was breathed from the hearts of the trees, and the axes came, and the breath grew narrow, and they did not find it, and they cannot hold it. / Stone holds the vow, and the grain holds the faith. / We remember our home. They remember their home too.
- *re hyl et hoss ul mynt* = "the breath changed into N·*bynt* 'narrow, thin'".
- *nath noss o hethyl lont* = "its holding is not at them": they cannot hold it.

## Epilogue

**S10-EP-01** The Epilogue *(lit. "the tale of the end", trenn + S·dask)*.

**S10-EP-02** What the Grey Let Answer.
- *lusk X ul* VN = "let X do V". The gap is X ("the thing that the grey let answer").

**S10-EP-03** The Stone out of the Grey.

**S10-EP-04** The fourth year after the Homecomings.

**S10-EP-05** Laid upon the leaf that stands before "The Torn Cloak", which Kael laid. From the first night that leaf was blank. Then I cut the wood-grain upon it, and one line in my hand: Untold. Halyna cannot yet hold this deep. In the end, no knowing of Halyna's filled it.
- *ross* = S·*doss* after *sa*.
- † *sa re hadh Kael* may belong to the leaf or to "The Torn Cloak". I read it as the tale.
- † *rytym meskdhrenn umo*: "I cut wood-grain upon it". The text does not say what that grain is (a drawing, or writing in the grain).

**S10-EP-06** Hold the stone, Seren.

**S10-EP-07** I write this alone, in one ink, because there is no one now who holds the wood for me. The two inks stand dry upon my table.
- *en nenth* = "my" + N·*genth* "a table; a shelf" (g > n after *en*). The lexicon's own *nenth* "a valley" does not fit.

**S10-EP-08** Halyna are dead.

**S10-EP-09** In the seventh winter they two died in one night, as the Bonded die: Rhyna first, in her sleep, and Halvard before the lamp went out, because he knew. They lie beneath the new lintel of the inner gate, in one grave, with one stone, and one name upon it. Upon them lie, face down, their sign, their vow, and the Title, where no one will see them again until the wall falls.
- *tevar hy sennyl et greller* = "before the dying of the lamp".
- *nahos pawast* = "no one a second [time]".
- *dem mommol et hald* = "until the falling of the wall". *mommol* is N·*ommol* (VN of *omm* "fall"); a broad vowel takes *m-*.

**S10-EP-10** At the grave Orvenna sang, Ormund to the comma and Penna the rest:

**S10-EP-11** Stone upon stone, and the stone holds faith; / hand upon hand, and the hand holds true; / two at the gate, when the grey sails gather, / two at the gate, and the gate stands whole.
- † *keth et garl venn*: "the hand holds true", parallel to the first line. The lexicon also offers *garl venn* "the right hand", so "the right hand holds" is possible.

**S10-EP-12** Idrenna lived on, who were old when Halyna were young, both. In the year of the Homecomings, before the two chisels went into the grey, Halyna cut Halvard's name below the names of his fathers, on the lintel of the western stair. Below that the stone is polished, and there is no one of his line who will cut it.
- *re hossa* is *hoss* "breathe; live; outlive, live on" + 3du.
- *yedheth* = S·*gedheth* "fathers". *o dhrenn* = his S·*trenn* "course, line".

**S10-EP-13** That night the Captain tore a hand's breadth from the half of the cloak, and the piece was tied on a pike above the gate. That was the last piece of the half. That winter he went in a plain grey cloak.
- *ul hosel covv myst* = "in only a grey cloak". The lexicon's note on *venn* gives *hosel* this "plain, only" use.

**S10-EP-14** I did not go with Halyna. They two told me once: You can go, alone, where we two cannot go; and you can carry this Book on. Now I understand the Covenant of Completion. God does not take the one, but only when he sets loose the work that the other holds.
- † *veth amm lusk galdat lo et ullen*: the verb *lusk* has no subject. I carry *Mardh* "God" over from the clause before. *galdat lo et ullen* = "the work at (held by) the other". Another reading is "when the work is let go by the other too".

**S10-EP-15** This I have.

**S10-EP-16** In the night when I laid my first tale, the fear of telling was upon me, and Halyna laid my hands on the stone.

**S10-EP-17** Hold the stone.

**S10-EP-18** Hold it, and it will hold you.

**S10-EP-19** There is no other now who makes it whole; I say both halves, alone.

**S10-EP-20** In the seventh spring, on a morning of thin rain, a small grey boat came out of the grey. It came ashore by itself, up the shingle, and there was no one in it.

**S10-EP-21** The old ship-masters stood at the head of the shingle, and they did not go farther. The children went down all at once. The first laid two hands flat on the bow. A child answered the green wood, when we had no answer.
- *lo wisk* = at the S·*bisk* "head".
- *amm nath* is listed as "unless". Here it reads as "when ... not".

**S10-EP-22** It was the very same child.

**S10-EP-23** Then Merrick went down, slowly, who had turned his face from ship after ship that burned.

**S10-EP-24** Merrick laid a hand on the boat's shoulder.

**S10-EP-25** Last of all I went down, and the children parted before me, as the sea parts before a keel. By its stem I knew the boat: the first heart, keyed, and Halyna's sign upon the joint.
- *ul lern wreller* = "in the face of a keel" (S·*breller*).

**S10-EP-26** It went home, and it came home. At the bow of the boat, where Halyna had laid the green sliver and the two chisels, lay a stone.

**S10-EP-27** It was not stone. Or it was stone that had been wood, like the Guest's gift. The Rivenmen name it Myststone, and say: It remembers everything.
- *Voll et Treskan o* = "the Rivenmen name it" (*voll* v. "name", 3sg with the collective).
- *gor yalat* = every + S·*galat* "thing".

**S10-EP-28** Stone does not translate. But it was never stone alone. A hand cut it that learned the chisel late, and well: young as they reckon, I think, and seasoned as we reckon.
- *Nath noss brodlymmyl lo dholm* is a lexicon phrase, "stone cannot carry words across".

**S10-EP-29** The rain stopped. The sea lay grey as slate, and the tide went back with a long sound.

**S10-EP-30** I knew the tooth.
- *dest* "a tooth; bite". From the next line, probably the chisel's bite.

**S10-EP-31** Twice I had held the lamp for that chisel: beneath the new lintel, and upon this stem.

**S10-EP-32** It was cut in our own letters. I did not lay my palms on it. It was cut for one who only sees. I read it aloud to the children.
- *dolmath* = N·*tolmath* "stones / letters".
- *vesk* "see; read". So the third sentence may mean "for one who only reads", as opposed to one who holds.

**S10-EP-33** No one at this hearth held (knew) it. [It is] laid for one who has learned the grain.

**S10-EP-34** In the memory of our home, of our families…
- The inscription carries a deliberate error. *Ul et dumol* puts the article *et* on the head of a construct, and the nasal *d-* of *dumol* has no trigger after *et*. It is the Title's *Ul dumol ol varn, ol theldeth* with one word added. The analyzer flags *dumol* as ILL-FORMED for exactly this, and EP-36 says the same in words.

**S10-EP-35** There the cut breaks off, and the last stroke is still sharp. He never saw our cloak, because he never sailed. He learned our letters. I name him the Last Carver, because I think: when he cut this, there was no one else left.
- *re hresk* = S·*cresk* "break".
- *ol govv* = our N·*covv* "cloak".

**S10-EP-36** There is one word in it that was never in our Title: "the", laid before "memory". I read it as it was cut, and no child laughed. I thought: Does it stop there because his hand stopped?
- *lern dhumol* = "the face of S·*tumol*", i.e. "before 'memory'".
- *Ho hess o* is present ("does it stop"). The *re hess o yarl* that follows is past.

**S10-EP-37** It came alone, and perhaps that is wise. We have not yet paid the Guest.

**S10-EP-38** Before the end of that spring the staff was gone from the corner of the gatehouse, and no one saw when the Captain took it. Where it leaned there is a clean stroke in the dust of seven winters. When you doubt the tale, go and look upon the dust. The work is whole. I know the stair that goes no farther.
- *hy yorn* = from S·*gorn* "corner".
- *bethirys* = *bethir* "doubt" + 2pl. *orra*, *veska* are imperative plurals.

**S10-EP-39** I did not go down to see.

**S10-EP-40** I keep this Book in the archive chest, among the quiet stones, beside the Guest's gift and this Stone. Grief is an inheritance, and it is paid on to the children. The Torn Cloak still flies above the one grave. The sea is not finished yet.
- *Dhenth* "a feather; fly".
- *devath* = N·*tevath*.
- † *Nath re dhunn et wadh tul*: *tunn* v. "complete, finish". The verb may have an unstated object, giving "the sea has not yet made [it] whole".

**S10-EP-41** What we shall let answer, I do not know. I know nothing but this: we wish to set the answer loose, kneeling.
- *luskol et tessyl* = "the loosing of the answer".

**S10-EP-42** Every night the inner gate stands open, as though a guest will find it; and upon the northern wall a child holds a lamp.

**S10-EP-43** This I lay as it was laid for me.

**S10-EP-44** And the hearth said: We remember.

**S10-EP-45** And the Captain let down his hood.

## TOK (the nine hearts)

Each line runs "It was X, in/on Y at Z". X is a Mystaeri loan-name, glossed from the lexicon. The haven names are read from their Orrowen forms.
