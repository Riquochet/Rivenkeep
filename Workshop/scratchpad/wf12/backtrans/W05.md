## 1 · The Orrowen text (`blind/W05.txt`)

The analyzer parses all 107 tokens as OK. No word is unknown to the lexicon, and no mutation is reported.

**W05-V3-01** · *Kaelir sa re vymm dem varn*
*lit.* wreck/ruin · REL(+S) · PST(+S) · S·return (*mymm*) · until/as-far-as(+N) · home
**E:** The Wreck That Came Back Home. *(A title: no article and no stop. More literally, "a wreck that returned as far as home". *mymm* is "return, come back; go back".)*

**W05-V3-02** · *Ul nunn et grem sullast.*
*lit.* in(+N) · N·deep (*gunn*) · the · winter · third
**E:** In the deep of the third winter.
⚠ *nunn et grem sullast* is a construct ("the deep of the third winter", that is, midwinter of the third year). *Gunn* is also "(n.) the deep", so an apposition, "in the deep, the third winter", is possible. I prefer the construct.

**W05-V3-03** · *Re yalant garlath Halvard um et sceth sy; nath re femm Rhyna o, eth stinel dhant lunnat ul ey garlremullath, veth re heth ey o hosen re heth o o, hosen keth lonn lonn.*
*lit.* PST were-3PL hands Halvard upon this ship; NEG PST touch Rhyna it/him, and cut S·salt opened in her palms, but PST S·hold (*keth*) she it/him as PST S·hold he/it it/him, as holds kin kin.
**E:** Halvard's hands were upon this ship. Rhyna did not touch it, and salt-cuts lay open in her palms. But she held it as he held it, as kin holds kin.
- ⚠ **o** (three times) is "he" or "it". I read "it, the ship" each time: hands on the ship, she did not touch it, she held it as he held it. The other readings are just as grammatical: "did not touch *him*", "held *him*", "as *it* held *him*". With *keth* "hold; (of the wood) know", the line can also be read "she knew it as he knew it, as kin knows kin" (§3.7: to know the wood is to hold it). I think that is the sense, because she does not touch it.
- *veth re heth ey o hosen …* is §3.5's case exactly: *o hosen* is "it … as", not "his equal".
- ⚠ *stinel dhant* is grammatically singular (*stinel* "a single cut"; *dhant* is the possessor, softened). The lexicon's phrase row glosses it as plural, "salt-cuts". The clause has no verb, so "lay open" is my addition.
- ⚠ (grammar note, not a reading problem) *ul ey*: §3.3 nasalises a slender vowel with *n-*, which would give *ul ney*. The text writes no bedding on the pronoun, and the analyzer accepts that. Either pronouns resist the bedding, which the grammar does not say, or the line breaks §3.3.

**W05-V3-04** · *— Tant. Tant ul grynir eth crynir. Brid, fedh sell.*
*lit.* salt. salt in(+N) N·plank (*crynir*) and plank. cold, time/season long.
**E:** — Salt. Salt in plank and plank. Cold, a long time.
⚠ *grynir eth crynir*, "plank and plank", I read as "plank on plank, every plank". *Fedh sell* is "a long time" or "a long season". The dash marks speech, but the speaker is not named. I read it as the ship's knowing, as Halvard and Rhyna hold it.

**W05-V3-05** · *— Tolm, ul drodol. Dusstyvil, vimm, ulo eth hyo. Et wadh ulo.*
*lit.* stone, in(+N) flashing (VN; a *d* before *r* is not bedded, §2.5). iron, low, in-it and out-of-it. the sea in-it.
**E:** — A stone, flashing. Iron, low, into it and out of it. The sea in it.
"In it and out of it" means "through it". *ulo* and *hyo* are 3SG.M, "him" or "it". I read "it, the ship".

**W05-V3-06** · *— Dem varn, amm grestet. Dem varn, niss. Brodhol dem lonn.*
*lit.* to/toward(+N) home, when wounded. toward home, slow. telling to/for(+N) kin.
**E:** — Home, when wounded. Home, slowly. A telling for the kin.
*amm* is also "if" ("home if wounded"). *dem* is also "for", and *lonn* is also "a kindred".

**W05-V3-07** · *— Vesk hosast —*
**E:** — Look first —
*Vesk* is a bare stem: an imperative to one, or a 3sg "sees". It can also mean "read" or "seem".

**W05-V3-08** · *— eth hy ull osk.*
*lit.* and · from that (*hy ull* "afterward, then") · trust
**E:** — and then trust.
Lines 07–08 are one sentence split across two dashes. That may be two voices taking one line, as in the antiphony of §3.9.5, but the text does not use the wedge, so this is my guess.

**W05-V3-09** · *— Pawast. Et nynth sost. Hosel, eth niss.*
*lit.* second / a second time. the spring/well/water self/very. alone, and slow.
**E:** — A second time. The water itself. Alone, and slow.
⚠ **nynth** means first "a spring, a well" (fresh water); "water" is only its third sense. *Et nynth sost* is "the very spring" or "the water itself". I cannot tell what it refers to: the sea held to (compare grain r10), or a real spring. Low confidence. ⚠ **Pawast** can also be a noun, "the second" or "a lieutenant". I read the adverb, because *hosel* "alone" follows.

**W05-V3-10** · *— Hos vorr, driget. Ul vodh re omm o — [ ]*
*lit.* one fire, thrown. in what (= where) · PST · fall · it — [ ]
**E:** — One fire, thrown. Where it fell — [ ]
- *Ul vodh* is the question word "where". No question particle is needed, so "Where did it fall?" is equally possible.
- ⚠ **[ ]** holds no letters, and Orrowen has no such mark (§6.7). By grain spec §16 (K3), `[ ]` is Seren's print of the grain's blind before K3: "the wood does not hold it". So I read it as **[what was there, the wood does not hold]**, an editorial blank rather than a word. It matches grain r11 n4, `→ ∅`.

**W05-V3-11** · *— Tolm, ul drodol. Dusstyvil, vimm, ulo eth —*
**E:** — A stone, flashing. Iron, low, into it and —
⚠ The line is broken off after *eth* "and". It repeats line 05 and stops before *hyo* "out of it" (compare grain r15, where the iron finds the same wound again).

**W05-V3-12** · *Eth re dhess nahos.*
*lit.* and · PST · S·answer (*tess*) · none
**E:** And no one answered.
- ⚠ (homograph) *dhess* unmutated is also the noun "the first light, dawn". After *re* a verb is wanted, so I read *tess* softened. A listener could hear "dawn" in it. The same holds for *heth* in line 03: S·*keth* "hold", or the noun "spring".
- The lexicon carries this whole line as a set phrase, "And no one answered", so it did not have to be worked out.

## 2 · The grain (`blind/W05_grain.gn2`), ring by ring

**Header.** `round V-3 {wood: life; pith: war; rings: 20; rules after: [2, 16]; …}`. This is a **telling**, because the seal `bark 0: HOLD` stands in the bark. It is cut in wood of a shore-man's life, with a war-pith (the square pith, *Grow until the shore is silent*). It has twenty rings. The band-rules after rings 2 and 16 give band I = rings 1–2, band II = rings 3–16 and band III = rings 17–20.

## 4 · Findings

1. **The blind inputs carry the Book.** W05_grain.md's "Where" column and its A15 rows, and grain spec §21.9.2–3, quote the leaf's English (see the caveat above). A blind reader given these files has the answer key for about fourteen phrases. *Proposed:* strip the quotation columns from what the blind reader is given, or give the blind reader only the `json` compounds block and the A15 *Grain → reading* pairs.
2. **The lexicon's set phrases translate whole lines.** *Eth re dhess nahos*, "And no one answered", is a lexicon row, and so is *stinel dhant*, "salt-cuts". The analyzer prints them as phrase glosses. This is a smaller leak of the same kind.
3. **Without `# file` comments the validator names file 12 "Hull", not "we"**, and file 15 "the one on file 15". A16b(c) and (d) would not help file 15, because its band lies over marks, not over an empty cell.
4. **Rays skip rings (r9, r13).** A ray from r4 to r11, or from r11 to r14, is a hairline along file 0 that passes the file-0 stones of r6, r7, r9 and r13. The spec does not say whether a ray that passes a mark on its own file makes that mark the same one too. This decides whether r13's flashing stone is the stone that spoke.
5. **`STERN½@1`**: the literal reading drops the ordinal, and `--expr` reports it as both "file referent" and "ordinal".
6. **The Orrowen and the grain agree** wherever the Orrowen speaks. Lines 04–11 are the ship's knowing in short words, in the grain's order:
   - salt in every board: r1;
   - cold, a long time: r2 and r18;
   - a stone flashing, the iron low and through, the sea in it: r3–r5;
   - home when wounded, home slowly: r6;
   - a telling for the kin: r7;
   - look first, then trust: r9;
   - a second time, alone and slow: r10;
   - one fire thrown, and where it fell `[ ]`: r11 n4 `→ ∅`;
   - the iron again, broken off: r15.

   Line 12, "no one answered", is narration, like lines 02–03. It echoes r11's "it did not answer our fire", but its subject is *nahos*, "no one", and not the stone. So I read it as no one answering the broken voice. Who would have answered is not said.
