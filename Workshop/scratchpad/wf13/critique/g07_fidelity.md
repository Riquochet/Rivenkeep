## Fixes

**1. IV.5, line 17. A spoiler: cut the stones' origin.** This is the writer's own unsure point 1, and the answer is to cut. The Book's IV.5 says only *What they said, none wist.* The Book first joins the three grey stones to the felling in the Last Note (Book line 2890: *At the feet of the black pillars they were cut, and carried off with the timber, and sold. Three generations they hung over a harbour-house fire at Eldhythe.*), which is H36. IV.1 tells only that *some* mute stones hung in harbour-houses. Linking those to Elspet's three is a plant that the Last Note pays off. Under primer §1.3 that link is left for the reader to make.
- Old: `Over the fire hung three flat grey stones, carved with letters as fine as the tracks of birds on wet sand. They'd come home with the timber from the edge of the grey, the great fog bank out at sea, and nobody knew what they said. Only the children ever asked.`
- New: `Over the fire hung three flat grey stones, carved with letters as fine as the tracks of birds on wet sand. Nobody knew what they said. Only the children ever asked.`
- The gloss on *the grey* is lost with that clause, so move it to its next use, line 19:
  - Old: `Whenever some bold man came home from out by the grey with a tale to tell,`
  - New: `Whenever some bold man came home from out by the grey, the great fog bank out at sea, with a tale to tell,`
- In the WRITER'S NOTES, change the lore bullet (line 203) to *The three grey stones: nobody knew what they said. The grey: the great fog bank out at sea (glossed at the bold men).* Also mark unsure point 1 as resolved: the clause is cut.

**2. V.1, line 131. The forest's thought is misread.** *the thought of a forest* and *the thought of the shore* read in modern English as "the idea of a forest" and "the idea of the shore." That suggests Halyna glimpsed a forest and had only the word *shore*. The Book means a forest's *way of thinking* against ours (*the rest was a forest's thought, and they had but the shore's*).
- Old: `But the rest was the thought of a forest, and they had only the thought of the shore to hold it with.`
- New: `But the rest was thought the way a forest thinks, and all they had to hold it with was our own way of thinking, the shore's.`
- This reveals nothing beyond H3, because the forest is the Book's own word. In the WRITER'S NOTES, update line 246 to match.

**3. V.1, line 107. Wrong lore on how a pair gets one name.** *They're a bonded pair … and so we call them by one name* makes the pair-name follow from being bonded. In fact the masters give it at the sealing (Book 874). Two paragraphs later the same tale calls Enno and Della *the youngest bonded pair*, and they still go by two names. The wording should not say when or how the name is given (that's H7). It should only say what the name is.
- Old: `They're a bonded pair, two people joined as one from birth, and so we call them by one name.`
- New: `They're a bonded pair, two people joined as one from birth, and the guild gave them one name, made from both of theirs.`

**4. V.1, line 111. *bare* is invented, and it contradicts I.4.** The Book says *wood was dear on the ridge*. I.4 (Book 532) has a stand of cedar on the ridge, where Stannard took {{Halyna}} for the Rite's cedar and the builders wouldn't fell a living tree. *Scarce* is right. *Bare* is not.
- Old: `because firewood was scarce up on our bare mountain ridge.`
- New: `because firewood was scarce up on our mountain ridge.`
- In the WRITER'S NOTES, also update line 235.

**5. V.1, line 121. *ever* overstates the seal.** The Book says *a bundle … that no pair would break*. The foreword (Book 118) says that no pair would break it *till the wood showed {{Halyna}} what the mute stones beside it were. Then {{Idrenna}} gave leave.* So in IV.3 the seal is broken, with leave. *Would ever break* makes the custom absolute, and that becomes false later.
- Old: `and no bonded pair would ever break another pair's seal.`
- New: `and no bonded pair would break another pair's seal.`
- In the WRITER'S NOTES, also update line 242.

**6. IV.5, line 31. Keep the outpost men's oath passive, as the Book has it.** The Book says *the outpost men sware in their cups to a boat shoved off burning, with a dead man in it*. They swear that it happened, not that they did it. *They'd pushed* turns the rumour into a confession by the outpost men, which goes a step toward the truth at the door. That truth stays dark until IV.3 (H16).
- Old: `They swore they'd pushed a boat off burning, with a dead man in it.`
- New: `They swore a boat had been pushed off burning, with a dead man in it.`

**7. IV.5, line 11. Put the ship on the shingle, not Voss.** In *Down on the shingle, the pebble beach below the wall, I heard one of their ships burn*, the opening phrase places Voss on the beach in the middle of the holding. But the old sailors are *along the wall* and Jory is on the east wall, and later the fire-ship comes in *below us … on the stones*. What was on the shingle is the burning hull.
- Old: `Down on the shingle, the pebble beach below the wall, I heard one of their ships burn. It screamed.`
- New: `I heard one of their ships burning on the shingle, the pebble beach below the wall. It screamed.` (Avoid *burning down on*, which reads as *burning down*.)

**8. Optional. V.1, line 111: Harl's epithet next to *Burn it*.** For a newcomer, *Harl of the burning shore* followed by *"Burn it"* can read as a man, or a people, given to burning. That is the custom primer §1.9 forbids. One clause naming Emberhythe (H0) ends that reading, and it keeps the temper:
- Old: `Harl has a quick temper.`
- New: `He comes from Emberhythe, a forge-town on a volcanic coast, and he has a quick temper.`

## Checked and cleared (no change)

- **Beats and order.** IV.5 has every one of the Book's paragraphs, in order. V.1 has every beat in order. Harl's *Burn it* now comes before the rib, as in the Book, which fixes the old Plain's reversal.
- **Facts.**
  - The numbers all match: twelve, seven and forty; two boats against twenty; three men, three words and three ship-masters; *nearly a month*; four hands against one; a finger's width; three times; seventeen.
  - *His mother's harbour-house* is right, since Daveth's mother is Elspet's mother.
  - Voss asked his question *at this fire* (I.5, Book 623–625) late in the first winter, so the headnote's *a question he once put to this hearth* is known by H12. Leaving out the night he asked it was the right choice.
  - *Fire-ship* is the Book's own word (V.5 I: *A fire-ship took him*).
- **Spoilers and mysteries.**

  - *Mystwood* is glossed no further than the primer allows.
  - In V.1, Jory is alive, with no hint of his death. His tune is I.3's *harbour tune the ship-masters can't bear*, not named as the grey-boat rhyme.
  - The knowing is explained without *takes two* (H7), and *third* without *unbonded* (H7). Enno and Della stay unpaired.
