# w01 · Newcomer read

**Reader:** a modern adult reader who has never heard of Rivenkeep. I read only `wf13/plain/w01.md`, top to bottom, in order: II.2, IV.2, IV.4, IV.6.

**Rendering assumptions.** I took `{{Halyna}}` to show as the name "Halyna". I took the `[⟦…⟧]` chips to show as a symbol or blank, the `::: note` blocks to show as a boxed side-note the reader sees, and the `<!-- … -->` lines to be invisible. The WRITER'S NOTES section says it is not reader text, so I left it out of this critique.

**Tags:** **[WORD]** a word, name or custom I didn't understand · **[WHO?]** · **[WHY?]** why does this matter? · **[HINT]** a hint I didn't get · **[REREAD]** I had to read it twice · **[BORED]** over-explained · **[CLASH]** seemed to contradict something else · **[LANDED]** worked, keep it.

---

## The biggest problems first

1. **"The siege" and "the enemy" are never set up.** The first line I read is *"in the fifth winter of the siege"*, and nothing tells me who is besieged or by whom. I learn in passing, in IV.4's headnote, that "we" fled *"out of our burning cities and up the mountains"*. Only at the very end of IV.6 do I finally put together that the enemy ships attacking "your wall" are the wood-people from II.2. That's the emotional core of the whole set: the people who begged for peace are now the enemy. I got it, but late and by inference. If the slow reveal is on purpose, fine. Still, II.2's when-line needs at least a few words saying whose siege this is.
2. **"Halyna, Halvard and Rhyna, the husband and wife…"** reads as a list of three people. I spent the whole first headnote thinking Halyna was a third person, until *"one of them awake at the hearth and the other asleep"*. Then IV.4 and IV.6 drop "Halyna" and say *"Halvard and Rhyna"*, so I wondered whether "Halyna" means something different, like the pair only when they work as one. Say once, plainly, that Halyna is the name for the two of them together.
3. **The two boxed notes are the hardest text in the file.**
   - The II.2 note gives the name *"Naelear"* right after the headnote says the name *"hasn't come to us yet"*. It also introduces *"Seren"*, who is never mentioned anywhere else.
   - The IV.2 note (*"the mouth, drawn as a mirror image, and tied back across the rings to the plea cut on the stone"*) is jargon to me. It also gives away *"It did not answer"* before the tale reaches that line.
4. **The same headnote paragraph comes four times.** *"This is one of the wood's tales, a memory held in the grain of wood … one taking up where the other stops."* The first time I needed it. By IV.4 I was skimming, and by IV.6 I skipped it. The same goes for the glosses repeated in every tale: *"the grey that you call fog"*, *"the shore-men, your people"*, *"the iron…, your axes"* and *"the long-lived, the people of our wood"*.
5. **Three things looked like contradictions:**
   - In II.2 the pillars hold the breath **up**, *"the way a mast holds up a sail"*. In IV.2 they hold it **down**, *"the way roots hold a riverbank together"*.
   - In II.2 the Guest's gift is *"dark, old wood"*. In IV.4 it is *"the dark stone"*. The tale explains this away, but only after I'd stumbled.
   - II.2 happens in the *fifth* winter of the siege, but the three Part IV tales happen in the *third* year. Read in book order, time seems to run backwards.
6. **Who is "I", and who is "we"?** The headnotes suddenly say *"I've set down only its sign"* and *"I'll say what the wood can't"*, and I never learn who that writer is. Inside the tales, "we" means the people (II.2), then the pillars, then a shoot (IV.2), then the gift (IV.4), then a boat that is somehow speaking through a different ship (IV.6). Each switch cost me a sentence or two to work out.

## II.2 · Of the Breath of the Wood

**When-line.** *"Halyna came to know this from the wood in the fifth winter of the siege."*
- **[WHO?]** Halyna. **[WHY?]** What siege? "Came to know this from the wood" is a good, strange hook.

**Headnote.**
- *"Halyna, Halvard and Rhyna, the husband and wife who lead our builders"*: **[REREAD]**, three people or two? (See problem 2.)
- *"They don't read the wood. Its memory comes into them whole, like something they remember. They give it to us in broken pieces, one taking up where the other stops."* **[LANDED]** This is clear, and it set me up for the italic dash-lines that follow.
- *"The Guest was a stranger who came to our shore long ago, out of the great grey fog at sea, with a gift of dark, old wood."* Clear.
- *"each time the sleeper woke up knowing it too"* is a nice quiet hint that the couple share their minds. I got it.
- *"our language has no word for a home made of air"* **[LANDED]** It answers the child's question about houses in a puzzling way and made me want to read on.
- *"so I've set down only its sign"*: **[WHO?]** Who is "I"? This is the first time a writer appears.
- The whole headnote is about 190 words of italics before the story starts. It's long, but this first time I needed nearly all of it.

**Reading (dash-lines).**
- *"— Breath. We two have it. Breath."* **[REREAD]** Have what, the breath? I worked out later that it means "we two have a word for it" (IV.6's *"For this we two have words"* confirmed it).
- *"Trees asleep, breathing it out. We — no, they breathe it in."* The slip from "we" to "they" is a lovely touch, as if the readers are losing themselves in the memory.
- *"[ ]"* at the end reads well as the missing name.

**"We were old before your fathers cut their first stone…"** **[LANDED]** Clear. It was a moment before I realised the "we" here is the Guest's people.

**Boxed note.** *"Naelear, those of the breath. Seren writes the word next to the sign, and the sign stays."*
- **[WHO?]** Seren.
- **[CLASH]** The headnote said the name hasn't come yet, and here it is.
- If the box stays hidden until later in the book, ignore this. As it stands in this file, it stopped me cold.

**"It was the air we breathed."** **[REREAD]** "It" has nothing to point back to, because the note box sits between this line and "the breath".

**"Our land lay flat under the trees… we tended it the way you tend the fire on your hearth."** Clear.

**"…neither day nor dusk. We call it the between-light. We were made for that light, and we were never children of the dark."**
- **[HINT]** Why deny being "children of the dark"? Does someone call them that? It reads like a reply to an accusation I haven't heard.

**"When evening came… they fell asleep in the singing."** **[LANDED]** The prettiest paragraph in the tale.

**"Take a fern… So do we."** **[LANDED]**

**"In the years of the iron… we watched the open sun wither our children."** **[LANDED]** A gut-punch coming straight after the children asleep in the song. The gloss for "the iron" is just right here.

**"…and the wood kept a will of its own as well."** **[HINT]** I noted it, and it paid off in IV.6 when the boat turns. Good plant.

**"…we live two and a half times as long as you do."** **[BORED]** The exact figure reads like a fact sheet and breaks the voice. "Many of your lifetimes" or "far longer than you" would do.

**"We held our council under the silver elders, because no one is ever laid to rest beneath a silver elder."**
- **[WORD]** "Silver elders": at first I thought this meant silver-haired old people. Only "beneath a silver elder" told me they are trees.
- **[REREAD]** **[WHY?]** The "because" doesn't follow for me. Why hold council where nobody is buried? It still felt like a riddle.
- It did pay off enormously in IV.6, though; see there.

**"When we speak, it sounds like a storm… The thunder is for the moment, and the softness is for always."** **[LANDED]** I understood that their speech frightens people and their carving doesn't. "Everything we carve is soft" made me pause on "soft carving", but I got it.

**"A carving is not heard. It is known…"** and **"We did not know there was any other way to be believed."** **[LANDED]** This is the key rule of the whole set, and it's said clearly.

**"…our pillars stood in long ranks… holding the breath up, the way a mast holds up a sail… the Naelsaen, the mist-hearts. They were the anchors of our sky…"**
- **[CLASH]** "Hold up like a mast" and "anchors" pull in opposite directions, and IV.2 later says the pillars hold the breath *down*.
- *"the only trees of ours you ever found"* **[LANDED]** Ominous.

**"From among them we watched your shore…"** Clear. "Havens, your cities along the coast" is a good gloss.

**"Once a boat came closer… We wove the breath thicker. He saw nothing but grey. His face went white… We did not know why."**
- **[REREAD]** Didn't know why *what*? Why he was afraid? Why we wove it thicker? Why we hid? The next paragraph suggests "why we hid", but I read it as "why he went white".

**"Here is our fault… We hid, and we were glad to hide."** and **"…you had never once heard us. We had made sure of it."** **[LANDED]** An honest admission, and it made me trust the voice.

**"This is our home: …"** **[LANDED]** A good circle back to the child's question about houses.

**"If any green is left of us…"** Ominous and clear.

***"And no one answered."*** **[HINT]** In this first tale, no one has asked anything yet except the child, so I didn't know who failed to answer what. It became powerful from IV.2 on. Here it's haunting but unearned.

### Verdict: II.2
- **Did I follow it?** Yes, the story itself: a hidden people who live in a breathing wood, whose children die when the trees are cut, and who never showed themselves. The frame (Halyna, the siege, the note box) was what confused me.
- **Did it move me?** Yes, at the children falling asleep in the song and then withering in the open sun, and at *"We did not know there was any other way to be believed."*

## IV.2 · The Axe in the Grain

**When-line.** *"…in the third winter of the siege."* **[CLASH]** The previous tale was the fifth winter, so I wondered whether these tales are out of order on purpose.

**Headnote.**
- *"a memory held in the grain of one of the enemy's grey ships"*: **[WHO?]** Who is the enemy? Are the ships made of the same wood? This is the first time I learn the enemy has wooden ships, and it's dropped in as a clause. I didn't yet connect the enemy with the wood-people.
- *"This piece is a heart, the core of a ship's wood, where its orders are carved."* **[WORD]** "Orders" means what? Commands carved into a ship? Who carves them? It stopped me. It paid off later with *"hearts to carry their carvings"*, which is chilling once you get it.
- *"the shingle, the pebble beach below our wall where wrecks wash in"* is a good gloss. Later, though, IV.4 says we went *"up the mountains"*, and I couldn't picture a shingle beach below a mountain wall. **[WHY?]** Where exactly is "our wall"?
- *"For a long time Halyna had only its shape, the broken pieces that come first here."* **[REREAD]** I had to work out that "the broken pieces that come first here" means the italic lines just below.
- *"…because it was in them the way a wound is, not the way a story is."* **[LANDED]**
- **[BORED]** The wood-leaf paragraph is repeated word for word from II.2.

**Reading.** *"Holding the breath down."* is where I first saw the up/down clash. *"Soft letters, on the mute thing —"* intrigued me.

**Boxed note.** *"Two of the wood's marks that Halyna could see but couldn't tell yet / … The second is the mouth, drawn as a mirror image, and tied back across the rings to the plea cut on the stone: It did not answer."*
- **[WORD]** "Marks", "the mouth", "drawn as a mirror image", "across the rings": I don't know what any of these mean.
- **[REREAD]** It says Halyna couldn't tell them yet, and then the note tells them.
- It spoils *"It did not answer"* before the tale reaches it.
- This was my hardest stop in the file.

**"We stood in our long rows at the edge of the breath, the grey that you call fog, tall and dark-barked."**
- **[LANDED]** "The grey that you call fog" finally tied the fog at sea to the breath for me.
- **[REREAD]** "We" is now the trees, not the people. I needed a sentence to adjust.

**"We held the breath down to the earth, the way roots hold a riverbank together."** **[CLASH]** with II.2's "held the breath up… like a mast".

**"…no one thinks about breath until it is taken away."** **[LANDED]**

**"…we marked every one of your boats that came near us. Each brush against us was a cut, and each trespass was a wound. The wood keeps every count."**
- **[REREAD]** Did boats physically brush the trees? I took it as a metaphor in the end.
- The idea that the wood keeps a grudge came through.

**"In the high summer of your havens… the iron came, your axes… The shore-men, your people, waded up out of the surf."** Clear. "The iron came, your axes" is a little clunky, but fine the first time in this tale.

**"What one pillar felt, all the rows felt, root to root…"** Clear.

**"The first pillar they came to… had marked the most of your boats."** I felt the irony: the most watchful tree goes first.

**"They set the iron to it at first light… Then its roots let go."** **[LANDED]** The best short line in this tale.

**"…like daylight through a rip in a sail. It was pale."** and the build *"pale" → "pale and hard" → "pale and hard and wide"*. **[LANDED]** The repetition tightens nicely.

**"The stumps put up shoots… It came again."** **[LANDED]**

**"The young of the long-lived, the people of our wood, turned their faces away… and wept, and they did not know why."** "The long-lived" is a new term, but it's glossed at once, so fine. Moving.

**"We carved, as we always had when a thing must not be mistaken."** **[WHO?]** If "we" are the pillar trees, how do trees carve? Or does "we" now mean the people? I lost track of the speaker here.

**"So we cut our plea into the mute thing, stone… We thought a people must understand best whatever is cut into the thing it loves."** **[LANDED]** Clear and heartbreaking.

**"…as near to its sound as our letters could go."** Good. It reminded me of a foreigner writing your words phonetically.

**"Everything we make speaks… That was a child's belief. Stone has no mind."** and **"It did not answer."** **[LANDED]** The strongest beat in the tale (and it would hit harder if the note box hadn't given it away).

**"The shore-men took the stones away with the timber, and we never knew where they went."** **[HINT]** I took it to mean the plea stones are sitting in our own walls somewhere. If that's the intended hint, it worked and gave me a shiver.

**"The iron will tire. When it stops, the wood will come again."** Ominous. Good.

**"…we who tell you this were never a pillar. We were a thin green thing at the edge."** It explains the voice switch, but only after I'd already been confused by it.

**"…we were the last shoot its stump put up… too thin to make a pole."** Clear.

**"…the long-lived who sit in council came looking for hearts to carry their carvings… They chose us, because of the axe in our rings."** **[LANDED]** Chilling. Here I finally understood: they picked the angriest wood to build a warship's heart.

**"When one of our ships is struck, we go to the blow, the way your tongue goes to a broken tooth."**
- **[REREAD]** "Go to the blow" sounded physical at first, as if the heart moves toward the hit. The next line showed it means memory.
- "Our ships" is where it clicked that the enemy ships belong to the wood-people. That's a big moment, carried by two words.

***"And no one answered."*** Earned this time. It echoes "It did not answer."

### Verdict: IV.2
- **Did I follow it?** Mostly. The felling, the stone plea and the shoot that became a warship's heart were all clear. The note box, the up/down clash and the changing "we" cost me.
- **Did it move me?** Yes, at *"Then its roots let go"*, and above all at the plea cut into stone because *"a people must understand best whatever is cut into the thing it loves"* followed by *"Stone has no mind."*

## IV.4 · The Gift Held an Hour

**When-line.** *"Halvard and Rhyna came to know this…"* **[REREAD]** Why not "Halyna" this time? (See problem 2.) *"…in our grandparents' time, at the outpost on the very edge of our land, and of the long road after."* Clear, and the "long road after" is a good tease.

**Headnote.**
- **[BORED]** The wood-leaf paragraph again, for the third time.
- *"On the night we read out Brenn's confession…"* **[WHO?]** Brenn? **[WHY?]** A confession of what? I guess a previous tale covers this, but here it's a loose thread.
- *"Brenn had been a runner at that outpost"*: "Runner" as in messenger, I assume. Fine.
- *"the stranger out of the fog who came kneeling to its warden's door"*: "Warden" is clear enough as the man in charge. "Came kneeling" is a good image.
- *"His gift was the dark stone that Brenn picked out of the dust."* **[CLASH]** In II.2 the same gift was "dark, old wood". Is it the same object? I think so, but it made me stop.
- *"It lay wrapped in Brenn's pages in our guild's chest of records"*: **[WORD]** Which guild? The builders? This is the only mention.
- *"…and it came with us out of our burning cities and up the mountains."* **[WHY?]** This is huge news (our cities burned!) arriving as half a sentence in the third tale. It's the first real picture I get of "the siege", and it belongs much earlier.
- *"…what one of them knows, the other knows, and he was the one who began the telling."* **[LANDED]** It made the shared mind explicit.

**Reading.**
- *"Leaves. No — words, like leaves. One, and one, and one."* **[LANDED]**
- *"— and nothing to join them. We two have it."* **[REREAD]** Have what? The joining words? It's the same puzzle as II.2's "We two have it".
- *"A neck, held. Two foreheads, one — / — the blade — [ ]"* Very tense. It made me read on fast.

**"We are the oldest wood of all… You thought we were stone. We are not stone."** **[LANDED]** It clears up the wood/stone puzzle and reads beautifully.

**"Wood that time has forgotten does not forget… We were given only once."** **[REREAD]** Only once ever, or this one sliver was never passed on again? Small.

**"The one who carried us was named Aelrhen, bond-to-stone, when he took up his errand."** **[WORD]** "Bond-to-stone": I took it as a man pledged to the stone-loving shore people. Good. The idea that he was named for his errand is intriguing.

**"He gathered your words the way a child gathers fallen leaves… he had three…"** **[LANDED]**

**"You join your words together with small words, and small words make no sound across water."** **[LANDED]** A clever, clear explanation of why he has three bare words. One of my favourite lines.

**"Anyone who held us while a tree's shadow crept the width of its own trunk… We were the sentence."** The time measure worked, because the title says "an hour".

**"He chose the farthest of your posts… because its doors and benches were made of our wood. He thought that meant you honoured us… They were dead."** **[LANDED]** Quietly devastating.

**"…he spoke the name of the rite a guest makes with his host, Ael'thar. After it he spoke his three words, in yours."** Clear. "His own tongue, which sounds like thunder" links back to II.2, and I saw how this would frighten the host.

**"Stop… Sky… Dying…"** **[LANDED]** As a reader I could hear how it might sound like a threat. That's exactly the right tension.

**"A foot kicked us into the dust by the wall. Through the dust we heard laughter."** **[LANDED]**

**"He stood up and cupped the back of the host's neck… just where life climbs up into thought. A fledgling could have slept in that hand."** Beautiful. I also felt how it would look from the host's side, a stranger seizing your neck. Good double view.

**"Then, with his left hand, he laid a small blade against the host's arm."** It shocked me, as it should. I didn't know why yet, and the tale tells me as it goes. Good.

**"And the host drew back. We do not know why. Fear leaves no mark on wood."** **[LANDED]**

**"The blade was meant to go only a little way, but the arm was moving, and it went deep."** Clear, and tragic because of how ordinary it is.

**"Then Aelrhen's blood ran down… So we know it just as he knew it."** It answers "how can the gift know this?" Good.

**"Held between their hands, he went down on his knees again…"** **[REREAD]** Whose hands? I worked out that it's the warden's men holding him. *"He would ask for no blood that he did not give, and he gave all of his"* **[LANDED]**

**"The two arms were never laid together, shoulder to shoulder, to say that harm to one is harm to both. He died there, with the rite half made."** **[LANDED]** Now I understood the whole rite: each cuts, and the arms are laid together. It was shown, never lectured.

**"Many things that look like stone have lain at a shore-man's feet. Of them all, we were the only one that could have spoken to him."** **[LANDED]** **[HINT, caught]** This tied back to the mute plea stones of IV.2, and it felt great to catch.

**"And we were the one his foot found."** **[LANDED]**

**The road paragraph** (*"In the morning a hand picked us up… Then one pair, alone, a long way."*)
- The headnote let me follow most of it: Brenn's run, the chest, the pages ("leaves wrapped around us" is a nice double meaning), the burning cities, the mountains.
- *"cut stones lying cold beside us"*: **[HINT]** What stones? The plea stones? Stone records in the guild chest? I didn't get it.
- *"old arms, two pairs"*, *"small arms, two pairs, children's arms"*, *"one pair, alone"*: **[WHO?]** I don't know who any of these people are. It's evocative, and I'm fine not knowing, but "one pair, alone, a long way" made me badly want to know whose they were.

**"Then you held us…"** "You" now means Rhyna, not the shore people in general. It's a small shift, but it works.

**The Guest's sentence** (*"Stop cutting down the pillars at the edge of the grey…"*)
- **[LANDED]** The clearest and most moving passage in the file. *"We are not your enemies"* is devastating, given that by now they *are* the enemy.
- A small taste note: *"they aren't just wood"* felt a touch flat and casual for the weight of the moment. The rest is perfect.

**"That's all he came to say…"** and ***"And no one answered."*** Fully earned here.

### Verdict: IV.4
- **Did I follow it?** Yes, completely. It's the clearest of the four.
- **Did it move me?** Most of the four. The foot kicking the gift, *"small words make no sound across water"*, the half-made rite and the plain sentence at the end all landed. Brenn, the guild and the burning cities came in as loose threads from outside the tale.

## IV.6 · The Voyage of the Aelvaren

**When-line.** Clear.

**Headnote.**
- **[BORED]** The wood-leaf paragraph, for the fourth time. It also re-tells IV.4 (*"He came kneeling to the door… and he was killed there"*), which I had just read. If the tales are read one by one this recap helps; read in a row, it drags.
- *"a memory held in the grain of the enemy's wood"* is fine.
- *"Rhyna, up on the wall, plunged both hands into the rain barrel, and she wouldn't say why."* **[HINT, caught]** The reading's *"Fire. Hands burning"* made this click. She felt the burning through the shared mind. A good hint that pays off fast.
- *"Of the long-lived, the people of the wood, I'll say what the wood can't: They did not know it was ignorance."*
  - **[WHO?]** "I" again.
  - **[REREAD]** It was cryptic up front: whose ignorance, and what didn't they know? It only made sense at *"They saw only an act of war"*, when I went back to it. That's a good structure, but on first reading it stopped me.
  - **[WHY?]** I also didn't follow "what the wood can't". Is it that the wood can't judge, only remember?

**Reading.** *"Grief. For this we two have words."* **[LANDED]** It finally explained the "We two have it" lines from earlier. Strong.

**"And not in this grain only. The ship that brought this heart to your wall was not there that day. But it knows, because it grew out of what we were."**
- **[REREAD]** The hardest paragraph in this tale. Who is "we", the boat? But the wood I'm holding is a ship's heart, not the boat. How can a ship that wasn't there "know"?
- It only made sense at the last paragraph (*"Every young tree that was to become a ship drank it in"*). The payoff is good, but I was lost for a whole tale.

**"We were the Aelvaren, the Bearer of the Binding…"**
- **[WORD]** "The Binding": is that the guest-rite? I guessed so but wasn't told.
- I noticed the shared *Ael-* in Aelvaren, Aelrhen (bond-to-stone) and Ael'thar, worked out that "Ael" must mean bond, and enjoyed that. (See the note on romanization below.)

**"We were a small boat… all the way his hand lay on our boards."** Clear and tender.

**"…The tide came in under us, and turned, and he did not come."** **[LANDED]**

**"The men of that house dragged him down to us by the heels. They threw him into us like a torn net."** **[LANDED]**

**"…Now his blood soaked into them. So, in his blood, all of it came to us."** Clear, and it matches IV.4.

**"Then they set us on fire. The old wood raged in its grief… and it screamed, because wood can."** **[LANDED]** "Because wood can" is wonderful.

**"…He lay inside us, and he was cold."** **[LANDED]**

**"Then we turned… Even burnt, even dying, the wood knows the way home."** **[LANDED]** The payoff for II.2's *"the wood kept a will of its own"*.

**"Twice on that road… with the iron in them, your axes, and with our kin, the great trees of the edge, cut and stacked in their holds."** **[BORED]** By the fourth tale, "the iron… your axes" and "the shore-men, your people" no longer need glossing. The picture of their kin stacked in the holds is strong, though.

**"…The iron had gone into us. Now we went into the iron."** **[LANDED]**

**"Of the men in them, the grain holds nothing."** **[LANDED]** Cold, and the first moment the wood turns to violence. It unsettled me in the right way.

**"…All that was left of us was our keel, our backbone, and a few ribs, and in them the bones of Aelrhen."** Clear. "Keel, our backbone" is a fair gloss.

**"…under the silver elders, the trees where the long-lived hold their council…"** **[BORED]**, mildly, since I already knew this from II.2. Still, the reminder helped me with the next beat.

**"Many of them laid their hands on our keel at once… Wood can hide nothing."** Clear.

**"They had sent out their peace… They knew a murder when they saw one… But they did not know why the host's arm drew back from the blade. And they did not ask. They saw only an act of war."** **[LANDED]** The turning point of the whole set, and it answers the headnote's "ignorance" line.

**"They laid what was left of us… among the roots of the silver elders."** **[HINT, caught]** II.2 said *"no one is ever laid to rest beneath a silver elder"*. Here they bury him there. I caught it, and it gave me a chill: they broke their deepest rule for him. But the tale doesn't mark it at all, and a reader who has forgotten II.2's line will miss the biggest beat in the ending. A single half-line would be enough, something like "where none had ever been laid".

**"And the roots drank."** **[LANDED]**

**"…Every young tree that was to become a ship drank it in with the rain. So every ship that came against your wall carried our grief in it…"** **[LANDED]** Now everything joins up: the wood-people's grief grew into the enemy fleet. This is where I finally fully understood "the siege".

### Verdict: IV.6
- **Did I follow it?** Yes, apart from the opening paragraph about which ship "knows". That only came clear at the end.
- **Did it move me?** Yes. The burning boat turning for home with the cold body inside, *"because wood can"*, the council that *"did not ask"*, and the burial under the silver elders.

| *Naelsaen* | "the mist-hearts" | It made me guess that *Nael-* = breath or mist. |
| *Aelrhen* | "bond-to-stone" | It made me guess that *Ael-* = bond. |
| *Ael'thar* | only "the name of the rite a guest makes with his host" | I wanted its literal meaning, the way the others have one. |
| *Aelvaren* | "the Bearer of the Binding" | This one confirmed *Ael-* for me. |

Decoding the shared roots was one of the quiet pleasures of the read. Word-by-word glosses (for example *Ael* = bond, *rhen* = stone) would make that pleasure open to every reader, not only the ones who spot the pattern.

## Where the explaining felt too much or too little

**Too much (I got bored):**
- The wood-leaf paragraph in all four headnotes.
- IV.6's headnote retelling IV.4.
- The repeated glosses: "the grey that you call fog", "the shore-men, your people", "the iron…, your axes" and "the long-lived, the people of our wood".
- "Two and a half times as long."

**Too little (I was lost):**
- The siege, the enemy and our burned cities.
- Halyna as a pair.
- Seren, and the "I" of the headnotes.
- Brenn's confession, and "our guild".
- Both note boxes.
- "Where its orders are carved".
- "Never children of the dark".
- The "because" about burial under the silver elders.
- "We did not know why".
- "We two have it".
- "Cut stones lying cold beside us".
- "We carved" said by trees.
- Which "we" is speaking at the start of IV.6.

## Overall

The prose is easy to read: short sentences, plain modern words, and no tangles inside the tales themselves. Almost every stop I had was in the frame around the tales (headnotes, note boxes, names from outside) or in a change of who "we" is. Very few were in the storytelling itself. As one story told in four pieces (a hidden people, the axes, a mute plea, a gift kicked into the dust, a murder misread as war, and grief grown into a fleet), it's clear, and it's genuinely sad. IV.4 is the high point. The refrain *"And no one answered."* means nothing in II.2 and becomes crushing by IV.4.
