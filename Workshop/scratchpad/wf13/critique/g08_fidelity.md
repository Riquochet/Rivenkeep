## Fixes, most important first

**1. V.2, line 21: remove the invented law about bonded pairs (it's wrong lore).**
The Book says only *Never ere then had I seen them work apart. They were not far apart: the length of the vault.* The sentence *A bonded pair … is seldom apart, and never far apart* invents a rule. Later tales break it: II.3 has *a mile apart, and each knew*, and V.7 Part I has Orvenna sending Enno and Della to breaches a mile apart. This same file breaks it too, in V.5 Count IV (*a mile on, in the north, Della*). It would also take the edge off V.7's *a mile is far*.
- Old: `I'd never seen them work apart before. A bonded pair, two of our builders joined as one from birth, is seldom apart, and never far apart. Even that night they weren't far apart: only the length of the vault.`
- New: `I'd never seen them work apart before. They're a bonded pair, two of our builders joined as one from birth, and a bonded pair works as one. Even that night they weren't far apart: only the length of the vault.`

**2. V.2, line 19: the gaps were in the *telling*, not the *knowing*.**
The Book says *there were gaps in every telling*. Line 17 has just said that a knowing *comes into them whole*, so *gaps in every knowing* contradicts it two sentences later. It also breaks V.1 (H3), where the knowing is whole but the telling falls short: *More than the word they knew … one word was all they could tell.*
- Old: `{{Halyna}} had learned to take in both signs at once, but there were gaps in every knowing.`
- New: `{{Halyna}} had learned to take in both signs at once, but there were gaps in everything they could tell me.`

**3. V.2, line 17: show why a whole knowing gave only one word (goes with fix 2).**
As it stands, a newcomer reads *comes into them whole* and then *a single word*, and can't tell what changed on this night. V.1 gives the reason, and the hearth has known it since H3. One sentence carries it:
- Old: `Every plank but one had given {{Halyna}} a single word, and I'd written each word on a page of its own.`
- New: `Every plank but one had given {{Halyna}} a single word. They knew more than that word, but the rest was the thinking of a forest, not of people from the shore, and one word was all they could put into our speech. I'd written each word on a page of its own.`

(This explains nothing about *why* that night was different. The fitted halves stay dark, as §4 item 7 requires.)

**4. V.2, line 13: keep the vault at the Book's level, *where the Rite was found*.**
The Book says *the oldest room under this hall, where the Rite was found*. The Plain makes that *the vault where we found the first builders' black chest*, which says more than the Book does. In I.4 the chest stands at the foot of a shaft that Halvard went down on a rope. This vault has a stair that morning light comes down, and Seren and the boatwrights go in and out of it all through the war. If the planks' vault is where the chest lay, the Epilogue's *I know the stair that goes no more. I haven't gone down to look.* stops making sense, and that line holds mystery §4.11, where the staff went. The primer says the Book wins where the two disagree, and the writer's own note 7 offers this fallback.
- Old: `The room is the vault where we found the first builders' black chest, with the Rite inside it that made the Captain our Commander.`
- New: `It's the vault where the Rite was found, the first builders' ceremony, written on slate, by which we made the Captain our Commander.`

**5. V.5 Count V, line 183: the gloss on *the young wood* can be read as describing the fifth Tide.**
*They came the way the young wood came in the first winter, the youngest of their ships, loud and alone and straight.* Here the appositive *the youngest of their ships* sits where it can describe the Deceit fleets. The point of the count (and of V.7 Part II's carving, *Come as the young wood came*) is that these are not young ships, only ships pretending to be. Line 185's *was no green ship* depends on that.
- Old: `They came the way the young wood came in the first winter, the youngest of their ships, loud and alone and straight.`
- New: `They came loud and alone and straight, the way the young wood, their youngest ships, had come in the first winter.`

**6. V.5 Count VI, line 197: the Last Tide's going home was a pattern, not one event.**
The lexicon and §2 H31 give it as *fleets that go home when their bearer and its second fall*, and V.7 Part III's carving is a standing order. *When one of its fleets lost* reads as a single case.
- Old: `When one of its fleets lost its bearer, and then the second ship that took up the standard, it went home.`
- New: `Whenever one of its fleets lost its bearer, and then the second ship that took up the standard, it went home.`

**7. V.5 Count VI, line 223: the strips come from his *half* of the cloak.**
Count I sets up that he wears only the unwritten half, and the close (*his half of the cloak hung down to his knees*) depends on it.
- Old: `the Captain has cut a hand's breadth from his cloak,`
- New: `the Captain has cut a hand's breadth from his half of the cloak,`

**8. V.5 Count VI, line 213: say once that the havens are fallen (so the tale stands alone).**
Nothing in V.5 tells a newcomer that the ten cities were burned before the siege. Without that, *settled down in our havens* reads as ten ships moving into living towns. The fact is H0, and the lexicon describes the Thrones as settling *in the ten fallen havens*.
- Old: `and they settled down in our havens, our ten cities along the coast.`
- New: `and they settled down in our havens, the ten cities along the coast that the grey ships burned before we came to the Keep.`

**9. V.5 Count VI, line 225: one Book inversion is left.**
Brief rule 2 asks for normal word order.
- Old: `The rest of the dead in the east cairns I named at dusk among the stones, every name, twice.`
- New: `At dusk, among the stones, I named the rest of the dead in the east cairns, every name, twice.`

**10. Merge note: strip the non-Plain matter before merging.**
Line 1 (`<!-- PLAIN WORDS · g08 … -->`) and the whole `## WRITER'S NOTES` section (lines 237–283) are not in the old Plain. A stray `##` heading would break the heading skeleton, which must match character for character. Take out both, and splice only lines 3–81 (V.2) and 83–235 (V.5).
