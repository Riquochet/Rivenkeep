The fixed lines are word for word: the founders' vow; *where no eye will see it again until the wall falls* (both tales); Jack's Stonwryt formula after a colon; Enrella's jest; *Harm to one is harm to both.*; *Separation, God did not permit.*; *When the left hand dies, does not the right one die too?*; *God does not take the one without leaving the other a work to do.*; *"A net is only knots holding hands."*; *Birth united them; death could not separate them.*; and the liturgy in its Plain form. The markup matches the old Plain exactly. I compared them by script: the three headings are identical; II.3 has `<!-- HALYNA -->`, `<!-- MARKED -->`, the `::: native legend_stonwryt_halyna chip` block (copied whole) and `<!-- HALYNA END -->` at the same beats; II.1 has no markers, as before; the pair-name sets are {{Halyna}}/{{Aldwena}} and {{Halyna}}/{{Idrenna}}/{{Enrella}}, the same as the Book's, all with plural verbs; and neither tale has a blot, which is correct. The only missing `---` is the one before BOOK THREE, and the assembler takes that rule from the Book's skeleton. There is no singular *they*, no custom of burning, no Christian word, no old word, and no semicolon outside the fixed saying. The clock is kept. II.1 says nothing of the founders' war, nothing of Halvard being the last of his line (that comes in the Epilogue), and nothing of the unbonded or of Tarnel. II.3 gives the Covenant its name only, leaves *a mile apart* and the *house with one door* as unpointed plants, and doesn't tie the small girl to anyone. I checked every fact the draft adds against the Book, and each one is sound: *I was twelve* (I.2's headnote), the builders *sitting in the grass with their chisels in their laps* (I.1 and I.2), the chest at *the bottom of a shaft under this hall* (I.4's rhyme and descent), *Two weeks later* for the Rite (day 14), and *A pair cuts its mark under the last stone* (II.3's own joke, and V.7).

The fixes are ordered by weight. Fix 1 corrects a misleading piece of lore. Fixes 2–5 are fidelity repairs in II.1. Fixes 6–7 make II.3 stand on its own, as the brief requires. Fixes 8–13 are small and optional.

## Fixes

1. **II.1, line 57: the wardens' chisel-bites are called "marks."** Change *Next to them was the bite of the wardens' chisels, the marks Halvard's own forefathers had cut.* to **Next to them were the bites of the wardens' chisels, the dents Halvard's own forefathers made when they cut this stone.** In this tale *mark* means a Stonwryt, a bonded pair's sacred sign that only that pair may cut, and the paragraph has just used *mark* that way for {{Aldwena}}'s. Calling the chisel-bites *the marks Halvard's forefathers had cut* tells a newcomer that the quarry-wardens cut a mark of their own under the capstone. The Book says only *the bite of the wardens' chisels*, the tool-scars left from cutting the stone, which the founders' leaf credits to the wardens (*The wardens of the quarry cut the stone*). Leave the next sentence (*Halvard set his thumb in a chisel-bite his fathers had left.*) as it is.

2. **II.1, line 23: a gloss has been put inside the founders' own words.** Change *With our four hands, we two laid the capstone of the inner gate, the great stone across its top.* to **With our four hands we two laid the capstone of the inner gate.** Seren's headnote says of this page, *I haven't dared to change a word of it*, and the founders had no reason to explain what a capstone is to their own guild. Carry the gloss in Seren's voice instead, at line 27 (see fix 3), where the stone in her hands is first called the capstone. *The stone over the western stair* (for *lintel*) can stay, because that is translation, not explanation.

3. **II.1, line 27: Seren doesn't hold the stone "in" her hands, and "it takes two" is unclear.** Change *That mark and that vow are under this stone, the one in my hands. It's that same capstone. I can't turn it over, because it takes two.* to **That mark and that vow are under this stone, the one my hands are resting on. It's that same capstone, the great stone that once lay across the top of the inner gate. I can't turn it over, because it takes two people to turn it.** The tale-stone is a gate's capstone, so heavy that *it is a stone that two must turn* (the foreword). *The one in my hands* pictures her lifting it, which is impossible. The Book's own picture is *under my two hands*, and the tale closes on it (*Tonight it's under my two hands.*). *Because it takes two* could mean two hands. The Book means two people, and that matters, because Seren is one person alone (line 29).

4. **II.1, line 31: "Two lines were one" is made vaguer than the Book makes it.** Change *So their two families were already joined, in the stones of this Keep, long before those two lay as babies in one cradle.* to **So the two families were already joined in this very gate: Rhyna's forebears laid it, and Halvard's cut its stone out of the mountain. That was long before those two lay as babies in one cradle.** *In the stones of this Keep* is a guess at the meaning, and a newcomer can't picture it. The Book says the meaning plainly in I.2 (line 319: *Her line had laid that gate. His had cut its stone out of the mountain.*), and the founders' leaf just above says the same thing (Aldwena laid the capstone, and the wardens cut the stone). The I.2 Plain (g02) already says *Her family had laid that very gate … and his family had cut its stone out of the mountain*, so this wording keeps the two tales in step. It also sets up *It was their own.* without giving it away, since the turning is what shows both families' work on one stone. It says nothing about marriage, and it doesn't call Halvard the last of his line.

5. **II.1, line 33: the sentence about the chest can be misread as listing four things.** Change *Before they left, the founders laid the Keep's slates, the writings they had cut, and its first staff, the rod carried by the overseer of a great work, in the black chest, the way masons put away their tools at dusk. We found it at the bottom of a shaft under this hall, in our first nights here.* to **Before they left, the founders laid the Keep's slates, the writings they had cut, in the black chest, the way masons put away their tools at dusk. With them they laid its first staff, the rod carried by the overseer of a great work. We found that chest at the bottom of a shaft under this hall, in our first nights here.** With two appositives and four commas in one sentence, a reader can easily count *slates, writings, staff, rod* as four things in the chest. The Book has two (*the Keep's slates and its first staff*), and I.4 confirms them (the slates, and *along the floor a staff*). *We found it* also points back to the wrong noun, so make it *that chest*.

6. **II.3, line 77 (the headnote): the stone and "laid" are never explained in this tale.** At the end of the headnote, after *because children like to know.*, add: **(At our hearth, whoever tells a tale holds the old gate-stone, and we say a tale is laid, like a stone in a wall, because a laid tale stays.)** II.3 opens with *They laid their hands on the stone together* and *We hold the stone together*, and it closes with *We have laid this tale*. A reader who opens II.3 first, which the brief's rule 4 allows for, has no idea what the stone is or what *laid* means. The primer's model leaves this out too, but the aside costs 30 words and matches the wording that g07 and g08 use for their headnotes. A short parenthesis is allowed once in a headnote (§1.1). It adds no pair-name.

7. **II.3, line 93: the Stonewrights are never explained in this tale.** Change *The guild calls its children while they're still in the cradle, from any home at all.* to **The guild of the Stonewrights, our master builders, calls its children while they're still in the cradle, from any home at all.** *Stonewright* comes up in Halvard's first line, and later in *Hours, say the Stonewrights*, but it's never glossed, and the headnote says only *our guild* and *our builders*. Putting the gloss in this sentence, and not inside *not every Shorelander was a Stonewright, but every Stonewright was a Shorelander*, keeps that balanced line whole. §1.1 asks for the Stonewrights to be explained in every tale, *in half a clause*.

8. **(Optional) II.3, line 131: "she is one" can be read the wrong way.** Change *So she lived, and she is one.* to **So she lived, and she is one alone.** A newcomer can read *she is one* as *she's one of us*. The Book means that she is a single person and not a pair (the unbonded *keep only one name*). *One alone* keeps the word *one*, which answers Enno and Della's *stand up as one* from the start of the tale, and it makes the meaning plain.

9. **(Optional) II.1, line 35: keep the tale's thread of "kept".** Change *a gate that nobody guarded any more* to **a gate that nobody kept any more**. The Book builds II.1 on that word: *as the founders kept it, and as we kept it not* (line 15), *the gate that no one kept*, and *The stone kept its vow. It was we who did not keep ours.* (line 61). The draft keeps the first and last of these, and *kept* is plain modern English for a gate that someone watches over. If *kept* seems too thin, use **a gate that nobody kept watch over any more**.

10. **(Optional) II.1, line 63: "Before the light was off the snow" is the Book's turn of phrase, not plain English.** Change it to **Before the last light had gone from the snow,**. (The rest of the sentence, and the added *Until then our builders had been sitting in the grass with their chisels in their laps.*, check out against I.1 line 209 and I.2 line 307. Keep them.)

11. **(Optional) II.1, line 53: Tidesmeet isn't explained.** Change *the fishermen of Tidesmeet* to **the fishermen of Tidesmeet, one of our havens,** (or *the river city of three bridges*, as II.3 line 129 has it). It's the only place-name in II.1 left without a word of explanation.

12. **(Optional) II.3, line 95: "there" is added and points nowhere.** Change *they read what was written there before we were born* to **they read what was written before we were born**. The Book has *read what was written before ever we were born*. *There* makes a reader wonder whether something is written on the cradle or on the child.

13. **(Optional) II.1, line 45: "the shadow of a fortress".** Change *and found only the shadow of a fortress* to **and found only a ruin, the shadow of a fortress**. A twelve-year-old may take *shadow* literally, and the tale goes on to describe the ruin (the fallen capstone, frost in every joint). Adding *a ruin* makes the Book's meaning plain and keeps its picture. (This is not the Glasspire shadow of §4 item 9. Nothing here touches that.)

**On length.** Fixes 1–5 add about 30 words to II.1, which is already at 150 per cent of the Book (1,225 against 815). Clarity comes first under the brief (*whatever clarity needs*). If the length must come down, the cheapest cut is line 49's *The column, all of us who had climbed the mountain road, came at their backs.* → **The whole column came at their backs.**, because the Road is explained again at line 45.

## Checked and left as they are

- **The Argument (line 5), *Who we were, and who they were.*** It's kept word for word, as in the Book and the old Plain. Other groups have glossed their Arguments lightly, but making *they* clear here would point to the wood's people before II.2 brings them in, and the Contents copies the line. Keep it.
- **II.1, *It's that same capstone* (line 27) and *This is that stone.* (line 67).** These don't give anything away. The Book itself says *Under this stone are that mark and that vow*, and the hearth has known since H1 that the old capstone is the tale-stone.
- **II.1, *I'm not one of a pair* (line 29).** This is allowed at H6. The hearth has known since H1 and H2 that Seren writes alone and came into the courtyard alone. The word *unbonded*, the cradle and Tarnel stay in II.3, as they should.
- **II.1, *The Keep had never fallen to an enemy* (line 37).** It's a fair reading of *It had never fallen*, and it keeps the three falls distinct: the Keep never fell, the capstone fell and no one heard, and the havens fell and the whole coast heard. It doesn't say who the enemy might have been (§4 item 6).
- **II.1, *I love that word* moved to just after the gloss of *point* (line 39).** That is what the Book means (*point* is the word in italics), and the move makes it clear.
- **II.1, the when-line (line 9).** This is the primer's §7 line. It doesn't mention that the turning of the stone happened at dusk on the day of the banner, but the body says so in its first sentence about it (line 49), so no change is needed.
- **II.3, *A pair cuts its mark under the last stone of a finished work* (line 91).** The Book's joke implies it, Aldwena and {{Halyna}} both cut their marks under capstones, and V.7 shows it again. It isn't new lore, and it doesn't point to Enno and Della's house.
- **II.3, *because everyone who hears it knows someone is missing* (line 127).** This explains what *a mourning* means. It doesn't give a new cause, and it comes from the primer's model.
- **II.3, the two changes from the model (line 131).** *The carved wood of the enemy's wrecks* is H3 knowledge, so it's correct. *Their book was shut away in the vault after them* is closer to the Book's *went into the vault behind them* than the model's *no one read it again*. *The vault* is rightly left unglossed: these scribes lived in the havens, so this is not the Keep's vault under the hall.
- **The writer's notes** list *the vault* among the terms the model explains, but the model doesn't explain it. That's a slip in the notes only, and the prose needs no change. The `## WRITER'S NOTES` heading isn't in the Book's skeleton, so the assembler will list it as an extra and leave it out.
