# g05 fidelity critique: BOOK THREE heading and Argument, III.1 Of Their Raising, III.2 Of Their Fall

Checked `wf13/plain/g05.md` line by line against `wf11/book_v3.md` (lines 919–1108), `wf11/plain_v3.md` (Book Three) and `wf13/plain_primer.md` (§1, §2 at H8 and H10, §3, §4, §5, §7).

**Verdict: sound. No spoilers, no broken mysteries, no wrong lore, and the fixed lines and markup are intact.** Every beat, name and number in the Book is present. The fixes below are small: three places where the draft drifts from what the Book says, one lore point that is slightly off, and some newcomer gaps in III.2, which has to stand on its own.

---

## Fixes, most important first

1. **III.1, Seren's italic reading of the rolls: the draft adds a cause the Book doesn't give.** The Book says *The open water they feared, and built where the mountains go down in long falls to the sea*. That is two facts side by side. The draft's *so* says the fear caused them to build on the coast, which is a new reason and doesn't quite make sense (a people afraid of the water building at its edge). Change
   > They were afraid of the open water, so they built where the mountains run down to the sea in long slopes,

   to
   > They were afraid of the open water, and they built where the mountains run down to the sea in long slopes,

2. **III.2, Sandreach: restore the Book's passive voice.** The Book has *Gallery by gallery it was taken from us*, the same agentless voice it uses for Rimewatch (*On the first ice it was taken*). The draft's *they took it from us* brings in an unnamed *they*, and a newcomer will picture people taking the galleries, although Kael has just sworn that no one stood on the decks (primer §1.9: no one sails in the grey fleet). Change
   > Sandreach was dug into a canyon wall, gallery under gallery, and they took it from us one gallery at a time.

   to
   > Sandreach was dug into a canyon wall, gallery under gallery, and it was taken from us one gallery at a time.

3. **Both tales: the banner is half of the cloak, not the whole cloak.** In I.1 the Captain tears the cloak, writes on one half and raises that half on a pike, and he still wears the unwritten half, which later becomes the strips over the graves. The draft says *raised his torn cloak* twice, and the wording will clash with later tales. Make two in-place edits:
   - III.1 headnote: *when the Captain raised his torn cloak over the wall as our banner* → *when the Captain raised half of his torn cloak over the wall as our banner*
   - III.2, Kael: *and the Captain raised his torn cloak over the wall* → *and the Captain raised half of his torn cloak over the wall* (fix 6 rewrites this sentence anyway)

4. **III.2, Emberhythe: say "survivors", not "the people".** Those who had died in the cities that fell weren't in the column. The Book's sense, which Kael repeats at the Road, is that the column *gathered haven by haven*. Change
   > By then the people of every haven that had fallen had gathered into one long column.

   to
   > By then the survivors of every haven that had fallen had gathered into one long column.

5. **III.1, Tamm's stones: keep the Book's "in their order".** The Book has *in their order* both here and in VI.1 (*On the bank I set out my three stones in their order*), and it fits III.2's upper, lower and middle spans. *each in its place* loses the echo. Change
   > *Tamm set three stones out by the fire, each in its place, the way he sets them out by his blanket every night.*

   to
   > *Tamm set three stones out by the fire in their order, the way he sets them out by his blanket every night.*

6. **III.2, Kael's first paragraph after the stone: the sentence starting "This was" has nothing for *This* to point back to.** *This was the last spring of peace…* is the first sentence of the story, so *This* has no antecedent. The Book opens *In the last spring of the peace*. Replace the first two sentences
   > This was the last spring of peace, twenty months before the day of the banner, when we reached this Keep and the Captain raised his torn cloak over the wall. That spring the swallows nested in the arches of the havens' gates.

   with
   > The last spring of peace came twenty months before the day of the banner, when we reached this Keep and the Captain raised half of his torn cloak over the wall. That spring the swallows nested in the arches of the havens' gates.

7. **III.2 headnote: help a newcomer through the tale (rule 4, primer §1.1).** The Book never names the tellers in III.2, so a reader who opens this tale first can't know whose *I* is speaking, and doesn't know that Pellow, who closes Glasspire with *I looked close*, is Glasspire's man. Kael also goes unexplained, because in III.2 he never says *oath-keeper*. The sentence about the gate-stone also sits loose between the parenthesis and the frost. Replace the headnote with this one (about the same length, no new facts):
   > *The True Men, the Captain's own company of twelve, laid this tale too, each for the fall of his own haven, in the order they fell, from Corlen for Eldhythe, the first, to Pellow for Glasspire, the last. (We say a tale is laid, like a stone in a wall, because a laid tale stays.) It was a night of hard frost. The guns were still cooling from the day's fighting, and the mortar of the day's building hadn't set. Kael the Counter, the True Men's oath-keeper, held the old gate-stone, which whoever tells at this hearth must hold, and called the men. I, Seren, who write this Book, spoke for our builders' guild.*

   Everything in it is H0 or H1. The Book's *Kael the Counter called them, and I spake for the guild* is kept.

8. **III.2, Rimewatch: explain *taper* again in this tale.** III.1 explains it, but primer §1.1 asks for each tale to explain its own words, and *taper* is the one word in III.2 that a newcomer won't know and that has no explanation. Change
   > the way my mother's taper used to go at dusk, lighting the lamps one by one.

   to
   > the way my mother's taper, a thin candle, used to go at dusk, lighting the lamps one by one.

9. **III.1 when-line: don't use *holding* in two senses in one tale.** The when-line says *our first year of holding this old mountain fortress*. Later in the tale, *holding* is explained as a lore term: *at every holding, the hours when the enemy attacks*. A newcomer meets the same word with two meanings. Use the house gloss for the Keep instead. Change
   > *Told in the first autumn of the siege, our first year of holding this old mountain fortress, the Keep, against the enemy's fleets.*

   to
   > *Told in the first autumn of the siege, our first year of defending the Keep, the old fortress on the mountain ridge, against the enemy's fleets.*

10. **III.2, the staffs (optional, one clause).** Primer §7 lists *the law of the staff* as something III.2 newcomers stumble on. The draft explains what a staff is, but the fixed line then reads like a passing saying. You could put three words in front of it without touching the echo: *That's the guild's law: A staff is cut for its work, and never leaves its work.* The echo stays word for word, and the law's second half (*laid down in it*) stays out, as in the Book.

11. **Headnote length (advice, not a fidelity fault).** III.1's headnote runs 127 words and III.2's 103, against the primer's 30 to 70. The writer offered to cut the *laid* parenthesis. Don't cut it: it is the house gloss that the closing *I have laid this tale…* depends on, and the Book's newcomer gets it nowhere else in these tales. If III.1 must come down, cut *and called each haven by name* (two paragraphs later Kael says *I'll call each haven*), and shorten *as the True Men had answered on our first evening here, when…* to *as all twelve had answered on the day of the banner, when…*.

## Checked, and right as it is (no change)

- **Beats, names and numbers.** All of them are present in both tales, including fifty years, three spans, a hand's breadth a lifetime, seven generations, a day's sail, ten havens, ten staffs and ten oaths, twelve under the banner, twenty months, three months, three days, one night, five months and a month in the passes. All ten tellers are present, along with Stannard's knock, Kael's *I have no haven*, Ebba nodding, Pellow's *Look close* to the children, the young men's laughter and the hall waiting with Corlen.
- **The hearth's clock.** Nothing is explained beyond H8 in III.1 or beyond H10 in III.2. The wreck-wood explanation (*Wood is scarce up here on the ridge, so we burn them*) is H3 and matches the Book's V.1 (*wood was dear on the ridge*). **Keep it** (the writer's Unsure 1): it is accurate, and it heads off any reading of Aske's hand as a burning rite. The *chest I carried up the mountain* stays at the Book's own words, and nothing of H16 leaks in.
- **Plants and mysteries.** *It was the last time he held it.* stays a bare plant. The Emberhythe plank gets no word and no origin, and *I told him to burn it.* stays Harl's temper. The Glasspire shadow stays unsolved, closing on *I looked close. I saw nothing.* *Those sails were simpler…* doesn't say how. *No messenger came with words that anyone could understand* keeps the Book's own double edge without pointing at the Guest. No hint is given of Corlen's, Hesk's or Pellow's deaths, or of Voss bringing the first boat home.
- **Writer's Unsure 2, Tamm's stones joined in III.2.** Keep it. The hearth has watched him set them by his blanket (III.1, H1 in who's-who), so *Those are the three I set out by my blanket every night* makes the Book's implication plain. It doesn't reveal anything from a later tale.
- **Writer's Unsure 3, *Ten cities, ten harbours*.** Keep it as the Book has it.
- **Lore explanations.** Each one is correct: course, staff (the overseer's rod, matching Jack's formula), the holding, harbour-houses, spans, fen, canopy, wall-walk, cairns, galleries, mere, crags, far-glasses, the grey, the ghost fleet and the Mystaeri, the drums, the column, the masters, banking the fire (shown as plain habit, not a rite), the Road and the night of the pass. - **Fixed lines and echoes.** All are word for word: *"Wait. The sea hasn't finished."*, *the sea hadn't finished*, *"Look close."*, *I looked close. There are no songs in these tales.
- **Markup.** The heading, the Argument, both `###` headings and all twenty bold haven heads match `plain_v3.md` character for character. `{{Halyna}}` appears once, in III.1's headnote, with a plural verb, as in the old Plain. There are no comment markers or native blocks (the old Plain has none either). The draft leaves out the `---` rules around Book Three, as g04 and g06 do, so the assembler must supply them.
- **Rules of the world.** No singular *they* (all 47 instances of *they*, *them* and *their* checked). No custom of burning. Nothing Christian. No old words and no semicolons.
