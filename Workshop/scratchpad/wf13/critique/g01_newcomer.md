# g01 — Newcomer read (Title, Contents, Of This Book, Invocation, I.1)

I read only `plain/g01.md`, top to bottom, the way a stranger would. I know nothing about Rivenkeep. Line numbers refer to `g01.md`.

The `## WRITER'S NOTES` section (line 301 on) is not reader text. I stopped reading when I got to it. It **must be stripped** before this goes to a reader. It also names who dies, which a reader would be spoiled by.

I'm assuming `{{Halyna}}` and the other `{{…}}` names show up as the special one-stroke pair-name and not as raw braces. If they ever reach a reader as plain text, they look like code.

---

## The ten things that stopped me most (in order)

1. **Where is the Keep?** The note says the people "walked for a month up into the mountains" (l.93), and I.1 calls it "a castle up here in the mountains" (l.211). Then the foreword says wreck-wood "wash[es] up on the beach below our wall" (l.128), and the note says "every winter after, the grey fleets came against it" (l.93). How do ships attack a mountain fort, and how is there a beach below it? This was the one place I lost the picture completely. One clause would fix it, for example that the ridge runs out to the sea and the Keep looks down on the water from the far side.
2. **The same things get explained four times.** The Long Hearth is glossed as "the great fireplace" at l.95, l.103, l.170 and l.201. "Laid, like a stone in a wall" is explained at l.95, l.120, l.183 and l.201. *We remember* is explained at l.95 and again at l.122. The Keep is glossed at l.93 and again at l.199. The grey sails are glossed at l.91 and again at l.215. By the third time I was skimming. I.1's headnote (l.201) and the Invocation headnote (l.170) can drop their glosses completely. A reader who opens I.1 cold still gets "the stone" and "laid" from what Kael says.
3. **The note gives away I.1's climax.** "There a soldier they call only the Captain climbed the broken wall, tore his cloak in two, wrote on one half, and raised it as a banner" (l.93). The foreword says it again at l.103. By the time Kael tells it, I already knew how it ends. (The Invocation quoting the Title first is fine. It plays as "oh, *that's* where the nightly words come from.") The note should say less, something like *a soldier they call only the Captain raised a banner on the broken wall*, and leave the cloak to Kael.
4. **The dark stone paragraph (l.130) is the hardest one in the group.** I read it three times. It puts the mute stones in a clause and only explains them in the next sentence. It doesn't say whether the dark stone is inside the sealed bundle. It doesn't say why learning what the mute stones *were* made it all right to break the seal, or why {{Idrenna}} get to decide. It also comes between "the wood can't lie" (l.128) and "Nobody reads the wood. It's known" (l.132), so I lost track of how wood tales work. Fix the order: the chest, then the unreadable carved stones in it, then the sealed bundle and the seal rule, then the wood showing {{Halyna}} what the stones were, then {{Idrenna}}, the oldest pair, giving leave, then the dark stone inside and the tale that came from it. After that, go back to the wood.
5. **"Two people joined as one from birth" (l.89, l.116).** Joined how? My first thought was conjoined twins. Then I read "the husband and wife … are called {{Halyna}}" and wondered whether they were married as babies. Later, "a child who has no pair" (l.148) made me think pairing happens in the cradle. This is the most important custom in the book and I still don't know what it is. One concrete sentence would do it: who pairs them, when, and whether every pair is a married couple.
6. **Words that are never explained:**
   - **"course-hand"** (l.173, l.243, l.295) appears three times and is never explained. I worked out that it's a kind of handwriting, since the Captain and Seren both write in it, but no more than that.
   - **"the Hal"** (l.112): "in their old writing, the Hal". Is that a script? Is it connected to *Hal*vard and *Hal*yna?
   - **"Stonwryt"** (l.112) next to "Stonewrights" (l.89). It reads like a typo. If it's an old spelling, say so: *their old word for themselves*.
   - **"The Title of Liberty"** (l.173) and **"The Title"** (l.243). Nobody tells me the Captain's words are called this, or why. "Title" could mean a heading or a claim to land. Both meanings would be interesting, but give me the name once in plain words.
   - **"the year of the Tide of Remembering, as the wall named that year's enemy fleets"** (l.99). I read it twice. Does the wall have a name for each winter's fleet? Try something like: *the year we called the Tide of Remembering, for on the wall we give each winter's fleet a name.*
   - **"These are the Stone's own rings"** (l.291). The prose just above says "the grain of the wood". Stones don't have rings, and I can't tell which Stone has a capital S. If this is a planted mystery (the title page says *The Book of the Riven Stone*), it works as a hook. If it's a slip, it's confusing.
7. **Lines that contradict themselves:**
   - "That was the last **unbroken** wall in the Shorelands" (l.249), after "the **broken** face of the wall" (l.228) and a gate that "hung by one hinge" (l.211). I think it means the last wall the enemy hadn't taken. Say that.
   - "there **was** never a greater man, and I don't think his equal ever lived" (l.223). The past tense reads like a eulogy, and then the Captain is sitting on the bench at the end. The two halves also say the same thing.
   - "I have laid this tale the way it was laid **for me**" (l.274). Kael was there, so who laid it for him? If it's a fixed closing formula, I need to see it again somewhere before I'll read it that way.
   - The crimson **pennant** (l.266) and the cloak **banner** (l.103, l.249). Are there two flags? And the cloak had been "worn … down to the colour of old brick" (l.230), so is the pennant brighter than the thing it honours?
8. **Numbers from a man who counts.** Kael names 11 True Men plus himself (l.256), says "most" came from havens, and says he came from none. That leaves 11 men for 10 havens, so which one isn't from a haven? The Contents says "the ten men who speak for them" (l.35). Then come "six companies", with "one troop to each of us True Men" (l.260). Is that two troops to a company? Kael of all people should leave the numbers clean.
9. **Guns.** "The guns are cooling" (l.179). Until then everything was cloaks, pikes, chisels and coal, so I was picturing a medieval world. Guns are fine, but they arrived as a jolt. One early mention would help, maybe in the note or in the foreword's view of the wall.
10. **The romanized lines need a word-by-word gloss.** The Title (l.174, l.244) gives *Ul dumol ol varn, ol theldeth, ol Mardh, ol lunnath, ol sollan* and then the English line. I tried to match them up and got stuck on **ol**. Is it "of", "our", or "of our"? It seems to mean "of our" before *varn* and just "our" after that. I'd love an interlinear line under it, like this:
    > *Ul* in · *dumol* memory · *ol* of-our · *varn* home · *ol* our · *theldeth* families · *ol* our · *Mardh* God · *ol* our · *lunnath* freedoms · *ol* our · *sollan* peace
    
    (That's my guess. A reader can't check it, which is the problem.) The **Stonwryt vow** (l.112–113) gives only English and none of the old words, and **Seren's course-hand note** (l.295–296) is the same. They felt thin next to the Title. Even a short romanized line with glosses would make the language feel real.

## Paragraph by paragraph

### Title page (l.1–5)
- "*The Book of the Riven Stone*" is a good title. I know "riven" means split. Which stone? I don't find out in this group, and that's fine.
- "retold here in plain words" is helpful. It tells me this is a retelling.

### Contents (l.9–81)
- It's very long and full of names I don't know yet: "Bonded", "Theoliths", "Rivenmen", "Aelvaren", "Black Pillars", "Brenn of the outpost", "the Thin Sky", "the First Glyph", "the Book of Knowings". For a contents page that's fine. I read it as a teaser and skimmed it.
- **"facing the Epilogue" / "facing II.2"** (l.19, l.29 and others). I had no idea what "facing" meant until l.136. A two-word hint the first time it appears would help, for example *(on the page opposite)*.
- The best lines are the book subtitles: "the wrong that was done, told from both sides of the grey" (l.42), "a doom set in motion by the dead, crewed by the living, against a people who had never known they were the foe" (l.53), and "How the havens came home, and the war went home" (l.65). Those made me want to read on.
- "in six counts, one for each year's enemy fleets" (l.59) tells me the siege lasts six years. It's a little spoiler, but a harmless one.

### A note for new readers (l.87–97)
- Overall this is clear and it helped. I needed most of it.
- "two people joined as one from birth": see #5.
- "nobody could understand a word from whoever sent them" (l.91). This comes just after "no one on their decks". So did they send words, like messages or signs? If so, how? It's a small snag, and maybe one meant to make me curious.
- "walked for a month up into the mountains" next to "every winter after, the grey fleets came against it" (l.93): see #1.
- "climbed the broken wall, tore his cloak in two, wrote on one half": see #3.
- "Each tale opens with a line in italics that says when it was told" (l.97) is very useful.
- Seren "was twelve when they reached the Keep." I liked knowing that. It colours everything she writes.

## Tale by tale: did I follow it, and did it move me?

**Of This Book (the note and Seren's foreword).** I followed most of it, with two exceptions: the dark-stone paragraph (l.130) and the question of where the Keep is in relation to the sea. It moved me in places: Ebba starting *We remember*, "I write alone", "Mine is the hand that writes, not the hand that knows", and "Only the chest was heavy". Seren's voice is the real draw. It's young, exact and a little lonely. Two things drag it down. It repeats the note, and it front-loads a lot of lore in a row (seal rules, mute stones, four pairs). Trim the note's overlap and untangle l.130, and this will be a lovely opening.

**The Invocation.** I followed it. It's short, warm and clear. The line about the forgotten wrong coming back "under grey sails", and "the wrong we did", is the best hook in the whole group. The headnote gloss and the unexplained "Title of Liberty" / "course-hand" are the only snags. It reads even better after I.1, when you know where the words came from.

**I.1 · The Torn Cloak.** I followed it completely, and it moved me. This is the strongest piece in the group. Kael's voice carries it: the counting, the thirty-one stones, "I stopped counting", "It was like that". The parts that pulled me out were the repeated glosses (headnote and grey sails), the note having already told me the climax, and a few small contradictions (unbroken/broken wall, "was never a greater man", "laid for me", pennant/banner, troop counts). Fix those and it's ready.

**What I'd most like to see next time:** a one-line answer to "how do ships reach a mountain fort", one plain sentence on what being bonded actually means, the glosses cut down to one each, and a word-by-word line under every romanized phrase.
