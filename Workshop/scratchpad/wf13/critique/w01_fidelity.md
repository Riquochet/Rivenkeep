# w01 fidelity critique: II.2, IV.2, IV.4, IV.6 (new Plain Words)

Checked `wf13/plain/w01.md` against `wf11/book_v3.md` (and IV.3 for the outpost facts), `wf11/plain_v3.md` (markup and fixed wording), `wf13/plain_primer.md` (clock, lexicon, mysteries, §7 watch-points) and `wf11/notes_v3.md` §2035.

**Verdict.** The draft is faithful. I went through every Book paragraph, reading line, number and name in all four tales, and nothing is lost. There are no spoilers, every mystery is kept, and the fixed lines and markup are right. The fixes below are small. Four of them correct wording that is slightly wrong about the lore, one sets a convention across files, and two are optional clarity changes. Line numbers are in `w01.md`.

## Fixes

1. **II.2, line 48. "ever" makes the silver-elder line false.**
   - Old: `because no one is ever laid to rest beneath a silver elder.`
   - New: `because no one is laid to rest beneath a silver elder.`
   - Why: II.2 is laid at H27, after IV.6 (H21). By then the hearth knows the Aelvaren's keel, with Aelrhen's bones in it, was laid among the silver elders' roots. Notes §2035 says *none lie beneath a silver elder, save the Aelvaren's keel*. The Book's *none lie* states a custom. Adding *ever* turns it into an absolute that the hearth already knows was broken.
   - Keep the burial sense, as primer §3.7 reads it. If the orchestrator wants Jack's possible pun to survive (V.4's elders *keep all they hear*), the old Plain's `because nobody lies under a silver elder` is the alternative. Either way, drop *ever*.

2. **IV.4, line 164, and IV.6, line 242. "came kneeling to … door" gives the wrong picture.** In IV.3, Brenn says the stranger *walked up to the warden's door* and then *kneeled he on the step*. *Came kneeling* reads as if he crossed the shingle on his knees.
   - IV.4 old: `and the Guest was the stranger out of the fog who came kneeling to its warden's door.`
   - IV.4 new: `and the Guest was the stranger out of the fog who knelt at its warden's door.`
   - IV.6 old: `He came kneeling to the door of the warden of the outpost on the edge of our land, and he was killed there.`
   - IV.6 new: `He knelt at the door of the outpost's warden, at the very edge of our land, and he was killed there.`

3. **IV.4, line 164. "Brenn's pages" says Brenn wrote them.** {{Wendhessa}} *took them from his mouth* (IV.3 headnote). Brenn didn't write the confession.
   - Old: `It lay wrapped in Brenn's pages in our guild's chest of records,`
   - New: `It lay wrapped in the pages of Brenn's confession in our guild's chest of records,`

4. **IV.4, line 192. "sawn into planks" doesn't fit what was cut.** The kin were doorposts and benches (Brenn: *doorposts … table … the frame of his bed*). Posts aren't planks, and the Book says only *sawn and polished*.
   - Old: `It was our own kin, the great trees from the edge of the breath, sawn into planks and polished.`
   - New: `It was our own kin, the great trees from the edge of the breath, sawn and polished.`

5. **IV.4 and IV.6 when-lines and headnotes. The pair-name convention needs one rule across files.** This is a consistency decision, not an error.
   - What w01 does: it writes *Halvard and Rhyna* in IV.4 and IV.6, because neither the Book nor the old Plain has `{{Halyna}}` in those tales.
   - What the primer and w02 do: primer §7 gives both when-lines as `*{{Halyna}} came to know this from the wood in the third spring/summer of the siege.*`. w02 already follows it in V.3, V.4 and V.6, which also have no pair-name in the Book. II.2 and IV.2 in this same file use the `{{Halyna}}` form.
   - Recommended change (lines 162, 164, 240, 242):
     - when-lines: `*Halvard and Rhyna came to know this from the wood …*` → `*{{Halyna}} came to know this from the wood …*`
     - headnotes: `Halvard and Rhyna, the husband and wife who lead our builders, came to know it by touch.` → `{{Halyna}}, Halvard and Rhyna, the husband and wife who lead our builders, came to know it by touch.`
     - Leave the rest of the IV.4 headnote as it is (*This spring Rhyna held it … Halvard was across the courtyard …*).
   - If the orchestrator instead rules that a tale's pair-name set must not grow, w02 has to change to match. Either way, the two files must agree.

6. **IV.4, line 192. "posts" collides with "the post" (optional, for clarity).** *He chose the farthest of your posts* is followed two sentences later by *laid his hand on the post*, which is a doorpost. A newcomer can read the first as a doorpost too.
   - Old: `He chose the farthest of your posts, out at the very edge of your land,`
   - New: `He chose the farthest of your outposts, out at the very edge of your land,`
   - Optionally also: `At the door he laid his hand on the doorpost to greet them,`

7. **IV.2, line 84. "For a long time" is more than the Book says (optional).** The Book says only that {{Halyna}} *had but its shape*. By the clock, the heart was found in the second summer and came whole in the third winter.
   - Old: `For a long time {{Halyna}} had only its shape, the broken pieces that come first here.`
   - New: `At first {{Halyna}} had only its shape, the broken pieces that come first here.`

## Checked and clean (no change)

- **Beats, facts, numbers, names.** Every Book paragraph is present in all four tales. Every number is right: *two and a half times*, *forty summers*, *three words*, *three nights*, *a day and a night*, *half a lifetime*, *two pairs*, *one pair*, *Twice*, *both boats*, *an hour*. Every name is right, with the Book's own glosses: Aelrhen (*bond-to-stone*), the Aelvaren (*the Bearer of the Binding*), the Naelsaen (*the mist-hearts*), *Ael'thar*. II.2's reading puts back *Only for it.*, which the old Plain had dropped.
- **Headnote facts.** All of the Book's headnote facts are kept: the child's question, the sleeper waking with the knowing, the shingle, the vault, the three nights, *as a wound*, the hour, *like water over stone*, Halvard at the mortar, the rain barrel, *She wouldn't say why*, and *They did not know it was ignorance.* (word for word, left unexplained).
- **Fixed matter.** These are all exact:
  - *Stop… Sky… Dying…*
  - the Guest's sentence, character for character the same as `plain_v3.md`
  - *harm to one is harm to both*
  - *into the wind and against the current*
  - *Even burnt, even dying, the wood knows the way home.*
  - *This is held in the grain.*, *… and the grain still holds it*, and *\*And no one answered.\**
  - no *We remember* after any of the four
- **Markup.** Matches `plain_v3.md` in every tale: headings, READING, both RINGs, the chips, `<!-- RIPENS: VI.3 -->`, and both `::: note` blocks byte-identical. The extra `{{Halyna}}` uses in II.2 and IV.2 are the same pair-name. There's no ▒▒▒▒ in the old Plain for these tales and none was added. The missing `---` after IV.6 matches every sibling file, because the assembler emits rules from the Book skeleton.
- **Clock.** Nothing is used before the hearth knows it:
  - II.2 (H27) gives the Guest one clause, says nothing of the door or his death, and leaves the name untold.
  - IV.2 (H17) uses *heart*, *the long-lived*, *the iron*, *the mute thing*, *the grey that you call fog*, and the council choosing hearts. The vote isn't mentioned.
  - IV.4 (H19) uses the outpost, Brenn, the chest's road and the gift as wood, all known from H16 and H19.
  - IV.6 (H21) uses *Aelrhen* and *he was killed there*, and says *Every young tree that was to become a ship*. That last is the Book's own line, and nothing is added about growing.
- **Mysteries.**
  - Why the host drew back: *We do not know why. Fear leaves no mark on wood.* and *they did not know why … And they did not ask.*
  - Why the boat turned: *Then we turned.*, with no cause added.
  - Rhyna's hands: *While he did* only makes plain the timing the Book's semicolon already gives. The reason stays dark.
  - The warden is never named. The wood calls him only *the host* or *the master of that house*.
  - No *ael* is glossed and no likeness of sound is pointed out.
  - IV.3's *Leave nothing to show* and IV.6's *Wood can hide nothing* are not joined.
  - IV.2's *the first pillar … we were the last shoot its stump put up* is a fair reading of *Where the iron first went in, a stump was cut low*. Keep it.
- **World rules.** No singular *they* (every *they* is a pair, a group or a plural). The burning of the boat is a deed, not a custom. No Christian words and no old words. No semicolons outside the native blocks.
- **Outside fidelity, for the orchestrator.** The headnotes run 160 to 190 words against the primer's 30 to 70. Fidelity doesn't need that length. If they're cut, the Book facts listed under *Headnote facts* above must stay. Each headnote needs one wood-leaf sentence, and the added context (the Guest clause, Brenn, the chest's road, *he was killed there*) can be trimmed.
