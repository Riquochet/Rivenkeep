# Fidelity critique: w02 (V.3 The Wreck That Went Home, V.4 The Council Under the Thin Sky, V.6 Of the Growing, VI.2 The Thrones That Walk the Sea)

I checked `wf13/plain/w02.md` against `wf11/book_v3.md` (V.3 lines 1814–1877, V.4 1879–1952, V.6 2108–2186, VI.2 2500–2576, plus III.1's havens, V.5 Count VI, V.7 Part I and VI.3's token), the primer `wf13/plain_primer.md` (§1.6 and §1.7, §2 H18/H23/H24/H33, §3.7, §4, §7), the old Plain `wf11/plain_v3.md`, and `wf11/notes_v3.md` for the token.

**Overall.** The draft is faithful and careful. I compared every paragraph of all four leaves with the Book, and no beat is missing. Every name, number and teller is kept. The fixed matter is word for word: *But war is for the short-minded and the angry young.*, the italic carvings (*Home when wounded.*, *Look before you trust.*, *Grow until the shore is silent.*), all ten judgements in the old Plain's and Knowings table's wording, *This is held in the grain.* / *and the grain still holds it* / *\*And no one answered.\**, and Seren's facing-page frame line. The Song of the Years has its seven lines, and each one means what the Book's line means.

I ran `_w02chk.py` on the markup. All four `###` headings, every `<!-- READING -->` and `<!-- RING -->`, the `[ ]` gaps and the line-end breaks match the old Plain. None of these leaves has a native block, chip, blot or token. There is no singular *they*, no semicolon, no old word (the only *yet* is *not yet*) and no Christian word. Esthaer's kin *burned slowly* as timber, which is not a custom of burning.

The mysteries hold: the small fire over Eirlenth isn't named, the Glasspire night stays dark, *Thae —* isn't matched to a name, the last two growings stay unknown, *Ael* is never taken apart, nothing says why the host drew back, the warden and the outpost aren't named, and V.3's *one fire, thrown* is never tied to Wick. Prose lengths run from 129 to 142 per cent of the Book.

The fixes below are ordered by weight. Fixes 1–6 correct something the draft gets wrong or gives away. Fixes 7–10 are small repairs of accuracy or clarity.

## Fixes

1. **VI.2, headnote (line 227): the city order is stated as the order the Thrones fell, which breaks the game's token.** Replace *The wood gives the ten in the order of our cities, from Eldhythe, the eldest, to Glasspire, the last.* with **Here the ten stand in the order of our cities, from Eldhythe, the eldest, to Glasspire, the youngest.**
   The player chooses the order in which the havens are retaken. VI.3's `{LAST_THRONE_AT_HAVEN}` is *the Throne the player felled last* (`notes_v3.md` line 1963), and primer §4 item 21 says never to name that haven. The headnote's own when-line says the knowings came *one Throne at a time, as each of our cities was won back*. So *the wood gives the ten in the order of our cities* claims they were known in haven order, and *Glasspire, the last* reads as the last Throne to fall. Both contradict VI.3 whenever the player finishes anywhere other than Glasspire. *The youngest* keeps III.1's fact (*Glasspire, the last and proudest*) without that double meaning. The list itself is in haven order, and VI.1 is too, so the corrected sentence is true however the game was played.

2. **V.4, line 131: "the ships they were growing" makes the pleading elders the growers.** Change *As long as even one of the elders who had pleaded was still alive, the ships they were growing lay ready at the edge of your sea, and didn't go. When the last of those elders died and went down into the moss, the ones who had cried for war cut the fleet loose against your shore.* to **As long as even one of the elders who had pleaded was still alive, the new-grown ships lay ready at the edge of your sea, and didn't go. When the last of those elders died and went down into the moss, the ones who had cried for war cut them loose against your shore.**
   The only plural noun before *they* is *the elders who had pleaded*, so the sentence says the peace party grew the war fleet. The Book's subject is impersonal (*the growing lay ready*). The war party grew the ships, and the elders' lives held them back.

3. **V.6, line 183: "its own fear" loses the wood's word and gives away a later night's discovery.** Change *Each ship kept its own grain, and its own fear.* to **Each ship kept its own grain, and its own dread, the places in the water it had learned were places where wood dies.** If that reads as too much, the minimum fix is **…and its own dread.**
   The Book says *its own dread*. *The dread* is a defined wood word: V.3 says *The dread is but this: one place in the water is become a place where wood dieth*, and V.7 Part I uses it as a place (*stand at the edge of the dread*). That meaning is what makes the next sentence follow: *One might shy away from burning water.* Plain *fear* also states at H24 what Kael only realises at H31, in V.5 Count VI: *The wood can fear.* As one bare line, that discovery is weakened if the hearth was already told it two years earlier.

4. **V.6, line 193: an added length of time.** Change *It lay waiting at the edge of your sea for a whole lifetime of yours, while your children grew up and had children.* to **It lay waiting at the edge of your sea while your children grew up and had children of their own.**
   The Book gives no length (*while your children had children*). Primer §6 says the Book uses no numbers before the banner, so the Plain should keep to the Book's own phrases. The draft also names three different spans with the same measure: this wait, the growing (*longer than one of your lives*, line 203) and the Remembering wood in the Song (*as old as one of your lives*, line 213). A newcomer will try to match them up, and they don't match.

5. **VI.2, line 249: "more than a word" breaks the echo of the Song.** Change *because a judgement is more than a word, more than a sentence, more than a day's orders.* to **because a judgement is not a word, and not a sentence, and not a day's orders.**
   The Book's VI.2 line (*not a word, nor a sentence, nor a morrow*) deliberately repeats the last lines of V.6's Song: what the eldest *hold* is *not a word, … not a sentence, … not a morrow*, and *it is grief*. The draft's own Song line 216 has *It is not a word, and it is not a sentence, and it is not a day's orders*, so the same shape here lets the echo land. That echo is all the Book offers about what a judgement really is, and *more than* closes it off. The weight is still said, in the Book's place, two paragraphs on: *A judgement is heavy to carry.* The lexicon's *more than* is a gloss for writers, not fixed wording.

6. **VI.2, line 249: "the last carvers" echoes the Epilogue's Last Carver.** Change *Then the last carvers of the long-lived, the people of our wood, came to us.* to **Then the last of the long-lived who could still carve, the people of our wood, came to us.**
   This is closer to the Book (*the last of the long-lived that could carve*). *The last carvers* differs from Seren's name *the Last Carver* by one letter, and primer §4 item 4 forbids tying that figure to anything. The writer's notes (call 4) mean to avoid that link, but this wording makes it.

7. **V.4, when-line (line 70): make it plain that Aelrhen is the Guest.** Change *…when the stranger we call the Guest had been killed on our shore.* to **…when the stranger we call the Guest, whose own name was Aelrhen, had been killed on our shore.**
   The wood calls him only *Aelrhen* (lines 93, 101, 115). The writer's note says the when-line ties him to the Guest, but it never gives the name, so a newcomer can't join the two. The hearth has known the name since H19 (IV.4), and V.4 is H23. Primer §0, point 4, says to make the Book's meaning plain. Line 93's *Aelrhen was the one of us who went down to your shore* can stay as it is.

8. **V.3, line 22: the memory and the warning are carvings, not cargo.** Change *More than one word was carved into us. We carried a memory, and a warning.* to **More than one word was carved into us: a memory, and a warning.**
   The Book says *More than a word was cut in us: a memory, and a caution.* Splitting the sentence turns the two carvings into things the hull carried. The memory is what the roots taught (*where a stone flashes, the iron is*), and the caution is *Look before you trust.* The difference matters, because the tale is about what the wood holds in its grain.

9. **V.4, facing page (line 141): Seren's new first sentence contradicts itself.** Change *In this Book each tale of the wood's faces a tale of our own, but here we have none to set beside it.* to **In this Book a tale of the wood's is meant to face a tale of our own, but for this one we have none.**
   *Each … faces* states as fact what this very page shows to be false. The foreword gives the arrangement as {{Halyna}}'s wish (*would have the wood's leaf on the right, facing ours*), so *is meant to* is the true wording. The added gloss is otherwise welcome, because the Book's *Here there is no tale of ours* needs it for a newcomer.

10. **Before merging: strip the material that isn't Plain.** Delete line 1 (the `<!-- PLAIN WORDS · w02 … -->` comment) and the whole `## WRITER'S NOTES` section (lines 301–323). Neither one is in `plain_v3.md`.

## Optional (clarity, not fidelity)

- **V.4, line 115.** *or only one house at its edge* could become **or only one house at the very edge of your land**. The hearth has known the outpost since H16, and this names neither the warden nor the outpost. It just tells a newcomer whose *edge* is meant.

## Checked and left as they are

- **The when-lines.** All four use the primer's §7 wording, or keep to its facts. V.3 correctly leaves out the Tide of Remembering, which is named at H22. V.4's *Joined Tide* is in the Book's own dateline and is glossed. V.6 leaves *Aelthae* unglossed, which is safe, because V.7 Part I is where *The carving named that Tide Aelthae*.
- **`{{Halyna}}` in V.3, V.4 and V.6, where the Book's text has none.** The when-line formula (primer §1.5, §7) and the wood-leaf sentence (§1.6) require it. Every verb with it is plural. The assembler's pair-name check will flag it, and that's expected.
- **V.3's added glosses.** *That was one of your guns firing, though to us it was only a stone that flashed*, *the green wood, the young ships that came against you first* (V.3's own text implies the green wood came earlier), *the iron, your shot* (this tale's iron is shot, and VI.2's is axes, and both are glossed correctly), and *the bearer's heart carved the next day's orders* (V.3's own event, not V.6's general law). The puzzle lock is kept: *The grain holds nothing of what our fire found there.*
- **V.3's headnote, *cut open and raw from the salt, so she didn't touch it*.** The Book's *with the salt-cuts open in her palms, touched it not* plainly implies the reason, so this is making an implication plain, not inventing one.
- **V.4's *between-light* gloss** (*the soft light that came to us through the breath*). This is only what V.4's own sentence implies (the breath ran out, and the light went hard). It doesn't spend II.2's *neither day nor dusk, which the long-lived were made for* (H27).
- **V.4's *the elders, the oldest of the long-lived*.** This keeps the people apart from the silver elders, which are trees, and agrees with *the elders were dying*.
- **V.4's *hidden channels*.** This comes from IV.6, *the old channels, the hidden waters among the roots* (H21).
- **V.6's Song.** *its brother ship* is right, because the Second Tide's hulls came in pairs. *as old as one of your lives* and *the next day's orders* are faithful. *the eldest of us, the pillars of the breath* stays unexplained until VI.2.
- **VI.2's seats.** The draft names each seat by what it is, never by a city: *the three great bridges of your river city*, *the houses under the forest's canopy*, *the cairns, your old mounds of piled stone*, *the dark tunnels under the canyon*, and *the cranes … that lifted your stones until they seemed to float*. III.1's *Highreach, the tower of the floating stones* confirms that last one, because its courses *hung on the wind as hawks hang*. The draft also gives *the great glass at the top of your spire*, which the primer places in VI.2. The second gloss of *the iron, your axes*, in Rhenvael, earns its place, because the judgement has just used *gun*.
- **Headnote length** (130–150 words, against the primer's 30–70). This affects readability, not fidelity, and I leave it to the newcomer critic.
