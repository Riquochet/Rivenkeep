# g03 fidelity critique: I.4 The Rite of the Cornerstone · I.5 The Naming of the Rivenmen

Checked `wf13/plain/g03.md` against the Book (`wf11/book_v3.md`, lines 477–697), the primer (`wf13/plain_primer.md`) and the old Plain (`wf11/plain_v3.md`, lines 411–594).

**Verdict.** The draft is faithful and close to final. I checked every beat of both tales line by line against the Book, and none is missing. The fixed lines are verbatim and the markup is identical. Two fixes are needed: an idiom that changes the meaning (fix 1), and a vow cut in half by its glosses (fix 2). The rest are small corrections of lore precision. Line numbers are g03.md's.

---

## Must fix

**1. I.5, line 163: "stood down" means "resigned".**
- Now: *The Captain stood down among us, and the staff of command the Rite had given him stayed in its corner of the gatehouse.*
- Change to: *The Captain stood there among us, and the staff of command the Rite had given him stayed in its corner of the gatehouse.*
- Why: the Book says *The Captain stood with us*. A modern reader takes "stood down" as the idiom for giving up command, which is the opposite of the scene: the new Commander stands with the people, as one of them.

**2. I.5, line 175: the vow is split by its glosses.**
- Now: *That day we laid the Keep's name, Rivenkeep, on the whole land. Every haven the grey sails hold is Rivenkeep. The havens are our ten cities along the coast. The grey sails are the enemy's fleets, which came out of the fog at sea and burned them. The edge of the sea is the edge of the Keep. That's a vow.*
- Change to: *That day we laid the Keep's name, Rivenkeep, on the whole land. The grey sails, the enemy's fleets that came out of the fog at sea, had burned our havens, our ten cities along the coast. But every haven the grey sails hold is Rivenkeep, and the edge of the sea is the edge of the Keep. That's a vow.*
- Why: in the Book the vow is one sentence with two clauses (*Every haven the grey sails hold is Rivenkeep, and the edge of the sea is the edge of the Keep. It is a vow.*). With two gloss sentences between the clauses, *That's a vow* seems to cover only the last one. Give the glosses first, then the vow whole. The glosses stay H0-true.

## Should fix (lore precision)

**3. I.4, line 30: "every bonded pair has a mark", and "the stone in my hands".**
- Now: *Every bonded pair in our guild has a mark of its own, and this was theirs. The same mark is cut under the stone in my hands, the old capstone they laid, which we found lying face down in the gateway when we came.*
- Change to: *That was their own mark. The same mark is cut on the underside of the stone my hands are on now, the old capstone they laid, which we found lying face down in the gateway when we came.*
- Why: (a) A pair's mark is given at its sealing (Book II.3: *With it they give the mark the seers read*). That spring {{Enrella}} weren't sealed yet, because their sealing comes in the first high summer. So *every bonded pair … has a mark* isn't true at the time of telling, and it leans on lore from II.1 and II.3 (H6 and H7) in an H5 tale. (b) The tale-stone is the gate's old capstone, *a stone that two must turn* (the foreword). A teller lays her hands on it (I.2: *{{Halyna}} laid my two hands on the stone*) and doesn't hold it up in them.

**4. I.4, line 11: the Keep in the present tense.**
- Now: *…our first evening at the Keep, this ruined fortress on its mountain ridge.*
- Change to: *…our first evening at the Keep, this old fortress on its mountain ridge.*
- Why: Seren is speaking in the first spring, after months of building, so *this ruined fortress* makes the Keep sound like a ruin still. The house gloss (§3.1) is *the old fortress on the mountain ridge*.

**5. I.4, line 96: the Captain's standing before the Rite.**
- Now: *Until that day he'd only been the captain of one small company.*
- Change to: *Until that day his only rank had been captain of one small company.*
- Why: in I.1 the Twelve and then the whole column answered him on the day of the banner, and he had led everyone for two weeks. The Rite changed his title, not whether he led. The new wording still sets up the closing line (*In his own heart he was still only the Captain*).

**6. I.4, line 72: "coffin" adds a burial custom.**
- Now: *…the way a street goes quiet when a coffin is carried past.*
- Change to: *…the way a street goes quiet when the dead are carried through it.*
- Why: the Book says *as a street doth for a bier*. Coffins are a burial custom the Book never states (primer §1.2: no new customs). At the Keep the dead go under cairns.

**7. I.5, line 147: made-up geography for the east face.**
- Now: *…on the east face, the slope under our east wall. The ground there is too hard for a spade…*
- Change to: *…on the Keep's east face. The ground there is too hard for a spade…*
- Why: the Book never puts the east face under the east wall. The primer (§3.4) and g10 both say *the Keep's east face*, and the primer says not to invent a plan of the Keep.

**8. I.5, line 151: "bitterly" adds a feeling.**
- Now: *I was cold, and I asked it bitterly.*
- Change to: *I was cold, and I meant it as a hard question.*
- Why: the Book says *Cold I was, and I meant it hard.* *Bitterly* adds an emotion the Book doesn't name (primer §1.2: no new feelings). The writer flagged this reading too.

**9. I.5, line 173: "any more" changes the claim.**
- Now: *We weren't two peoples sharing a ruin any more.*
- Change to: *We weren't two peoples sharing a ruin.*
- Why: the Book says *We were not two peoples sharing a ruin. One were we.* That's a recognition of what they already were, not a change from two peoples to one. *Any more* says they had been two.

## Optional

**10. I.5, line 209: where the keeps stand.** The Book places the keeps nowhere (*the keeps we raised in this war, like posts along a quay*). *Along the coast* is the lexicon's gloss, so it's allowed, but it's an added claim. If you want to stay with the Book, write *small forts standing in a line like posts along a quay*.
