# Newcomer read: g11 (Epilogue and Appendix)

**Reader:** a modern adult who has never heard of Rivenkeep. I read only `wf13/plain/g11.md`, lines 1–179 (the story text). I did not use the Writer's Notes to understand anything. A short reader's-side reply to the notes' open questions is at the very end.

**Key:** **STOP** = stopped me or made me guess. **TWICE** = had to reread. **WHO** = "who is this / why does it matter?" **HINT** = a hint I could feel but didn't get. **BORED** = over-explained or glosses piled up. **GOOD** = worked, keep it.

---

## The ten things to fix first (worst first)

1. **The enemy is never pinned down in the Epilogue.** I meet "the wood", "the enemy's carved wood", "the last great enemy ship" with a "dead heart", "a hand from behind the fog", "their count", "branches", "groves", then on the last page "the Mystaeri". Are the ships the enemy? Are there people on them? Are the Mystaeri the trees? The plainest sentence in the file is in the Appendix, L120: *"The enemy's grey ships were living wood, with their orders cut into the grain."* I needed that sentence (or half of it) in the Epilogue around L17. With it, L50, L58 and the grain passage at L80 would click on the first read.
2. **Halyna = Halvard + Rhyna is never said in the Epilogue.** L13 "too deep for Halyna to hold" reads as one person. L19 "Halyna **are** gone" looked like a typo. L21 names Halvard and Rhyna but never joins them to "Halyna". I worked it out from the sounds (Hal- + -yna), and once I had it I liked it. I then decoded Orvenna = Ormund + Penna by myself, which felt like a reward. But one clause would save every newcomer, for example *"Halyna, as we called the two of them together, are gone."*
3. **L32, the Idrenna paragraph,** is the hardest paragraph in the file (details below).
4. **L50, "None of us could answer it. A child did."** How? What did she do or say, and was it last autumn or now? This is the emotional key of the boat scene and I missed it.
5. **L78, "written under the round in the wood's words".** What is "the round"? It appears nowhere else. The whole sentence (*"The letters are read right away, below. The grain is told only by this"*) reads like a note to a typesetter and threw me out of the story right before its most powerful passage.
6. **Kael, the Captain and the Commander.** L13 says "Kael's tale of the torn cloak". L23 says "the Captain wrote on his torn cloak". L94 says "the Rite that made him our Commander". I guessed these are one man, but it's a guess. Kael is never named again.
7. **L23, "the five things".** "Their mark, their vow and the Title, the five things the Captain wrote." I counted mark + vow + Title = three and thought I'd missed two. I only learn at L90 that the Title has five parts.
8. **L94, "the staff is laid down in it."** Laid down in what? The work? The shaft? I read it three times. It sounds like a saying I'm meant to know already. *"the stair that goes no more"* in italics gives the same feeling: a quotation from somewhere I haven't been.
9. **Thirteen names before the boat lands.** Between L13 and L58: Kael, Seren, Halyna, Halvard, Rhyna, the Captain, Orvenna, Ormund, Penna, Idrenna, Tarnel, Ebba, Merrick. I'd drop Kael (just say "the Captain") and Ebba, and either give Idrenna a job or cut them.
10. **L173, "the shore laughed at three axemen who came home clinging to a spar."** Who laughed, and why? It reads like a reference to a tale I missed.

## EPILOGUE: The Stone out of the Grey

**L7, part line: "What the grey sent back."** GOOD. "The grey" isn't explained until L32, but as a hook it works.

**L11, when-line.** "The homecomings" is glossed at once, good. Small snag: the Epilogue keeps two calendars, "the fourth year after the homecomings" and "the seventh winter since we came to the Keep" / "the seventh spring" / "the dust of seven winters". I tried to line them up, gave up, and kept reading. Not fatal.

**L13, headnote.**
- WHO "**Kael's** tale of the torn cloak, the first in this Book." Who is Kael? (See fix 6.)
- STOP "kept for **the wood's side** of the story". At this point I have no idea the story has a wood side. Wood as the enemy hasn't come up yet.
- TWICE "I drew the grain of the wood on it, with one line in my hand: Not told yet. This is still too deep for Halyna to hold." The switch from italic to roman text made me unsure where the quoted line ends. On a second look, both sentences are the quote. Fine.
- WHO "Halyna": first appearance, with nothing to say it's two people (see fix 2).

**L15, "Hold the stone, Seren."** WHO: who is speaking to whom? I guessed the narrator is Seren talking to herself, but I wasn't sure until L40–46. When I got to *"Now there's no one to finish it, and I say both halves alone,"* I went back to this line and it hurt in a good way. GOOD on the reread. Keep it.

**L17.**
- GOOD "I wrote what they told me in two inks, one for each of them. Their two inks stand dry on my table now." Lovely.
- STOP "no one left now to **know the wood** for me." "Know" is used as a special verb. The next clause ("knew the enemy's carved wood by touch") rescues it.
- STOP "the enemy's carved wood": does the enemy carve wood, or are they wood? Fix 1 belongs here.

**L19, "Halyna are gone."** STOP: the plural verb on a single name (fix 2).

**L21.**
- GOOD "Rhyna went first, in her sleep. Halvard went before the lamp burned out, because he knew, the way one of a pair always knows." The best sentence in the opening. It explains the custom through feeling, not definition.
- STOP "joined as one from birth": literally, magically, or by a ceremony? L36's "shared my cradle" and "sealed as a pair" suggest babies are paired by a rite. I wanted one concrete picture.
- "the Keep" is never named. I took it to be a fortress. Fine.

**L23, the capstone.**
- TWICE "the five things" (fix 7). One possible fix: *"Cut face down into its underside are their mark and their vow, and the Title: the five things the Captain wrote…"*
- GOOD "They're where no eye will see them again until the wall falls."

**L25–30, Orvenna and the gate-song.**
- GOOD. I decoded Ormund + Penna on my own.
- "Up to the comma" is a slightly odd cue for a sung song (singers don't see commas), but it's clear and charming.
- The song is simple, and "Two at the gate" lands on the theme of pairs. GOOD.

**L32: STOP, the worst paragraph.**
- (a) Idrenna are introduced and praised for outliving Halyna, and then do nothing. The paragraph turns to Halyna. Why are they here? I expected them to be the ones who cut the name.
- (b) "before **their** chisels went into the grey" comes right after the Idrenna sentence, so I read them as Idrenna's chisels. Only at L64 do I learn they were Halyna's.
- (c) "into the grey, the fog out at sea, in the boat we sent there, Halyna cut…" Three comma-chunks in a row. I read it three times.
- (d) "**the boat we sent there**" is the first mention of any boat, spoken as if I already know it. L64 finally explains it, and I relaxed there, but for thirty lines I was lost.
- (e) "Halyna cut Halvard's name" means Halvard cut his own name? And why only his, not Rhyna's? Is it because the stair is "each son under his father"? HINT, not got.
- GOOD "Below his name the stone is smooth, and no one of his line is left to cut it." I got it: no son. Sad and quiet.
- Suggestion: split this into two or three sentences, say whose chisels, say "the boat we sent out into the grey" as its own sentence, and give Idrenna the act of cutting or drop them.

**L34, the cloak.**
- GOOD, the whole sequence, and "He went into that winter in a plain grey cloak." Moving.
- TWICE "tied it to a pike **over the gate**". Then "Over the graves of our dead he'd tied strips". It took me a second to realise the gate *is* their grave, because of the capstone. "Over the gate, over their grave" would close it.

**L36.**
- STOP, density. One paragraph brings in "third", "unbonded", "the guild", "Tarnel", "shared my cradle", "sealed as a pair", "this hearth" and "Covenant of Completion", eight new things at once.
- GOOD "alone I could go where they couldn't".
- TWICE "God does not take the one without leaving the other a work to do." Who are the one and the other? Not Halvard and Rhyna, because the Bonded die together. Tarnel and me? Halyna and me? I settled on "they're taken, I'm left with the Book", and it works, but I had to argue with it.
- GOOD "This work is mine." It earns its own line.

**L40–46, laying a tale.**
- GOOD "We say a tale is laid, like a stone in a wall, because a laid tale stays."
- TWICE "the old gate-stone **that whoever is telling holds**" is clumsy. "the old gate-stone the teller holds" would do.
- GOOD "one of them began and the other finished", and the two halves of the saying. With "I say both halves alone" it's one of the strongest beats in the file.

**L48.** GOOD "a small grey boat came out of the grey. It ran itself up the shingle". Eerie, and "ran itself" is perfect. The gloss "the pebble beach below our wall" is fine.

**L50.**
- GOOD: the old men stop at the top of the beach and the children run down. A great image.
- STOP "a living green sliver from the **dead heart** of the last great enemy ship". A ship has a heart, and it was alive? I got "a piece of their ship was still alive", but only roughly.
- HINT "None of us could answer it. A child did." (fix 4). Is her laying her hands on the bow now the answer? If so, say "Now a child did." If she answered last autumn, I need one clause about how.

**L52.** WHO "the fisher-girl of **the east keep**, old **Ebba**'s great-granddaughter, who keeps **the lamp on the north wall**." Three tags, and I know none of them. Only the lamp pays off (L102). Keep the lamp. Ebba is noise for a newcomer.

**L54, Merrick.**
- GOOD as a character in two lines.
- TWICE "turned his face away from every ship that burned". Whose ships, enemy or ours? What his hand on the rim means depends on which. I guessed he'd refused to see the enemy ships as living things, so touching the boat is a kind of forgiveness. If that's right, it's moving. Clarify it a hair.

**L56.** GOOD as a one-line beat.

**L58.**
- GOOD "the children parted in front of me like water before a bow."
- STOP "the two halves of **the first heart**, keyed together the way masons key stone: the first carved plank that ever spoke to us, which said *shore*, and the silent plank." I don't know what "the first heart" is, and the colon makes it stranger: a heart made of two planks? I took "keyed" to mean interlocked, which is fine.
- GOOD: the idea that the boat was built from those two planks.

**L64.** GOOD "It had gone home, and it had come home." The best line in the file. This sentence also finally explains L32's boat.

**L66.**
- GOOD, the Guest gloss. It's exactly enough, and "was killed there" pays off twice later (L92, L100).
- "Rivenmen": I learn our people's name at the very end. Fine.
- GOOD "Myststone … say it remembers everything."

**L68.**
- TWICE "Stone doesn't translate: carved stone never gave Halyna anything. But this was never only stone." OK after a beat.
- Order: "The hand that cut it learned the chisel late, and learned it well." Seren reads the carver's hand before she has described looking at the carving (that comes at L72–74). It felt out of sequence. Consider moving this paragraph after L74.
- GOOD "young by their count, I think, and seasoned by ours, because they live far longer than we do."

**L70.** GOOD, a weather beat that gives breathing room.

**L72.**
- Brief STOP "I knew the **bite**." The bite of what? The next sentence clears it.

**L74.** GOOD "It was cut so that one person could read it alone, with no need of two to know it." I got it: the carver wrote for Seren, the unbonded one. For me this is the most moving idea in the file.

**L76–83, native block.**
- STOP "written **under the round**" (fix 5).
- The italic passage itself is GOOD and very powerful once I decoded it. "Stone-folk" = us. "His sail, the red one" = the cloak. "A hand's worth" of marks = five. "Home. Family. - TWICE "Every branch that came against that stone saw it. What the branches saw went home through the roots, and the groves held it." Branches = ships, roots = the way home. I got it only because by now I was guessing the ships are trees (fix 1).

**L86–88.**
- GOOD "its last stroke is still sharp."

- GOOD "there was no other carver left." Chilling.

**L90.** GOOD. I got the extra *the* as a foreigner's tell, and "no child laughed" is tender. "whether it stops there because his hand stopped" quietly broke me.

**L92.** GOOD "We haven't earned a guest yet." It lands because L66 told me the Guest was killed.

**L94, the staff.**

- TWICE "The work is done, and when the work is done, the staff is laid down **in it**" (fix 8).
- STOP *"the stair that goes no more"* in italics signals a phrase from elsewhere, maybe a rhyme. I don't have it.

### Did I follow it? Did it move me?

**Followed: mostly, yes.** The main line is clear: the bonded pair die together, a grey boat comes back out of the fog, there's a stone in it carved by someone behind the fog in our letters with Halyna's chisel, the Captain lays down his staff, and the gate is left open. The first third (L13–36) is the hard part, a thicket of names, customs and two calendars. Once the boat lands (L48), the story moves cleanly.

**Moved: yes, strongly in places.** "Halvard went before the lamp burned out." "I say both halves alone." "It had gone home, and it had come home." "with no need of two to know it". "We stood for the same things, on the other side of it." "We haven't earned a guest yet." The weak spots are all about knowing who's who (L32, L50–52, L94), never about the feeling.

### Did I follow it? Did it move me?

## Patterns across the file

- **The "X, the Y" gloss** ("the shingle, the pebble beach below our wall"; "its stem, its front post") is mostly invisible and good. Where three or more stack in one sentence (L32, L50, L52, L98), the sentence loses its spine and I start skimming.
- **The best glosses are folded into feeling, not definition:** "because he knew, the way one of a pair always knows"; "with no need of two to know it". More of that, fewer appositives.
- **Over-explaining:** rarely boring. The only places I felt lectured were L98's chest sentence and L126–132 in the Appendix. Nowhere did I feel the writer explained a *feeling* I'd already got.
- **Under-explaining:** almost all of it is about *who and what the enemy is* (fix 1) and *who is who* among the pairs (fixes 2, 3, 9).
- **Invented words:** I enjoyed discovering that the pair-names are blends (Halyna, Orvenna). Idrenna's two members are never named, so I couldn't decode them, and that felt like a missing piece. The Throne names would gain a lot if their English titles were marked as translations.

## Reader's-side reply to the Writer's Notes questions (I didn't use them while reading)

1. *The first heart*: yes, it stopped me (L58), and the colon made it stranger. I'd want five words saying what a ship's "heart" is.
2. *The Last Carver as one of "them"*: it didn't feel like a spoiler. It felt like the point of the chapter.
3. *The Guest "was killed there"*: necessary. Without it, "We haven't earned a guest yet" and "sent kneeling" would mean nothing to me.
4. *"The same three words"*: clear, no problem.
5. *"I think that's where he took it"*: it helped. Without it I'd have had no idea where the staff went.
6. *The hood, "only the second time"*: it works on weight alone, even without knowing the first time.
7. *"Along its edge"*: I did read it as the stone's rim, not as "the writing of the fog's edge", so the note's worry is real.
