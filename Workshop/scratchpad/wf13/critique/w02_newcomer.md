# w02 · Newcomer read

**How I read it.** I read only `plain/w02.md`, as someone who has never heard of this world. I read it the way it would render: HTML comments hidden, `{{Halyna}}` shown as the name "Halyna". I looked at the Writer's Notes at the end, but I didn't use them to clear up anything that confused me in the tales. Line numbers (L##) point to the paragraph's first line.

Legend: **STOP** = I had to stop or reread. **WHO/WHY** = I didn't know who someone was or why something mattered. **HINT** = I could see it was a clue, but I couldn't work it out. **BORED** = it explained too much or repeated itself. **OK** = I read straight through. **+** = a line that worked well.

---

## The six biggest stoppers (across all four tales)

1. **"{{Halyna}}, Halvard and Rhyna, the husband and wife who lead our builders"** (all four headnotes). On the page that's three names followed by "husband and wife", which is two people. On my first read I counted three people: a woman called Halyna, plus Halvard and Rhyna. I only worked out that "Halyna" is the *pair's* name in VI.2, from "one of {{Halyna}} went in among the timbers" (L227). This is the worst stumble in the file, and it hits in the second sentence of the first headnote. Something like *"Halyna (that is Halvard and Rhyna, the husband and wife...)"* or *"Halvard and Rhyna, the husband and wife we call Halyna"* would fix it. (If the braces ever show up literally, it's worse.)
2. **Who is "I"?** Every headnote says *"After their pieces, I tell it whole."* I don't learn who "I" is until V.4's *"The facing page, in Seren's hand"* (L139), and even then I'm guessing that Seren is the "I". Nobody tells me who Seren is.
3. **One word, several meanings.** I tripped on each of these:
   - *tide* means a fleet ("the tide the roots taught", L22), a year ("the rest of that Tide", L72; "the year of the Joined Tide", L70) and the sea ("the tide worked us loose", L60).
   - *elders* means trees ("the silver elders", L87) and also old people ("The elders, the oldest of the long-lived", L97).
   - *bearer* means the lead ship (L28) and also a title in a name ("bearer of new branches", L267).
   - *iron* means cannon shot ("The iron, your shot", L32) and also axes ("Your iron, your axes", L93).
   - *green wood* means the first young fleet (L28) and also one of the "nine kinds of tree" (L185).
4. **Ships with roots, at sea.** In V.3, "into the roots, and through the roots to them" (L36) and "root to root, to the groves" (L58) stopped me cold. Do ships out on the water have roots? It's only explained two tales later, in V.6: "the roots that bind each ship to the ships around it" (L177). V.3 is the first tale, so it carries the most strangeness with the least help.
5. **The headnote repeats.** Each headnote opens with the same 80 to 100 words of *"This is one of the wood's tales... They don't read the wood..."*. The wording changes a little each time, so I had to re-read to check whether anything new was in there. By V.6 I was skimming (**BORED**). Glosses repeat inside the tales too: "the breath, the grey that you call fog" (V.3, V.4, VI.2), "your havens, your cities by the sea" (V.6, VI.2), and "the iron, your axes" twice in VI.2 alone (L245, L273).
6. **"*And no one answered.*"** It closes all four tales. Answered what? Nobody asks a question. By the fourth time it worked on me as a sad refrain: the wood speaks, and we never answer. The first time, though, I thought a line had gone missing.

## Tale by tale: did I follow it, and did it move me?

**V.3 · The Wreck That Went Home.**
- *Followed:* yes, on the main line. The ship is shot, limps home, warns its kin, comes back alone to scout, and is killed by a *different* gun that had been waiting. That last twist landed.
- *Lost me on:* roots at sea, "tide" meaning a fleet, what the fire "found", and "kept a count too".
- *Moved:* moderately, and mostly at the end: *"Your hands are on us now"* and *"We came back to look, and died looking."*
- *Main problem:* the headnote and the first paragraph in the wood's voice carry the heaviest load (Halyna's name, the bond, Ralenthae, the roots), right when I know the least. Read first, it's the hardest of the four.

**V.4 · The Council Under the Thin Sky.**
- *Followed:* fully. It's the clearest of the four for a newcomer: a sky dying because *we* cut the trees, the elders saying wait, the Guest's boat coming home with his bones, the young voting for war, and the axes stopping too late.
- *Moved:* yes, strongly. *"The grove hadn't waited long enough to see it"* and Seren's empty facing page both hurt.
- *Snags:* elders vs silver elders, the shifting "we", the unexplained "But war is...", and "one house at its edge". Seren is never introduced.

**V.6 · Of the Growing.**
- *Followed:* the big picture, yes. This tale explained the most: how ships are sung out of trees, carvings as orders passed through roots, the green wood's one word, the burned seaside cities, and *why there's a wall in the mountains*. Much of what puzzled me in V.3 cleared up here.
- *Lost me:* in the middle. The "nine grains" paragraph and the eight unnamed "Some..." temperaments read like a list, and "green wood" changed meaning there.
- *Moved:* yes, at *"that word was Burn"* and the Song's last two lines. The middle went cold.

**VI.2 · The Thrones That Walk the Sea.**
- *Followed:* yes. The pillar trees from V.4 are pulled up and turned into ten Thrones, and their leaving dissolves the fog and kills the children they were holding the sky up for. That's the strongest idea in the file, and it's told plainly.
- *Moved:* the most of the four. *"To come to you, we spent the last of our own sky"*, Vaelress made into rot, Eirlenth that no one roofed, and *"you were hard to look at. So were we."*
- *Weaker spots:* the ten-name roll is long. Rhenvael (no dead kin at its seat) and Senneir hit softest. Only three of the ten get the "Our judgement was" label.

## The invented words, from a newcomer who wanted more of them

The names were one of the pleasures of reading. By VI.2 I was decoding them from the glosses on the Throne names:

- **thae** = tide (Thaesaen "tide-heart"; Ralenthae "the tide the roots taught")
- **saen** = heart (Thaesaen; Ralensaen "root-heart")
- **ralen** = root (Ralensaen; Ralenthae)
- **vael** = grain (Rhenvael "stone-grain"; Vaelress "the grain that rots")
- **rhen** = stone; **ress** = rot
- **nael** = sky, **thar** = blood (Naelthar "blood of the sky")
- **varen** = bearer? (Leavaren "bearer of new branches"; Aelvaren the boat)

Where I wanted the English and didn't get it:

- **Naelsaen.** It's glossed as "the great black pillars", but by my decoding it's *sky-heart*, which the Song echoes ("the hearts of the grey, the anchors of the sky"). Giving me that would have been a small gift.
- **Ralenthae.** It's glossed by meaning ("the tide the roots taught"), but I only back-solved the literal *root-tide* two tales later.
- **Aelthae, Aelrhen, Aelvaren.** No meaning at all. I wanted to know what *ael* is. If it's being saved for later, the gap is fine, because I noticed it and it made me curious.
- **Esthaer, Senneir, Eirlenth, Neivaere.** Each has a meaning ("the burning", "the deep-beneath", "the long winter", "the silvered"), but nothing let me split them into parts the way the others could be split.

If the romanization showed its word-by-word English at first use (*Ralenthae*, "root-tide", the tide the roots taught), a reader like me would be decoding from V.3 instead of from VI.2.
