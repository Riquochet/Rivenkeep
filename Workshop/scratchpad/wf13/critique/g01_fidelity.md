# Fidelity critique: g01 (Title page, Contents, Of This Book, the Invocation, Book One heading, I.1 The Torn Cloak)

Checked `wf13/plain/g01.md` against `wf11/book_v3.md` (lines 1–287), the old Plain `wf11/plain_v3.md` (lines 1–251) and the primer (§1–§8).

**Verdict.** This is a sound leaf. There are no lore errors that would mislead a reader, no spoilers of later tales, and no broken markup. The fixes below are small: two places where the Plain now says something untrue (fixes 5 and 9), a few spots where the note for new readers goes past the opening tales, and some clarity gaps where the brief asks for the Book's meaning to be made plain. Each fix quotes the exact current text (it occurs once, unless noted otherwise) and gives the replacement.

## Fixes (do these)

**1. Note for new readers, paragraph 2 (line 91): the note goes past the opening tales.**
- Now: `Out of it came grey ships with no one on their decks. Over twenty months they burned the havens one after another, and nobody could understand a word from whoever sent them.`
- Change to: `Out of it came grey ships. Over twenty months they burned the havens one after another, and nobody alive ever understood a word from them.`
- Why: The empty decks are III.2's sworn moment (*No one stood on the decks. I swear to that.*). They aren't in any opening tale, and saying it here takes the weight out of III.2. *Whoever sent them* also isn't in the opening tales, and it leans on V.2's turn (*They had not picked anything. They had been sent.*). The replacement uses I.1's own fact. Word count: see fix 4.

**2. Note for new readers, paragraph 1 (line 89): the Bonded is never named.**
- Now: `The Stonewrights work in bonded pairs, two people joined as one from birth, and each pair goes by one name made from both of theirs.`
- Change to: `The Stonewrights work in bonded pairs, two people joined as one from birth, and the guild calls such pairs the Bonded. Each pair goes by one name made from both of theirs.`
- Why: The brief lists *the Bonded* for the note. I.2's title (*The Cry of the Bonded*) and Seren's *I'm not one of the Bonded myself* both use the lore name, and right now nothing ever introduces it.

**3. Note for new readers, paragraph 3 (line 93): the siege wasn't winter-only.**
- Now: `The people made their stand behind that wall, and every winter after, the grey fleets came against it.`
- Change to: `The people made their stand behind that wall, and year after year the grey fleets came against it.`
- Why: *Every winter* suggests the attacks came only in winter. Later tales have assaults in summer and spring (the three roads in the fourth summer, Pellow's death in the fifth spring). The replacement is the same length.

**4. Note for new readers, paragraph 3 (line 93): the Captain is called something else too.**
- Now: `There a soldier they call only the Captain`
- Change to: `There a soldier these tales call only the Captain`
- Why: After the Rite, the wall calls him Commander, and I.1's own when-line says so. What's actually true is that the tales never name him.
- Word count after fixes 1–4: the note comes to 346 words with its bold title, still under 350. I checked this against a full draft.

**5. Foreword, the lintel (line 105, the first of its two occurrences; the second is in the WRITER'S NOTES): the Plain now says something untrue.**
- Now: `The beginning of a book is like that. You write it last.`
- Change to: `The first page of a book is like that. It waits until enough of the book is standing to hold it up.`
- Why: Seren writes this page when the Book is *half full* (Book: *The Book is half full … Tonight have I turned back to the first leaf*). Many leaves come after it (I.5, II.2, the Epilogue, the Last Note), so *last* is false. The replacement keeps the meaning of the lintel image: the top stone is cut only once the posts are up.

**6. Foreword, the wood's tales (line 128): a newcomer meets "the wood's" tales before learning what they are.**
- Now: `Some of the tales in this Book are the wood's. The enemy's ships are made of wood,`
- Change to: `Some of the tales in this Book are the wood's own, memories the wood holds in its grain. The enemy's ships are made of wood,`
- Why: Brief item 8 says to state plainly, once, what a wood tale is. Here the reader is told only that the ships are timber, and *memory* doesn't come until two paragraphs later. This clause is H3-level and gives away nothing.

**7. Foreword, "known" (line 132): the line can be read the wrong way round.**
- Now: `Nobody reads the wood. It's *known*.`
- Change to: `The wood isn't read, the way you'd read a page. It's *known*.`
- Why: *Nobody reads the wood* sounds like "nobody can get anything from it", which is the opposite of the point. The Book's line is *The wood is not read but known*.

**8. Foreword, Seren's palm (line 148): make the Book's meaning plain.**
- Now: `Once I laid my own palm on the grain, and I let go.`
- Change to: `Once I laid my own palm on the grain. What came up into it was more than I could hold, and I let go.`
- Why: A newcomer can't tell why she let go, so *Mine is the hand that writes, not the hand that knows* has nothing to rest on. The Book's meaning (V.1, H3: *Something came up into it out of the wood, too great to hold … I let go.*) is something the hearth already knows. The replacement doesn't use V.1's *river in flood* picture, and it doesn't hint that a knowing takes two (H7).

**9. The Invocation (line 185): the Plain now says something untrue.**
- Now: `Some are the wood's, the memories held in the grain of the enemy's ships,`
- Change to: `Some are the wood's, the memories held in the grain of the enemy's wood,`
- Why: Not every wood tale comes from a ship. II.2 and IV.4 were known from the Guest's gift, the *dark stone* (see their Book headnotes), and the foreword itself says only *most* come from wreck-wood. *The enemy's wood* is the primer's own phrase (§1.6), and it stays accurate for every leaf.

**10. I.1, the shouting (line 215): the gloss buries the Book's "though".**
- Now: `burned our cities one after another, and nobody alive had ever understood a single word from them.`
- Change to: `burned our cities one after another. But nobody alive had ever understood a single word from them.`
- Why: The Book says *beg terms of the grey sails, though no man living ever understood one word they spake*. The point is that begging for peace from an enemy nobody can talk to is hopeless. Joined with *and*, it reads as one more fact about the fleet.

**11. I.1, the roll of the Twelve (line 256): the added sentence contradicts Kael.**
- Now: `Each of us goes by the place he came from, and most of those places were havens, our ten cities on the coast.`
- Change to: `Each of the others goes by the place he came from, and most of those places were havens, our ten cities on the coast.`
- Why: Two lines later Kael goes by *the Counter* and says *I come from no haven*. So *each of us* isn't true of him. (This sentence was added in the Plain. The Book doesn't have it.)

## Judgement calls (optional; the orchestrator decides)

**12. Title page (line 5): use the Book's word "laid".**
- Now: `*The tales told around the Long Hearth of Rivenkeep, as Seren Two-Inks wrote them down, retold here in plain words.*`
- Option: `*The tales laid at the Long Hearth of Rivenkeep, as Seren Two-Inks wrote them down, retold here in plain words.*`
- Why: The foreword insists *we don't say it was told. We say it was laid*, and the note explains *laid* a page later. The case for keeping *told* is that the reader meets it first, before anything has been explained.

**13. Contents, II.1 (line 29): the founders' leaf is a slate.**
- Now: `*told by Seren Two-Inks, with the founders' own page*`
- Option: `*told by Seren Two-Inks, with the founders' own words, cut on slate*`
- Why: The Book's II.1 says *Its first leaf {{Aldwena}} cut on slate*, and *page* suggests paper. The line still begins `- II.1 · `.

**14. I.1 song, line 1 (line 278): its meaning has shifted.**
- Now: `> *The gate was broken. The lords had run.*  `
- Option: `> *The gate was broken. The lords were scattered.*  `
- Why: The Book's verse and Kael's own prose both say *scattered*, and *had run* adds cowardice. Primer §1.7 explicitly tells writers to keep the old Plain's verse, so this is the orchestrator's call. I'm flagging it only because the song's meaning has moved.

**15. Foreword, Wendhessa (line 130): the writer asked for a ruling.**
- *They were the pair who kept the guild's records* hints softly that they're gone. The hint is tolerable, because Seren's *I carried … a chest* already implies the chest changed hands, and the Book's own past tense (*that bare the chest out*) does the same. No change is needed. If the team wants it neutral, change `in a bundle sealed with the mark of {{Wendhessa}}. They were the pair who kept the guild's records at Eldhythe, our oldest city, and they carried this chest out when Eldhythe fell.` to `in a bundle sealed with the mark of {{Wendhessa}}, the guild's record-keepers at Eldhythe, our oldest city. They carried this chest out when Eldhythe fell.`

**16. Foreword (line 130): readability.**
- `Then {{Idrenna}} gave leave.` could become `Then {{Idrenna}} gave their permission.` *Leave* in this sense is formal for the twelve-year-old reader.

**17. PROLOGUE EXCERPT (line 230): "the road" for a first-launch player.**
- `before the road wore it down` could become `before the long road here wore it down`. The excerpt plays alone, and nothing inside it says what *the road* is. That makes it 358 words, still under 380.
