# Fidelity critique: g02 (I.2 The Cry of the Bonded, I.3 The Captain's Long Breath)

Checked `wf13/plain/g02.md` against `wf11/book_v3.md` (I.2 lines 289–393, I.3 lines 395–475, and V.1 for the plank), the primer `wf13/plain_primer.md` (§1.7 echoes, §2 H2/H4, §3 lexicon, §4 mysteries, §7 watch-points), and the old Plain `wf11/plain_v3.md`.

**Overall.** The draft is faithful. I checked every beat of both tales against the Book, and only one is missing (fix 3). All names, numbers and tellers are kept. The fixed lines are word for word: the cry, *Rise! Rise! Rise!*, the creed, Jack's Aetherbonded formula after a colon, the runners' oath, *Hold the stone / Hold it, and it will hold you*, *The last of the light caught on their two chisels.*, *It's only a rhyme.*, *I ran it.*, *I don't know what the Captain thought. I know what he did.*, and Kael's correction in the echo wording. The markup matches the old Plain token for token. I compared headings, the two HALYNA pairs, `{{Halyna}}` ×3 + `{{Idrenna}}` ×2 + `{{Orvenna}}` ×2 in I.2, and `{{Halyna}}` ×1 in I.3, and found no native blocks, chips or blots in either. There is no singular *they*, no semicolon and no old word. (*Yet* appears only in the sense *not yet*.) There is no custom of burning, because the *soldiers' fires* are given no reason. Nothing from a later step leaks: the boy has no name, cradle or cause of death, Jory and Wick are alive with no hint, the rhyme is only *a harbour rhyme from long ago*, the stretched hour is left unsettled, and the late hull's meaning is untold. The fixes below are ordered by weight. Fixes 1–4 matter for fidelity. Fixes 5–10 are small accuracy or clarity repairs.

## Fixes

1. **I.3, line 179: an added sentence breaks Hale's beat and points to another tale.** Delete the whole paragraph *It was the first carved plank the sea ever brought us, and what came of it is Seren's tale.* The Book has no such line. It sits between the fixed echo *I ran it.* and Hesk's lamp, and the Book's rhythm there is bare: "Marks on the wood." / I ran it. / That night I found my way back… *What came of it is Seren's tale* also points the reader to another tale, which goes against §1.10, trap 11 and §1.3, *keep the plant a plant*. In Book order V.1 hasn't been read yet. Hale himself has sworn to carry *nothing else*. If the context is wanted, fold it into line 173 as a plain fact, which the hearth has known since H2: change *In the shallows I saw marks cut into a plank.* to **In the shallows I saw marks cut into a plank, the first carved plank the sea had ever brought us.** Say nothing about what came of it.

2. **I.3, line 123: Kael's line misreads as an idiom.** Change *"Nothing can change his mind but his own voice at my side, lad, and a man can only stand in one place."* to **"Nothing changes those orders but his own voice, here at my side, lad, and a man can only stand in one place."** In the Book, *that mind* means the orders the Captain has just given Kael (*The Captain gave Kael his mind*). In modern English, *change his mind* means the Captain reconsidering, and *his own voice changes his mind* doesn't make sense. This line sets up the whole tale: only the Captain, standing in person at the guns, can alter what they do, and so he has to walk there while the hour stretches.

3. **I.2, line 88: Enno and Della's split of each line is dropped (a lost beat).** Change *Enno and Della stood up and led it between them.* to **Enno and Della stood up and took the lead between them, one of them singing each line up to the comma and the other singing the rest.** The Book says: *took the first voice between them, the one to the comma and the other the rest.* Don't say which of them took which half. The beat has to be explicit, because Seren's *with no one beside me, I sang the rest* depends on it. The pair shared each line, and she sang both halves alone. The general sentence before it (*one singer takes each line…*) describes the song, not what Enno and Della did.

4. **I.2, line 29: "might … have to" weakens the Book's "must".** Change *that walls built for peace might in the end have to be held with blood* to **that walls built for peace must in the end be held with blood**. The Book says *must at the last be held with blood*. The doubt belongs to the next sentence, *And if that was so*, which is already there.

5. **I.2, line 19: "it" points to the wrong thing.** Change *We call it that because of what happened that evening, when we'd only just reached the Keep.* to **We call that evening the day of the banner because of what happened when we'd only just reached the Keep.** As written, *it* reads as *the Keep's old courtyard*, which is the nearest noun.

6. **I.2, headnote (line 5): the grammar slips, and *hearth* is used before it's explained.** Replace the headnote with:
   **\*This tale is my own, Seren's. I write this Book, and this was the first tale I ever laid. We say a tale is "laid," like a stone in a wall, because a laid tale stays. Whoever holds the old gate-stone by the fire is the one who tells. This hearth, the great fireplace in the Keep's hall where we tell these tales, had heard only one tale before mine. On the day I'm telling about, I was twelve years old, one of the guild's children.\***
   *Seren's, who writes this Book* doesn't parse. *This hearth* first appears here, but the draft explains it only at line 63. Since §1.1 says not to explain a thing twice in one tale, trim line 63 to **That night this hearth, where you're sitting now, had its first fire in more years than anyone could count.** If Jack wants it, *before mine* may become *before mine, Kael's tale of the torn cloak*, which has been known since H1. That's optional, because it pushes the headnote past 70 words.

7. **I.2, line 65: "old" is invented lore.** Change *\*Aetherbonded\* is the guild's old word for its bonded pairs, who work as one.* to **\*Aetherbonded\* is the guild's word for its bonded pairs, who work as one.** Neither the Book nor the lexicon says the word is old (§1.2: *never invent … a history*). The rest of the gloss is right, and correctly leaves God's bond until H7.

8. **I.3, line 183: "the old builders' ceremony" is ambiguous.** Change *a gift of the Rite, the old builders' ceremony that made the Captain our Commander* to **a gift of the Rite of the Cornerstone, the first builders' ceremony that made the Captain our Commander**. *Old builders* can be read as the elderly builders, since {{Idrenna}}, the oldest pair, performed it. Per the lexicon, the Rite was the founders' ceremony, found on a slate in the black chest. This also gives the full name once, as I.1's when-line does.

9. **I.2, line 65: "the whole of it" is vague.** Change *Then {{Idrenna}} gave the Captain the whole of it in two halves, one of them saying each:* to **Then {{Idrenna}} put the whole bargain to the Captain in two halves, one of them saying each:**. The Book's *it* means the terms. *The whole of it* on its own could mean anything.

10. **Small clarity points in I.3 (optional, but cheap).**
    a. Headnote: *Hale told his standing up* → **Hale told it standing up**.
    b. Line 139: *Kael's lookout saw a hull through a far-glass* → **Kael's lookout saw an enemy hull through a far-glass**. *Hull* means a ship (lexicon §3.6), and *enemy* makes Kael's three words, *Hull hangs back*, clear on first reading.

## Checked and left as they are

- **I.3, line 159: *Then I had time to stop being afraid.*** This interprets the Book's *Then I had time to stop.* It's the natural reading, and it doesn't settle whether the hour really stretched (§4, item 17). Keep it.
- **I.2: the masons' chant, *while the sea is empty*.** This is correct, because the Book's *lies bare* means empty of sails (lexicon: *building while the sea is empty*).
- ***Two at the Gate*, line 3: the added comma.** It's harmless, and it makes *up to the comma* work for every line. The meaning of all four lines is right, and the old Plain's verse is kept as §1.7 asks.
- **I.2: *Even the guild's children come in pairs* / *with both of them still living* / *He never reached the Keep*.** This is the Book's own implication, and it's what §7 asks for. It gives no name, cradle or cause of death.
- **I.2: *Her family had laid that very gate long ago, when the Keep was first built*.** This is the Book's own statement in I.2, made plain. It's correct, since Aldwena, of the First Days, laid it.
- **I.3: *The smoke … so he couldn't see it* and *carried word of them*.** Both are faithful readings, and V.1 confirms them: Hale came up past the fires to {{Halyna}}, who then went down to the plank.
- **The when-lines** both use the primer's §7 wording. In I.2 the added house gloss of the Keep is fine.
- **Before merging:** strip the `## WRITER'S NOTES` section (lines 191–256). It isn't part of the Plain.
