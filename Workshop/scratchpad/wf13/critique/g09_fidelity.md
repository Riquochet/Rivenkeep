## Fixes

1. **V.7 Part I, line 25: *Harm to one is harm to both* is set in the very tale where Seren won't write what she thought about *Ael*.** Change *Bonded pairs are seldom apart, and never far apart. Harm to one is harm to both, the old masters said, and when one of a pair dies, the other dies too. A mile of wall is far.* to **Bonded pairs are seldom apart, and never far apart. When one of a pair dies, the other dies too. A mile of wall is far.**

   Why it matters:
   - In IV.4 (Book line 1470), the *Ael'thar* rite is the laying together of two arms *that harm to the one should be harm to both*.
   - Fifty lines later in this same Part (line 80), Seren sets *Aelthae* beside *Ael'thar* and refuses to say what she thought.
   - With the Bonded's saying planted just before, the Plain lays down the bridge between the guild's bond and the wood's word *ael*. That is exactly the kinship the Book keeps dark (§4, item 2).
   - The Book's V.7 doesn't use the saying, and neither did the old Plain.
   - The writer's own note 2 says no *harm to one* stands near *Ael'thar*, but this line is in the same Part.

   The law of dying together (H7) is enough to give a newcomer the dread the Book's reader feels.

2. **VI.1 headnote, line 197: *each man at his own city's fire* is wrong for Rimewatch, and *the stone* is never glossed.**

   - Kael tells Rimewatch for Hesk. He says himself (line 321) that he comes from no haven, and he opens that telling with *I don't tell other men's cities*. The Book says only *at each haven's fire*.
   - Line 199 opens *Give me the stone.* A newcomer reading VI.1 alone doesn't know what the stone is.

   Replace the first two sentences of the headnote with:

   > **The True Men, the Captain's own company of twelve, laid this tale at each city's own fire, one man for each city, and I wrote each part down right there beside him. (At our hearth, whoever tells a tale holds the old gate-stone, and we say a tale is laid, like a stone in a wall, because a laid tale stays.)**

   Keep the rest as it is.

3. **VI.1 Emberhythe, line 261: Harl's line has become a standing habit of burning, taken from a later night.**

   - *Of their wood, I always said one thing: "Burn it."* turns Harl's temper into a saying. §1.9 is plain: *Never write a saying, rite or habit of burning.*
   - *Always* comes from VI.3 (Book line 2643, *he had said of every plank of the war*). VI.3 is H34, a later night.
   - The Book already has the exact fact this payoff needs. In III.2 (line 1056), when Emberhythe fell, the mason carried the charred plank out, and Harl *told him to burn it*. That is this same plank, so *I didn't burn it* lands on it directly.

   Change the last two sentences of the paragraph, *He'd carried that scorched piece of their wood out of Emberhythe when it fell, and when its carving was known at last, it said one word: *Burn*. Of their wood, I always said one thing: "Burn it."*, to:

   > **He'd carried that scorched piece of their wood out of Emberhythe when it fell, and I told him then to burn it. When its carving was known at last, it said one word: *Burn*.**

4. **V.7 Part I, line 39: Hale is not *our fastest runner*.** Change *Hale, our fastest runner, came at a run* to **Hale, one of our runners, who carry word along the wall to the Captain, came at a run**.

   - In I.3 (Book line 407), Wick is *the youngest of us and the swiftest*.
   - The Book never calls Hale the fastest. The primer's who's-who entry (*the fastest on the wall*) has no source in the Book, and where they differ the Book wins.
   - This also gives Part I its gloss of *runner*. *Never before had a runner come too late* depends on that gloss, and until now it came only in Part II.

5. **V.7 Part I, line 55: a rule for the strips is stated that the Book never gives.** Change *Then the Captain, who leads us, tied a strip on a pike over them, cut from the torn half of the cloak he wears. He ties one over each of our dead, and this was his first for Stonewrights.* to:

   > **Then the Captain, who leads us, tied a strip on a pike over them, cut from the unwritten half of his torn cloak, the half he still wears. It was the first strip he had tied for Stonewrights.**

   - The lexicon is explicit (§3.4, *the half-cloak · the strips*): *The Book gives no rule for who gets a strip, so don't state one.* *He ties one over each of our dead* is such a rule. The Book says only *his first for Stonewrights*, which already implies the earlier strips.
   - *the torn half* is unclear, because both halves are torn. *The unwritten half* is the lexicon's term for the half he wears.

6. **V.7 headnote, line 7: the lamp is put *beside the old gate-stone*.** The Book says only *and it is this lamp*. Placing it beside the stone is invented. Replace the headnote's last three sentences with:

   > **(At our hearth, whoever tells a tale holds the old gate-stone, and we say a tale is laid, like a stone in a wall, because a laid tale stays.) All three nights I held the lamp down on the shingle, the pebble beach below our wall. It's this same lamp, the one here tonight.**

   The stone stays glossed for *Hold the stone, Seren.*, in the same parenthesis the other Seren headnotes use (g04, g06, g07, g08).

7. **VI.1 Highreach, line 305: *All through the war* is invented.** Change *All through the war, the wind came a day ahead of their sails, with the smell of the grey on it.* to **When Highreach fell, the wind came a whole day ahead of their sails, and I smelled the grey on it.**

   This is the Book's own fact, from III.2 line 1078 (*A whole day ahead of them I smelled the grey on it*), and it sets up *It brought no sails* just as well. The Book never says the wind did this all through the siege.

8. **V.7 Part II, line 96: *When the firing was done* loses the Book's point.** Change it to **When his own guns had fired**. The Book says *When his guns had spoken*. It is Pellow's own volley that he looks past, and it shows his battery to the hunters. That is the cost the tale turns on.

9. **V.7 Part II, lines 96–100: the reason behind *"They are not green."* is still missing.** After *like a man's face.* (line 96), add **The ships of our first winter had never done that.** Leave line 100 as it is.

   - A newcomer is told that sails go pale and then that this means *not young wood*, but nothing joins the two.
   - The hearth knows the join that night. The first winter's ships came each alone (H9), and the answering sail was first seen in the Second Tide (H15, Book line 1990).
   - It is the meaning of Pellow's own line inside the tale, so §1.3 says to make it plain.
   - This doesn't borrow V.5 Count V's *a green hull that blanched was no green hull*, which is laid later (H29).

10. **V.7 Part I, line 63: *only they can know the wood* is stronger than the Book.** Change it to **and they're the ones who know the wood for us**. The Book of Knowings (line 2847) says *the rest the wood giveth only to the Bonded*. The gift belongs to bonded pairs, and in practice Halyna are the ones who do it. In V.1, Enno and Della also laid their four hands on the plank. *Only they can know* claims more than the Book does.

11. **V.7 Part III, line 152: *a whole tomorrow* is left as a figure.** Change *It had a whole tomorrow carved into its heart, the core of its wood, where a fleet's orders are cut.* to **It had a whole tomorrow carved into its heart, the core of its wood, where a fleet's orders are cut: the plan for the whole of the next day.**

    The primer's watch-point for Part III lists *a whole day's plan in one heart* as a stumble. That the bearer's heart carves the next day's orders is H24, so it is safe at H30. It also makes Halyna's closing *They carve a tomorrow now* and *build a tomorrow they didn't carve for* readable.

12. **VI.1 Rimewatch, line 283: *he used to say* makes Hesk's line a habit.** Change *"Someone has to keep a light," he used to say.* to **"Someone has to keep a light," he told us once at our hearth.** In the Book, Hesk says it once, as the last line of his telling of Rimewatch's fall (III.2, line 1074). The fisher-girl's echo works because it is that line. This fix is optional but closer to the Book.

13. **Optional, VI.1 Glasspire, line 313.** After *It was his city.* add **He swore at our hearth that his would be the first eye on it.** This is Pellow's oath from III.1 (line 990). It makes *Pellow should have seen it first* land the way Corlen's oath makes *Corlen should have* land. It adds 12 words, so skip it if length matters more.
