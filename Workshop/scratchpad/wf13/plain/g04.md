## BOOK TWO · THE BOOK OF THE BUILDERS

*Who we were, and who they were.*

### II.1 · Of the Keep That Was Forgotten

*Told in the first summer of the siege, while the enemy's grey ships came at our wall from the sea. It tells how the Keep was built, long ago, and how it was forgotten.*

*Laid by me, Seren Two-Inks, who writes this Book. At our hearth, whoever tells a tale holds the old stone in the niche by the fire, both hands resting on it, and we say a tale is laid, like a stone in a wall, because a laid tale stays. One page inside this tale belongs to the founders, the first builders of the Keep, and not to me. {{Halyna}} read it to me from the founders' old writing, and I haven't dared to change a word. {{Halyna}} are Halvard and Rhyna, the husband and wife who lead our builders. They're a bonded pair, bound as one from birth, so they share one name.*

Hold the stone, Seren.

This is the history of the Keep, the old fortress on this mountain ridge. It tells how the founders kept it, and how we, their children, didn't.

Its first page comes from a slate that {{Aldwena}}, a bonded pair of the founders, cut in the First Days, long before our cities stood on the coast. Their letters are no bigger than grains of barley.

> We two, who laid the gate, wrote this down.
>
> We came up onto the ridge and found it split from top to bottom, as if a great blade had come down on it. In the split we built a fortress out of the ridge's own stone. The wardens of the quarry cut that stone. The first of them cut his name into the stone over the western stair, and left the stone below it blank, for his sons' names.
>
> With our four hands we two laid the capstone of the inner gate. Under it we cut our mark, and our vow beside it, where no eye will see it again until the wall falls.
>
> This wall will stand. We two laid it, and we two answer for it, as long as stone is stone. It stands.

That mark and that vow are under this very stone, the one my hands are resting on. I can't turn it over, because that takes two people. But late at night, when no one is at the fire, I've slipped my hand underneath it and traced their mark with one finger.

The masters of our guild of builders call a mark like that a Stonwryt: *a vow sealed in living rock, earned by toil and skill*. It's a bonded pair's own promise, cut into stone, and no hands but theirs may cut it. No gold can buy one. But a mark could make gold trusted. The master pairs of the havens, our ten cities along the coast, stamped their marks on coins, and people trusted the coins as they trusted the marks: *trust, sealed in stone*. I have no mark. A mark belongs to a pair, and I'm not one of a pair. I'll never cut one.

Over the western stair, under the first warden's name, his sons cut theirs, each one beneath his father's, and below the last name the stone is smooth. Halvard Stone-Warden comes from that line, and Rhyna of the Inner Gate comes from {{Aldwena}}'s. So the two families were already joined in this very gate: Rhyna's forebears laid it, and Halvard's cut its stone out of the mountain. That was long before those two lay in one cradle as a bonded pair.

Then the people went down to the warm harbours on the coast, and the guild went after them. Before they left, the founders laid the Keep's slates, the writings they had cut, in a chest of black stone, the way masons put away their tools at dusk. With the slates they laid the Keep's first staff, the rod of the master who oversaw the building. We found that chest under this hall in our first nights here. On the coast they built Eldhythe, which people call the Eldest. But it's only the eldest of the havens. The Keep is older.

No slate gives the names of the last ones to leave. Behind them, the wind blew in under a gate that nobody kept watch over any more.

The Keep had never fallen to an enemy. It had only been left behind.

People forgot it, until only the old songs remembered it. No pair ever came back up to *point* its mortar, to renew it where it wears away between the stones. I love that word. So the rain found every joint. After the rain came the frost, and after the frost, the ice.

In some winter that no one wrote down, the capstone of the inner gate, the great stone laid across the top of it, came down off its two posts.

No one heard it fall.

But the whole coast heard the havens fall. That was the Fall: twenty months in which grey ships came out of the fog at sea and burned our ten cities one after another. After Glasspire, the last of them, we climbed up here to the one place the songs said would hold, and found only a ruin, the shadow of a fortress.

Forgetting had done to it what no enemy ever could.

It was dusk on the day of the banner. That's what we call the evening we reached the Keep, deep in the winter, when our Captain tore his cloak in two to make our banner. Halvard and Rhyna came in under the inner gate, with all of us in a long column at their backs. I came close behind them with the water-skin. I was twelve, and I didn't know their names.

The capstone lay in the gateway. Halvard swept the snow off it with his sleeve. On its top face the bloom had grown, as pale as frost on a gatepost. Bloom is the crust that creeps over worked stone where no one comes.

They knelt down in the snow and put their four hands under it. They turned it over the way the fishermen of Tidesmeet, one of our havens, turn over a boat they gave up for lost long ago.

It was their own.

On its underside were the mark and the vow of {{Aldwena}}, as sharp as on the day they were cut. Next to them were the bites of the wardens' chisels, left when they cut the stone out of the mountain. Rhyna laid her fingers on the mark of her forebears. Halvard set his thumb in a chisel-bite his fathers had left.

The stone was whole. Out in the ward, the courtyard inside the walls, people were still coming in. Their feet made no sound on the snow.

The stone had kept its vow. We were the ones who didn't keep ours.

Before the last light had gone from the snow, Halvard and Rhyna cried out to the guild to rise and build, and it rose. Until then our builders had sat in the long grass poking up through the snow, chisels in their laps, saying nothing.

Two weeks later came the Rite of the Cornerstone, the ceremony that made the Captain our Commander. When it was done, a new capstone went up into the gate. The True Men, the Captain's own company of twelve, carried the old stone into this hall and set it in the niche in the wall beside the hearth.

This is that stone. Whoever holds it holds the vow of the first builders, and the shame of their children, who forgot it. Tonight it's under my two hands.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

### II.3 · Of the Bonded and the Theoliths

*Told at midsummer in the first year of the siege, on the night of a sealing, our guild's wedding of a bonded pair.*

*That morning {{Idrenna}}, the oldest pair in our guild, had sealed Enno and Della under the inner gate. It was the first sealing at the Keep, our old mountain fortress, since its first builders left it long ago, and I held the lamp. That night Halvard and Rhyna, the husband and wife who lead our builders, told this to the children at the Long Hearth, the great fireplace in our hall. They're a bonded pair, two people bound as one from birth, so we call them by one name, {{Halyna}}. (Whoever tells a tale at our hearth holds the old gate-stone, and we say a tale is laid, like a stone in a wall, because a laid tale stays.) This time they wanted me to write down whose words were whose, because children like to know.*

<!-- HALYNA -->

*They laid their hands on the stone together and began, one of them starting and the other finishing:*

"We hold the stone together,"

"the way we hold everything."

<!-- MARKED -->

**Rhyna.** Come closer, all of you, where the fire can see your faces. The children get the front, and the old can listen too, if they're quiet. This morning you watched Enno and Della kneel down as two and stand up as one. Now they are {{Enrella}}, one name for the two of them.

*A small girl asked them what it felt like. "It is like being one person," said Enno, and Della finished it for him: "with four hands and no excuses." Every bonded pair has a mark of its own, and it's cut under the last stone of a finished work. But Enno and Della said they'd build a house with one door and cut their mark under its first stone, because they couldn't wait for the last. The whole hearth laughed. Then the girl turned to me and asked when I would be sealed. I had no answer, and I went on writing.*

**Halvard.** Before we took the name Rivenmen here at the Keep, our people were called Shorelanders. Not every Shorelander was a Stonewright, but every Stonewright was a Shorelander. The guild of the Stonewrights, our master builders, calls its children while they're still in the cradle, from any home at all. Once, the records say, it even called a child from a jailer's house in Carnhold, the forge-town. No mother or father ever said no, because the guild looked after our people's faith as well as their walls.

**Rhyna.** We believe God made each pair one before either of them drew breath. So the seers go among the cradles with a lamp and bend down low, and they read what was written before we were born. They don't choose. They only read. That bond, made by God and read at the cradle, is what we call the Aetherbond. A seer bent over his cradle and mine like that, and from that night on we slept in one cradle, at his mother's house and at mine by turns. In our whole lives we two have never spent a night under different roofs. Remember this, because you'll need it: the bond is made before the cradle, read in the cradle, and made whole only at the sealing.

**Halvard.** Our teachers were {{Idrenna}}, who are sitting by the door tonight. The guild calls such teachers Theoliths: the old master pair who raise a young pair and teach them. They taught us to cut stone, to lay it and to set it. They taught us to pray, and to keep the guild's records in the old hand, the writing of the first builders. They told us the name of our vow, the Covenant of Completion, and nothing more, and we'll tell you nothing more either. You'll understand it when it's yours to understand. {{Idrenna}} will nod when we two get this right.

*By the door, {{Idrenna}} sat with their two white heads close together. They didn't nod.*

**Rhyna.** At a sealing, the guild says out loud what the cradle said first. The masters give the two of them one name, made from both their names, the way one gate is made from two posts and the stone laid across them. With the name, they give the pair their mark, which the seers read at the cradle too. That's a name for the mouth, and a mark for the stone. We call that mark the Stonwryt. No hands but the pair's own may cut it, and no other pair will ever break it. That's how we two were sealed and named {{Halyna}}. He is my husband and I am his wife, but we were one pair long before we were either.

*Halvard leaned toward the hearth, and the children leaned with him.*

**Halvard.** The ash is soft tonight. Watch my finger, because I'll only draw this once. This is our mark. On the night of the Rite, when the Captain was made our Commander, we two cut it on the underside of the new capstone, the stone across the top of the inner gate. It lies face down there, where no eye will see it again until the wall falls. Look once.

*No child moved. In the fire a coal settled.*

**Rhyna.** Now I sweep it away with my hand, and it's gone. Ash is for the wind. A mark is for stone, and stone holds.

**Halvard.** You've seen the two of us on the wall in the holding, the hours when the enemy's grey ships come in from the sea and the wall has to be held. We work as one, and never need a word between us. The old masters counted the work of one pair as the work of twelve. I'm Elder Halvard and she's Elderess Rhyna, and neither of us is the elder of the other. There was never a stone she laid second.

*A boy at the front counted to twelve on his fingers, and ran out of fingers.*

**Rhyna.** If I cut my hand in the south tower, he feels it on the north wall, and he can tell you which hand. The old masters had a saying for it. Harm to one is harm to both.

**Halvard.** The Bonded, as we call all such pairs, are seldom apart, and never far apart. In all the guild's records, no bond was ever broken. We put it this way: Separation, God did not permit.

*The fire had burned down a finger's width. Enno and Della sat hand in hand, and the small girl had fallen asleep against Della's knee.*

**Rhyna.** So the Bonded die together, and we two aren't afraid of it. If one of a pair is killed, the other has no wound and no sickness. But the heart breaks, because it knows, and the body goes still, because its work is done. People say the longest anyone has ever lived on alone is eight days. But we Stonewrights keep the records, and our records say hours. Many pairs have drawn their last breath at the same moment, a mile apart. Each knew the other was going, and so each went too. When the left hand dies, does not the right one die too?

**Halvard.** One last thing, and then bed. Sometimes one of the two children in a cradle died before the sealing. Because the bond wasn't whole yet, the other one lived. We call that child unbonded, and the guild gives the child to a bonded household as its third. The unbonded keep only one name, and among the guild a single name is a mourning, because everyone who hears it knows someone is missing. But the old masters said this: God does not take the one without leaving the other a work to do.

**Rhyna.** Seren, who held the lamp this morning and is writing all this down, is our third. She lay in one cradle with Tarnel, a boatwright's son from Tidesmeet, one of our cities on the coast. All along the Road, our long march over the mountains to this Keep, he knotted a net as he walked, so he could fish the mountain lakes when we came to them. He taught her his knot. On the night of the pass, the coldest night of the Road, the cold took many of the children, and some of the old. Tarnel and Seren lay down together under one cloak. In the morning she woke up beside him. The cold had taken him in the night.

**Halvard.** So she lived, and she is one alone. Tarnel told her once, "A net is only knots holding hands," and this Book is bound with his knot. When we two lay our hands on the carved wood of the enemy's wrecks, which the sea washes up under our wall, the marks carved in it come into us whole. We know them the way you know a thing you remember, without reading it. We call that a knowing, and it takes two to hold one, the way it takes two hands to lift a stone that one hand can't. So Seren can't know the wood. But she won't die of anyone else's death. She can go alone where we two can't, and carry this Book on after us. Every scribe before her was one of a pair, and when a pair died, their book was shut away in the vault after them. A book that nobody reads aloud is only ink. Look, her pen hasn't stopped. This Book has to outlive the people who tell it. That's why Seren writes it.

*My pen had stopped when Rhyna told of the cloak. I dipped it, and wrote on. By the door, {{Idrenna}} nodded at last.*

*Together, {{Halyna}} ended it with the oldest saying of the guild:*

Birth united them; death could not separate them.

We have laid this tale the way it was laid for us.

<!-- HALYNA END -->

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

### Declined, and why

- **Fidelity fix 3's *It's that same capstone, the great stone that once lay across the top of the inner gate*.** The newcomer found that the explicit sentence spent the closing *This is that stone*, and that the Book's bare *That mark and that vow are under this very stone* was already enough to know it. The fidelity concerns are all met another way. The scale is right (*resting on*). *Two* is *two people*. The capstone is glossed in Seren's voice, at the fall. And the stone's identity is plain from the first sentence of the paragraph.
- **Explaining *Two-Inks*.** The newcomer enjoyed it as a hook, and it didn't stop the reading. Its meaning (the two inks of Halyna's wood tales) would bring wood-tale lore into a headnote that is already the longest in Book Two. II.3's *This time they wanted me to write down whose words were whose* hints at the custom without explaining it.
- **Why a *new* capstone if the old one was whole.** The Book gives no reason, and the primer forbids inventing one.
- **What *break* means in *no other pair will ever break it*.** The Book doesn't say whether it means damaging the mark or breaking a seal made with it. A gloss would choose for the Book, and *seal* would collide with *sealing* in the same speech.
- **Why the people left the Keep, who the grey ships are, the Captain's name, the Covenant's meaning, what became of the wardens' line, and the *house with one door*.** All are mysteries or plants (primer §4, and the H6/H7 clock). The newcomer marked them as *made me want to read on*.
- ***Hold the stone, Seren.* is kept bare**, as the primer's echo table asks (it's said in full only in I.4). The headnote now explains holding the stone, which is what the newcomer needed.
- ***Forgetting had done to it what no enemy ever could.*** stays. The newcomer called it *slightly on the nose … but it lands*, and it's a Book beat.
- **More room for the Theoliths.** The Book gives them one speech and the nod, and adding any would be new matter.
