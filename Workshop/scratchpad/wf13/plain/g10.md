### VI.3 · The Knowing of the Green Wood

*Told in the sixth autumn since we came to the Keep, our mountain fortress, on the night the boat went out.*

*I, Seren Two-Inks, who write this Book, laid this tale, and I held the lamp that night. (We say a tale is laid, like a stone in a wall, because it stays, and every teller ends one with the same words.) The teller holds the old gate-stone by our hearth. Tonight {{Halyna}} held it with me: Halvard and Rhyna, the husband and wife who lead our builders. They're a bonded pair, joined as one from birth, so we call them by one name, and I write their words in two inks, one for each. Each laid a hand beside mine, and we held it a long time before any of us spoke. Then I told myself what I always do:*

<!-- TOKEN {LAST_THRONE_AT_HAVEN} -->

Hold the stone, Seren.

All that day the tenth Throne lay where it had fallen, black-grained and split open, a hill of timber taller than a harbour-house.

The Thrones were the enemy's greatest ships. Our enemy is the people of the wood, a forest behind the grey, the great fog bank out at sea. They grew the Thrones out of the oldest black pillars, the last giant trees still standing at the fog's edge after our grandparents' axemen cut the rest for timber. At the end of last winter, ten Thrones came out of the grey and settled in the ruins of our ten cities, which the enemy had burned years before, one Throne in each. This year we'd taken our cities back, one by one, and this Throne was the last: {LAST_THRONE_AT_HAVEN}. The soldiers wouldn't go near it, because even dead it frightened them.

Since spring {{Halyna}} had known the hearts of nine Thrones. A ship's heart is the oldest wood at its core, where its orders are carved. Only {{Halyna}} can know carved wood. When they touch it, what its grain holds comes into them whole, like a memory, and we call that a knowing. All nine hearts had been dead wood.

Toward evening the Captain, who leads us, came down to it himself and stood beside it a long time with his hood up. Then he looked at {{Halyna}}.

At dusk they went in, and I followed them with the lamp. Water dripped from the timbers overhead. Deep in the oldest part of the grain lay the heart, grey and hard and still.

Beside it, in a crack in the heartwood, lay a sliver no longer than a hand.

It was green.

I brought the lamp down close to it, because I didn't trust the light.

For six winters we'd searched the wrecks for orders, and the ships' hearts for their names, and the fog for sails. Not one of us had ever looked for anything alive. But this had been living in the dark of a dead heart, the way a seedling lives in the crack of a wall.

Since the first winter {{Halyna}} had needed only one hand between them on any wood. All the same, this time they each put out a hand and touched it at the same moment.

A drop fell from the timbers into the lamp's flame and hissed.

Their two faces changed together.

It wasn't an order, like the carvings, and it wasn't a wound, like the pain some older wood had given them. Rhyna spoke as if someone had put the word into her mouth and gone away.

"Home."

Halvard said she had it right. Here at our hearth we answer every tale with two words, *We remember*, and there we stop. But the green wood gave more than one word, and it didn't stop where we stop. {{Halyna}} said it together, in one breath:

<!-- W -->
*We remember our home too.*

Rhyna wept, and Halvard didn't, because she was weeping for them both. I held the lamp until Rhyna had finished.

Then they gave me the other thing, folded inside the answer the way a seed is folded inside a fruit. It was the name the wood's own people give themselves: *Naelear*, those of the breath. The breath is their word for the grey fog, the breath of their sleeping trees. I said the name over three times before I wrote it, and it held. *Mystaeri*, our old name for them, from the mist, is only what we on the shore called them.

<!-- RIPENS: II.2's untold name -->

The Thrones had already told us that the pillars hadn't put up a single leaf since the iron came. The iron is the wood's word for our axes. The pillars had been waiting for their sky to come back, the grey breath they once held up between their crowns.

So the green sliver was their first new growth since the axes came. It was seed, a beginning.

Rhyna carried it up to the Keep on a bed of moss, with Halvard's hand under her elbow. A fisher-girl of the east keep ran into the ward, the courtyard inside our walls. She's the one who keeps a lamp burning on our north wall now. Hesk, one of the Captain's twelve True Men, lit his mother's lamp there every dusk of the war, until he died this year. The girl asked where the green wood would go.

{{Halyna}} answered her, one beginning and the other finishing:

<!-- HALYNA -->

"Home."

"And we two have to find it a boat."

<!-- HALYNA END -->

In all the records of the Stonewrights, our guild of master builders, there's no boat built by one of us. The sea was always something we built walls against, never a road.

But on the Road, our long march over the mountains to this Keep, the boatwrights of our cities had carried their adzes, just as the masons carried their chisels. The next day they came down to the shingle, the pebble beach below our wall. The eldest of them was Tarnard, the father of Tarnel. Tarnel and I shared a cradle, and if he had lived, the guild would have sealed us as a bonded pair. He died of the cold beside me on the Road. Tarnard laid the keel himself.

Harl of the burning shore, another of the True Men, carried the first plank on his shoulder out of the vault under this hall, where we keep the carved planks from the enemy's wrecks. *Burn it*, Harl had said of every plank in the war. This one he set down at Tarnard's feet, and he said nothing. Tarnard laid his palm flat on it and said:

"Wood that knew the sea knows the way home."

They built the boat from the vault's planks. Each of them had once spoken to us, giving up the order carved into it, and now they would speak for us.

For the stem, the boat's upright front post, Tarnard took two planks whose raw, split edges I had once laid together, years before, and found they fit like the two halves of a split log. One was the plank that said *shore*, the first the sea ever brought us and the first word we ever knew from the wood. The other stayed silent so long, before it spoke at last. Our wall-masters, Ormund and Penna, the bonded pair we call {{Orvenna}}, locked the two together with keys, the way masons lock stone. Penna held the last key, and Ormund struck it, once.

The broken grain met, and held.

That evening {{Halyna}} cut their mark across the join, one setting the chisel and the other striking. Every bonded pair has its own mark, which no hands but their own may cut, and the mark has its own name, the *Stonwryt*. Halvard once drew theirs in the ash of our hearth to show the children, but no mark of ours had ever gone into wood before. I held the lamp while they worked, and the lamp burned down a finger's width.

The wood took it.

Rhyna laid her palms on the cut, and Halvard laid his hands over hers.

Tarnard left me the lashing of the bow, the cords that bind the boat's front. I tied it with Tarnel's knot, the one he taught me on the Road. He tied one for every mile, knotting a net as he walked. Tarnard looked at it a long time. Then, quietly, he said his son's saying:

"A net is only knots holding hands."

For a whole day the boat rode the hard swell off the point, and it took in no water. It was small, and it was sound.

At the evening tide everyone from the hearth came down to the shingle, the old leaning on the young. {{Halyna}} laid the green sliver in the bow on its moss, against the stem. Beside it they set a Stonwryt coin, part gold and part black stone, struck with the mark of one of our master pairs. People called such coins trust, sealed in stone, and it was our dearest gift. Then they laid their chisels beside it, and there in the bow the last of the light caught on their two chisels.

<!-- HALYNA -->

"Let them go with it,"

"because we two have cut our last mark."

<!-- HALYNA END -->

No one on the shingle spoke. {{Halyna}} knelt on the wet stones in front of the bow, and behind them the whole hearth knelt. The soldiers laid down their pikes. Kael the Counter, one of the True Men, who counts everything and won't even sit down to tell a tale, knelt with the rest. Last in the line, with his hood up, the Captain knelt.

He's sitting there now, at the end of the bench. The wet of the shingle still shows dark on his knees.

In our grandparents' time the Guest, a stranger from behind the fog, knelt like this at a warden's door on our coast, and set down his best gift. The warden kicked it into the dust, and his men killed the Guest on that step.

From the front, {{Halyna}} said:

<!-- HALYNA -->

"This is as far as one people can go alone: the gift, and the kneeling."

"The rest needs the other."

<!-- HALYNA END -->

A wave ran up the shingle and wet the keel. Then Harl stood up, took the bow in his two arms, and walked it down into the tide. The fisher-children pushed it off. In front of them all, up to her waist in the cold sea, was the girl who had asked where it would go.

Well out on the ebbing tide, the boat turned. It went into the wind and against the current, into the grey.

The old ship-masters, who had captained our boats all their lives, stood along the shingle, with Merrick, the eldest, at their head. His father had once come home clinging to a spar, after a boat came burning out of the grey and set his timber-boat alight. For six winters, whenever a ship burned, the ship-masters had turned their faces away from the sea.

They did not look away.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And no one answered.*

When the three of us took our hands off the stone, the hearth was silent a long time. The fire burned down, and nobody got up to put wood on it. In six winters we had never once answered the wood's words with *We remember*. And I think we were waiting for the width of one old voice. Most nights old Ebba began our answer, a breath ahead of everyone else. But Ebba of Fenholm lies under the east cairns now, our graves on the Keep's east face.

Then a child near the front spoke, not loudly. It was the girl who had gone first into the sea. Old Ebba was her great-grandmother.

"We remember."

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

*Then, for the first time, the hearth sang in the wood's way, and didn't stop where we stop. We call the song* The Two Homes:

<!-- W: line 3 only -->
> *Our home was cut out of the mountain's bone.*  
> *We forgot it, and found it, and held it with blood.*  
> *Their home was breathed out of the hearts of the trees, and the axes came, and the breath got thin, and they haven't found it, and they can't hold it.*  
> *Stone keeps the promise, and the grain keeps the truth.*  
> *We remember our home. They remember theirs too.*
