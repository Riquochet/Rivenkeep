**The Plain, rewritten for a newcomer**

The reader is a modern person who has never heard of Rivenkeep, perhaps twelve years old, perhaps sixty, reading for the story. That reader should understand each paragraph the first time through. The writers worked from one primer (`wf13/plain_primer.md`): the brief on one page, the craft, the hearth's clock of what is known when, a lexicon of every word a newcomer trips on, the mysteries never to explain, who's who, the timeline, a *when* line and watch-points for every leaf, and two model leaves, I.1 and II.3. The rules:

1. **Every paragraph rewritten whole,** in natural modern English: normal word order, ordinary sentence lengths, contractions where a speaker would use them, no old words and no semicolons. Paragraphs may be merged or split.
2. **The lore explained as the tale meets it,** in a clause or a sentence woven into the telling, with the plain word first and the lore name once beside it (*our teachers, the old master pair the guild calls Theoliths*), and never a lecture. Every tale stands on its own, so each gives its own short reminders of who and what.
3. **No spoilers.** Nothing is explained before the tale in which the hearth learns it, and nothing the Book keeps dark is explained at all: the primer lists twenty-four such mysteries, from the warden's name to what the Stone's grain says. What the hearth does know is made plain: where the Epilogue says *It was the same child*, the Plain says which child, and why it matters.
4. **Embellishment as context only:** who someone is, why a thing matters, what a custom means, where a place is. No new event, picture, speech, reason or lore.
5. **A plain *when* line** opens each leaf in place of the Book's dateline: a sentence or two saying when the tale was told, and when its events happened where that differs. The headnote follows in plain words, with one short fixed reminder of the hearth or the wood where the frame used to be retold every time.
6. **A note for new readers** opens the foreword, set apart before its *when* line: 343 words on the Shorelands and their ten havens, the Stonewrights and their bonded pairs, the grey and its ships, the walk to the Keep, the Captain's banner and the siege, and the Long Hearth where Seren writes the tales down.
7. **Everything kept:** every beat, fact, name, number and teller, the teller's warmth and the build of each tale; the fixed lines, the songs and the liturgy; the pair-names, blots, markers and native blocks exactly as before.

**How it was made.** Thirteen groups, cut as v3's were (g01 to g11, w01 and w02; §1.12), each rewrote their leaves from the primer. Each group was then read by a newcomer and checked for fidelity against the Book (`wf13/critique/`), and revised. The assembled whole was read once more by a newcomer, and the editor's pass (`wf13/plain_notes.md`, its EDITOR section) cut what repeated: the frame re-explained in nearly every headnote (the same 104 words opened V.3, V.4, V.6 and VI.2), and recaps of earlier tales, each cut to a clause. It also mended points of fidelity: the Book makes Wick *the swiftest*, not Hale, and II.2 no longer says what the Guest's gift is, so IV.3 and IV.4 keep their turn.

**The word-by-word romanisation**

Every line in a *Romanisation* fold of the *Original* tab now stands word over word: each romanised Orrowen word in italic, with its English beneath it in a small, quieter type (`wf13/orig/build_panes.py`, `rom_p`). The fold is closed until the reader opens it. The line still wraps as a line of words, each word keeps its punctuation and a pair-name its lintel, and a screen reader hears the romanised line once and then its English, word by word. Only these folds carry the gloss.

| | |
|---|---|
| **The Book** (Of This Book) | *On the wall I see the banner lift, in a wind I hear not.* |
| **The romanisation** | *Um et hald veskym et movenn ul memmyl, ul helvhoss sa nath hunnom.* |
| **The analyzer's draft** | `upon(+S) · the · wall · see-1SG · the · banner · in(+N) · N·lifting · in(+N) · wind · REL(+S) · NEG(+N) · hear-1SG` |
| **On the page** | on · the · wall · I see · the · banner · in · lifting · in · wind · that · not · I hear |

**How the gloss was made.**

1. **The analyzer's drafts.** `wf13/extract_rom.py` took every romanised line from the Original's folds and grouped the lines by the twenty units that set them down: S01 to S11 and S03x (the Title under the new capstone, I.4) for the stone and the Book's furniture, and W01 to W08 for the wood. The Orrowen analyzer gave each word a mechanical reading as its draft (`wf13/gloss_in/`). Every word had one, and none was unknown.
2. **The shared table.** `wf13/gloss_table.md` is the one rulebook for every gloss writer, so that a word reads the same on every page. Its authority runs the lexicon first, then the grammar, then the analyzer, whose draft is only a draft. Its rules: one gloss to a word, in the sense the paragraph needs (*keth* is "hold" or "know"); plain modern English, in lower case except where the Book's English has a capital; literal, so an idiom shows through its own words (*ul vimm et hellur* is "in" "low" "the" "hall", not "under the hall"); no mutations shown; parentheses only for the particles English has no word for, "(past)", "(future)" and "(question)", and for a pair-name, "Halyna (the two)"; a person by the Book's spelling (*Wik* is "Wick"), and a place of two words by its words ("Keep" "Riven" for Rivenkeep). No name is taken apart: a wood name such as *Aelthae* is glossed as itself, as the Plain keeps it whole. The table also lists where the analyzer goes wrong (*Kael* is always the man, never "tally-notch").
3. **Glossed, then verified.** The twenty units were glossed from their drafts and the table (`wf13/gloss/`), and each was then verified against the lexicon and the Book's English. In the eight units that kept their copy from before that check, it changed 72 words in 63 lines. Of the 25,734 words in distinct lines, the writers kept the analyzer's word for 9,969 and rewrote the rest: its tags into English (`see-1SG` to "I see"), and its sense where the sentence needs another (*lern* from "face" to "side").

**The counts.** `wf13/check_gloss.py` passes for all twenty units: **1,288 lines, 0 problems.** Every line has its gloss, with exactly the analyzer's words in their order, and no gloss is empty, a question mark or a raw tag. The page builder stops on any line that lacks its gloss or has other words, and it set **1,288 lines word over word, 26,532 words**. One fold line holds no word and stands as it was.
