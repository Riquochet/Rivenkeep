## 0 · What applies to every tale, so (f) below lists only what is specific

1. **The style resets to v1.3.** Use v1.3's short, plain, end-stopped sentences and each teller's own voice. The difficulty comes from Old English diction, grammar and word order (*ere*, *nigh*, *whither*, *thou/thee* in prayer, inversion such as "Long were the masters dead", *-eth* where it fits), never from length.
   - v2.0's run-on engine is the joined clause ("…; and… and… ; and…"). The other main source is the Homeric "As when …: so …" simile. v2.0 uses 13 of those full scaffolds across Part 2 (IV.1, IV.5 ×2, V.1, V.2, V.5 ×2, V.7 ×3, VI.3 ×3), and every one pads.
   - Seren's painted pictures should **stay as pictures**, one clause each, and lose the scaffold. Each tale's list of similes is in (c), so the writers can keep the image and cut the frame.
2. **Economy.** Part 2 runs **16,879** words in v1.3 and **32,514** in v2.0 (×1.93). Jack likes every element, so the cut has to come from the joining, the scaffolds, the re-explaining and the cross-references, not from the beats.
   - (h) gives each tale a *suggested* ceiling. It is only a suggestion, for the orchestrator to set. The ceilings add up to about 20,000.
3. **"They don't build."** In every tale v2.0 deflates the climb in two ways:
   - **front-loading:** headnotes that explain provenance, mechanism and who knew what, before the tale begins;
   - **after-the-fact glossing:** lines like "I did not understand it then. I think I do now", or the cross-references back to earlier tales.
   
   Each tale should climb to its ★ beat and stop soon after. v1.3's endings were already short. Keep them short.
4. **Seren's playfulness (Jack note 3).** Her love of words shows in a few recurring moves:
   - she says a word three times before she trusts it to the ink (6 times in Part 2: IV.1, V.1, V.2, V.7 II, VI.3, the Book of Knowings);
   - she names the roots;
   - "fortune… gives under the tongue as mortar gives that has gone to sand";
   - "short and square… as a dressed stone sits in its bed of mortar".
   
   Keep the gift. Ration the tic: suggest it be shown fully once (V.1, the first word) and echoed at most twice (VI.3 *Naelear*; the Book of Knowings).
5. **The wood leaves (Jack note 4).** In v2.0, IV.2, IV.4, IV.6, V.3, V.4, V.6, VI.2 and the Stone's grain in the Epilogue are all whole, polished prose in the wood's "we". Jack wants something else:
   - {{Halyna}} give **pieces**: phrases and half-ideas, as the wood gives them up;
   - Seren makes the memory **a story**.
   
   Two models are already in the text:
   - IV.2's *"As first known… the shape only"* fragment, with its gap-marks;
   - IV.4's road told in the wood's senses ("Then old arms, two pairs, slow, a long while; and fire near and far").
   
   Each wood tale's (f) suggests the pieces. **Open question for the orchestrator:** does Seren's story stay in the wood's "we" (as v1.3 and v2.0 have it), or does she tell the wood's memory in her own voice? Either way the rule holds: the story may hold only what the wood could know. Shore facts go in Seren's headnote or her added lines.
6. **Plain Words (Jack note 2)** is a separate retelling. The beat lists below are the spine it should retell in easy modern English.
7. **The rites stay, unexplained, every time:**
   - Seren's opener, "Hold the stone, Seren. I tell myself so every time." (13 times in v2.0);
   - Kael's opener, "Give me the stone. No, I will stand.";

## IV.1 · Of the Felling of the Black Pillars *(stone; faces IV.2)*

**(a)** Seren, out of the guild's Harvest rolls in the archive chest, which {{Halyna}} read to her nightly through the first year (in v1.3, {{Halyna}} laid it). Laid in the second spring, in the Second Tide · of the Harvest, five to three generations before the banner, the forgetting and the late turning.

**(b) Beats**
1. Seren's opener. {{Halyna}} read; Seren copied, her letters "leaned like reeds in a wind". {{Halyna}}'s first words: "We did not live it. Our fathers' fathers lived it, and wrote it down, and did not read what they had written."
2. The oldest rolls: the grey as a blackish veil; then only a question, asked in the night-watches and never answered (what it was, what it kept, who breathed it). "The answer, when it came, was not written in our hand."
3. The high summer of pride, five generations before the banner. The Stonewrights' counsel had dwindled to grandmothers' talk. Ships along the edge found the black pillars "straight as a plumb-line"; the ship-masters named them **Mystholders**: "harder than oak, lighter than iron, proof against rot."
4. ★ **The count, cargo by cargo**, a litany of "Black wood, for…":
   - the needful uses: buttresses and bridges, the three spans of Tidesmeet, the pilings of Fenholm, the roof-trees of Holtward, the cranes of Highreach, the galleries of Sandreach, the great hall at Rimewatch, the forges of Emberhythe, and the iron of the axes beaten out at Carnhold;
   - then the things that "needed nothing of the kind": a lord's dancing-hall, the trade-tables and dice-boards, *[stained, three lines lost]*, and the merchant's bed: "He slept in it, and was proud."
   - "Halvard turned the leaf, and of the two of them neither said a word."
5. **The stranger count.** The regrowth was cut and cut again ("the guild bought them for scaffold"). Then the clerk's tiny hand: *All along the edge, the stumps put up nothing.* "No one wrote down why, and no one asked."
6. The grey thinned "like a dying enchantment"; the Council named it **fortune**. Seren tests the word: it "gives under the tongue as mortar gives that has gone to sand."
7. The Stonewrights' warning, verbatim. It was read once in the Council hall at Eldhythe, and laid on a shelf.
8. The carvings at the pillars' feet: letters "as beautiful as frost on a window", which no man could read. "Stone does not translate." Some were locked in the archives ("it was we who turned the key"); some were hung in the harbour-houses, "reduced to ornament, and men drank beneath them and did their reckoning". "That is our share of it. We will not lay it all on the Council."
9. ★ **The found letter.** Between a count of poles and a count of oars, a clerk copied, "in the same hand and with the same care", the outpost's report, with the name cut out at its foot: ▒▒▒▒. On the next leaf, two timber-boats were struck off, *lost at the edge*. "What I held, I did not know."
10. The end of the Harvest, "not by wisdom but by the fickleness of men": scarcity, long voyages, cheaper northern timber. And after the year of the burning boat no crew would row within sight of the grey, "nor do the rolls say why". "So the axes stopped. No one decided it."
11. The forgetting: the stones hung on over the ale and dice. "Some of you who sit at this fire were those children."
12. The late turning (the sea-wall simile). "But humility came too late. / The first dark sails were already on the sea."
13. The close, and two lines added later in Seren's hand: the carvings were **pleas**, "holy pleas, reduced to ornament"; and the sound-writing, "the sounds were close, and the symbols wrong."

# APPENDIX · THE BOOK OF KNOWINGS

**(a)** Seren Two-Inks, at the back of the Book, one carving at a time, as {{Halyna}} knew each from the wreck-wood and the Thrones; "the fleet's own half of the Book". Written through the six winters and the year of the homecomings, closed in the fourth year after · of every carving, from the Hasty Tide to the Thrones. The Last Note was laid last, in the fourth year after the homecomings, on the night after the reading.

**(b) Beats**
1. Every carving begins with one stroke, its root, "as the first course is the root of a wall"; there are ten roots.
2. She learned their shapes by eye and named them herself, "for a shape that has no name slips out of the hand like a wet stone"; said each name three times; they held.
3. "So the eldest carvings are only the youngest grown old" (the ring-on-ring image).
4. In the late Tides she knew a root by eye "in the time it takes a wreck to roll over in the surf", but never more than the root: "it must be known, not read."
5. Only the heads are given here. The soldiers' seven names, "worn smooth with handling, like the haft of an old mallet".
6. **The table**: ten roots, their look in the grain, the carvings youngest to eldest by Tide, and whose each was at the last (the Thrones).
7. The count: 7 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 7 = 38 from the Tides; the Last Tide carved no new head but whole morrows; the Thrones gave 8 judgements, and 2 carried an older carving whole; 46 in all. "Not one of them did I know."
8. *Burn*, apart: cut in the Fall, never against our wall; laid on the crust at Emberhythe in the year of the homecomings; "forty-seven carvings in this Book, and forty-six in its count."
9. ★ **The Last Note · Three Stones over the Hearth:**
   - the stones' road, from the pillars' feet to the harbour-house to this hearth;
   - a child asked what they said, and Seren said "I did not know" (not "nothing");
   - night after night she held the lamp to the lowest stone for the sounds only, knowing from the third winter that they cut our words by sound, and having the three sounds from Brenn;
   - the lamp burned down a finger's width;
   - ★★ ***Stop… Sky… Dying…*** "the sounds close and the symbols wrong";
   - "They hung over a harbour-house fire for three generations, and the ship-masters knocked their pipes out on them, and no one read them. I think I have read them. I say I think.";
