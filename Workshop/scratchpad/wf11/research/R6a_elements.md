## 0 · ACROSS PART 1: READ THIS FIRST

### 0.1 The custom of burning in Part 1

**There is no direct trace.** Tobe's saying, "the old way of the coast" and the burning of what the grey sends all begin in IV.3 and later tales, which are Part 2. Three soft echoes sit in Part 1. Each one survives the removal, and each one gets better without the custom:

| Where | The line | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| III.1, Aske | "no wood under my hand but the wreck that the grey casts upon the shingle, and I lay my hand on every plank of it ere it burns. These were trees once." | **Keep.** With no custom, the wreck burns because a bare mountain has no other wood. Aske's own line says so. This also plants V.1's burning as need (or fear), not lore. |
| I.1, III.1, III.2 → VI.1, Harl | "would burn the sea, if the sea would take fire" · "the grey I will burn" · the charred plank: "I told him to burn it" → VI.1 "I did not burn it" | **Keep.** This is Harl's temper and grief, one man's arc. No saying or "old way" may ever be attached to it. In III.2 the order is his alone, so keep it that way. |
| I.3, Merrick and Jory | Jory whistles "a tune that the old ship-masters hate"; Merrick tells him to stop; "It's only a rhyme." | **Keep, as the mend's seed.** Merrick's dread comes from his father Daveth, who came home on a spar (the Epilogue and VI.3). That is exactly the "plain fear of the grey boats, which the survivors' rumours spread" that the reading notes prescribe in place of the custom. |

*The Grey Boat*'s line "Burn it and shove it and give it the tide" (IV.5, verbatim) describes the deed, not a custom. Jory's whistled tune is that song, so I.3 stays clean.

### 0.2 IV.3 (Brenn only watched) and its seeds in Part 1

These Part 1 seeds pay into IV.3, and all of them survive Jack's change:

- **I.1: "twelve of us in all"**, against IV.3's "There were twelve of us and the warden." The outpost's twelve and the Captain's Twelve still mirror each other, with Brenn a boy among the twelve who did not stop them.
- **I.1 and II.1: the quarry-wardens' names cut *into* the lintel**, against the warden's name cut *out* (▒▒▒▒). Unchanged.

### 0.3 Jack's note 3 contradicts two v2.0 lines in Part 1

- **The foreword:** "The words are the teller's, and I change not one of them; the measure is mine."
- **I.1's headnote:** "He would not let me change a word of it, and I have not."

Jack's note says Seren *does* rewrite: "She knows how to write them so they sound better… they all agree that her version is the best." Both lines must turn round. One possible mend, offered only: each teller hears Seren's leaf read back and owns it. For Kael: *He would not let me change a word of it. I changed a good many; and when I read it back to him, he said that was what he had said.* This keeps Kael's stubbornness and makes it the joke. The foreword then says plainly that the words are hers, the tale is theirs, and no teller has yet asked for his own words back. A small headnote touch of the same kind could recur on other tellers' leaves (Hale, Voss, the True Men). That is a device to flag, not to decide here.

### 0.4 Jack's note 4 changes how wood leaves are told

In Part 1 this touches the foreword (two beats) and II.2 (the whole leaf). v2.0 has Halyna give the wood in finished paragraphs, "a paragraph to each". Jack wants Halyna to speak "in phrase and partial ideas as the wood tells the memory… piecing parts together," and **Seren makes the memory a story.** The two-inks-by-paragraph device needs a decision. The options are under II.2 (f).

**(a)** Seren Two-Inks · written at the front in the third summer of the siege, with the Book half full · of the hearth, and of this Book.

**(b)**

4. **The tale-stone.**

   - {{Halyna}} turned it at dusk on the banner, "for it is a stone that takes two".
   - Halvard's fathers cut it and Rhyna's forebears laid it. The founders' vow keeps its edge on its underside (native drawing).
5. "Whoever holds that stone tells." A tale is *laid*, not told.
6. **The lintel sentence**, and each pair named once:
   - {{Halyna}};

   - {{Orvenna}}, the wall-masters, never heard to raise their voices;
   - {{Enrella}}, the youngest, sealed in the first summer, "the one of them cannot begin a jest but the other ends it".
7. ***We remember.***
   - One voice, two words, out of the Title's first words, which the Captain wrote in coal from this hearth. It is never said another way.
   - **Ebba said it first, and still begins it a breath before.**
8. **The wood's tales.**
   - They come from the wreck-wood, from the dark stone in the chest she carried, and from the hearts of broken hulls.
   - *Mystaeri* is "our word for them, and not theirs".
   - The wood is known whole, not read, and the telling is the hard part.
   - The hearth falls silent after a wood tale, and she writes the silence down.
9. **Seren.**
   - Unbonded; Halyna's third. "When they know the wood, I hold the lamp."
   - She laid her palm on the grain once and let go.
   - The hand that writes, not the hand that knows.
   - Every scribe before her was of a bonded pair; why not now is told in II.3.
10. ★ **Her craft.**
    - She sets each tale in measure, as a mason sets a course, every joint broken.
    - She says each word aloud three times before she writes it.
    - The coals go to ash with the last word still in her mouth.
    - ★ "I love our tongue." ★ "Up the mountain I carried two things, our tongue and a chest; and only the chest was heavy."
11. **The facing leaves.** The halves line, then the bare leaves "with only the grain showing", that the reader may see the shape of what was not yet understood, "and wait".
12. **The two inks.**
    - A paragraph each; they would not let her mark whose is whose. "You cannot tell."
    - A line they share is set in both inks.
13. **The Captain.**
    - He does not tell. He sits hood up and says only "it was not like that".
    - Commander on the wall, Captain here.
    - **He puts his hood back when the dead are named.**
14. She has not mended broken carvings with guesses, and where tales disagree she has kept both.
15. "This Book is ink." It is to be cut into the granite of the hall, course upon course, and until then kept in her chest with the mute stones.

## BOOK ONE · THE BOOK OF THE WALL

### I.1 · The Torn Cloak

**(a)** Kael the Counter · laid on the 15th night after the banner, the night after the Rite and the hearth's first asking · of the banner's dusk and the long retreat. Its facing leaf is the Stone's unmarked rings, which the Epilogue fills.

**(b)**

2. "Give me the stone. No, I will stand." A soldier stands to give his count.
3. He swears on the lintel of the western stair, where the old wardens cut their names. Counting is his trade, not singing: "what I did not count I will not tell you."
4. **The column at dusk, counted.**
