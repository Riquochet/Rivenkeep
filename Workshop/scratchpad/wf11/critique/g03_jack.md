# g03 · Jack's Eye: I.4 The Rite of the Cornerstone and I.5 The Naming of the Rivenmen

*Critic: read as Jack would, against voice_v3 §10, craft §8, the three model leaves and memory_theme. The source is `wf11/drafts/g03.md`. The trial rewrites, used here only for counting, are `critique/_g03j/I4_rw.md` and `I5_rw.md`. The draft as split is `I4.md` and `I5.md`. A rough old-grammar counter is `critique/_g03j/markers.py`.*

## The verdict

**These two leaves are far better than v2.0, and in the right voice.** The fixed matter is exact: the cradle-rhyme, the slate's line, the oils, the staff, the Title, Jack's three lines and the liturgy. The locks hold. There are no run-ons. Seren's hand shows in many places, and none of the following should be touched:
- *Naught moved in them.*
- *I set my bucket down and forgat it.*
- *on a wet night its stones breathe it out yet*
- *flowereth where none come.* / *Then came we*
- *On the flasks they shook not.*
- *Neither of them had mended one stroke.*
- *as mine are yet*
- *Child have I none of mine own, and yet I said* our.
- *Old Ebba bideth on the east face.* The hearth waits for her, and she bides for it. That is the leaf's best turn.

I.4 also carries a quiet triple on forgetting. The bucket is forgotten (a light touch), then the fathers (grave), then the hearth, which is forgotten *and found again*. This is memory theme 2, felt rather than preached.

**Jack would still say two things:**
- **I.4 is a procession, not a story.** It runs: find, list, rule, oil, oil, oil, rite, carving, leaf. Nothing is at stake in the halt, because the man the Rite is for never appears until he is standing in it. Three passages in its first half read as a textbook.
- **I.5 opens on Jory's cairn and then forgets him.** Its halt is two lines of scene followed by three paragraphs of decree.

Under both faults sits a register problem: **the road stretches read as modern English with a few old words in them.** A first-time reader goes straight through I.4's first half.

## The worst problems, with rewrites

### 1 · I.4: the Captain is missing from his own Rite, so the halt has nothing at stake

The tale's question as drafted is *can the Rite be made when the flasks are dry?*, and it is answered without one setback. The real tension of this tale is the Captain: *he sought not to lead* (I.1), *He did not like it. He stood there.*, and *A long while the Captain looked on it ere he put out his hands.* But between the slates and the Rite he appears only as a name in a passive clause: *the Captain would be anointed*. So when he hesitates over the staff, the reader has not been made to fear a refusal. And Jack's closing line, *In his own heart he was still only the Captain*, lands on a man who has not once stood on the page before the Rite.

Plant his reluctance as a deed. Pay it at the staff, and close the bracket at the gatehouse. The deed comes from v2.0's own *led as he had always led, from the wall… with mortar upon his hands*, which the draft cut. It adds no lore.

**Draft (line 62):**
> All night the empty flask stood on the hearthstone, and none of us would touch it.

**Rewrite:**
> All night the flask of {{Idrenna}} stood empty on the hearthstone, and none of us would touch it. Late came the Captain in off the wall, mortar to the wrist, and looked on it, and went out again.

**Why this works:**
- He sees the dead's myrrh that is meant for him, and walks out. Nothing is explained.
- *looked on it* now rhymes with *A long while the Captain looked on it ere he put out his hands*. That gives the halt its held breath: will he take it?
- *the flask of {{Idrenna}}* also mends an ambiguity. The draft's *the empty flask* could be any of the chest's three empty stone flasks.

Close the bracket so that the wall frames the Rite, wall, then Rite, then wall. Write the staff sentence as one clean sentence, not an object-first sentence carrying two verbs:

**Draft (line 95):**
> The staff the Captain leaned that night in the corner of the gatehouse, and went back to the wall with his half of a cloak on his back.

**Rewrite:**
> In the corner of the gatehouse the Captain leaned the staff, and went back to the wall with his half of a cloak on his back.

That also removes one of three *that night*s within forty lines.

### 2 · I.4: three rulebook passages in the first half

Between *Naught moved in them.* and *My fingers were raw with it.* the leaf turns three times into the voice Jack called "a history text book":
- a law recited;
- a feeling stated (*The slates beneath troubled them*), followed by an era summarized (*Twelve generations the guild had taught that walls are for peace*), which I.2 has already told (*The faith of twelve generations sat on us yet, like a wet cloak*);
- a dictionary definition (*Bloom is a pale crust…*).

Each one can become something heard or seen, and each rewrite is shorter or level.

**Draft (lines 40–42):**
> Below it stood the law of the staff. A staff is cut for its work, and leaveth it not. When the work is done, it is laid down in it.
>
> The slates beneath troubled them. On those slates the walls were sloped to turn a shot, the gates set crooked against the ram, the courses laid so that a breach would fall outward. The first builders of the Keep had known war. Against what, the slates say not. Twelve generations the guild had taught that walls are for peace. The first fathers had known better, and we had forgotten them.

**Rewrite:**
> Lower stood the law of the staff.
>
> "A staff is cut for its work, and leaveth not its work,"
>
> "and when the work is done, the staff is laid down in it."
>
> At the slates beneath, the breath between the lines grew long. Walls sloped to turn a shot. Gates set crooked against the ram. Courses laid so that a breach would fall outward. The first builders had known war. Against what, the slates say not. The first fathers had known better, and we had forgotten them.

**Why this works:**
- **The law is read aloud in the pair's halves,** as the Rite's own first line is. It becomes a scene, not a rule.
- ***the staff is laid down in it*** mends the draft's *it is laid down in it*, where both *it*s are unclear. It also matches the Epilogue (g11: *the staff is laid down in it*).
- ***leaveth not its work*** carries III.2's wording (g05 flags *A staff leaveth not its work* for Book-wide use), so that echo lands.
- **The trouble is shown by Seren's own plant breaking.** *A line and a breath and a line* becomes a breath that grows long. Then the lines come one at a time, as fragments, as the reader hears them.
- **The I.2 repeat goes.** *had known better* still reads, because I.2 told what the guild believed.

**Draft (line 52):**
> Bloom is a pale crust on worked stone left untended for lives of men. Masons name it so, for it flowereth where none come.

**Rewrite:**
> Bloom the masons call the pale crust on worked stone left untended for lives of men, for it flowereth where none come.

This turns a definition into a naming, in one sentence, and keeps the draft's best play (*where none come* / *Then came we*) one breath apart.

### 3 · I.4: Seren's own ring is cut at both ends

The headnote gives her *naught but water*. She gets *the first work… that was not water*, then *I held the lamp*, then she is sent down alone. That is the tale's human through-line, and the draft states it more than it lets it be felt.
- At the top, the child who watched the light go and kept the secret is gone (v2.0: *I did not call out, but lay and watched it go*).
- At the bottom, the descent she makes alone down that same stair is a clause.
- Between the two, the opening picture compares the lamp to a lamp (*it went… like a lamp let down a well*).

**Draft (line 11):**
> Down from me it went, smaller at every turning, like a lamp let down a well.

**Rewrite:**
> I called not out. Down from me the light went, turning by turning, till it was a spark in a well.

**Draft (line 99):**
> …{{Halyna}} sent me down alone with the lamp to lay the slates back. I lifted the empty flasks.

**Rewrite:**
> …{{Halyna}} sent me down alone with the lamp to lay the slates back. Down I went, turning by turning, where I had watched their light go. I lifted the empty flasks.

The child who watched the light go down now carries it. Thirteen words close the ring, plant the Epilogue's *Mine is the lamp now, and I know the stair*, and give the foreword's *When {{Halyna}} know the wood, I hold the lamp* its origin. The leaf she then finds is the reward for that descent.

### 4 · I.5: Jory is planted and never paid

The tale's question is born at Jory's cairn, and that opening is one of the best in the Book: *The last stone on him I laid myself. / His name was Jory.* He comes back alive at the Naming, *not whistling*, and then he is never seen or named again. The answer, six winters in the cutting, says nothing of the man it was asked for. That is a gun on the wall that never fires. It also misses memory theme 4: the hearth should get one line of Jory's good, not only his cairn. Two short lines pay him, and both come from the existing elements: the whistle (I.3), and v2.0's *Jory, for whose sake I asked it, never heard it asked at all*.

**Draft (line 157):**
> Each laid a hand on it and sware, as men make fast their ropes on the quay at evening. By dark the Keep was held.

**Rewrite:**
> Each laid a hand on it and sware, as men make fast their ropes on the quay at evening. Jory went up ere me, and came down whistling. By dark the Keep was held.

**Draft (line 181):**
> Now here is mine, and ye may weigh it. A slow answer is a slow tide, so my grandmother told it me: it cometh in over the same stones.

**Rewrite:**
> Now here is mine, and ye may weigh it. Jory, for whose sake I asked it, never heard it asked.

**Then move the grandmother's saying down,** to stand alone just before the last line of the answer:

> It is a place to go out from.
>
> A slow answer is a slow tide, so my grandmother told it me: it cometh in over the same stones.
>
> We fight not for ice and stone. Every stone of it is Rivenkeep…

**Why this works:**
- **The whistle comes three times, and the third turns.** He whistles in I.3, is silent at the Naming, and after the oath *came down whistling*. The habit comes back to him whole at the moment of the vow. That is the life remembered, it plants IV.5's tune, and it makes the opening cairn hurt in hindsight.
- **The proverb now does its work.** As drafted, it stood between *ye may weigh it* and the answer and delayed the climax. In the new place, the slow tide comes in over *the same stones*, and the next line is *ice and stone… Every stone*. The saying becomes the hinge onto Voss's own words, which is his circling mark (voice §4).

### 5 · I.5: the halt is two lines of scene and three paragraphs of decree

After *The Stonewrights built.* the leaf goes into declaration:
- what the people were (*Two peoples sharing a ruin we were not… neither half would have stood without the other*);
- what the land is called;
- what the people are called;
- what the Book calls their fathers.

The v2.0 deed that carried the meaning, *We looked at one another then, soldier and mason*, was cut, so only the conclusion is left. Two lines also tip into parody: *Two peoples sharing a ruin we were not* has the verb at the end, Yoda-fashion, and *ourselves we call we* is a tongue-twister.

**Draft (lines 151–155):**
> That was all the speech there was. It was enough. Two peoples sharing a ruin we were not. We were one, as two courses become one wall when the mortar takes, and neither half would have stood without the other.
>
> …
>
> Ourselves we named the Rivenmen. Our fathers we call Shorelanders, for so they were, and ourselves we call we. In that ward it began.

**Rewrite:**
> That was all the speech there was. It was enough. Then we looked at one another, soldier and mason. We were not two peoples sharing a ruin. One were we, as two courses become one wall when the mortar takes.
>
> …
>
> We named ourselves the Rivenmen. Our fathers we call Shorelanders, for so they were. Ourselves we call but *we*. In that ward it began.

**Why this works:**
- **The look is the deed,** and the mortar picture carries the meaning. *neither half would have stood without the other* goes, because it explains the picture.
- **The inversion now sits on the antithesis,** *One were we*, where it has weight.
- **The naming becomes a mirror pair** (*Our fathers we call… / Ourselves we call…*), and *but* works twice. It means *only*, and it sets *we* against them. Voice §2.2 teaches *but* as *only*, so a reader can learn it here.

### 6 · Both leaves: the road is too easy for a first-time reader

Jack asked for a Book "hard to read to… someone [who] hasn't read the text before." I.4's first half carries less than half the samples' old grammar (0.8 markers to 100 words, against about 2), and I.5's first half carries two thirds of it. The plain stretches are not hinges, where plainness is the rule. They are the road, where Seren should play. Use the forms the reader has already met in I.1 to I.3: a fronted word with the verb before the subject, the complement first, *spake*, and negation without *do*. Add no new words and no *-eth*.

**I.4:**

| Draft | Rewrite |
|---|---|
| *and none had asked what it meant:* | *and what it meant, none had asked:* |
| *Rhyna knew where to look, and Halvard went down. She paid out the rope, and neither said a word.* | *Rhyna knew where to look, and down went Halvard. She paid out the rope, and neither spake a word.* |
| *They would not fell a living tree. So Stannard knocked on the fallen trunks with his knuckle, and listened.* | *A living tree would they not fell. So on the fallen trunks Stannard knocked with his knuckle, and listened.* |
| *{{Idrenna}} had carried one flask the whole road* | *One flask had {{Idrenna}} carried the whole road* |
| *the hall fell quiet, as a street in the havens fell quiet for a bier.* | *the hall fell quiet, as a street doth for a bier.* (one *-eth*-class form, in a road sentence) |

**I.5:**

| Draft | Rewrite |
|---|---|
| *That night I asked a question at this fire. I was cold, and I meant it hard.* | *That night at this fire I asked a question. Cold I was, and I meant it hard.* (Brenn's *Nineteen I was* shape) |

- *My fingers were raw with it.*

- *Four hands laid it in two.*
- *In the morning she did not wake.*

## The rest, numbered

1. **I.4, line 76: *This was the first of them.*** It is unclear whether *them* means the overseers or the staffs. Write *It was the first staff of all.* It costs one word, and it pays the chest's staff and *the old stand where the first staff was cut*.
2. **I.4, line 58: *On the pass the ground would not take them.*** v2.0's *and the flask came up the mountain unopened* is cut, so the reader must work that out. This is acceptable, and *the flask of {{Idrenna}} stood empty* (worst problem 1) now confirms it. Add nothing more.
3. **I.4, the close stacks four endings:**
   - the leaf;
   - *So this hearth is older than the havens…*;
   - Kael's first tale;
   - Jack's line.

   With the Captain planted and bracketed (worst problem 1), Jack's line becomes a payoff and not a non sequitur, and the stack reads as a chain: the leaf, then the hearth, then the first tale (*It was of the Captain.*), then the Captain's heart. Cut nothing more. Do not add anything after *In his own heart he was still only the Captain.*
4. **I.4, headnote: *the third at the hearth of {{Halyna}}*.** The foreword (g01) says *the third of the hearth of {{Halyna}}*. Use one phrase Book-wide.
5. **I.4, the call is in quotation marks** (*"Hold the stone, Seren. I tell myself so every time."*). g04, g06, g07, g08, g09 and g10 print *Hold the stone, Seren.* bare, and g11 quotes it. The orchestrator should pick one form for the builder. Unquoted matches the samples' calls (*Give me the stone.*).
6. **I.4: *If ye doubt the tale, go and look at the dust on it.*** R6a (d) files *the dust* under fixed or Jack's lines. *ye* is the correct HEAVY form, and g11's echo also uses *ye*. The orchestrator should confirm that Jack's ear accepts the change from *you*.
7. **I.4, dateline: *The first spring of the siege.*** This is fine. *of the siege* is the only phrase in Book One's datelines that reads like an annal (I.1: *The first winter, the night after the Rite.*). *The first spring.* is enough.
8. **I.5, headnote.** It tells the tale's first two beats before the tale does: the question asked in the first winter, and the whole war spent answering it. The tale says both within its first eighty words. Swap in the human touch that v2.0's headnote already had, which also plants the grandmother for the slow-tide saying and for IV.5's Elspet:
   > *Laid by Voss who asked, of Eldhythe, True Man of the harbour battery, that was raised in his grandmother's harbour-house on the quay.* (23 words)
9. **I.5, the crisis stretch is the longest in the leaf.** Ebba's last night through *None of us have tried to fill it* averages 14.0 words a sentence as drafted, against a leaf mean of 11.4 (voice §3.1: the crisis stretch should be the shortest). Split the burial: *At dusk we laid her on the east face by the soldiers. {{Idrenna}} said the prayer for the dead in two halves.* The trial brings the stretch to 12.5. Splitting *Four nights since…* at *nearest the coals.* brings it lower.
10. **I.5, the turn may point at the bench (optional, one aside).** *Four nights since, she sat late on that bench-end, nearest the coals, her shawl drawn up to her chin.* The hall looks at the empty place. It is the turn, so voice §3.5 allows the aside, and it costs three words fewer than the draft.
11. **I.5: *Marl hath her course-stone in his pack, for the old course at Fenholm.*** In Book order, I.5 comes before III.1 and III.2, which hold the course-stone story. So *course-stone* and *the old course at Fenholm* arrive unexplained. Write *Marl hath in his pack the stone she bare out of Fenholm on her back.* It uses *bare* (stock), the deed is v2.0's own, and VI.1 still pays it.
12. **I.5: *The Captain bound a strip of his cloak on a pike above her.*** He has only half a cloak, and the strips matter because the half shrinks (*Tonight it does not reach his belt*). Write *a strip of his half-cloak*, which costs no extra words. V.5 Count I still tells the strip whole at Jory. Its writer should know that the reader meets Ebba's strip first, as a clause.
13. **I.5, three quay pictures** (the rope turned as boys, the ropes made fast, the posts along a quay) and the herons, all in Voss's harbour. This is right as a ring. Do not add a fourth.
14. **Where to find the 5% margin, if it is wanted.** In I.4:
    - *for their meaning* (3 words), only if II.1 carries the Hal note;
    - *copied that afternoon* becomes *copied* (2);
    - *the founders' annal* stays, because it seeds II.1.

    In I.5, *Mine hath taken me the whole war to cut.* and the headnote's old *waited the whole war to answer it* said the same thing. Item 8 already removes the second, outside the count.

    Beyond these, neither leaf has a clear cut that does not lose a liked element. Both are at the samples' margin (1.5–3.6% under), and that is acceptable.
15. **I.5: *None of us have tried to fill it*,** set beside *None of us corrected him* and *none of them*. Either number of verb is good Early Modern English, but pick one form for the two leaves. v2.0 had *has*.
16. **Strip the WRITER'S NOTES section** (`## WRITER'S NOTES`) before assembly. The builder reads `##` headings.
17. **The Plain Words twins are missing from this pass.** They must be retold to voice §11 from the beat lists, never paraphrased from these leaves. The I.5 retelling should land in 590–720 words, and the I.4 one in 840–1,015.
