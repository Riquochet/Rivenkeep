# VI.3 · The Knowing of the Green Wood: read with Jack's eye

*A critique of `wf11/drafts/g10.md`, set against `voice_v3.md` §10, `craft.md` §8, the three model leaves and the sibling drafts that VI.3 pays. Line numbers refer to g10.md.*

---

## 0 · Verdict

The first half is close to the models. *It was green.* / *I brought the lamp down to it, for I trusted not the light.* / the drop that hisses / *Their two faces changed as one.* is told close and plain, and the hinge is mostly clean. The custom is gone from the text. The draft's best mend is quiet: the turn at line 138 is IV.3's sentence with *burning* left off the end. The archaic register is right. It has 1.6 marked forms per 100 words, the same as I.1, with four *-eth*, none at a hinge, no false friends and no tushery. Every R6b must-survive element is present. The song is verbatim.

Jack would still send it back, for one main reason. From *So it was seed* to the Amen (lines 56–158) the leaf reads as a list of payoffs set in white space. That is the "clinical", "doesn't build" fault in a new form: the v2.0 run-on chain has been replaced by a chain of one-line paragraphs. Five smaller faults stack on top of it:
- explanation at the two moments of wonder;
- a leftover trace of the custom;
- Harl's turn told a second time, after VI.1 has already told it;
- a close that explains Ebba's habit again instead of paying the I.5 image;
- one of the three cross-references that `craft.md` says to cut, kept, with a lock line rewritten inside it.

All of this is fixable within the draft's own words. A sketch with every fix applied counts **1,161** words of tale proper (ceiling 1,300; 10.7% under), keeps every element, and is printed in §1.1 below.

## 1 · The worst problems, with rewrites

### 1.1 The second half is a ledger of payoffs in white space, not a rising (lines 56–144)

**What is wrong.** 25 of the 54 narrative paragraphs are a single sentence. The models run 10–30%. From the seed to the turn of the boat, almost every beat gets a paragraph to itself:
- *So it was seed.*
- *Of the vault's planks they built it…*
- *The broken grain met, and held.*
- *The wood took it.*
- *Rhyna laid her palms on the cut…*
- *The lashing of the bow Tarnard left for me.*
- *Long looked Tarnard at it…*
- *No one on the shingle spake.*
- *{{Halyna}} kneeled…*
- *Behind them the whole hearth kneeled.*

At lines 116–120 three of these one-liners stand in a row, and at 78–86 five beats of the build stand in a row. When every beat stands alone, none of them stands out. *The broken grain met, and held.* and *The wood took it.* are the two great lines of the build, and they have to compete with *Of the vault's planks they built it* and *The lashing of the bow Tarnard left for me*, which are stage directions.

Between the beats the joint is *and then*, never *but* (`craft.md` §2.1, the test of the joints). The building section is exactly what R6b warned against: "Do not let the building section flatten it into a craft log." Jack will read it as a checklist of callbacks: the shore plank, Orvenna, Penna and Ormund, the mark, the ash, *harm to one*, the knot, the saying, the swell.

**What it should be.** The build has a real question, and the draft states it well: *In all the annals of the guild there is no Stonewright's boat. The sea was ever a thing we walled against, and never a road.* Three tests should press on it:
1. Will the broken grain hold?
2. Will the wood take a mark of ours?
3. Seren's own test, Tarnel's knot under his father's eye.

Put the joints into running prose so that only the answers stand alone. Keep these and only these as lone lines in the second half:
- *So it was seed.*
- *The broken grain met, and held.*
- *The wood took it.*
- *This time it carried a gift, not a grave.*
- *They did not look away.*

Fold in the fixes from §1.2–1.7 at the same pass. They are marked in the sketch.

### 1.2 The hinge is crowded and the climax is announced (lines 35, 39, 41)

**Line 35** is 28 words at the first instant of the knowing: *Not an order was it, nor a wound. *The green wood holds one word*, the groves sing, and Rhyna spake it, as though one had put it in her mouth and gone away.* That sentence holds a negation, a cross-reference to V.6's song, the speaking and a simile, all at the hinge. Voice §1.6 says the plainest words win at the first instant of a knowing.

**Line 41**, *It gave the answer of this hearth*, tells the hearer what the wood is about to say. *We remember our home too.* then arrives already explained. This is the most important line in the Book, and it should land on a hearer who recognises his own Amen coming out of the enemy's wood.

**Line 39**, *as once she had said it of him*, footnotes V.1 at the moment of wonder (craft §6.3). V.1 has *Rhyna said that he was right.* (g07 l.133). The mirror pays itself if it is left bare.

**Rewrite.** Move the song up so that it raises the question (*what is the one word?*) instead of glossing the answer. Then strip the hinge.

> Six winters had we searched the wrecks for orders, and the hearts for names, and the grey for sails. Not one of us had looked for a thing that lived. Yet this had lived in the dark of a dead heart, as seedlings live in the crack of a wall. *The green wood holds one word*, the groves sing.
>
> Since the first winter had {{Halyna}} needed but one hand on any wood. All the same they put out a hand each, and touched it in the same moment.
>
> A drop fell from the timbers into the flame, and hissed.
>
> Their two faces changed as one.
>
> Not an order was it, nor a wound. Rhyna spake, as though one had put the word in her mouth and gone away.
>
> "Home."
>
> Halvard said that she was right. But the green wood gave not the word alone, and it stopped not where we stop. They said it together, in one breath:
>
> *We remember our home too.*

*It stopped not where we stop* is now a riddle that the next line answers at once. That is Seren's play, and nothing is explained.

### 1.3 The custom's ghost, and an explained closing picture (lines 134, 142)

**Line 134, *None brought fire to the shingle.*** A negation makes sense only if someone expected the thing it denies. With no custom of burning, nobody would bring fire to a sending, so the only way to read the line is as the trace of the old rite. That is exactly the muddying Jack named. The writer's own note (§3.2) admits the line is there to answer the Guest's burning, and that answer is already made, and better, by the turn at line 138 with *burning* left off. Two fire-lines weaken the one silent payoff. **Cut it.** Seren's lamp, which is the light for seeing set against the warden's fire for hiding, is better used at the cutting of the mark (see item 3 in §2).

**Line 142, Merrick.** *Among the old ship-masters stood Merrick, whose father came home on a spar. Six winters had he turned his face from the sea, each time a hull burned. A grey boat going out against the wind: there is naught on the water they fear more.* In three sentences we get:
- a register entry (*whose father came home on a spar*: the Epilogue carries that, at g11 l.72);
- a reason;
- the fear stated as a rule of the coast.

All three sit on top of the closing picture. IV.5 and V.1 already planted the gesture to pay: *Along the wall the old sailors turned their faces from the sea, and Merrick first.* (g07 l.9).

**Rewrite.**

> Along the shingle stood the old ship-masters, and Merrick first. Six winters had they turned their faces from the sea when a hull burned. A grey boat going out against the wind is the thing they fear most on all the water.
>
> They did not look away.

If the budget wants it, drop the fear sentence too. The hearer has IV.5's *the boat that comes for you when you die*, which is plain fear from the survivors' rumour, and the boat has just turned against the wind in front of him.

**Does the sending land without a custom?** Yes, once line 134 goes. It rests on four things:
- the boat turns with the IV.3 sentence and the dropped word;
- *a gift, not a grave*;
- the ship-masters keep their faces to the sea;
- the child answers.

Nothing in it now needs a rite to explain it.

### 1.4 Harl turns twice (lines 68–76, against VI.1)

In VI.1 (`_g09_VI1.md` l.55–57) Harl has already made his turn. His mason lays the charred plank on the crust, and Harl says *I did not burn it.* In VI.3 he says *"Burn it."* again of the first plank, *what he had said of every plank of the war*, and turns a second time (*No more said Harl. From that day he carried planks.*). The hearer gets the same man and the same turn on two nights running, and the second undoes the first. Jack has complained of exactly this, a refrain that does not change (`craft.md` §1 rule 22).

**Rewrite.** VI.3 pays VI.1 with a deed, and with the word the hearer waits for and does not get:

> Out of the vault Harl bare the first plank on his shoulder. Every plank of the war had he bidden us burn. This one he laid at Tarnard's feet, and said naught. Tarnard laid his palm flat on it.
>
> "Wood that knoweth the sea will know the way home."

This also brings back v2.0's *set them at the feet of Tarnard*, which the draft cut. Tarnard's line now answers the silence, not a quarrel. Harl's last deed, walking the bow into the tide, stays as the draft has it. **Orchestrator:** if Harl's spoken *"Burn it."* must stay in VI.3 (R6b lists it among the must-survives), VI.1's *I did not burn it.* has to change. One turn, in one tale.

### 1.5 The close explains Ebba's habit again instead of paying I.5 (lines 150–154)

*We waited, I think, for old Ebba of Fenholm, that on most nights began the answer a breath before the rest.* is at least the fifth telling of that habit:
- I.5 (`_g03/I5.md` l.61);
- the III.1 frame (g05 l.83);
- V.5 (g08 l.153);
- VI.1's Fenholm fire;
- and here.

Meanwhile the I.5 writer planted the image this close was made for, and nobody pays it: *there hath been a space at the front of it, the width of one old voice. None of us have tried to fill it.* The child fills it.

*Then spake a child near the front, not loudly: the fisher-girl that went first into the sea.* The apposition after the colon reads like an entry in a register.

**Rewrite.**

> When we three took our hands from the stone, the hearth was silent a long while. The fire burned down, and none rose to lay wood on it. We waited, I think, the width of one old voice. Ebba of Fenholm is under the east cairns.
>
> Then a child near the front spake, not loudly. It was the girl that went first into the sea. Old Ebba was her great-grandmother.
>
> "We remember."

*The fire burned down, and none rose to lay wood on it* also echoes, without a word, I.5's account of Ebba's last night: *In the night the fire burned down, and no one mended it.* It is the best thing in the close. Keep it exactly.

### 1.6 A cross-reference kept against craft, and a lock line rewritten inside it (line 54)

`craft.md` §2.3, VI.3: "Cut: the three cross-references back into the Book." The table in craft §4.2 gives *So it was seed* "with no quotations around it". The draft keeps one quotation and introduces it with *the wood said to us once*, which is Seren footnoting her own Book at the moment of wonder.

It also changes the wood's line from a truth to a prophecy (*The iron will tire … and when it stoppeth, the wood will come again* for v2.0's *The iron tires; and when it stops, the wood comes again*). IV.2 would then have to match a recast made for VI.3. Meanwhile VI.2, the leaf the hearth heard just before this one, ends on *Since the iron we had put up no growth. We were waiting for the sky to come again.* (w02 l.237).

**Rewrite.** One sentence of Seren's own, then the payoff:

> Since the iron came, no pillar had put up so much as a leaf.
>
> So it was seed.

This removes the ask on IV.2 in the writer's notes §4. IV.2 keeps its own wording.

### 1.7 The rite is defined at the kneeling (line 128)

*"That is the first step of the Guest's rite, the gift and the kneeling, and the only step one people can take alone."* / *"The rest needeth the other."*

The first half is a definition of 24 words spoken at the climax. Against a second half of four words, the halves are not balanced. In IV.4 the rite's meaning arrives through what is done, never through a label (voice §5; craft §5.1). The hearer has just watched the gift and the kneeling. Let the second ink turn the first:

> "So far can one people go alone: the gift, and the kneeling."
>
> "The rest needeth the other."

## 2 · The rest, numbered

1. **Line 50, *Thrice*.** This is a one-off antique (voice §2.4): V.1 says *three times*, and no other leaf uses *thrice* in prose. Echo V.1's words for Seren's habit: *Three times I said it over ere I wrote it, and it held.* (craft §3.6: shown whole in V.1, echoed here).
2. **Line 160, *They call it* The Two Homes.** *They* has no antecedent: *the hearth* is singular, and v2.0's *the Rivenmen* is cut. Write *We call it* The Two Homes, since Seren is of the hearth.
3. **Line 84: *and I held the lamp* is missing at the cutting of the mark.** The Epilogue depends on it: *Twice had I held the lamp to it… when it cut the same mark across this stem* (g11 l.102). *The lamp burned down a finger's width* implies the lamp but does not put it in Seren's hand. It is also her one claim on the leaf as the third, the one who carries the light (V.1: *the light was mine to carry*).
4. **Line 110, the chisels.** *that took the last of the light on the day of the banner* is a gloss with a time-stamp. The sending is at the evening tide, so let the I.2 image happen again, unglossed: *Then they laid their two chisels by it, and in the bow the chisels took the last of the light.* This is the Chekhov payoff, the beginning and the end of {{Halyna}} in one picture. The draft has three *last*s in four lines (*Last they laid… the last of the light… our last mark*). Keep them only if they are meant as a toll. The sketch uses *Then they laid* to keep two.
5. **Lines 50 and 108, pronoun-subject inversions.** *Then gave they me the other thing* is good Early Modern and can stay. *Beside it set they a Stonwryt coin* is a second one six paragraphs later and clanks. Write *Beside it they set*. Inversion should carry weight (voice §1.5).
6. **Line 58, *In the ward ran out a fisher-girl*.** She ran *into* the ward: *Into the ward ran a fisher-girl, and asked whither it would go.* She has four beats in all: the question, the sea to her waist, the answer, and the Epilogue's hands on the bow. Optionally, give her one noun of her own at first sight so the later beats land on a person and not a role. Only one, and no biography.
7. **Lines 15–17, the Captain in three paragraphs.** Join them: *Toward evening came the Captain down to it himself, and stood long beside it with his hood up. Then he looked to {{Halyna}}.* *Then he looked to {{Halyna}}*, the reverse of I.1's *The True Men looked to their Captain*, is a good mirror. Keep it.
8. **Line 108, *It was our dearest gift, as the dark wood was the Guest's.*** This decodes the coin for the hearer. It is inherited and short, so it may stay. IV.4's *the dearest is a sliver of such wood* already makes a quiet rhyme with the green sliver in the bow, and the line should not reach further than that.
9. **Line 122, Kael *that will not sit to tell a tale*.** This is a good joke and earns its place. It is the leaf's one tag at the climax, so add no others: no masons' aprons, no *oath-keeper*, no *Captain of the Torn Cloak*.
10. **Line 74, Tarnard's line.** *Wood that knoweth the sea will know the way home.* Accept it. The *knoweth / know* play does what IV.4's *forgat / forgetteth* does, with one *-eth*. Note that the Epilogue draft (g11) does not currently quote it, so the writer's ask that the Epilogue must quote it in this form is moot unless the orchestrator wants the quotation.
11. **Line 100, *He taught it me on the Road, a knot to a mile*.** Keep it. It is memory theme 4 done exactly right: the dead man's habit, not his death. II.3 (g04 l.124–126) has already said that Tarnel was Seren's cradle-mate and that this Book is bound with his knot, so the knot in the bow carries everything without a word more. Do not add one.
12. **The memory theme** is light and felt throughout:
    - 4: the knot, and the width of Ebba's voice;
    - 3: the answer passed from great-grandmother to great-granddaughter;
    - 1: the child choosing to speak.

    Nothing is preached. Leave it as it is after the §1.5 fix.
13. **The token against VI.1's fates.** This is the orchestrator's call, not the writer's, and it is present in v2.0 too. VI.3 says the tenth Throne *lay where it fell, black of grain and split open*, and that {{Halyna}} *went in*. In VI.1 most Thrones do not end that way:
    - the Tide-Heart *sank into the sea… and rose not*;
    - the Bearer of Branches *went down where the middle span had stood*;
    - the Grain That Rots went into the fen;
    - the Root-Heart *would not rise* and was *left standing*;
    - the Stone-Grain *crumbled*;
    - the Deep-Beneath *burst… and broke*;
    - the Burning was covered by the crust.

    A player whose last Throne is one of these will see the contradiction. Either make VI.3's image fit any fate (*Where the tenth Throne lay…*, with the heart reached among its timbers), let the token carry a short clause of its own fate, or allow in VI.1 that the last one fell where a hand could go in.
14. **Line 9 against the Epilogue.** *Hold the stone, Seren.* has no quotation marks here, and the Epilogue has them (g11 l.11). Set one form for the whole Book.
15. **Line 11, *Each was dead wood*.** Keep it. It is the expectation that makes *It was green.* land, and the leaf's question (is anything in that wood alive?) is raised by the 60th word without being asked.
16. **Lines 50–51, three names for one people in two sentences** (*mist-folk*, *Naelear*, *Mystaeri*). This is heavy for a first reader but justified, because it pays II.2's blank and shows Seren's love of names. Leave it.
17. **No Plain Words twin.** The leaf is not finished for Jack's two tabs until it has one (voice §11). When it is written, watch three things:
    - The dropped *burning* cannot be explained in the Plain either. *It turned into the wind, against the current, and went into the grey* is enough.
    - *The width of one old voice* must carry over in plain words, as *the space Ebba's voice used to fill*.
    - {{Halyna}}'s halves must stay halves.
18. **Budget.** The sketch comes to 1,161 words (10.7% under the ceiling). If Jack misses something cut since v2.0, there is room to bring back one clause, for example *the masons kneeled in their aprons* in the kneeling's widening. The song after the Amen is outside the count, as before.
