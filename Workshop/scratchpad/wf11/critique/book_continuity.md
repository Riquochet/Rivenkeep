# Whole-Book continuity reading · book_v3_draft.md

*Read against wf9/reckoning.md, wf9/cast.md, wf9/puzzle.md, wf9/outline.md (§3.5 play order, §5, §6, §7, §10), wf11/research/R6a and R6b, wf11/voice_v3.md (§1, §6, §7) and wf11/craft.md (§2.2, §3). Line numbers are those of wf11/book_v3_draft.md as it stands. I diffed every song, fixed line and `::: native` block against wf10/book_v2.md, and ran count_v3.py on every tale (results at the end).*

**In short.** The draft holds together well. Every dateline agrees with the play order. Every death falls in its place, and no one speaks after their death. There is no custom of burning anywhere. No wood leaf names a shore thing it could not know. There is no singular *they*, and no pair-name takes a singular verb. Every native key and every marker is present and balanced, and every song and every one of Jack's lines is word for word. No tale is over budget. The breaks are few, and most were brought in when v2.0 was cut down: a seed was dropped while its payoff stayed, or a payoff was changed while its seed stayed. Fixes 1–8 must be made. Fixes 9–12 are frame and caps. Fixes 13–15 are grammar against voice §1.

I simulated all the fixes together on a copy of the draft and re-ran the counter. Every affected tale stays within budget. The new figures are given with each fix.

## A · Breaks (must fix)

**1. Epilogue, L2773: a payoff without a seed (Halvard's name in the lintel).**
- **The problem.** The Epilogue says *Beneath Halvard's name in the lintel of the western stair the stone is smooth*, but no tale ever has Halvard cut his name. II.1 (L729), laid in the first summer, says the opposite: *beneath the last the stone is smooth*, and the last name there is an older warden's. v2.0 carried the seed inside the Epilogue (*which he cut there with his own hand in the year the havens came home*). The v3 cut dropped that clause and kept the payoff.
- **The fix.** Replace the second sentence of L2773 with:
  > The year the havens came home, Halvard cut his name under his fathers' in the western stair. Beneath it the stone is smooth, and none of his line is left to cut it.
- **The offset**, so the Epilogue stays under 900. At L2807, cut *, with the grain yet in it,* so the line reads *Or it was stone that had been wood, like the Guest's gift.* The native caption already says the stone is "cut across a grain".
- **Count after:** Epilogue 898 of 900.

**2. III.2, L1032 · L1042 · L1094 · L1104: the tale-stone is passed from hand to hand, though it takes two to turn.**
- **The problem.** The foreword (L97) says *It is a stone that two must turn*, and II.1 (L725) says *Turn it I cannot, for it is a stone that taketh two.* Every other leaf has the teller lay hands on the stone where it sits: I.2 L295, II.1 L765, II.3 L856, IV.3 L1404, VI.1 L2502 and VI.3 L2729. puzzle §4.1 asked for exactly this. Only III.2's four stage lines move the capstone down a bench. (The formula *Give me the stone* is the hearth's call and may stay.)
- **The fix:**
  - L1032 → *Then each laid his hand on the stone in his turn, Corlen first.*
  - L1042 → *Corlen took his hand from the stone for Tamm. It was the last time he held it.*
  - L1094, last sentence → *His place at the stone he gave to me.*
  - L1104 → *Kael laid his hands on it again, and told the Road.* (This matches VI.1 L2502.)
- **Count after:** III.2 1,196 of 1,200.

**3. V.7 Part I, L2273: *Ael* was not written "once before".**
- **The problem.** Seren says *Ael I had written once before, in Ael'thar.* By the fourth summer, though, she had written *Aelrhen* and *Ael'thar* (IV.4, the third spring) and *Aelvaren* (IV.6, the third summer). She had also written the whole name *Aelthae* in V.6 (L2182, *Aelthae is ours*), which was laid in the fourth spring, a season before this part. v2.0 admitted this (*which I had first written that spring*). v3 compressed the line into a falsehood.
- **The fix.** Delete *once*, so the line reads *Ael I had written before, in Ael'thar.* That keeps the beat: she wrote the name in spring, and only that night did she know its first half.
- **Count after:** V.7 1,293.

**4. V.5 Count VI, L2096: *The wall named them for beasts* is a dangling seed, and I.5's *Mystarch* has no source.**
- **The problem.** The beast-names (Leviathan, Hydra and the rest) were rightly cut from VI.2, so this line now points at nothing. Meanwhile I.5 (L659) has Ebba speak of *the last Mystarch*, a word found nowhere else in the Book.
- **The fix.** L2096 → *The wall named them Mystarchs.* This plants Ebba's word in the same winter she speaks it. Leave *Throne* as Seren's word, and the wood's: VI.2 has *grew us into thrones*.
- **Alternative**, if the soldiers' name is wanted too: *The wall named them Mystarchs, and the soldiers, Thrones.*
- **Count after:** V.5 1,385.

**5. VI.3, L2661: *All but one had spoken to us once* contradicts V.2.**
- **The problem.** V.2 says *After that every plank spake* (L1777). That includes the silent half, which spoke with Halvard *word for word*, and the Burn plank. And the Burn plank is no longer in the vault anyway: VI.1 (L2446) sent it to Emberhythe.
- **The fix.** L2661 → *Of the vault's planks they built it. Every one had spoken to us once, and now they would speak for us.*

**6. VI.3, L2657: Harl's *Burn it, he had said of every plank of the war* has no seed.**
- **The problem.** v3 never has Harl say *Burn it* of the war's wreck-wood. III.2's *I told him to burn it* is said once, in the Fall. V.1 (L1683) gives Harl the deed but not the words. R6b §0.1 #3 asked for the plant here.
- **The fix.** L1683: replace *Harl of the burning shore broke a rib of it across his knee, and fed it in.* with:
  > "Burn it," said Harl of the burning shore. He broke a rib of it across his knee, and fed it in.

  This is his temper, not a custom. No one else says it, and nothing is said over the fire.
- **Count after:** V.1 842 of 850.

**7. I.4 L587 and Epilogue L2837: the two halves of the dust echo disagree.**
- **The problem.** I.4 has Jack's line, *If you doubt the tale, go and look at the dust on it.* (outline §10.2 lists "the dust" as one of Jack's lines, kept verbatim). The Epilogue's answer to it reads *If ye doubt the tale, go and look at the dust.*
- **The fix.** At L2837, write *If you doubt the tale, go and look at the dust.* The fixed line stays outside the dial in both places, and the echo lands word for word.
- **If the orchestrator rules the line archaizable** (voice §1.7 does not list it), make I.4 *ye* instead. Either way, the two must match.

**8. V.5 Count IV, L2048: the third road does not match the carving.**
- **The problem.** Kael has the sails come *north and east and the river mouth*. The carving that ordered them (V.7 L2261) names *the east and the north … the south*, and Count IV itself ends with the south turning in *on the east road*.
- **The fix.** L2048 → *At first light the sails came on three roads at once, north and east and south.*

## B · Frame and caps

**9. V.7 dateline, L2204: nine words, over the 8-word cap (voice §6, craft §3.1).**
- *The fourth summer, the fifth spring, the sixth winter.* → *The fourth summer, fifth spring and sixth winter.* (8 words)

**10. III.1, L1012: a frame line stacked after the Amen.**
- **The problem.** *Old Ebba of Fenholm began it, a breath before the rest.* comes after *And the hearth said: We remember.* voice §6 allows only IV.3's lines, the hood, the waits for Ebba, and VI.3's child, and says "Nothing else is stacked after the Amen". The line also repeats the foreword (L112, *a breath before the rest, and in behind her we come*), which the hearth has only just heard.
- **The fix.** Cut L1012. Ebba's first *We remember* stays in the foreword, and her waits stay in I.5, VI.1 and VI.3.

**11. V.5 headnote, L1966: it contradicts its own dateline.**
- **The problem.** The dateline says *At each Tide's end, for six winters*, and the counts speak from inside each year (*All that winter…*; *When Brenn had been read…*). The headnote says *In the sixth winter he brought me all six himself*, which suggests none was laid until the sixth winter.
- **The fix.** → *Laid by Kael the Counter, that would tell no Tide till it was over. In the sixth winter he bade me mend all six, as I had mended his first; I have mended them less.* (headnote 35 words)

**12. Epilogue headnote, L2756: the first-night note could not have been written on the first night.**
- **The problem.** *From the first night that leaf stood empty, with the grain drawn on it and one line in mine hand: Untold. {{Halyna}} cannot yet hold this deep.* I.1 was laid on night 15. The first knowing of any wood (V.1) came about a week later, and the facing-leaf rule is first named in the foreword, in the third summer. So on that night Seren had no reason to write *cannot yet hold*.
- **The fix.** → *From the first night that leaf stood empty. Later I drew the grain on it, and one line in mine hand:* (headnote 47 of 50 words)

## D · Checked and clean (no change)

- **Timeline and datelines.** All 30 datelines agree with the play order in outline §3.5, and with reckoning §1.3's rule of one Tide each winter:
  - the foreword in the third summer;
  - I.1 on the night after the Rite, and I.2 after the first assault, with *this hearth had heard but one before it*;
  - V.1 at the first month's end, and I.3 in its second month;
  - I.4, II.1, II.3 and III.1 through the first year;
  - III.2, IV.1, IV.5 and V.2 in the second year;
  - IV.3, IV.2 (whole), V.3, IV.4 and IV.6 in the third;
  - V.4, V.6 and V.7 I in the fourth;
  - II.2 and V.7 II in the fifth;
  - V.7 III, Count VI and I.5 in the sixth winter, then VI.1–VI.3;
  - the Epilogue four years on.

  The reckoning's muddles are all resolved in the text:
  - A1: the Rite falls on day 14, the Naming a week later, and the assault after that.
  - A2: the last elder's death cuts the sending loose (V.4).
  - A3: a wood's years are counted at its choosing (V.6, IV.2: *Forty summers had we stood*).
  - A4: the Thrones come at the end of the sixth winter (Count VI, I.5).
  - A5: IV.1 holds only what the rolls hold.
  - A6: no shore names appear in the wood.
  - A7: *Six winters have I told the grey sails* says the siege's length.
  - A8: the burning is the warden's order, *Leave naught to show*.
  - B4: V.1's *the grey man of the old tale* comes from the quay rumour, not from Brenn.
  - B5: *The year of the homecomings*.
  - B6: *died ere their years*.
  - B9: the first staff.
  - B12: one archive chest.
  - C2: *I think those hulls were the youngest*.
  - C5: *in your havens the word was Burn*.
  - C8: the Epilogue is given a year.

  The dust of *seven winters* (from day 14 of the first winter to the seventh spring) is right.
- **Who is alive when.** Each death comes after every leaf that shows the person alive:
  - Jory (V.1, *sitteth tonight*) dies near the first winter's end.
  - Corlen speaks for the last time in III.2 and is under the cairns by the foreword.
  - Wick swears *Not as Brenn carried* in the third winter, then dies.
  - Enrella are *the youngest* in the foreword and die in the fourth summer.
  - Pellow dies in the fifth spring.
  - Ebba begins the Amen in IV.3 and dies after the Thrones come (I.5).
  - Hesk answers Count VI and dies at Rimewatch.
  - Halyna die in the seventh winter.
  - Idrenna, Orvenna, Merrick, Stannard, Tarnard, Ruan and Hale live on.

  Count VI's *fourteen names, and twelve to answer* and VI.1's *eleven* add up: the Twelve plus Voss and Ruan, less Corlen and Pellow, and then less Hesk.
- **Names and epithets.** All the Twelve and their epithets are the same in I.1, III.1, III.2, V.5 and VI.1. So are the ten havens' epithets, and the pair-names with their cradle-names: Ormund and Penna, Enno and Della, and Wendhessa's *his hand … hers*. Tarnel is now *a boatwright's son of Tidesmeet* (II.3), which agrees with Tarnard in VI.3. Each Throne's own name and its English matches between VI.2, VI.1 and the Knowings table, and each sits in its proper haven. The warden is ▒▒▒▒ throughout, and "the master of that house" or "the host" in the wood. The Captain is never named.
- **The roads of the objects.**
  - *The torn cloak and its other half:* I.1 → I.2/I.4 *his half of a cloak* → the strips, each told once (Jory *the first strip*; Corlen *the second*; Wick, Enrella *his first for Stonewrights*, Pellow, Ebba) → Count VI *cannot reach his belt* → the Epilogue *the last of his half*, and *the torn cloak flieth yet*.

  - *The staffs:* the law of the staff (I.4, III.2) → ten staffs left behind → the first staff in the gatehouse → gone in the seventh spring.
  - *The archive chest:* Wendhessa → Tarnel and Seren → Seren alone → the rolls, the mute stones and the sealed bundle → at the end, the Book, the gift and the Stone.
  - *The sealed bundle:* Idrenna's leave (the foreword) → IV.3.
  - *The gift:* the dust → Brenn's pack → IV.4's road in the senses → II.2 → VI.3 *as the dark wood was the Guest's* → the Epilogue.
  - *The planks:* the raw edge (V.1) → the fifty-three, *Burn*, the halves meeting (V.2) → Burn laid at Emberhythe (VI.1, Knowings) → the stem (VI.3) → known by its stem (the Epilogue).
  - *The green sliver:* VI.3 → the Epilogue's bow.
  - *The Stonwryt marks:*
    - Aldwena's: the foreword, I.4 and II.1;

- **No custom of burning.**

  - IV.5: plain fear spread by the men from the spar and the outpost men.
  - V.1: the wood is burned because *wood was dear on the ridge*.
  - VI.3 and the Epilogue: the ship-masters' fear only; no fire beat.
- **The wood knows only what wood could know.** No wood leaf uses a Shore name, a date or a Shore motive. What the wood knows of the havens comes through roots, blood, the watching, or the wood's hands, and *the host drew back. We know not why* stays the wood's to say.
- **Pronouns and pair grammar.** No singular *they*. No pair-name takes a singular verb. Halyna say *we two* everywhere, and no tag "one said, and the other finished" survives.
- **Locks.**
  - The cross-sign is *the half of something, cut off in the middle*; there is no *holy* and no *Lord* (Idrenna's prayer now says *God*).
  - Nothing reveals the shared ancestry of the two tongues, and the parting stays untold (*Against what, the slates say not.*).
  - The Captain says only the I.2 question and the calls.
  - Halyna discover, Seren writes, other hands act.
  - No one is aboard the grey fleet.
- **Songs and fixed lines.** All six songs, the drill, the chant and the cradle-rhyme match v2.0 word for word. So do:
  - the seven Hasty orders;
  - the V.7 carvings;
  - the ten judgements and the Knowings table, whose 46 carvings recount correctly (7 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 7 + 8);
  - the Guest's sentence, the warning, the letter and the Title;
  - every line in outline §10.1–10.2.

  The archaized non-Jack lines are consistent within the draft: Stannard's, Kael's I.3 mend, Tarnard's, Ebba's, and *The iron will tire*.
- **Markup.**
  - Every native key that v2.0 uses is present, and each block is identical to v2.0's except the Wave chip, which now has caption and English.
  - The inline keys ⟦legend_naelear_chip⟧, ⟦legend_iv2_gap1⟧ and ⟦legend_iv2_gap2⟧ are present.
  - HALYNA / HALYNA END are balanced: 20 pairs, none unclosed.
  - Each of the 8 wood leaves has one READING block of 7–9 em-dash lines (all at 80 words or fewer; IV.2's is 57 without its note block) and exactly two RINGs.
  - PROLOGUE EXCERPT BEGIN/END, FACING LEAF, TOKEN, both RIPENS, MARKED, MIXED and all W markers are in place. v2.0's *W: the three italic lines only* went out with the three lines in VI.3, correctly.
