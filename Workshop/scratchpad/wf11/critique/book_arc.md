# Whole-Book Reader · The Arc

*Read: `wf11/book_v3_draft.md` (2,918 lines, about 28,100 words of tales proper), straight through as a first-time reader, then again as Jack. Line numbers below are the draft's. I measured with `_tools_v3/count_v3.py` on each leaf (split copies in `critique/_arc/leaves/`) and with simple greps. Every rewrite keeps Jack's verbatim lines and the R6 "must survive" lines. Where a fix touches an R6 or outline "keep" line, I say so, and the orchestrator decides. Locks checked Book-wide and holding: no custom of burning; Brenn only watched; ▒▒▒▒ only; no holy mark; no singular "they"; pair-names plural; the Captain unnamed; no one aboard the fleet; ancestry and parting untold.*

---

## The verdict

**The Book builds now.** v2.0's run-ons are gone. Tales average 8.6 to 12.5 words a sentence, half the sentences are under ten words, and only five in the tales proper run past thirty. The tellers are distinct men again, and Seren's hand shows on nearly every leaf. Book Four is the spine and it holds from the felling through the boat's homecoming: the Wound lands. The end lands too. From the green sliver to the child's *"We remember."*, through Halyna's grave and the Last Note's three stones, a first-time reader is carried.

**Where Jack will still say "clinical", and why.** It is no longer the sentences. It is three habits that repeat across tales:
1. **Explaining the mirror after showing it.** Seren and {{Halyna}} do this at almost every great turn of the last third: V.1, V.7, VI.3 and the Epilogue.
2. **The war's machinery told as a key or a manual.** This shows in V.2's glosses, V.5's tips, V.6's law and tempers, and every wood leaf re-explaining the roots.
3. **The forgetting theme said outright in Books One and Two** before anything has made the reader feel it.

**Build.** One structural fault spends Book Five's climax early: V.5 tells what V.7 will discover.

**Length.** The Book is at a master's length overall: 28,100 words against v2.0's 52,000 and craft's 29,000. But it is written to its ceilings. Seventeen of 29 leaves sit within 2.5% of their cap, and voice §7 asks for 5–10% under. The twenty fixes below take out about 340 words, and the list after them about 40 more. Almost all of it is explanation, and none of it is an element. That brings the Book to about 5% under its ceilings (from about 28,280 to about 27,900, against 29,350).

## The questions, answered

**Does it build?** Yes, with two dips. The first is Book Two, where nothing is at stake in the present. The second is Book Five's middle, V.2 to V.6, where the enemy's workings are explained before they are felt. The single worst arc fault is #1.

**Which tales still read clinical, and where?**
- II.1 at l.727 (the Stonwryt defined).
- II.2's second ring (l.812–822).
- II.3 at l.890 and l.904.
- III.1 at l.936.
- IV.1 at l.1200–1206.
- V.2's glosses (l.1780–1790).
- V.4 at l.1932–1938.
- V.5 at l.2004 and l.2068.
- V.6 at l.2154–2172.
- Jack's three lore lines, set bare as narration (#7).

None of these is a whole tale. Each is a paragraph that stops the story to define something.

**Is any tale too long for what it carries?** These four carry less than their length:
- **V.6** (878 words) tells its law of the years in prose and then sings it.
- **The foreword** (847 of 850) spends III.2's naming and makes a promise the Book breaks.
- **I.4** (1,196 of 1,200) runs 360 words after its climax.
- **VI.3** (1,201) doubles three of its payoffs and glosses four.

V.5 (1,388) is long but carries six deaths and six lessons. It is not padded; it is mis-placed (#1).

**Do the twelve deaths land?** Ten land fully. Two are thin: Wendhessa, whose death cannot be read (#9), and Wick, planted once 15,000 words before he dies (#10). The good of the dead is remembered at every death, and memory theme 4 is the Book's strongest nuance.

**Is the memory theme felt without being preached?** It is felt, and it is preached early.
1. **Chosen.** Said once, in the foreword: *Remembering we choose, as masons choose which stones to keep.* Exactly right.
2. **Forgetting brings ruin.** The Invocation says it nightly, as liturgy (v1.3's, rightly kept). After that, I.4 and II.1 state it four more times before Book Four lets it be felt (#5). Where it is felt it is superb:
   - IV.1: *Some of you that sit at this fire were those children.*
   - IV.3: *I carried the truth forty years, and gave it to no one.*
   - IV.3: *The Council kept the letter.*
   - V.4: *Learn their words.*
   - V.6: *and their own forgat them*
   - the Last Note's pipes knocked on the three words.
3. **Handed down as rite.**
   - *This I lay as it was laid for me.*
   - *Many hands have held it, and many shall.*
   - the child's leaf in I.4
   - VI.3's child answering
   - *I say both halves alone.*

   This is light and right. #12 would make it one person.
4. **The good remembered.** At every death (table above).

**Is the old English consistent and learnable?** The grammar is clean. The only pronoun slip I found is *If you doubt* (l.587), against the Epilogue's *If ye doubt*. My greps found no *-eth* on a plural, no singular verb on a pair, no contraction and no *upon*. The main stock comes back often enough to be learned:
- *ere* 46
- *naught* 43
- *spake* 32
- *wist* 19
- *bare* 19
- *sware* 13
- *morrow* 12
- *kneeled* 11
- *forgat* 9
- *bade* 7
- *yet* 59

A reader on page 40 reads faster than on page 1. Three things work against that (#16):
- **The small stock is met too rarely to learn** (R3: fewer than eight meetings and the word is lost): *aught* 1, *thither* 1, *bide* 2, *scarce* 2, *lest* 2, *nigh* 3; *oft*, *yonder*, *hither* and *sore* never appear.
- **The wood thins late.** Old stock per 100 words is 1.8 in II.2 and 1.0 in IV.4, but 0.4 in V.4 and 0.7 in VI.2, and voice §4 gives the wood the oldest grammar in the Book.
- **The difficulty moves from grammar to names.** VI.1 and VI.2 bring twenty proper names in 2,000 words, and five *Myst-* compounds each appear once (#14).

**Is Seren's playfulness visible?** Yes, on most leaves, and it is the Book's charm. Some of her best:
- the lintel not cut before the posts are raised (foreword)
- the comma (I.2)
- *Four hands laid it in two.* (I.4)
- *point*, *I love that word* (II.1)
- *fortune* giving like mortar (IV.1)
- *on* and *went on* (IV.3)
- *leaf on leaf* (IV.4)
- *The wall called them the tablets. They were but planks.* (V.2)
- *So rise the far knots of a net with the near* (V.2)
- *I know their number, and I will not tell it at this hearth. But I will tell ours.* (V.5)
- *A mile of wall is far.* (V.7)
- *From the shore I told his steps.* (VI.1)
- *Hold them, for they were ours.* (VI.2)
- *Rhyna wept, and Halvard did not, for she was weeping for them both.* (VI.3)
- *I say both halves alone.* (Epilogue)

It goes missing where the machinery is: III.1's opening, V.3 and V.6. #4 and #17 put a turn of hers in place of a gloss.

**The refrains that build. Protect these; they are the arc's sinews.**
- *Hold them, for they are ours*, which moves from the havens (III.1) to the wrong (IV.1), the dead (V.5) and *ours again* (VI.1), and then into the wood's own mouth (VI.2).
- *There he sitteth*, with his hood up in I.1, again in V.5, and in VI.3 *the wet of the shingle is dark yet on his knees*.
- *I came into that courtyard alone* (I.2), which returns as *where I came in alone* (V.7).
- *The width of one old voice*: I.5, then VI.3.
- *The sea hath not finished*: III.1, III.2, V.5, VI.1, the Epilogue.
- The strip of cloak, *to his knees*, then *cannot reach his belt*, then *the last of his half*.
- The lamp burning down a finger's width: V.1, then VI.3, then Seren alone in the Last Note.
- The laughter: the warden's men, the shore, *God forgive us, we laughed*, the whole wall laughing at the decoy.

## The worst twenty

### 1 · Book Five spends its climax before it reaches it (V.5 against V.7)

*l.2056–2060, 2080–2092; V.7 l.2208–2372.*

In reading order, V.5 already does three things before V.7 begins:
- names Enno and Della dead in the east and north breaches (Count IV);
- names Pellow under the cairns (Count V);
- gives away the war's turn (Count VI): *each fleet came with a morrow carved in it, and a turning for home at the end*, and *Now I know. It cannot be called home. But it can be sent home.*

Then V.7 tells all of it as discovery. Knowing the deaths ahead of time does V.7 Part I good: dread instead of suspense, and *A mile of wall is far* cuts deeper when we know. But knowing the turn kills Part III. *Yet it was the last two words that held us* has nothing left to hold. Count IV also keeps *I will not say more*, the very line craft rule 13 retired so that V.7's *"One."* would be said once in the Book.

**Rewrite (every element kept):**
- Count IV ends: *Ere dusk I walked the wall to count. Enno of the guild I found in the east breach, and a mile on, in the north, Della.* Cut *With that hour I count them. / I will not say more.*
- Count VI opens: *This winter, when a fleet lost its bearer, and the second, it went home. Not scattered. Went.*
- And: *I answered that we would send it home, and wist not what I meant. It cannot be called home. But it can be sent home.* (cut *Now I know.*)

Kael reports what the wall saw. V.7 Part III finds the carving and the hope. (−30 words)

**Stronger, for the orchestrator:** lay V.5 last in Book Five, after V.7. Count VI's roll of the dead then closes the Book of the Tides on *There he sitteth now, at the end of the bench.* The cost is that V.5 no longer faces V.6.

### 2 · At the last turns, Seren and {{Halyna}} explain the mirror they have just shown

*V.1 l.1725–1727; V.7 l.2358; VI.3 l.2616, 2679–2681, 2689, 2691, 2719, 2721; Epilogue l.2833.*

Craft is plain on this:
- §2.1.8: *Seren never says what it means.*
- Rule 7: pay *without a word of gloss*.
- Rule 14: *End on a picture, never on a moral.* Its own *before* is the V.7 line still standing here.

Across the last third, almost every great mirror is shown and then said. That is the textbook Jack heard, now at the very end where the reader needs it least.

**Cuts:**
- **V.1** close: *"The grey man of the old tale came to a door with three words, and no sentence."* / *"And we have one word."* This replaces *Now it is we who have one word, and no sentence. The right sign, known truly; and a sign without a sentence is only a mark.*, which is the third telling, after IV.3's *words without sentences* and IV.4's *We were the sentence*. (−19)
- **V.7 III**: cut *That night, for the first time, we saw the enemy hope.* The wives looking up when the fever-men ask for their boots already carries it. (−11)
- **VI.3**:
  - Cut the couplet *"It is in wood what our vow is in stone," / "harm to one is harm to both."*. Rhyna's palms under Halvard's hands, V.1's posture, already say it. (−17)
  - Write *It was our dearest gift.* in place of *…as the dark wood was the Guest's.* (−7)
  - Cut *This time it carried a gift, not a grave.* (−8)
  - Cut *A grey boat going out against the wind is the thing they fear most on all the water.*, so that *Six winters had they turned their faces from the sea when a hull burned.* is followed straight by *They did not look away.* (−18)
  - Two doublings go as well: *Every joint of the hull had {{Orvenna}} keyed as stone is keyed, that no sea should work it loose.*, said 26 lines after *{{Orvenna}} keyed them as stone is keyed* (−16); and the cross-reference *The green wood holds one word, the groves sang.* (−8).
- **Epilogue**: cut *It is like the Guest's three words, the right thing said truly by one that knew not yet how we say it.* Seren's wonder in the next sentence keeps the beat. (−22)

**For the orchestrator.** *A gift, not a grave*, the ship-masters' fear and the Guest's three words are R6b ★ lines. Every element they point at survives on the page, shown; only the sentence that names it goes. (−126 in all)

### 3 · Book Five's glosses read as a key to the enemy (V.2, V.5)

*l.1780–1790, 2004, 2068.*

V.2's seven orders are fixed. The glosses after them are not, and as written they are a key:
- *The lone hull that swerved, and was not followed.*
- *Those bent toward whichever gun had last spoken.*

V.2 opens on the hearth laughing (*They have picked the wrong fortress*). Let the glosses carry that laughter, so that *They had not picked anything. / They had been sent.* breaks something the reader watched grow.

**Rewrite (orders verbatim, glosses as the wall's memory):**

> *Break wall. First stone, only stone. Strike till it opens or wood is ash.* That was the closed square, their sign for the mute thing, stone. Those hulls had struck one wall till they sank, and we called them mad.
>
> *Go until struck. Where bark splits, turn aside.* That one swerved, and none followed it. We called it a coward.
>
> *Before bearer, thin wood. Thin wood falls; bearer keeps pace.* They were told to die first, and they died first.
>
> *Where it flashed, strike. Last flash, only flash.* Whatever gun spake last, to that they bent. We called it hunger.
>
> *Wait in grey, as seed waits out frost. When stone is still, come.* They came when we had done with looking.
>
> *Home when wounded. One bough falls; all turn, each by its own water.* At the first loss they scattered, and the wall cheered.

This is about the same length, and every joint is now a *but*.

**V.5 Count II.** *Choose which heart ye leave them.* is an R6b line. Keep it, but give it a hearer inside the tale so it is Kael's teaching and not a tip to the player: *To the young gunners I say it yet: choose which heart ye leave them.* (+7)

**Count V.** Four tells make a tutorial. Write them in two sentences: *Straw rode high, and left no wreck, and flinched not at a shell. A false rout kept its spacing, and a green hull that blanched was no green hull.* (−10)

### 4 · V.6 tells its law twice and its tempers as a stat line

*l.2154, 2162, 2168–2170.*

- **The law, told twice.** The law of the years is laid out in prose (*One season, one sign. Forty summers, a phrase. A shore-man's life, a sentence… The eldest pillars hold a judgement.*). Twenty lines later The Song of the Years, which is fixed and better, sings the same law. Cut the prose list. The paragraph then ends on *Against that law no council can carve.* (−28)
- **The tempers.** The list opens on a count: *Nine grains there were, and the first was too young for any temper.* Rewrite: *In grief were the bearers chosen, and we were chosen so. Each grain bare its grief its own way, save the green, that was too young to have a way.* The *Some… Some…* litany that follows can then read as sorrow, which R6b (f) asked for, and not as a roster.

This is the leaf Jack is likeliest to call a manual. It sits at 878 words and should be nearer 800.

### 5 · The forgetting theme is preached in Books One and Two, before anything has made it felt

*I.4 l.530; II.1 l.743–745.*

The Invocation says it nightly, which is right: it is the rite handing the theme on. Then:
- **I.4** adds *The first fathers had known better, and we had forgotten them.* It is muddy (better than what?) and it edges toward the untold parting of the peoples.
- **II.1** says it twice in a row (*What man could not destroy, time had withered. / Forgetting had done what no enemy ever could.*), turns on it, and closes on it.

memory_theme.md asks for one line, felt.

**Rewrite:**
- **I.4**: end the paragraph on *The first builders had known war. Against what, the slates say not.* (−10)
- **II.1**: *No one heard it fall. / The Fall of the havens the whole coast heard. After Glasspire we climbed to the one place the songs said would hold, and found a shadow. / Forgetting had done what no enemy ever could.* The rivers simile and *What man could not destroy…* go. The second is an outline keep, but its content is the ★ line's content. The mirror already on the page (*No one heard it fall.* / *The Fall of the havens the whole coast heard.*) then stands clean, with the ★ line straight after it. (−24)

### 6 · V.4: the wood steps out of the grove to judge itself, and knows a Shore fact

*l.1932–1938.*

After *And the patience broke.*, the wood argues its own case:
- *We that heard it know the difference. More could we have learned, for we live long…*
- *Nor asked we who else dwelt along that shore, in the ten havens, that had never heard our name.*

*The ten havens* is a Shore fact, and a wood leaf holds only what the wood could know. The sentence also repeats Book Five's Argument (Jack's *against a people who had never known they were the foe*), which the reader read three leaves earlier. *So the vote was carried* is council-house English, not a grove's.

**Rewrite:**

> Their answer was true, and not the whole of the truth. Long we listened from behind the breath, and never once went down to the shore with our hands open. Save once. When the one that went was killed, we sent not a second.
>
> Whether a whole people had killed Aelrhen, or one house at its edge, we asked not. We chose to grow hulls, and not to learn words.
>
> But war is for the short-minded and the angry young.
>
> So it was carried, by the weight of anger and grief.

This keeps Jack's line, *Save once*, the hulls-and-words mirror and R6b's *by the weight of anger and grief*. (−37)

### 7 · Jack's own lore lines stand bare in Seren's narration, and read as definitions

*I.2 l.353; I.4 l.566; II.1 l.727.*

Three of Jack's verbatim lines are definitions:
- *with the speed and precision that only Aetherbonded pairs could achieve*
- the staff, *a rod of polished wood capped with the finest polished ashlar, borne by every overseer of a great Stonewright work since the founding*
- the Stonwryt, *a vow sealed in living rock, earned by toil and skill … trust, sealed in stone*

Set as Seren's own sentences, they are the most modern English in her prose (*precision*, *achieve*), and each stops the tale to explain. The warden's letter shows how the Book handles another register: it is visibly quoted from a document. Do the same here. Keep each line word for word, but mark it as the guild's own formula.

- **I.2:** *But they would build between the volleys, and mend a breach under the guns. So the terms stand yet, in the guild's own words:* with the speed and precision that only Aetherbonded pairs could achieve. *Then {{Idrenna}} gave it him in two halves:* (+7)
- **I.4:** *{{Idrenna}} held out the staff of the guild.* A rod of polished wood capped with the finest polished ashlar, borne by every overseer of a great Stonewright work since the founding: *so the slate nameth it. This was the first of all. A long while the Captain looked on it ere he put out his hands.* Split this way, no sentence passes thirty words. (+3)
- **II.1:** A vow sealed in living rock, earned by toil and skill: *so the masters teach the Stonwryt, and no gold buyeth it. Yet the master-pairs of the havens struck their marks on coin, and men took the coin as they took the mark:* trust, sealed in stone. *I have no mark. Never shall I cut one.*

*I have no mark* in place of *The unbonded have no mark* plants Seren's loss a leaf before II.3 tells it. (±0)

### 8 · Book Two's two lore leaves: II.2 lists, and II.3 says aloud what {{Idrenna}}'s nod shows

Two lines leave. *The wood cannot lie.* goes because the foreword's saying gives it and V.7 pays it. *The thunder is for the moment…* can stay if Seren must have one. (−25)

### 9 · Wendhessa's death cannot be read: whose hands, whose handles?

*IV.3 annal l.1398–1402.*

The chest passes to *the youngest, Tarnel and me* on the Road. Then: *On the night of the pass {{Wendhessa}} lay down under one cloak. At morning frost lay on both handles. Neither rose.*

A reader cannot tell whose hands the frost is on. *Under one cloak* is II.3's phrase for Tarnel and Seren, so the two deaths blur, and *Neither rose* seems to take Seren too. Yet this is the token memory_theme names first, and IV.4's road (*old arms, two pairs… Then small arms, two pairs, going up, in the cold. / Then one pair*) depends on it.

**Rewrite (`count_v3.py` gives the annal 149 words against its cap of 150, and IV.3's tale proper 1,296):**

> So far the words of Brenn. At every blot {{Halyna}} were silent.
>
> The warden's arm bare one mark: the half of something, cut off in the middle. The Council kept the letter.
>
> Brenn died, as a man setteth down a load. The guild buried him under his name. The warden's name it cut away.
>
> {{Wendhessa}} sealed his words, and told no one. At the Fall they bare the chest out, his hand on one handle, hers on the other. On the Road their arms failed. The masons said it held naught of worth, and gave it to the youngest, Tarnel and me. Tarnel knotted his net with his free hand. I watched the knots.
>
> On the night of the pass {{Wendhessa}} lay down, and rose not. By the chest Tarnel and I slept, a hand each on a handle. At morning frost lay on both.
>
> Tarnel did not wake.
>
> There I stopped tonight, with my hands on the stone. Then {{Halyna}} laid theirs beside them.

Now *She carried it every step, and after the passes she carried it alone* says exactly what the reader has seen.

### 10 · Two of the twelve are planted once: Wick and Wendhessa

*I.3 l.407; IV.3 l.1424; V.5 l.2024; foreword l.118.*

- **Wick** is met in I.3 and dies in V.5, some 15,000 words later. His second plant, swearing *Not as Brenn carried* loudest, sits inside his own death count. Move it to the moment it happened, in IV.3's closing gesture: *In the morning the runners sware their oath again, and added four words: Not as Brenn carried. Wick, the youngest, sware it loudest.* Count III then opens on *Ye knew Wick.* (−16) A boy swearing loudest the morning after a boy's failure is the best plant he could have. It stays inside IV.3's one gesture (voice §6).
- **Wendhessa** is first named in IV.3's own headnote. The foreword's seal sentence (*One came out of a dark stone, sealed in the chest at my feet. When the wood showed {{Halyna}} what the mute stones beside it were, {{Idrenna}} gave leave to break the seal.*) is opaque to a first-time reader. Let it plant the name and the carrying instead: *One tale is neither. It lay in the chest at my feet, under the seal of {{Wendhessa}}, that bare the chest out of Eldhythe at the Fall. No pair would break that seal till the wood showed {{Halyna}} what the mute stones beside it were. Then {{Idrenna}} gave leave.* *Neither* means neither the rolls' nor the wood's, and it marks Brenn's tale as different before the reader reaches it. (+17; #13 takes 19 words out of the same leaf, so the foreword ends at about 845.)

### 11 · The Epilogue ends four times

*l.2835–2843.*

After the Stone is read, the Epilogue closes four times:
- on the open gate and the child's light;
- on the staff gone and *I have not gone down to look*;
- on the chest, the grief, the cloak and the sea;
- on *sent kneeling*.

Olrik's tableau wants one last picture. Reorder, cutting nothing:

> No one came with it, and perhaps that is wise. We have not yet earned a guest.
>
> Ere that spring was out the staff was gone… I know *the stair that goes no more*.
>
> I have not gone down to look.
>
> In the archive chest I keep this Book… The sea hath not finished.
>
> Every night is the inner gate left open, as a guest would find it, and on the north wall a child keepeth a light.
>
> What we will send back, I know not. I know only that it must be sent kneeling.

The last picture is now an open door and a lamp, and *kneeling* follows from *guest*. In the same leaf, cut *No one on the shingle spake.* at l.2811, which repeats VI.3 word for word (#18).

### 12 · The Book's inheritance is split between two unnamed children; make them one

*VI.1 l.2484; VI.3 l.2643, 2731; Epilogue l.2793, 2835.*

A child lights the north lamp the night the ice takes Hesk (VI.1). A fisher-girl asks whither the sliver goes, wades in first, is Ebba's great-granddaughter and answers the wood (VI.3). The Epilogue's first child at the boat *was the same child*, and the Epilogue's *a child keepeth a light* could be either. R6b left this open (decision 4).

Merge them. One child then receives the Book's three inheritances: Hesk's light, Ebba's answer and the Guest's road home. That is memory theme 3 in one small body.
- VI.1: *Then a fisher-girl set one there, and lit it, and said, "Someone must keep a light."*
- VI.3: *Into the ward ran a fisher-girl of the east keep, she that kept the north lamp, and asked whither it would go.*

No other change is needed; the Epilogue's two lines now resolve by themselves. (+6)

### 13 · The foreword spends III.2's naming and makes a promise the Book does not keep

*l.118, 140.*

- **The naming.** *The folk behind the grey we call the Mystaeri, a word of ours, not theirs.* hands the reader a name that III.2 later gives with its story: *So we called it the ghost fleet, and its senders the Mystaeri, after the mist. A man must call his death by some name.* That is one of the Book's best small moments. Cut the foreword's sentence. VI.3's *Mystaeri is but the word of the shore* still pays III.2. (−14)
- **The hood.** *When the dead are named, he will put back his hood.* The dead are named in I.5, V.5 Count VI and VI.1, and the hood stays up each time. It comes back only after IV.3 and in the Epilogue, as craft §3.6 rules. Write *The Captain will hear all, and tell naught, and keep his hood up.* Then I.1's *There he sitteth, with his hood up* is the rule, and the two bare-headed nights land as breaks. (−5)

### 14 · The ten great hulls have three names, and the lore words come in one-offs

*I.5 l.659; V.5 l.2096; IV.2 headnote l.1218; Epilogue l.2807.*

The problems:
- **Mystarch.** Ebba's *the last Mystarch lie broken* is the only *Mystarch* left in the Book.
- **Beasts, then Thrones.** V.5 says *The wall named them for beasts*, and names none (the beast-names went, rightly, per R6b's flag). VI.1 then calls them Thrones as if long known.
- **Blackthorn** appears once, in a headnote.
- **The *Myst-* words.** *Mystholders*, *Mystlands*, *Mystwood* (Jack's), *Myststone* and *Mystarch* appear once each.

Voice §2.4 bars one-off old words. One-off lore words are worse, because a reader thinks he has missed a page.

**One name, the soldiers':**
- **I.5:** *…when the last wall shall stand, and the last Throne lie broken.* (*the ten great hulls had sat down in the havens like herons* just before tells him what they are.)
- **V.5:** *The wall named them the Thrones.*
- **IV.2:** *Rhyna took up this heart on the shingle…*
- **Epilogue:** keep *Myststone* (an outline keep), but set it after the reading, so the discovery is not interrupted by a gazetteer line.

### 15 · III.1 opens with a geography lesson in the mouth of the one man who never makes a simile

*l.934–944, 1000.*

After *Hear now the names of the havens*, Kael says *Builders were our fathers, and no seafarers… Over the mountains they paved roads like veins. Ten havens they raised…*. That is a schoolbook paragraph, with a simile Kael's voice forbids. Then an italic says Seren reads, then *Then Kael spake again*, and the reader cannot tell who said what.

The geography is R6a content, so move it into the rolls that Seren reads:

> Hear now the names of the havens, and hold them, for they are ours.
>
> Another man's city I tell not. I call the haven, and its own man shall answer, and swear what he will do when it is come home. Oath-keeper am I. I hold them.
>
> *First I read from the rolls, as he bade me. Each haven's roll beginneth with the one line:* This day was the first course laid, and a staff cut for the work. *Small is the clerks' hand, and patient, as if they were loath to waste the leaf. Builders they were, and no seafarers, that feared the open water, and built where the mountains go down in long falls to the sea. Over the mountains they paved roads like veins. Ten havens, and ten staffs.*

(About −10.) *Ten cities, ten harbours, ten kindreds…* (l.1000) is a v1.3 keep and Kael's close in v2.0. Leave it.

### 16 · The wood keeps explaining its own wiring

*IV.6 headnote l.1598; V.3 headnote l.1826, l.1855; V.6 l.2154.*

The mechanism is shown well (root to root; *It had not seen. It had felt.*), then explained in *for*-clauses and headnotes leaf after leaf:
- **IV.6:** cut *The wood saith two, the ship-masters twenty, and I have kept both;*. The Invocation states that rule and IV.5 has just shown it. The fixed *They did not know it was ignorance.* then stands alone, as a verdict of Seren's should. (−13)
- **V.3 headnote:** replace *As it came to her, {{Halyna}} say, so went the hull's hurt among its kin.* with a turn of Seren's: *…touched it not, and knew it as he knew it, as kin know kin.* (−11)
- **V.3:** *As we went, we told our kin, and needed no words.* (−5)
- **V.6:** *Through the bond of roots we carved them on into every hull about us, and every hull obeyed. The wood obeyeth the wood.* (−12)

### 17 · Learnability: the small stock is never learned, the late wood is the plainest, and one pronoun slips

- **The pronoun.** I.4 l.587: *If you doubt the tale* → *If ye doubt the tale*. That matches the Epilogue's refrain of the same words (l.2837).
- **The small stock.** Give each word its eight meetings or let it go:
  - *Aught* (once, in the Knowings) and *thither* (once, II.3) should go to *more* and *to them*.
  - *Lest*, *nigh*, *scarce*, *whoso* and *whither* should each come back several times in Books Five and Six. Seed them where they fit, for example V.3's *whither the hurt hulls ran* and V.6's *lest the shore wake*.
  - Never start *oft*, *yonder* or *hither* now.
- **The late wood.** V.4 and VI.2 carry less than half II.2's old stock. Rough each up with three or four object-first or *be*-perfect sentences away from the hinges. For example: *Under a sky that went from them were the young of the long-lived grown.* · *Wait they could not.* (VI.2, for *and they could not wait*).
- ***Someone* is modern** in old dress:
  - V.1 and VI.3: *as though one had put the word into his mouth, and gone away*;
  - V.6: *for the first time, the shore struck back*;
  - Hesk's *Someone must keep a light* stays: it is his line.

### 18 · The breath before the strike has become a formula

*l.755, 1162, 2224, 2289, 2811.*

*None spake* or *neither spake* appears eight times (l.220, 496, 755, 1162, 1800, 2263, 2701, 2811). Voice §3.2 wants the stillness *given as a thing seen or heard*. I.1's, I.4's, V.2's, V.7's and VI.3's earn their place. The others:
- **II.1:** cut *Neither spake.*; the thumb in the bite has already said it.
- **IV.1:** *Halvard turned the leaf, and turned it back.* in place of *Neither of them spake.*
- **Epilogue:** cut the repeat (see #11).

*I did not see it. Hale did.* is Kael's (V.5 III). Seren uses it in V.7 I and again in V.7 II as *What followed, Hale saw, and not I*. Change V.7 II to *What followed, I have from Hale.*

### 19 · Small things a careful first reader trips on

- **IV.5 l.1538:** Elspet is twelve, and the pipes were the ship-masters'. *By day she laughed with it, and filled their cups under the lowest stone.*
- **IV.5 l.1544–1546:** three men give four sayings. Make *And one:* read *And the first again:*.
- **V.2 l.1758:** *to ask the fifty-three more* reads as fifty-three more planks. Write *to ask the fifty-three for more*.
- **V.5 l.2032:** *The shot took the parapet, and Wick with it.* Then Wick speaks. Write *and Wick fell with it*.
- **V.5 l.2102:** *fourteen names* means the Twelve, Voss and Ruan, but the Book never raises Ruan (cast.md does). In V.7 II, l.2329, add: *So they went to Ruan of the spire, his file-leader, and the Captain raised him a True Man.* (+7)

### 20 · I.5: Voss's slow-tide saying interrupts his answer

*l.685.*

*A slow answer is a slow tide, so my grandmother told it me: it cometh in over the same stones.* is an R6a (c) line. It sits between the pennants and the answer itself, where it breaks the run to the turn. In v2.0 it stood near the head, where it explains why Voss's answer took the whole war. - *Child have I none of mine own, and yet I said* our;
