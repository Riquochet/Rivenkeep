## A · Fixes in IV.5

**1. l.64, l.69 and l.77: Merrick hears the lines that were "never" sung. Fix this; it is a contradiction.**
- l.64 says *Two lines more we sang not on the quay*.
- l.69 then says *One evening Merrick heard them*. The nearest plural is the two lines, so this reads as the children singing them once.
- l.77 says *I heard the two lines we never sang.*
- **Fix:** l.69 should read *One evening Merrick heard us at it.* (+2 words). Merrick then objects to the rhyme itself, which agrees with his bidding Jory stop whistling *the tune and never the words* (l.75). It also fits I.3 (g02), where he stops the tune.
- **Free with it:** move *eldest of the ship-masters* off the quay scene and into the aside: *Merrick, eldest of the ship-masters, sitteth by the door tonight. He is not gone out.* Merrick is Daveth's son, and Daveth was a young axeman at B−75, so at B−33 Merrick can hardly be the eldest master on the quay. Tonight he can. The move costs no words.

**2. l.31: a dead man "that turned". Also restore *which no boat can do* (l.25).**
- *a boat shoved off burning, with a dead man in it, that turned* puts the relative clause on the corpse.
- **Fix:** *a boat shoved off burning, with a dead man in it; and well out, it turned.* (+3)
- voice_v3 §9 ruling 3 gives *which no boat can do* to IV.5 and forbids it to Brenn, so if IV.5 drops it the Book loses the line (v1.3 l.842). Put it where the motion is first told, l.25: *Out of the grey a boat came at them, burning, into the wind and against the current, which no boat can do.* (+5)
- Do not add it to the second master's saying. voice §4 fixes that saying as it stands.

**3. l.39: the shore's laughter at the three survivors has gone.**
- v2.0 beat 8, which R6b lists, reads *Year upon year the shore laughed at the three from the spar*. The draft only implies it with *By day she laughed with the shore*.
- Two other tales pay this line:
  - IV.3 (g06 l.195): *That autumn the shore laughed at three axemen, home on a spar*;
  - the Knowings' Last Note (g11 l.292, in the 22:12 revision): *the quay laughed at three axemen home on a spar*.
- IV.5 is the source of the beat, and its headnote already promises *the shore laughed at it*.
- **Fix:** *The shore laughed at the three from the spar. By day she laughed with it, and knocked out her pipe on the lowest stone.* (+8)

**4. l.23: *one of the last timber-boats* has been lost.**
- Both v2.0 beat 4 and outline §8 IV.5 scene 4 have it (*on one of the last timber-boats at the edge*).
- It is the hinge to IV.1 (g06):
  - *two timber-boats are struck off a cargo-list … lost at the edge*;
  - *After the year of the burning boat no crew would row within sight of the grey … So the axes stopped.*
  - Without *last*, the reader cannot see that this boat ended the Harvest.
- **Fix:** *That summer he was coming home along the edge of the grey in one of the last timber-boats, low with black wood, and a second in his wake.* (+5, after dropping the second *boat*)

**5. l.15: the coats steaming on the settle. Restore if there is room.**
- The writer cut it for budget. But voice_v3 §4 makes it part of Elspet's voice: *The coats steam on the settle, the three grey stones hang over the fire, and the ship-masters knock out their pipes on the lowest one.* Of the three, the draft keeps only two.
- **Fix:** *… with the weather in their beards, and hung their coats on the settle to steam.* (+9)
- Fixes 1–4 take the tale to about **985 (1.5% under)**, inside the samples' range of 1.5–3.6%. Fix 5 takes it to about 994, so take it only if fix 6 is left out, or if the orchestrator accepts a margin under 1%.

**6. Optional, voice §4: Voss does not come back to his question in the same words.**
- voice §4 says of Voss: *he asks, and he circles back in the same words.* The draft asks *Wherefore would a ship-master not hear that tale?* (l.13) and never asks it again. Its second question (l.43) asks something else.
- The answer, Merrick's rope and *His father was Daveth.*, comes with no question beside it.
- **Cheapest mend:** l.45, *Wherefore would a ship-master not hear that tale? Never would one speak of it. I have seen one set down his cup…* (+7)
- Fix 6 competes with fix 5 for the last 1% of budget. I would take fix 6: it is the tale's dramatic question (craft §2.3), and it builds toward the turn.

**7. Low. l.15: *What they said, none knew, nor asked.* against IV.1.**
- IV.1 (g06 l.93) says *The children grew up under them, and asked what they said, and were told they said naught. Some of you that sit at this fire were those children.* g06's notes count Voss among those children.
- The two do not strictly clash, because Elspet's girlhood (B−75) is earlier.
- If the orchestrator wants them to agree, write *none knew, and the grown asked not.* (+2). Memory theme 2 keeps its edge either way.

**8. Low. l.55: the song's frame line.**
- *Thus the children sang it, and sing it still:* breaks the formula every other song frame uses, in the samples, in g01, in g02 and in v2.0: *Then the X sang, as they sing it still:*.
- The sentence before it sets the children on the quay, so the old form cannot be heard as the hearth singing.
- **Fix:** *Then the children of the quay sang, as they sing it still:* (0 words, against writer's note §6.3)

## B · Fixes in V.1

**9. l.115: Harl's *"Burn it"* takes the slot of the custom's "old words". Cut it.**
- In v2.0 the soldiers *said the old words over it* as they dragged up the wreck. The draft puts Harl's *"Burn it," … of every plank* in exactly that slot, as the wreck goes to the fire. That makes it a phrase said over each plank as it burns, which is the shape of a rite and the echo Jack's fix removes. It also stacks on the warden's *Burn it* (IV.3) and the rhyme's *Burn it and shove it*, both three pages back.
- Two other sources ask for the burning with no words said over it: craft §2.3 for V.1 (*No words are said over it*), and g08's own note for V.2 (l.309), which leans on V.1.
- VI.3 does not need the plant. III.1 (g05 l.51) already roots VI.3's *what he had said of every plank of the war* with *Aske may lay his hand on the planks. I say burn them.*
- **Fix:** cut the sentence (−11). It is the writer's optional take (§6.2), and R6b offers it only as an option.

**10. l.115: the want-of-wood reason reads backwards, and the burning contradicts itself.**
- *for they hated it, and wood was dear on the ridge*: wood that is dear is a reason to keep it, and the bonfires on the tide-line look like waste. The reason itself is right: craft §2.3 says *plain hatred and want of wood*, and III.1's Aske says *On this mountain is no wood but wreck off the shingle*.
- *dragged it up and burned it* says all of it burned, and two sentences later *The Captain let them burn the most of it*.
- **Fix** (keeps the tide-line and the lamps, and makes the need into a thing):
  - *The soldiers dragged it up the shingle and made their fires of it, for they hated it, and wood was dear on the ridge.*
  - then *One by one the fires went up along the tide-line, as lamps go up along a quay at dusk.* (net about +4)
- If plain fear is preferred, as in Jack's mend, write *for they hated it, and feared it* instead.

**11. l.111: the four hands and the one have been lost (v2.0 beat 1).**
- v2.0 reads *with their four hands on the grain, and my one hand on the lamp*. It is Seren's play, the Book's method in one line, and it rings with Enno and Della's *Their four hands* (l.145) and with I.4's fixed *Four hands laid it in two.*
- **Fix:** *As it began, so it hath gone on: their four hands on the grain, and my one on the lamp.* (+12)
- With fixes 9 and 10 the tale stays at about **818 / 850**.

**12. l.107: the headnote.**
- **Match VI.3.** g10's VI.3 headnote is *Laid by Seren Two-Inks, that held the lamp.* V.1 and VI.3 are the Book's first word and its last (*shore* and *home*), and they mirror each other's lines. Use the same words: *Laid by Seren Two-Inks, that held the lamp.*
- **Fix *I have mended it not*.** With an auxiliary, the old order already puts *not* after *have* (*I have not sinned*). *mended it not* is parody order (craft §6.8). Write *and I have not mended it.*
- **Thin the triple telling.** The headnote, the tale (l.149) and the native caption (l.166, *as Seren drew it that night on the shingle*) each say that she drew the sign on the shingle. Cut the caption back to v2.0's *The Wave, as Seren drew it*. Keep the key `legend_wave_chip chip`, the English line *Shore.*, and no "unlocked by" line.

## C · What other drafts must change (the orchestrator should route these)

**13. g02 (I.3) l.167 against V.1: who carried the plank.**
- I.3 reads *I found a plank in the shallows with marks cut in it. Up past the fires I carried it to {{Halyna}}*. Here *it* is the plank.
- V.1 follows v2.0 and the outline: Hale brings only three words, {{Halyna}} go down at the turn of the tide, and *The men hauled one plank up out of the surf*.
- v2.0's I.3 had Hale carry *them*, the marks, *in three words*.
- **Fix g02, not V.1:** *… saw marks cut in a plank in the shallows. Up past the fires I carried them to {{Halyna}}, in three words:*

**14. g11 (Epilogue) l.50 against IV.5 l.47: the masters' saying. This now agrees, so lock it.**
- The 22:12 revision of g11 reads *for their fathers had told them of the boat that cometh for you.* That matches IV.5's *cometh*.
- An earlier g11 text had *when you die*. If any later edit adds the clause back, it must read *when ye die*, as IV.5 has it, since voice §1.1 makes *ye* the subject.
- I endorse the writer's §6.1: the masters' sayings are not §1.7 fixed matter, so §1.2 requires *cometh* and *laugheth*. The couplet keeps *comes*, because it is a song.
- This also answers g11's own question (its notes, l.454): IV.5 keeps *cometh*, so g11 stays as it is.

**15. Writer's notes §5 are out of date. No text changes; correct the notes so no one acts on them.**
- *I.2 … carry the second half*: g03's I.4 already speaks *Hold the stone, Seren. I tell myself so every time.* whole, and g02's I.2 gives only *Hold the stone.* V.1's half alone is right. Nothing should be added to I.2.
- *VI.1's* the long table was gone *is planted*: g09 dropped the long table. The pipes on the lowest stone are still paid (g09 l.195).

## D · Checked and clean

- **The custom of burning.** None, and no echo left once fix 9 is made:
  - Tobe's saying, *the old words of the coast* and *which they keep for that boat* are gone from IV.5;
  - *ill luck* is gone, a deliberate fold that R6b allowed either way;
  - *by the old way … said the old words* is gone from V.1;
