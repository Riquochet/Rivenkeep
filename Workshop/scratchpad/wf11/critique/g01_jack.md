# g01 · Jack's eye

*Share g01: the title page and Contents, Of This Book, the Invocation, Book One's heading and Argument, and I.1. Read against `voice_v3.md` §10, `craft.md` §8, the four models, `memory_theme.md`, R6a and the outline §OF THIS BOOK.*

## The verdict

- **I.1 stands.** It is the judged model, byte for byte. It builds: it opens on a moment, raises its question, holds one halt, turns on the sound of old cloth, pays a cost and closes on a picture. It is hard and learnable, it is Kael from the first line, and Seren's picture is in it. Jack would lean in. It has four small faults (items 13–16 below), and one of them is a clock slip.
- **The Invocation is nearly there.** One word is wrong all through it: it says *tell* where the foreword has just taught *lay* (Problem 6).
- **The title page and Contents are clean, with two spoilers on the threshold** (Problem 5).
- **The foreword is the leaf Jack will reject.** It does what every brief asked: each beat is present, each rule is said once, the counts are in budget, and nothing breaks a lock. It is still not a page anyone would lean into. It is a list of the Book's rules set between two short scenes. It is written in the lightest register of anything in the Book. It preaches the memory theme in the one modern formula on the page. And it drops five or six of the lines Jack liked. It is the first page of story a reader meets, so it sets the tone for everything after it. Problems 1–4 are all in it. A full alternative, at the same length, is at the end of this file.

## The worst problems, with rewrites

### 1 · The foreword is a rulebook between two scenes: the textbook fault in a new shape

The page opens on the night (the short dark, the coals, the banner) and returns to it at the close (*the coals are gone to ash*). Everything between is a rule of the Book, set down as fact:

- *So I write every bonded pair…*
- *Whoso holdeth the stone…*
- *Two words answer…*
- *Some tales are the wood's…*
- *The wood is not read but known…*
- *Where the wood's leaf could not yet be told…*
- *I am not of the Bonded…*
- *The Captain will sit…*
- *Where a carving was broken…*
- *Each night I set the tale…*

About fourteen of its twenty-one paragraphs work this way. The sentences are short, so v2.0's run-on fault is gone. But four flat statements in a row read as a primer, which `voice_v3.md` §3.1 warns against:

> *Beside me in its niche is the stone, the old capstone of the inner gate. {{Halyna}} turned it over on the day of the banner. It is a stone that two must turn. On its underside the vow of the first builders is sharp yet.*

The cast list has the same flatness: *{{Halyna}} are… {{Idrenna}}… {{Orvenna}}… Of {{Enrella}}…*.

The foreword already has a spine, and the draft leaves it unused in the middle. It opens on a teller already under the cairns and closes on *This Book is ink*: will the tales outlast the tellers? Nothing between those two lines keeps that question alive.

**Fix: thread the night through, let deeds carry the rules, and keep the spine alive.**

- **Give Seren's hands and room two touches in the middle.**
  - The chest is at her feet when she names the dark stone: *One came out of a dark stone, that lay sealed in the chest at my feet till {{Idrenna}} gave leave to break the seal.*
  - The last word is still in her mouth at the close (Problem 4).
- **Let a deed carry a rule.**
  - The stone was face down with its vow to the earth, and two must turn it.
  - The first night was quiet, and Ebba spoke into the quiet (Problem 3).
- **Keep the spine alive with one plant:** *Every scribe of the guild before me was one of a pair. I write alone.* (Problem 4). The close, *{{Halyna}} would have it cut in the granite of this hall, when peace is come*, then pays the question without saying it.
- **Re-order the seams** so that each paragraph takes up the last:
  - The draft goes stone → vow → lintel → pairs → *back* to the stone (*Whoso holdeth…*) → *We remember*.
  - Better: stone → turned → two must turn → *laid, not told* → the vow on its underside → the native block → the lintel → the pairs → *We remember*.
  - Leave *Whoso holdeth it* to the Invocation, which says it on the next page (item 2 below).
- **Split the seven-sentence wood paragraph** after *Words can be turned, and a knowing cannot.* (`voice_v3.md` §3.4 allows one to five sentences).

### 2 · The first page of the Book is its least old one

Jack's word was "hard to read to the uneducated or someone who hasn't read the text before". Seren is the teller who *loves our tongue*, yet she writes plainer than Kael does.

- Of the foreword's first nine sentences, two carry any Early Modern mark: *that held up a wet finger* and *masons cut not … ere*.
- About half the page has none at all. Examples: *It is the short dark of the year. The hall is asleep about me…* · *Tonight I have turned back to the first leaf.* · *{{Halyna}} turned it over on the day of the banner.* · *{{Orvenna}}, our wall-masters, never raise their voices.* · *I set them down as they fell, in two inks.*
- Six of I.1's first nine sentences carry one: *Enough have I sat* · *A teller am I* · *Heads I tell* · *What I told not, that will I not tell* · *ere*.
- So a reader who opens the Book meets nearly modern English first, and the register lands on him at I.1 as a change of voice, not as the Book's own tongue.

There is plenty of room: the foreword has 3 *-eth* forms in 839 words, against a cap of about one in 100–150. The marks to add are cheap ones, and none of them is a hinge:

| Draft | Rewrite |
|---|---|
| *The hall is asleep about me, and the Long Hearth is sunk to coals.* | *The hall sleepeth about me, and the Long Hearth is sunk to coals.* |
| *On the wall I see the banner lift, in a wind I cannot hear.* | *On the wall I see the banner lift, in a wind I hear not.* |
| *Tonight I have turned back to the first leaf. I left it bare, for masons cut not…* | *Tonight have I turned back to the first leaf, that I left bare, for masons cut not…* |
| *The first builders of the Keep laid tales at this hearth.* | *At this hearth the first builders of the Keep laid their tales.* |
| *{{Orvenna}}, our wall-masters, never raise their voices.* | *Never yet were {{Orvenna}}, our wall-masters, heard to raise their voices.* |
| *Till there be peace to cut it in the granite of this hall, it is kept in the chest I carried…* | *…when peace is come. Till then it lieth in that chest, among the mute stones.* |

Keep the inversions to one in three, with no run of three. The rewrites above keep to that.

### 3 · The memory theme is preached, in the one modern formula on the page

> *It is more than an answer. It is a vow. Forgetting came on our fathers as the weather came on this Keep, a little each winter, and none chose it. Remembering we choose, as masons choose which stones to keep.*

- *It is more than an answer. It is a vow.* is the "not X but Y" lecture. `memory_theme.md` forbids it in so many words: "they feel it, they do not lecture it".
- It is also the most modern-sounding pair of sentences in the share. Jack would hear it as the essayist talking, not Seren.
- The last sentence is good. The play on *keep* is Seren's.
- Meanwhile the concrete form of the theme was cut: the founders' tales were forgotten when the Keep was left. The draft keeps only *The first builders of the Keep laid tales at this hearth*.

**Minimum fix:** cut the two lecturing sentences, and keep the weather and the *keep*.

**Better fix:** make the forgetting a picture, make the choosing a deed, and leave Seren a mirror.
- At the founders: *At this hearth the first builders of the Keep laid their tales. When the Keep was left, the tales went cold with the hearth. Now sit we where they sat…*
  - This is the cold hearth of I.1 (*snow lay on the hearth where ye sit now*) seen in advance. It does not spoil I.4's chest.
- At Ebba, as a scene of two lines and not a report:
  - *The first night this hearth asked a tale, none of us wist what should be said. Then old Ebba of Fenholm spake them into the quiet, and the hearth took them from her. None bade her, and none bade us follow. Most nights she is first yet, by a breath, and in behind her we come, as a tide behind its first wave.*
  - Then, as its own paragraph: *Forgetting chose our fathers. Remembering we choose, as masons choose which stones to keep.*
- Why this is better:
  - *None bade her* is the vow, felt as a choice that no one ordered.
  - The mirror is the shape Seren uses (`voice_v3.md` §3.3), and it is hard but learnable.
  - The theme is said once, in her own words, as the theme file allows.

### 5 · Spoilers on the threshold

1. **Book One's Argument spends Kael's punch twice before he lands it.** *How we came to a heap of stones, and stood.* sits in the Contents and again under the Book One heading. The reader then reaches *A month had we walked from the ashes of Glasspire to come to a castle. We came to a heap of stones.*, and the drop is already gone. Use v1.3's tag, which craft §3.5 names as the model: ***How we came to stand.***
2. **The Epilogue's Argument and credit tell the ending on the Contents page.** *The tellers dead, one scribe left, and a grey boat come back* tells the reader that {{Halyna}} die, and so does the credit *Seren Two-Inks, alone*. The writer flagged this, rightly.
   - Argument: ***In one ink.*** A first reader cannot decode it. It pays at the end, and it is the epithet turned (craft §4.2).
   - Credit: plain *Seren Two-Inks*.
3. **The foreword spends I.1's closing aside.** *The Captain will sit at the end of the bench, hood up, and tell naught* is I.1's best picture (*Look to the end of the bench… There he sitteth, with his hood up.*), given away a page early.
   - Craft does want the hood habit told once in the foreword, so keep the habit and drop the picture: *The Captain will hear all, and tell naught. When the dead are named, he will put back his hood.*
   - The pun on *tell* survives, and so does the bench in I.1.
4. **The foreword also answers I.1's small mystery.** *They come out of the Title the Captain wrote in coal from this hearth* explains the coal before Kael says *and why, none of us wist*.
   - The Invocation sets the Title directly above the hearth's *We remember* on the next page, so the reader sees where the words come from without being told.
   - Cut the sentence, or cut it to *They come out of the first words of the Title.*

### 6 · The Invocation unsays the foreword's own pun

The foreword has just taught: *we say not that it was told, but laid. A tale that is but told is gone by morning.* The next page, the hearth's own liturgy, says *We tell these tales, for once we forgat… So tell we all of it*. Seren would not let that stand.

**Rewrite:** *We lay these tales, for once we forgat. … So lay we all of it: the building and the breaking, the wrong that was done to us, and the wrong we did.*
- With the fixed *Hold the stone. Lay the tale.*, that makes three *lay*s, and the third is the command. That is a build by three.
- *those are hard to tell* stays. Telling the wood is the hard part, and the pun there is right.

Everything else in the Invocation works:
- *Sit, if there be room, and stand if there be none* is right in the subjunctive.
- *forgat* comes three times, which teaches the old past.
- *the wrong that was done to us, and the wrong we did* is clean.
- *Many hands have held it, and many shall.* is memory theme 3 at exactly the right weight. It is an addition to the outline's "verbatim" Invocation, but it adds no lore, and I would keep it.

## The rest, numbered

**Of This Book**

1. **Ambiguous *it*.** *a dark stone that lay under a seal in the chest I carried, till {{Idrenna}} gave leave to break it.* A first reader may think they broke the stone. Write *till {{Idrenna}} gave leave to break the seal*.
2. **The liturgy is doubled on facing pages.** *Whoso holdeth the stone is the teller* (foreword) and *Whoso holdeth it now is the teller* (Invocation). The writer moved *kept both* and *the guns are cooling* to the Invocation to avoid exactly this, but left this one.
   - Let the Invocation have it.
   - The foreword's *laid, not told* paragraph needs only *When a tale is done we say not that it was told, but laid.*
3. **The Mystaeri line is dropped in after the seal**, as a non sequitur. Let it close the wood paragraph, after the dark stone, so that it follows from *the wood's*.
4. ***masons* three times.** The page has three mason comparisons: *masons cut not the lintel*, *as masons choose which stones to keep* and *as masons set a course*, plus *as a lintel on two posts*. The last two are required elements. Recast the first without the word: *I left it bare, for the lintel is not cut ere the posts are raised.*
5. ***the chest I carried* twice, and *chest* four times.** The second *chest I carried* comes right after *only the chest was heavy* and flattens it. Write *that chest* (as in Problem 2's rewrite).
6. ***hearth* in three senses on one page:** the Long Hearth (the place), *the hearth said* (the people) and *the hearth of {{Halyna}}* (a household, in the byline). The first two are the Book's own, and they work. The third collides with them and is never explained (Problem 4).
7. **The rules litany shifts tense:** *have not mended … have set … I write*. Make the third *I have written that, and not ours*.
8. ***Two words answer every tale of ours, and never other words*** limps. Write either *…and they are never said another way* (v2's sense) or Seren's wink, *…and never other two*.
9. **The Ebba moment is reported, not staged.** Two clauses make it a scene: the quiet after the first song, and her speaking *into* it (Problem 3). Keep *in behind her we come, as a tide behind its first wave*. It is one of the best pictures on the page.
10. **Keep the wet finger as it is.** *One of its tellers, that held up a wet finger to every wind, is under the east cairns already* is memory theme 4 done perfectly: a habit, not a death. It is the best line the draft added. III.1–III.2 must plant the finger and V.5 Count II must pay it.
11. **Budget.** The draft sits at 839/850, 1.3% under the ceiling, while cutting liked lines. The alternative below carries every restoration in Problem 4 and the scene touches in Problem 1 at 843. To reach the guide's 5–10% under (765–808), cut joints, never beats:
    - fold *The Book is half full* into the next sentence;
    - shorten the pairs' clauses;
    - drop *after an assault*, which the Invocation's *The guns are cooling* already says;
    - drop *They come out of the first words of the Title* (Problem 5.4).
12. **Markup.** *"A tale told from one side of a wall,"* / *"is half a tale."* is set as two bare paragraphs with no tag. That is right by the IV.3 model, which sets {{Halyna}}'s annal line the same way. But the builder has no `HALYNA` marker here to set the two inks.
    - Confirm that the builder inks an untagged split line in a Seren leaf.
    - If it does not, wrap the two lines in `<!-- HALYNA -->` / `<!-- HALYNA END -->`.

**I.1 · The Torn Cloak**

13. **A clock slip.** *Crimson is the pennant on every keep of ours.* I.1 is laid on the fifteenth night of the first winter, when there is one keep. The Plain twin has the same slip (*all our keeps fly red pennants*). v2.0 had it right. Write *Crimson is the pennant on this Keep, and on every keep we raise.* (+5 words, which the leaf can afford at 1,075).
14. **A one-off antique.** *The other half, whereon naught was written…* *whereon* is not in the stock and never comes back (§2.4). Write *The other half, with naught written on it, he put back on his shoulders.*
15. **A pronoun slip at the end of the roll.** *And Kael the Counter, their oath-keeper, of no haven, for he found me on a road.* After *Kael … their oath-keeper*, the *he* reads as Kael. Write *for the Captain found me on a road*. The Plain twin already does.
16. **The one rulebook sentence in the tale.** *After us answered the six companies, that are our troops now, a troop to each True Man; and they trust us as we trust him.* It has the leaf's only semicolon chain and its only system explanation. Write *After us answered the six companies, a troop to each of us, and they trust us as we trust him.*
17. **For the orchestrator, not the writer: the bare roll.** Eleven names in a row is a muster sheet, and the never-list warns of "names arriving like entries in a register". It works here as Kael's drumbeat, and III.1 carries the tags. But Jack liked the tags, and Corlen, Pellow and Hesk will die. `voice_v3.md` §9.7 is still marked *Orchestrator to confirm*, so confirm it, and make III.1 carry all eleven tags in full.
18. **No change needed.** The question (*will they stand?*) is raised near word 170, not by word 100, because the oath and the count come first. It is right for this teller: the seed (*many feet, and no voice among them*) comes at word 60.

**Title page, Contents, headings**

19. **I.1 is the one left leaf with no facing mark.** The Epilogue's entry says *bound facing the first tale*, but I.1's entry does not say *facing the Epilogue*. Add it, or the "facing" marks are inconsistent.
20. **The credits are fine.**
    - *the wood, as {{Halyna}} knew it* (×8) keeps {{Halyna}} discovering. Seren's name on the title page covers her hand.
    - *the words of Brenn of the outpost* is better than *confession*.
    - The Book Five Argument carries Jack's line verbatim.
21. **For the orchestrator: strip the `## WRITER'S NOTES` section** at the foot of `g01.md` before assembly. It sits after the I.1 rule, where a builder could take it for Book text.

**Locks, checked and holding.** No singular *they*, and every pair-name takes a plural verb. No custom, and the word *custom* is avoided. No Christian mark: *God* appears only in the Title. The warden is a blot. Every song is verbatim against v2.0. Every native key that v2.0 uses in these leaves is present: `legend_stonwryt_hal`, `legend_title_coal` (×2), `legend_stone_rings` and `legend_seren_note`. There are no contractions. No sentence has two *-eth* forms.

## One way the foreword could run

*843 words of tale proper by `count_v3.py`, against the draft's 839. Mean sentence 12.4; 29% of sentences under 10 words; none over 30 (the longest is 24); 4 -eth forms; one semicolon. It carries every restoration in Problem 4 and the fixes in Problems 1–3 and 5, and it changes no beat. It is offered as a shape, not a lock: the writer's own lines are kept wherever they work.*

>

>
> "A tale told from one side of a wall,"
>

>
> I am not of the Bonded. When {{Halyna}} know the wood, I hold the lamp. Once I laid mine own palm on the grain, and let go. Mine is the hand to write, and not the hand to know. Every scribe of the guild before me was one of a pair. I write alone.
>
> The Captain will hear all, and tell naught. When the dead are named, he will put back his hood.
>
> Where a carving was broken, I have not mended it with guesses. Where the guild cut out a name, I have set a blot. Where the wood gave a name of its own, I have written that, and not ours.
