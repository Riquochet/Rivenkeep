# g09 · Jack's Eye: V.7 The Mirror in the Grain; BOOK SIX's heading and Argument; VI.1 The Homecomings

*Critic: I read this the way Jack would, against voice_v3 §10, craft §8, the three model leaves, I1_plain and memory_theme. The source is `wf11/drafts/g09.md`. I also checked it against the neighbouring drafts it leans on: g02 (I.2), g04 (II.3), g05 (III.1 and III.2), g07 (IV.5), g08 (V.5), w02 (VI.2) and g11 (the Epilogue). The trial rewrites below, used only for counting, are in `wf11/critique/_g09j/V7_rw.md` and `VI1_rw.md`.*

## The verdict

**V.7 is the best-built leaf in this round, and it is cut too fine.**
- Part I's climb survives whole: *They sent a pair apart* → *A mile of wall is far* → the mallet in the air → the soft mortar → *Ye cannot tell* → *"One"* → the silent first voice → the drying trowel.
- Pellow's whole glass lands, and so does Hale's *I run.* Part III ends on a picture.
- The faults are of a different kind from v2.0's:
  - The leaf is broken into so many one-line paragraphs that Seren beats like Kael, and Part I ends five or six times after its halt.
  - The best withheld line in the leaf is explained in the sentence just before it.
  - The opening paragraphs, where a first-time reader should have to work, are nearly modern English.
- In the Book's order, V.5 (g08) has already told the reader all three outcomes of V.7 (problem 6).

**VI.1 has the right halt and the right close, and a register in between.**
- Kael's frame works. Rimewatch is close to right.
- The roll pays V.5 well: *At Hesk's the north lamp was lit, and no answer came down.*
- But nine of the ten havens open on the same move: the Throne's name, a verb of falling, sometimes a *like*-simile. Then comes the man's deed, then a one-line saying, then the Amen.
- Jack's word for this is "clinical": entries in a book of record. Two havens even share the same phrase, *every night of the war*.

**BOOK SIX's heading and Argument pass.** *How the havens came home, and the war went home.* spoils nothing, and its play on *home* is set up for Aske, Hesk and VI.3. Keep it.

## The worst problems, with rewrites

### 1 · VI.1: the middle of the homecomings reads like a register

Ten havens, and nine of them open on the Throne's fall:
- *The Tide-Heart sank…*
- *All three heads we struck…*
- *The Grain That Rots went into the fen like a log gone soft…*
- *The Root-Heart stood still…*
- *The Stone-Grain crumbled…*
- *The Burning went out like a forge left untended…*
- *The Deep-Beneath burst up…*
- *…the Long Winter waited*
- *The Blood of the Sky came down…*
- *…the Silvered broke like a mirror…*

Seven of the ten make the Throne's name the subject of the first sentence. Three put a *like*-simile in the same slot. After that every haven runs the same course: the man's deed, a curt saying, *And the hearth said: We remember.* The craft never-list names this exactly (§6.2): "names arriving like entries in a register." It is also why the walk to Rimewatch does not climb. Holtward, Carnhold, Emberhythe and Sandreach are four flat payoffs of the same shape in a row.

The repetitions give the formula away:
- *every night of the war* comes in Tamm's haven and again in Ulden's;
- Garvel says *first* three times in 41 words (*the first quarrymen… the first smithy… The first thing I make*).

**The rule for the fix:**
- Let each man come in by his own sense: Tamm by threes, Garvel by sound, Ulden by the ear, Voss by a question (he already does), Kael by counting.
- Put the Throne second, or into a clause. The fall is VI.2's matter in any case, because VI.2 faces this leaf.
- Keep every Throne's image, since Jack likes every element. Change only where it sits in the sentence and what the sentence does with it.
- Keep the short havens short. The litany should quicken into Rimewatch the way III.2's falls quicken (g05 note), and then slow on Hesk.

**Tidesmeet.** As drafted: *All three heads we struck in the one holding, and none grew back. The Bearer of Branches went down where the middle span had stood. On the bank I set out my three stones in their order, as I have set them by my blanket every night of the war.*
> Three heads it had, and three we struck in the one holding, and none grew back. Where the middle span had stood, the Bearer of Branches went down. On the bank I set out my three stones in their order, as by my blanket every night of the war. One span is a plank. Three is a city.

Tamm now counts in threes from his first word.

**Carnhold.** As drafted: *The Stone-Grain crumbled where it stood, and the cairns of the first quarrymen took it. In the first smithy I struck the cold anvil once, to hear it.*
> Carnhold, that rang all day, was quiet. Stone into stone the Stone-Grain crumbled, and the cairns of the first quarrymen took it. In a cold smithy I struck the anvil once, to hear it. It rang true. The first thing I make on it shall not be a gun.

III.1 gave Garvel a city where *all day it rang with its smithies*. Its silence is now the stake, and the one ring is the pay.

**Sandreach.** As drafted: *The Deep-Beneath burst up under the east keep, and broke, and the canyon rang an hour. Then I went down to the lowest gallery, and lay with mine ear to the ground, as every night of the war.*
> An hour the canyon rang, when the Deep-Beneath burst up under the east keep and broke. When it was still, I went down to the lowest gallery, and laid mine ear to the ground. I heard naught. Tonight I sleep under mine own roof of stone.

The draft's *every night of the war* also clashes with III.1, where Ulden listened *till the Fall*. Cutting it removes the duplicate and the clash.

**Eldhythe.** The compression *The first walls of the havens stand again.* reads as "the first of the havens' walls to be rebuilt". Put it first, where the place is set:
> Here were the first walls of the havens laid, and here they stand again. The Tide-Heart sank into the sea it came from, and rose not. Who should bring the first boat home? Corlen should.

### 2 · V.7 is chopped into great lines, and Part I ends six times

- V.7 runs 58 prose paragraphs to the thousand words, and 22 of its paragraphs are a single sentence of narration.
- For comparison: I.1 has 32 paragraphs to the thousand and 5 single-sentence ones, IV.3 has 42 and 13, and IV.4 has 36 and 9.
- Voice §3.4 says to expect four to eight lines a tale on their own, and to let two one-line paragraphs stand together only when they are a pair.
- V.7 Part I alone sets about ten lines on their own.

When every line is set as a great line, none of them is one. On a first read the leaf goes in Kael's rhythm rather than Seren's (*They sent a pair apart. / Enno took the east breach, and Della the north. / … I did not see it. Hale did.*).

The cost is worst after the halt. Between *She was gone.* and the Lay, Part I closes on:
- *Never before had a runner come too late.*
- *Ye cannot tell.*
- *"One," he said.*
- *…as though it had been sung.*
- *On Halvard's trowel the mortar was drying.*
- *I will not write what I thought.*

Each is a fine line. Six in a row is the stacked ending that craft §6 charges v2.0's V.7 with ("closes ten times"), cut down but not cured.

**The fix:**
- Stand only these alone: *They sent a pair apart.* · *Then the mallet stopped in the air.* · *She was gone.* · *Ye cannot tell.* · *"One," he said.* · the trowel.
- Fold everything else into its paragraph.
- End Part I on the picture (craft rule 14), with the withheld thought just before it.
- Split the 24-word sentence in front of the mallet, so that the halt is the shortest stretch in the Part (voice §10.7).

The halt, re-set:
> At the north I was beside Della with the mortar. The last stone she had turned over, to cut their mark beneath it. She set the chisel herself, and struck, and her hand shook not.
>
> Then the mallet stopped in the air.
>
> She looked east. Then she bent again, and cut the last stroke, and laid the chisel by the mark, and lay down with her cheek against the stone.
>
> I put my thumb to the mortar, as masons do. It was still soft.
>
> She was gone.

The grave, as two paragraphs instead of five: *Over the stone Ormund began the old jest, and Penna finished it. That night the Captain bound a strip on a pike over them, his first for Stonewrights. They were his company too. Kael stood by it and counted them.* / *"One," he said.*

The close of Part I. This also carries the fix for problem 3:
> Long neither spake. I asked what ailed them, though I think I knew.
>
> "It is how we work a wall."
>
> "The one waiteth for the other's stroke, and need not be told."
>
> The carving named that Tide *Aelthae*. Its first half, *ael*, I had written once before, in *Ael'thar*. I have written the name, and I will not write what I thought.
>
> On Halvard's trowel the mortar was drying.
>
> This I lay as it was laid for me.

The soft mortar and the drying mortar now hold the Part between them, which is the mirror of the title, and the leaf never says so.

### 3 · V.7 explains its best line, and tells three things a second or third time

- **The *ael* gloss.** *Its first part I knew:* ael, *the joining, as in* Ael'thar, *the Guest's rite at the warden's door.* This breaks two of Seren's never-rules at once: a lecture on a word, and a footnote to her own Book. It also does the reader's thinking just before *I will not write what I thought*. "The joining" plus "the Guest's rite" hands over the thought she then claims to withhold, so the withholding becomes a pose. The rewrite above keeps *ael* (an R6b must-survive) and *Ael'thar*, and drops the gloss and the cross-reference. *written once before* then sets up *I have written the name*, and *first half* plays on the Book's halves. The reader who heard IV.4 will join the two words alone, and the gap belongs to that reader.
- ***"The wood cannot lie," I said, for so the Guest's gift first taught us.*** This is a *for* of explanation and a cross-reference. Cut it after *I said.* IV.4's *Slow is its giving, and true* has already taught it.
- ***held his glass up to all things, his supper too.*** This is the third telling. III.1 (g05) has *his supper among the rest*, and V.5 Count V (g08) has the same words, *his supper too*. g05's own note says that V.7 may drop its copy. Rewrite: *Pellow of the spire, youngest of the True Men, had two words for every man on the wall.* Then *"Look close."* The good of the dead is still remembered, because Ruan inherits *Look close* at the cairn.
- ***a sail went pale in it, as faces go white.*** V.5 Count II already gives this simile (*like a man's face*). Cut it here: *a sail went pale in it. Then a second. Then a third.* The plain version is colder, and the climb by three does the work.

### 4 · The old grammar goes missing where the reader should work

The *-eth* count is right, at two a leaf, and I would not add any. The fault is that inversion, *that* for persons and the stock words go missing in the road passages, which are exactly where voice §1.6 says the archaism should live.
- The first hundred words of V.7's story carry three marks of the old grammar: *One course high it stood*, *higher it stood never* and *At dawn came the fleet*. I.1's first hundred carry about ten.
- Part II's first two sentences carry one (*spake*).
- A first-time reader goes straight through these openings, as through calibration B, and Jack asked that this Book be hard to read "to someone who hasn't read the text before."
- *sat it out* is a modern phrasal idiom (never-list 7).

V.7's opening, with Seren's one painted picture for the movement added, from a trade and under fifteen words. The picture plants the mallet that stops:
> All that short night the drums knocked in the grey, like mallets in a far quarry. Through it sat Enno and Della in their house of one door. Enno set the chisel, and Della struck. One course high it stood, and higher it stood never.
>
> Hand in hand had they come into this ward, where I came in alone.
>
> At dawn the fleet came by three roads in one hour, and broke the wall east and north. There were more breaches than pairs. {{Orvenna}} the wall-masters, that never raise their voices, did without a word what no masters ere then had done.
>
> They sent a pair apart.
>
> Ormund gave Enno the east breach, and Penna gave Della the north.

The last line also restores an R6b ★ beat that the draft dropped (see the rest, item 1).
- Three inversions now fall across eight sentences, which is inside the cap of one in three.
- *At dawn came* is un-inverted so that *Hand in hand had they come* does not make three in a row.

Part II's opening:
> On a grey morning a fleet ran at the wall in the old way, each hull alone and loud and straight. Then laughed the whole wall, and every gun on it spake, Pellow's with the rest.

*Then laughed the whole wall* is IV.3's *Then laughed his men*, turned. The Shore's laughter is the Book's old fault: at the warden's door, at the three axemen, and in V.5 Count I's *God forgive us, we laughed*. Here the laughter is answered by the hunters. Nothing in the text says so, and the subtitle *What We Taught Them* is left to bite.

### 5 · Rimewatch: the halt has a slip, a stacked ending, and an unpaid line

1. ***Now he would light it on the ice again.*** The sentence before has the lamp already *lit in his hand, in full day*. It also states III.1's want (*When we go home, I will light it on the ice again*) instead of letting the lamp *on the ice, burning* pay it without a word. Cut it.
2. ***That night the Captain bound a strip for him over the mere.*** This comes after *He came home.*, which is the line the haven must end on. It is the strip as a whole sentence, where craft §2.2 allows a clause or nothing, and it is a second ending. Cut it. V.5 Count VI and the Epilogue pay the strips.
3. **III.1's *Another man's city I tell not* is never paid.** The head *Kael, for Hesk* is a doom glimpse that Homer would have liked: the hearth knows that Hesk will not speak, and watches him walk out. Kael ought to say why he is breaking his own rule. The writer's note sees this and leaves it.
4. *the great hall* comes twice in three sentences.

> **Rimewatch.** *Kael, for Hesk.*
>
> Another man's city I tell not. This one I must.
>
> All that morning the mere lay white, and in the white the Long Winter waited.
>
> Hesk stood on the shore with his mother's lamp lit in his hand, in full day. Six winters he had lit it at dusk on our north wall, and the night-watch steered by it.
>
> Ere noon he went out on the ice alone. From the shore I told his steps. At two hundred and twelve he stopped, where the great hall had stood. The ice spake under him, as it spake the night he carried that lamp out of Rimewatch.
>
> That night it held.
>
> At noon the white lifted, and the ice took the Long Winter down.
>
> The ice took Hesk too.
>
> At dusk we went out along the break with ropes, and found his lamp on the ice, burning. I swear to that.
>
> He came home.
>
> *And the hearth said: We remember.*

- *The ice took the Long Winter down. / The ice took Hesk too.* is now a strict pair, and the place-phrase that blurred it is gone.
- The count stops where Hesk stops. Kael's way of knowing is at the halt, and it holds the doom.

### 6 · In Book order, V.5 has already told the reader all of V.7 (for the orchestrator; the fix is in g08)

V.5 comes before V.7, and g08 gives more than the ledger's "a line" to each of V.7's three parts:
- **Count IV** has *Enno of the guild I found in the east breach, and a mile on, in the north, Della. With that hour I count them, for it was the same thing.* This is the "counted them as one" understatement that craft rule 13 allows **once in the Book**, at V.7's pike: *"One," he said.* The g09 writer asked for exactly this (notes §5), and g08 did not do it. Cut *With that hour I count them, for it was the same thing.* and keep *I will not say more.*
- **Count V** has *Ruan of the spire hath his glass.* That is V.7 Part II's closing beat, given away. It also has *A green hull whose sail blanched was no green hull.*, which is Pellow's own deduction explained before he makes it. Cut both. V.5 keeps *Pellow looked, and he is under them too.*
- **Count VI** has *each fleet came with a morrow carved in it, as we carve ours*, which pre-empts {{Halyna}}'s turn in Part III, *"They carve a morrow now, as we do."* Cut *as we carve ours*. Kael's bare effect (*it went home. Not scattered. Went.*) may stay, because effect before cause makes V.7 III the answer rather than a retelling.

If these stay in g08, every outcome in V.7 is known before the leaf opens, and Jack's "they don't build" will be true of the Book's best climb for reasons that have nothing to do with g09's text.

## The rest, numbered

1. **Ormund and Penna are not named where they part the pair.** The draft's *Enno took the east breach, and Della the north.* drops R6b's ★ beat 4 (*Ormund gave Enno the east; Penna gave Della the north*). A first reader then meets *Ormund* and *Penna* at the grave without knowing that they are {{Orvenna}}. The point of *Over the stone Ormund began the old jest, and Penna finished it* is that the two who sent the pair apart are the two who finish their jest, and *with their own four hands … mark down* carries the same cost. Restore it, at a cost of 3 words (it is in the problem 4 rewrite).
2. **Part III's lamp contradicts itself.** *{{Halyna}} knew it in the filling of a lamp, the longest knowing they ever held. Twice I filled the lamp ere they had told it.* The knowing takes one lamp and is also the longest, and the telling takes two. v2.0's distinction between knowing and telling was lost in the compression. Rewrite: *It was the longest knowing {{Halyna}} ever held. Twice I filled the lamp ere they had told it all, and at the end was this:* Then *I filled it not a third time* pays cleanly.
3. **Part III says the hope three times.** The boots picture says it, then *That night, for the first time, we saw the enemy hope.*, then *"They carve a morrow now, as we do."* All three are R6b must-survives, and the middle one now sits mid-scene and not as the close, so it can stand. But it is v1.3's moral line, which craft rule 14 quotes as the "before". If Part III needs words, fold this one first.
4. **Ambiguous *it*.** In *Their word for us was cut small beside it*, the nearest *it* is the word for a lie that *we gave them*. Rewrite: *Small in that carving was cut their word for us:* Rhenear, *the stone-deaf.*
5. ***This too Hale saw, and not I.*** comes straight after *"Look close."*, so it reads as if Hale saw Pellow saying it. Rewrite: *What followed, Hale saw, and not I.* Join it to the next paragraph.
6. **The gate-song and I.2 disagree.** g02 has Enno and Della take *the first voice between them, the one to the comma and the other the rest*, then *the guild after them*. V.7's *the rest came in at the comma* has the guild singing Della's half but not Enno's. Rewrite: *No one took the first voice. The first line went by in silence, and the rest came in after it, as though it had been sung.* *the rest* still plays on Della's *the rest*, and the Epilogue's *Ormund to the comma and Penna the rest* then answers it exactly.
7. ***the bearer's plank.*** V.7 never names a bearer, so this leans on V.5 Count IV's *Lanner's battery sank their bearer*. That is fine while g08 keeps the line. If g08 cuts it, write *the plank of their bearer, that Lanner sank*.
8. **VI.1's catalogue call is III.1's word for word.** *Hear now the names of the havens, and hold them, for they are ours.* is g05's line 13. Craft rule 22 says a refrain changes or goes, and III.2 and V.5 both turn it. Add one word: *…for they are ours again.* The homecoming is in the refrain.
9. ***for a count is no count till it be named.*** This is the third use: I.1, V.5 Count VI, and now here. It has become a tic. Cut it in VI.1, so that *Tonight in the ward I called the roll.* stands alone, and the roll's changed numbers do the work.
10. **Marl has lost his laugh.** III.1 makes him the wry fen-man (*had best go and live on a hill*). In VI.1 he is tender only. Give him four words: *A town is but the last course on the old, we used to say, and laughed.* Then *Perhaps* and *It hath not sunk* land as his and not as a general sorrow. Ebba's *Perhaps* is the right touch of memory theme 4. Keep it.
11. **Oaths or wants?** *ten of us had sworn what each would do* and Lanner's *as I sware I would* turn III.1's wants (*I will steer it in*, *I mean to climb them again*) into oaths that Kael *held*. That is a mild retcon. Either III.1 gives one clause of swearing (for instance, Kael: *Each sware it at this hearth, and I have it by heart.*), or VI.1 says *as I said I would*. This is the orchestrator's call, so that both leaves agree.
12. ***"Shift your ground."*** is given *at each True Man's shoulder*. Voice §1.1 gives the Captain *thou* to a True Man. The call is defensible as an order to the whole battery, and V.5 has *"Change your shot."* the same way. Make one ruling for both and record it.
13. **Seren's turns are mostly the guide's.** *Enno set the chisel, and Della struck*; *One course high it stood, and higher it stood never*; *She set the chisel herself, and struck*; *Kael stood by it and counted them. "One," he said.*; *The lamp went out on the shingle, and I filled it not a third time* are all craft.md's own after-lines, which craft says are "illustrations, not canon". The leaf passes §10.8 on its own turns:
    - *she threw him the rest, running*;
    - *the rest came in*;
    - *He hurried not, as lamplighters hurry not, lest the taper die*.

    Adding the mallet picture (problem 4) gives Part I one turn that is entirely Seren's.
14. **The Part III title and its first line say the same thing.** *The Plan That Carved Its Own Defeat* is followed by *They had carved their own defeat into the plan.* Both are v1.3 and must-survive, so keep them. Varying the line's order would at least stop it reading as a caption: *Into the plan they had cut their own defeat.*
15. **The halt's last sentence is long.** *Then she bent again, and cut the last stroke, and laid the chisel by the mark, and lay down with her cheek against the stone.* runs 25 words with four *and*s, at the crisis. I would keep it: it is a deed-list, the slow single motion of her last breath, and *laid … lay* plays on the Book's *laid*. Splitting the sentence before the mallet (problem 2) is enough to make the halt the shortest stretch.
16. **VI.1 has two closes.** *Every haven is Rivenkeep. I swear to that.* and then *Ten havens. I counted them twice, and it took not long.* Craft fixed the second as the close, and it reads well after the first: ten counted as one take no time, which is V.7's *"One"* in Kael's dry key. Leave it.
17. **The budget.** V.7 sits 5 words under its ceiling. The rewrites above bring it to 1,265 (−30) with every beat kept and one restored. VI.1 comes to 1,042 (−17).
18. **No Plain Words in this file.** When the twins are written, §11 holds. Two things for that writer:
    - the Rimewatch pair (*The ice took the Long Winter down. / The ice took Hesk too.*) and *He came home.* stay as plain lines alone;
    - *Ye cannot tell.* becomes *You can't tell.* and stands alone.
