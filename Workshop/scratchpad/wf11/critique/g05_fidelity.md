**Verdict.** This is a faithful draft. All the songs, fixed lines and locks hold, and the datelines agree with the reckoning (details at the end). The fixes below fall into four groups:
- two of the eleven roll tags are not yet given whole;
- one new line echoes the burning;

The rest are seeds that other tales now lean on, which have to match word for word. After every fix, both leaves still sit under their ceilings (estimates at the end).

## Fixes, most serious first

### 1. Aske's tag is not given whole, and his new line contradicts I.4 (III.1, line 43)

**Where.** *On this mountain is no wood but wreck off the shingle. Every plank of it have I touched ere it went to the fire. These were trees once.*

**What.** Replace it with:

> *Wood is dear on this ridge, and the fires have the wreck off the shingle. On every plank of it have I laid my hand ere it burned. These were trees once.*

That is +3 words and adds no *-eth*.

**Why.**
- **The habit is not whole.** v2.0's I.1 tag was *who will not pass a felled tree but he lays his hand on the bark*. voice §9.7 says III.1 must carry every tag whole. *Touched* loses the gesture.
- **III.2 pays that exact gesture.** Line 131 reads *I laid my hand on the last tree standing. Its bark was as warm as a living hand.* The plant has to be the same act.
- **Fix 2 cuts the only other "lay his hand" in III.1.** That was Harl's line.
- **"No wood but wreck" is false in the Book.**
  - In I.4 (g03 line 46), Stannard leads {{Halyna}} *to the old stand where the first staff was cut*, and they *would not fell a living tree*. So there are trees on this mountain.
  - V.1 (g07 line 115) gives the reason as *wood was dear on the ridge*.
- **The rewording keeps R6a §0.1's mend.** The wreck still burns because wood is scarce, not by any lore. It also matches V.1's own words, so V.1's fires on the tide-line are planted here.

### 2. Cut Harl's new *Aske may lay his hand on the planks. I say burn them.* (III.1, line 51)

**What.** Delete both sentences (−11 words). Keep *My shore I will have again, and the grey I will burn, and the sea too, if it would take fire.* That line is his tag whole.

**Why.**
- **Echo of the custom.** The custom Jack struck was the burning of what the grey sends. This line turns burning the grey's wood into a stance that two True Men argue at the hearth. That is the shape of the custom, even with no saying attached. The brief asks for *no echo*.
- **It is already planted.** R6b §0.1 #3 puts the root of Harl's *Burn it* in V.1. V.1 has it: *"Burn it," said Harl of the burning shore, of every plank* (g07 line 115). VI.3 pays it at g10 line 68, *what he had said of every plank of the war*.
- **It over-plants.** With this line, Harl says *burn* three times in a four-sentence entry, then again in III.2, V.1, VI.3 and VI.1. voice §4 gives a True Man *one image and one want, in one to three sentences*.
- **The logic fails.** Aske's planks already *went to the fire*, so *I say burn them* answers nothing.

### 3. Ulden's tag is not given whole: the present habit is missing (III.2, line 145)

**Where.** *Every night since, I have slept on the open ground.*

**What.** Change it to *Every night since, I have slept on the open ground, mine ear to it.* (+3 words). If the writer would rather plant it in III.1, use a present clause after line 55's *heard it hold*.

**Why.**
- The I.1 tag is in the present: *who sleeps with his ear to the ground*.
- The draft gives the ear only *till the Fall* (III.1) and during the Fall (III.2).
- VI.1 pays it as a wartime habit: *lay with mine ear to the ground, as every night of the war. I heard naught.* (g09 line 237). Without a plant, *as every night of the war* has no root.

### 4. Restore *twenty months*: J3 dated the Fall, and the date is now nowhere in Book Three (III.2, line 97)

**Where.** *In the last spring of the peace the havens had no fleet of war.*

**What.** Change it to *In the last spring of the peace, twenty months ere the banner, the havens had no fleet of war.* (+5 words).

**Why.**
- Outline III.2 lists *Jack's notes: J3 (the Fall dated: twenty months)*. Reckoning §1.2 and §2.6 give *the Fall … the two years before the banner … twenty months*.
- v2.0 had it twice (*twenty months ere the banner* and *In all the twenty months of the Fall*). The draft has neither, and the Argument dropped it too.
- I.2 (g02 line 17) is now the only place it survives.
- The tale's inner clock (three months, three days, one night, five months, the first ice, the first snows, a month in the passes) needs its whole span said once.

### 5. Name the wants as oaths, because VI.1 pays them as sworn (III.1, line 75)

**Where.** *Ten havens have I told tonight, and told them twice. I have no haven.*

**What.** Change it to *Ten havens have I told tonight, and told them twice. Ten oaths I hold. I have no haven.* (+4 words).

**Why.** VI.1 says the wants were sworn at this hearth:
- *At this hearth ten of us had sworn what each would do when his haven came home. Oath-keeper am I. I held all ten.* (g09 line 185);
- Corlen: *He sware it* (line 191);
- Lanner: *as I sware I would* (line 267).

III.1 gives the wants as wishes, and nothing in the leaf makes them oaths. Kael is *their oath-keeper* in I.1, so four words of his make the litany a vow, and *I held all ten* answers *Ten oaths I hold*.

If the orchestrator prefers, g09 can say *said* for *sworn* instead. One of the two must change.

### 6. Name Ebba in Marl's line, and give the course-stone its house (III.1, line 39)

**Where.** *My grandmother's course-stone is in my pack, and I would carry her too, if she would let me.*

**What.**
- At least: *My grandmother is old Ebba. Her course-stone is in my pack, and I would carry her too, if she would let me.* (+3 words).
- Better, closer to v2.0: *Old Ebba, my grandmother, laid the last course of our house. A stone of it is in my pack, and I would carry her too, if she would let me.* (+10 words).

**Why.**
- v2.0's line was *My grandmother, old Ebba of Fenholm, laid the top course of our house with her own two hands*. The draft drops both the name and the house.
- The leaf's own closing gesture is *Old Ebba of Fenholm began it, a breath before the rest.* It pays *She is there by the fire, nodding* only if the reader knows they are the same woman.
- g03's notes (line 349) ask III.1 and III.2 to carry *the fact that she is Marl's grandmother* and *I.5 now leans on them*.
- VI.1 pays it as *My grandmother carried the last course of our house* (g09 line 207).

### 7. The rule *both tellings are kept* is now gone from the whole Book (cross-tale; the fix belongs in g01)

**What.** The writer was right to cut Seren's added line *Of Glasspire's night … I have kept both* (craft §3.5 cuts *III.2's records*). But the foreword was to carry the rule (craft §2.3, Of This Book: *both tellings kept where they disagree*), and g01 does not. The foreword's rules paragraph (*Where a carving was broken … Where the wood gave a name of its own, I write that, and not ours.*) should gain one sentence: *Where the wood and the Shore tell a thing two ways, I have kept both.* If g01 will not take it, restore one clause in III.2 after line 167.

**Why.**
- The line is a v1.3 element (*The tales do not agree. I have kept both.*), and the outline marks it (keep).
- III.2 has Pellow's *I looked close. I saw nothing.* against *Some that fled say a shadow stood off in the fog*. VI.2 now answers *No throne sailed the night the spire fell. Of that night we hold naught.* (w02 line 275). The reader needs Seren's word, once, that she keeps disagreements.

### 8. Pass the stone to each man in III.2, or Corlen's doom line rests on a rite shown for him alone (III.2, line 109)

**Where.** *Kael put the stone into Corlen's hands.*

**What.** Change it to *Kael gave the stone to each man in turn, and to Corlen first.* (+5 words). Line 119, *Corlen gave back the stone. It was the last time he held it.*, then stands as written.

**Why.**
- The foreword (g01) sets the frame's one rule: *Whoso holdeth the stone is the teller.*
- The III.2 headnote says each man laid *the fall of his own city*. As drafted, only Corlen is handed the stone, so Tamm through Pellow lay their falls without it. The doom line *the last time he held it* then pays a custom the leaf shows for one man only.
- III.1 is consistent as it stands: Kael holds the stone and the men *answer*, as at a roll, which is what the headnote says. Leave it.

### 9. Corlen's mark must take one form Book-wide (III.1, line 31)

**What.** Change *"Wait. The sea has not finished."* to *"Wait. The sea hath not finished."*

**Why.**
- voice §1.2: *every third-person singular present takes -eth in the Book*. The fixed matter of §1.7 is the only exception, and §1.7 does not list this line.
- V.5 Count II prints *The sea hath not finished.* (g08 line 124) and asks that *III.1, III.2 and VI.1 should match* (g08 line 411).
- III.2 line 117 (*the sea had not finished*) and VI.1 (*till the sea had finished*) are past tense and stay.
- Hesk's *Someone has to keep a light.* (III.2 line 157) matches the child's line in VI.1 (g09), so the pair agrees. The orchestrator should rule whether this refrain also goes to *hath*. If it does, change both.

### 10. Tamm's *spans of stone* now contradicts VI.2 (III.1, line 35)

**Where.** *bound across the water by three spans of stone.*

**What.** Change it to *bound across the water by three great spans.* (−1 word). This is v1.3's own wording, *three great spans joined it across the water*.

**Why.** VI.2 now says Leavaren's *spans were our kin, laid flat, and walked on by your feet* (w02 line 257). v2.0 reconciled the two with a roll-note (*the spans … were laid upon the black wood*), and w02 has cut that note. *Of stone* now reads as a flat contradiction. Tamm's *a stone of each* still holds, because the piers were stone. Without *of stone* the gap between the two leaves is the reader's, as voice §5 wants.

### 11. Make the Book Three Argument agree with the Contents (heading, line 3, against g01 line 35)

**What.** The heading reads *Ten havens, and ten men to tell them: what we had, and how we walked away from it.* The Contents in g01 read *What we had, and how we walked away from it.* Pick one and set it in both places.

**Why.** The builder prints both. craft §3.5 wants *one line each … v1.3's tags are the model*, which favours g01's shorter line. g05's version keeps the tell/count play. Either is fine, but they must match.

### 12. The Tidesmeet epithet: the orchestrator should rule; I recommend the canon *meets* (III.1, line 33)

**Where.** *Tidesmeet, where the river meeteth the tide.*

**Why.**
- R6a III.1 (d) lists *the ten names and epithets* as *fixed canon*, including *Tidesmeet, where the river meets the tide*. craft §6.2 quotes it the same way.
- The writer flagged it. HEAVY grammar wants *meeteth*. Canon wants the name left untouched, as with the Throne names.
- Recommendation: treat the ten epithets as fixed matter under voice §1.7 and keep *meets*. That also brings III.1's *-eth* count down to 6.
- Whichever way it goes, it must be Book-wide.

### 13. A saying that comes back must come back in the same words (III.2, line 127)

**What.** Change *A town is only the last course on the old* to *A town is but the last course on the old*.

**Why.** VI.1 repeats Marl's saying as *A town is but the last course on the old, we used to say.* (g09 line 207), and *but* is the stock word for *only* (voice §2.1). (0 words.)

### 14. Restore Hesk's taper as one clause, for V.7 (III.1, line 59) [low]

**What.** Before the strike line, add *Every dusk a taper went from window to window.*, so the entry ends on *My mother kept those lamps.* (+9 words).

**Why.**
- R6a III.1 (b) lists *a taper carried window to window at dusk* among Hesk's beats. The writer cut it for economy.
- V.7 Part II still has the Captain going along the wall *as lamplighters hurry not, lest the taper die* (g09 line 104). In v2.0 that picture echoed this taper on purpose.
- The picture works without its plant, so this one is optional.

### 15. *night-watchmen* should be *night-watch* (III.2, line 155) [low]

**What.** Change *the night-watchmen steer by it* to *the night-watch steer by it*.

**Why.** This matches VI.1's *the night-watch steered by it* (g09 line 245) and v2.0's *the night-watch*.

- **Brenn, ▒▒▒▒ and the cross-sign.** None appear in these leaves, and nothing breaches their locks.
