## Fixes, most serious first

### V.5

**1. Count II, l.136: restore Voss's raising.**
- *What:* change *Voss took his battery, and at the next holding wet a finger, as Corlen did.* to *Voss took his battery, and was raised a True Man. At the next holding he wet a finger, as Corlen did.* (+5 words)
- *Why:* Count VI's roll is *fourteen names, and twelve answered*. The Twelve of I.1 (g01 l.232) with Corlen and Pellow dead come to ten, so the fourteen needs two men raised. v2.0 raised Voss in this count (*was raised a True Man that winter*), and I.5's headnote (g03 l.123) already calls him *True Man of the harbour battery*.
- The writer's own note (l.403) says *the roll… needs Voss raised (Count II)*, but the clause was cut. Ruan's raising can stay implied in V.7 (g09 l.130–132, *I am not a True Man… So it went to Ruan*). R6b (g) asks only that the counts agree.

**2. Count V, l.188: restore the premise of the last tell.**
- *What:* before *A green hull whose sail blanched was no green hull.* add *The young wood's sails never went pale.* (+7), or a shorter HEAVY form such as *No green sail ever blanched.* (+5).
- *Why:* in v2.0 the tell rests on *the young wood's sails never once went pale*. The draft dropped that line, and also dropped Count II's label *the first answering sail*, so nothing in the Book now tells the reader that green wood cannot blanch. Without it the tell does not make sense.
- V.7 Part II leans on the same fact: Pellow sees the sails go pale and says *"They are not green."* (g09 l.92–94). R6b (c) lists *the tells* as must-survive.

**3. Count III, after l.160: give Wick his strip.**
- *What:* add one clause before *So a boy died of a gun that was not there.*, for example *That night the Captain cut his strip.* (+7) or *The Captain cut his strip.* (+5).
- *Why:* every other named death gets its strip somewhere:
  - Jory and Corlen here;
  - {{Enrella}} and Pellow in V.7 (g09 l.55, l.128);
  - Ebba in I.5 (g03 l.177);
  - Hesk in VI.1 (g09 l.259).
- Wick's death is told only here (craft §2.2 ledger), so with the strip cut he is the one named dead the Captain never cuts for.
- Outline §6 says to keep the rites of mourning the same every time, so the listener learns them. Craft §2.3 gives V.5 *one thing learned, one death, and one strip* to a count. Craft's *a clause, or nothing* permits leaving it out only where the strip is told elsewhere.

**4. Count IV, l.178: *At dusk* clashes with V.7.**
- *What:* change *At dusk I walked the wall to count.* to *Ere dusk I walked the wall to count.* (0 words)
- *Why:* V.7 Part I says *At dusk Stannard carried Enno the whole mile, and laid him by her* (g09 l.47). If Kael walks at dusk, he cannot find Enno still in the east breach. v2.0 had *ere the dusk came down*. *Ere* is stock.

**5. Count VI, l.216: keep VI.1's mirror.**
- *What:* change *fourteen names, and twelve answered* to *fourteen names, and twelve to answer*. (+1)
- *Why:* VI.1 answers this line word for word: *Fourteen names, and eleven to answer* (g09 l.279). Craft §2.3 (V.5 halt) and the outline (V.5, Count VI) both give *twelve to answer*. As drafted, the refrain does not echo.

**6. Count II, l.134: restore Kael's definition.**
- *What:* change *That is a True Man.* to *That is what a True Man is.* (+2)
- *Why:* outline §6 (D6) keeps this exact line in Kael's mouth as Corlen's memorial, and R6b (c) lists it as must-survive. *That is a True Man* reads as praise of the man, and loses the definition that *It is also what killed him* turns on.

**7. Count II, l.122: the standard mechanic is garbled.**
- *What:* change *another took up the standard, and the fleet its temper* to *another took up the standard, and the fleet took the new bearer's temper* (+3), or an equivalent.
- *Why:* *its temper* points back to the fleet, so the line seems to say the fleet kept its own temper. v2.0 and R6b (g) say the fleet *takes on the temper of the new one*. *Choose which heart ye leave them* only follows if that is clear. Keep it to one soldier's sentence, as R6b (g) asks.

**8. Dateline and headnote, l.82 and l.84: date the laying, not the span.**
- *What:*
  - dateline: *At the end of each Tide, six winters.* (7 words);
  - headnote: *Laid by Kael the Counter, that would tell no Tide till it was over. This time he brought me the counts himself, and bade me mend them as I mended the first. I have mended them less.* (40 words).
- *Why:*
  - *From the first winter to the sixth* reads as the date of the events, which voice §6 and craft §3.1 forbid.
  - The reckoning lays the counts at each Tide's end (Cam 6, 14, 22, 26, 31, 32). That runs from late autumn of year 1 to the end of the sixth winter.
  - The headnote then no longer repeats *at the end of each Tide*.
  - The verdict line now matches craft §4.4 item 2 word for word: its full stop before *I have mended them less*, and the echo of the I.1 headnote.

**9. Count VI, l.222: name the cloak at the payoff.**
- *What:* change *His half hung to his knees* to *His half of the cloak hung to his knees*. (+3)
- *Why:* this is the refrain's payoff (craft rule 22; outline §6 rite 3, which quotes *His half of the cloak*). The sentence before it says only *cut a hand's breadth*, and the cloak was last named about 1,200 words earlier, in Count I.
- Keep *Tonight it cannot reach his belt.* It obeys §1.2 and §1.6, and the Epilogue (g11 l.284) is already built on it.

### V.2

**10. l.64: restore *raw edge to raw edge*.**
- *What:* change *I laid them side by side, edge to edge.* to *I laid them side by side, raw edge to raw edge.* (+2)
- *Why:* VI.3 pays this in Seren's own words: *the two planks I had laid raw edge to raw edge* (g10 l.80). Its writer lists it as one of the two things V.2 must keep (g10 l.242). Outline §8 V.2 scene 10 and v2.0 both have it. *their two raw edges* in the sentence before can stay.

**11. l.15: the count contradicts itself.**
- *What:* change *Each had given {{Halyna}} one sign and one word* to *Each of the rest had given {{Halyna}} one sign and one word*. (+3)
- *Why:* l.11 has just said *One of them was silent*. v2.0 had *every one save one*.

**12. l.48: anchor the *Burn* plank to the Fall.**
- *What:* change *had carried it up the mountain, charred black at one end* to *had carried it up the mountain after the Fall, charred black at one end*. (+3)
- *Why:* the turn needs the reader to know this plank is not from our wall. It is the order the havens died under (V.6, w02 l.190: *in your havens the word was* Burn). v2.0 said so twice (*after the Fall*; *it was not from our wall at all*), and the draft now says neither. III.2 (g05 l.141) and the Knowings (g11 l.163) carry the same fact. Three words make it land here.

**13. l.13: the runners must carry.**
- *What:* change *Two runners to a plank they came home* to *Two runners to a plank bare them home*. (0 words; *bare* is in the stock, voice §1.3)
- *Why:* as written, the planks are the subject, and *their arms* then has no clear owner. R6b (b) beat 2 is that the runners carried every carved plank home. *Other hands act* should be visible here.

**14. l.5, optional: put the Tide in the dateline.**
- *What:* *Late in the second summer, in the Second Tide.* (8 words)
- *Why:* this matches the samples' form (*The third winter, in the Tide of Remembering.*) and the reckoning (Cam 13, *near the end of the Second Tide*). The current dateline is not wrong.

The writer left them *to Book Six*, but VI.2 cut them (w02 l.379), and g01 l.399 keeps them to a chip at most. Either add one clause after *sat down in our havens* (for example *and the wall named them for beasts*, +7, which would need a further offset) or accept that the beat is gone.

- **Kael's *what I did not count I will not tell*** (R6b (c)) is folded. The I.1 sample carries it (*What I told not, that will I not tell*), and Count VI's *I will not tell it at this hearth* echoes it. That is acceptable.
