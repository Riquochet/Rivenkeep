# w02 · Jack's Eye

*Critic's reading of `drafts/w02.md` (V.3, V.4 with Seren's empty leaf, V.6 with the Song of the Years, VI.2), against `voice_v3.md` §10, `craft.md` §8, the three models and the memory theme. Severe on purpose: Jack has turned down two versions.*

---

## Verdict, in five lines

- **V.4 is nearly there.** It has a real arc (patience, the break, the plea, the answer, the carving, the bitter irony), and its best lines are Book-quality. It needs small fixes and one change of order.
- **V.3 is a good report, not yet a story.** It plants *A silent stone may be waiting* and never pays it. Its halt reads as a contradiction, and its last ring backs away from its best picture.
- **V.6 is a manual with a song at the end.** It has no scene at all. Ring 2 is a bestiary: the command structure, the unit types, nine behaviours and six wave names. This is the "history text book" Jack named, in its purest form.
- **VI.2 is a register.** It has ten entries in one mould (*Our judgement was* ten times, *[place] we sat* six times), and its strongest beat (the children in the light) comes before the catalogue, so the leaf peaks early and then drones.
- **All four readings use one template.** Each has a "— no." correction, a broken line and a [ ] gap, and V.3 has the "Our —" slip that IV.2, IV.4 and IV.6 already use. Each reading also sums up its tale in order. They are fragments in costume, not piecing.

The mechanics are clean:
- the Song, Jack's line, the ten judgements, the three carvings and the facing leaf are all verbatim (checked against v2.0);
- the locks hold, and there are no native keys to keep (v2.0 has none on these leaves);
- the budgets and the *-eth* caps are met;
- the old grammar is mostly correct.

The faults are in the build.

## The worst problems, with rewrites

### 1. V.6 has no scene, and ring 2 is a game manual

**What Jack would say:** "It's like you are writing a history text book."

Ring 2 runs from *A hull with no one aboard is ruled by carving* to *someone struck back*. That is about 600 words, and nearly every paragraph opens on a mechanism:
- the bearer and the heart;
- the warriors of the landings and the winged seed;
- the nine tempers;
- the law of the years;
- the six tides by name.

Craft §2.3 says of V.6: *"It is not a manual."* The leaf's one great move, *Naught but song had ever been asked of those trees. Now one word was asked of them… Burn*, arrives after the reader has waded through all of that.

There are three further faults:
- **A broken joint.** The writer moved the tides paragraph between the law of the years and *The crudest came first*. That breaks the leaf's one "therefore": the law, *so* the crudest came first.
- **Mixed tenses.** In one paragraph the roots *are* the wood's hands and the seed *ripened*.
- **Native names.** V.6 carries seven of them: *Aelralen* and six tide-names. Of the six, only *Ralenthae* (V.3) and *Aelthae* (V.7) are paid anywhere. The judges threw out register C because invented words muddied it (voice §0), and this is the same fault arriving through lore.

**Fix.** The heart that tells V.6 is itself a bearer, an old tree of the first groves, of *Aelthae*. Give it its own cutting as the halt, told close. It is the same deed as V.4's *It was our kin, and it felt the blade*, now told from inside the tree.

1. **Fold the unit list into the singing litany** in ring 1, where it becomes part of the song and not a manual:

   > Hulls they sang from the breathing trunks, keels from the roots that fed them, masts from saplings trained by many summers of song in the wind. Of the fine roots, that find their way in the dark, they sang the warriors of the landings, for none of the long-lived could be spared. What they touched, the wood knew, for they were the wood's hands. In the crowns of the broad hulls they sang a seed with wings.

   The past tense here also thins the *-eth* count, which ring 1 overloads (*lieth*, *knoweth*, *saileth*).

2. **Open ring 2 on the halt:**

   > A tree we were, and old, in the first groves. Many summers they sang to us, and we bent. Then the singing ceased.
   >
   > The blade went into our heart.
   >
   > In the wound they cut the orders. Through the roots we carved them on into every hull about us, and every hull obeyed, for the wood of the hull and the wood of the command were one. The wood obeyeth the wood.
   >
   > The long-lived made that bond into a leash.
   >
   > In the night, while ye laid your stone, we carved the morrow. Ye and we were both at work in the dark.

   - *Ye and we* is worse than *Ye and the heart*, and better for it: the wood owns its part.
   - *Aelralen* goes, because *the bond of roots* is enough and the name is paid nowhere. Let the orchestrator overrule this if it is a canon term he needs.

3. **Make the grief the wood's own:**

   > In grief were the bearers chosen, and we were chosen so. The grief is in our grain.

   Keep the tempers as they stand. They are the leaf's good catalogue, and *And some know you best, and bend least.* lands.

4. **Mend the law of the years,** and put the joint back:

   > As many years as a wood had when it was chosen, so much could it hold, and the older it was, the later it came ready. Against that law no council can carve. One season, one sign. … The eldest pillars hold a judgement.
   >
   > So the crudest came first.

   *The years a wood had at its choosing set what it can hold* is opaque, and the version above replaces it. It uses the past tense, and adds no *-eth* beside *holds it still*.

5. **Move the tides to ring 3,** just before *We were sent*, cut to what is paid. There the growings going out year by year narrow to *ours*, and then to the doom line:

   > Each growing went out at the leaf-fall, when its ring was laid, and came out of the breath with the winter. Ralenthae was the tide the roots taught. Aelthae is ours. Two more were growing behind us, and what is cut in them, we know not.
   >
   > We were sent. We are still sent. No voice among us can call us home.

   This keeps the outline's one-line law of the Tides (§3.1) and drops *Leathae*, *Vaelthae*, *Rhenthae* and *Eirthae*. They are wave labels, and no tale pays them.

6. **Cut *None of the long-lived had we to spare, so of root were the warriors of the landings grown.*** It is two inversions in one sentence, the clumsiest line in the file. Step 1 replaces it.

The leaf should land near 800 words, with every element R6b requires still in it.

### 2. VI.2 is a register: ten entries in one mould, and the peak spent before the catalogue

**What Jack would say:** "These stories just blah. They don't build."

- **One mould, ten times.** Every entry runs: *We are **X**, gloss. [Place] we sat. Our judgement was J. [Closing line.]* That is *Our judgement was* ten times. *[Beneath the harbour / Under the three spans / On the mountain / In the ash / In the cellars] we sat* comes five times, and *Among them we sat* a sixth time just before. This is the never-list's "names arriving like entries in a register". Craft III.1 says a catalogue builds by varying one gesture and pausing on the last; here nothing varies but the nouns.
- **The peak comes too early.** The leaf's two strongest beats both come in ring 2, before the catalogue:
  - *The children of the long-lived lay down in the light, and could not breathe.*
  - *To come to you, we spent the last of our own sky.*

  Ten even-weighted entries follow, so the leaf peaks at word 200 and then files.

**Fix: keep the anchor and vary everything after it.**
- *We are **X**, gloss* stays as the litany anchor.
- *Our judgement was* stays only three times: the first entry, the halt (Eirlenth) and the turn (Neivaere). Elsewhere the judgement stands bare, as its own italic sentence.
- The cause comes before the judgement in some entries and after it in others.
- The entries quicken from Rhenvael to Senneir (two or three sentences each), slow for Eirlenth, quicken again for Naelthar, and slow for Neivaere.

Three models:

> We are **Leavaren**, bearer of new branches. The three spans were our kin, laid flat, and your feet walked on them. Under them we sat. *Strike one head; two will answer.* Where we were cut we grew again, as our kin would have grown, had the iron let them.

> We are **Vaelress**, the grain that rots. The one wood in the world that rotteth not were we. Into the fen our kin were driven, and a town stood on them. *Rot the wall; wait for the mending to fail.* So we were made into rot.

> We are **Eirlenth**, the long winter. Our kin roofed a great hall on the ice. All the long nights the warmth of many came up into them, and they held it. Our judgement was *Wait in the white; strike the stone that cannot mend.*
>
> We waited in the white a long while, and no one roofed us.

*No one roofed us* needs the warmth to have a body. The wood can know warmth rising into its timbers, though not feasting. The draft's *and held its warmth* is too thin for the halt to bite.

**Reorder the close so that it ends on the cost.** At present the mirror is followed by a roll-call that sums up.

> These were the ten, the last pillars of the breath. All are fallen now, and your havens are your own again.
>
> Once we held the sky up between our crowns, and under it the children of the long-lived slept. The sky over the groves is thin, and the grain holds it still.

**Two optional strokes, for the orchestrator:**
- **The orphaned line.** *Our own names we had.* is orphaned now that *You named us for beasts* is cut (correctly). Either drop it, or make it the call that heads the catalogue as a mirror of the facing leaf. VI.1 says *Hear now the names of the havens, and hold them, for they are ours.* VI.2 could say: *Our own names we had. Hold them, for they were ours.* That is Seren's shaping, not the wood knowing Kael. The reader who flips the tab feels *are* against *were*.
- **The lamp.** After *no one roofed us*, one line could give the other side of VI.1's Hesk, in the wood's senses only: *At the last a small fire came out on the ice over us, and stood where the hall had been.* The death ledger gives VI.2 only *no one roofed us*, so this needs the orchestrator's word. But it is the "switch one tab and it all changes" effect Jack praised, and it names nothing.

**Smaller VI.2 faults:**
- *In the ash of our kin we sat, that had burned slow*: the relative clause is split from its noun, so it seems to say *we* burned slow. Write *We sat in the ash of our kin, that had burned slow, and not as hulls burn.*
- *Our own names we had. Heavy to carry is a judgement*: with *Among them we sat* before them, these make three inversions in a row (the Yoda fault). Item 5 below covers this.

### 4. V.3 plants its turn and never pays it; the halt reads as a contradiction

**What Jack would say:** "They don't build."

**The halt reads as a contradiction.** The draft says *One fire we threw at the stone that had spoken… The stone answered not.* Two lines later it says *Your wall still flashed.* A child at the front will ask how the wall flashed if the stone never answered. V.5 Count III gives the answer: the gun was moved forty yards in the night, so the flash came from a stone that had been silent.

**That is the payoff of V.3's own plant.** The heart carved the plant overnight: *A silent stone may be waiting.* The caution was true, and it was not enough. The wood can know where a flash comes from. It does not need to know who moved the gun or why, so the puzzle stays for the reader.

**The kin's feeling is not paid either.** The first coming has *our kin felt it in their own boards*. The death never returns to the kin, so the cost lands on no one but the hull.

**Rewrite of the halt and the death:**

> Behind us our kin stood off in the breath, and waited. Flat lay the water, and we were slow.
>
> Your wall still flashed.
>
> It flashed not from the stone that had spoken. It flashed from a stone along the wall, that had been silent.
>
> With the sea in us already, we could not turn a second time. The iron found us where it had found us before, low and through, as though it too kept a count.
>
> Behind us, in the breath, our kin felt it in their own boards.

**Ring 3 backs away from its best line.** *Your hands are on us now.* is the present pressing on the past. It is followed by a paragraph explaining how the knowledge travelled (*The hulls that lived bare it back through the breath. From the edge it went home, root to root…*). Put the transmission first, as the cost, then the long lying, then the hands, then the saying:

> On that second morning we went down in the lee of your wall. But ere we went down, we had told it. The hulls that lived bare it home, root to root, to the groves where the next hulls stood growing, and they grew knowing it.
>
> Long we lay in the cold, and the keels of our kin went over us. Then the tide worked us loose, and the surf brought us to your shingle.
>
> Your hands are on us now.
>
> So we went home once, and told; we came back to look, and died looking. The groves have it now, and the grain holds it still.

### 6. Headnotes: an "it" with nothing to point to, a garbled sentence, and a gloss up front

- **V.3.** *Of the wound it told its kin, {{Halyna}} say it went among the hulls as the Bonded feel each other's hurt.* This parses first as "the wound told its kin". It also explains the leaf's central beat before the tale begins. The headnote already shows the bond: Rhyna *touched it not, and knew it as he knew it*. Let that be the comparison:
  > *Halvard's hands were on this hull. Rhyna, with the salt-cuts open in her palms, touched it not, and knew it as he knew it; and so, {{Halyna}} say, the hull's hurt went among its kin.* (36 words)
- **V.4.** *Rhyna laid a hand on it…* gives *it* no antecedent. Write *on this heart*:
  > *Rhyna laid a hand on this heart on the shingle, and took it away at once; Halvard came up from the vault with his face wet. The knowing took a moment, the telling the rest of that Tide, for a council is many minds, and they had one pair of mouths.* (49 words)

  *One pair of mouths* is the best headnote turn in the file. Keep it.
- **V.6.** *On his shoulder Halvard bare it up* also has no antecedent:
  > *Halvard bare this heart up on his shoulder, and ere he set it down it came to them both, though Rhyna sat at the hearth. Too great it was, and lay in the vault till spring; its song I set as verse, as near as ink will go.* (47 words)
- **VI.2.** The headnote is good. Take *A name, last and hardest* out of the reading, where it repeats Seren's sentence.

## The rest, numbered

1. **V.4: put the break after the answer.** At present *And the patience broke.* comes before the debate, so the plea and the answer happen after the outcome is known. Move the line after the young's answer, and the scene becomes the cause of the break. That also pays *counselled patience still* at the right moment. The order becomes:
   - the young put the question;
   - the elders plead;
   - *But few were the old voices…*;
   - *"We listened three of their lives…"*;
   - **And the patience broke.**;
   - *That was true, and not the whole of the truth…*;
   - Jack's line;
   - the vote.

   Every beat is kept.
2. **V.4: give the grove scene one physical thing,** taken from IV.6, where the keel was *laid among the roots of the silver elders*. The young put the question in the very grove where the bones lie: *Into the grove they came, where the keel lay among the roots, and put the question over it.* This adds no lore and gives the halt a body.
3. **V.4: a tautology.** *The silver elders keep all that is said beneath them, as they keep all they hear* says one thing twice. Write *The silver elders keep all they hear.* Then the ring-3 echo (*The silver elders keep them, as they keep all they hear, and so do we*) has the phrase once on each side, not three times.
4. **V.4: the question is put twice.** *So the question was put* repeats *put the question*. Write *So the vote was carried, by the weight of anger and grief.*
5. **V.4: the seventy-year wait** runs four sentences. R6b (g) asks for two: *While one elder that had pleaded yet lived, the growing lay ready at the edge of your sea, and went not. When the last went down into the moss, they that had cried for war cut it loose.*
6. **V.4: two *ere* in three sentences** (*ere Aelrhen came home*, *Ere their years were full*). The stock rule is never two close together. Recast the second: *The eldest dwindled, their years not full, their strength running out like water from a cracked jar.*
7. **V.4: the memory line.** *Their words went not into the moss… and so do we: "Learn their words."* sits on the edge of a moral. Keep it, because the mirror with *Nor went the carving down* earns it. Do not add a word to it, and make sure no later leaf glosses it (Knowings included).
8. **V.3: a double *first*.** *At first light on the first coming* is clumsy. Write *The first time, at the grey of morning, our kin were about us, and the bearer at our head.*
9. **V.3: keep the gun's *spake*.** *it spake again* · *A stone that spake yesterday* · *the stone that had spoken* is Seren's play at its quietest: the wall's stones speak, while the wood's stone at the warden's door could not. Say nothing about it.
10. **V.6: *The long-lived had ever guided the wood*.** *Ever* in the sense *always* is a silent false friend (voice §2.4), and the same leaf uses *ever* in the modern sense two sentences later (*naught had ever been asked*). Write *From the first the long-lived guided the wood.*
11. **V.6: the Book Five line.** The writer is right that it belongs in Book Five's Argument, not in the wood's mouth. Confirm that whoever sets `## BOOK FIVE` (the V.1 writer) carries it word for word, or the Book loses one of Jack's fixed lines.
12. **VI.2: the Throne names must match VI.1 and the Knowings.**
    - VI.2 has *the rotting grain*, while VI.1 (`g09.md`) and the Knowings table (`g11.md`) both have *the Grain That Rots*. These are names, and they stand outside the *-eth* rule like the judgements. Revert VI.2 to *the grain that rots*.
    - The same holds for *bearer of new branches* in VI.2 against *the Bearer of Branches* in VI.1 and the Knowings. One form, Book-wide. VI.2 gives the name, so the orchestrator should pick, and the other two follow.
13. **VI.2: the two *came we* lines.** *Late came we.* (the opening) and *Long after came we.* (Neivaere) stand as a deliberate pair. Keep both, and do not add a third.
14. **V.3: *Twice in one cold season came we to your wall.*** It raises the question, but the stakes come late. With the kin's refrain paid at the death (item 4), the stakes are carried. No doom line is needed, since *Now are we broken wood* already gives the end.
15. **Deaths, and the good of the dead (memory theme 4).** With items 2 and 4 applied, every death in these leaves has its kindness or its token:
    - V.3: the hull told, and its kin felt it;
    - V.4: the elders' *Learn their words*, kept;
    - V.6: the trees that only ever sang;
    - VI.2: the sky held up, and the children asleep under it.

    Do not add more. The wood is colder than a teller, and should be.
