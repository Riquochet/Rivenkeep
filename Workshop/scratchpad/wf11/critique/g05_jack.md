# Jack's eye on g05 · Book Three (III.1 Of Their Raising · III.2 Of Their Fall)

*Read as Jack would read it: a first-time reader who has turned down two versions. I checked it against voice_v3 §10, craft §8, the three model leaves, R6a, the outline, and the drafts beside it (g03 I.4/I.5, g08 V.5, g09 VI.1, g11 Epilogue). I counted each tale on its own with `count_v3.py`; the split files are in `critique/_g05j/`.*

## The verdict

**III.2 is close.** The falls get faster, the men's lines are lean, and the plant-and-pay design works: in III.1 each man shows a token, in III.2 we learn how he carried it out, and in VI.1 it pays. Corlen's last telling, done as a deed, is the best new thing in the draft. Two things hold it back. Its proportions are upside down, with about 255 words of explanation before the first city falls and a halt that is shorter than the Eldhythe beat. And a few lines go clerical.

**III.1 does not build yet.** It is a gazetteer: ten entries, each opening with a description of the city. Most of those openings have the same fronted shape (*On both banks it stood… On pilings it stood… Deep in the southern woods it lay… Hewn it was… Where the mountain smoketh into the sea it stood… Dug it was*). Then come a habit, a want and a saying. Jack would call it "a history text book" by the fourth haven. The parts are good. The tale has no question pressing on it, no stakes and no shape except the list. VI.1 (g09) has already handed us the fix, and g05 misses it: VI.1 says *At this hearth ten of us had sworn what each would do when his haven came home. Oath-keeper am I. I held all ten*, and then *He sware it* and *as I sware I would*. **Make III.1 the night of the oaths.**

**What is clean:**
- Budgets: III.1 is 1,053 of 1,100 and III.2 is 1,156 of 1,200. Both are a little short of the 5% aim.
- No run-ons. The longest sentence is 28 words.
- One semicolon (Jack's own line). No contractions.
- *-eth* density: 7 in III.1 and 3 in III.2, every one on the right person.
- No custom of burning. All the locks hold.
- The archaism is correct and consistent.

## The worst problems, with rewrites

### 1 · III.1 is a list of cities, not a tale. Make it the night of the oaths.

**What is wrong:**
- The first ~190 words are preamble: the call, a paragraph of geography and ethnography, the east tower, Seren's report on the rolls, and Kael's rule. Rule 4 asks the tale to open on a moment. The moment is already in the draft, buried as sentence ten: *From the east tower that coast may be seen yet… Not one hearth on it sendeth up smoke.*
- Each man gets four to six sentences: the city, the habit, the want, the saying. voice §4 allows the True Men "one man, one image and one want, in one to three sentences each."
- The wants are uneven, which is fine, but they carry no weight. *I mean to climb them again* is a wish. Nobody is on the hook.

**The fix.** It adds no lore, because VI.1 already states it. Kael is the oath-keeper (I.1). He calls each haven, and its man answers and swears what he will do when it is come home. Kael holds the oaths, and at the end he has no haven to swear by. That makes the catalogue build:
- every oath raises the question *will it be kept?*, which hangs over the Book until VI.1;
- the reader who knows Corlen, Hesk and Pellow will die hears three oaths that cannot be kept;
- Kael's close turns the gesture at the last place;
- the hearth's *We remember*, said after ten vows, is felt as a vow itself (memory theme 1) with no word about it.

**Opening.** This replaces lines 11–25. It is about 150 words against about 190.

> Give me the stone. No, I will stand. At a roll men stand.
>
> At first light I went up the east tower. From there that coast may be seen yet, as far as the haze. Not one hearth on it sendeth up smoke.
>
> Hear now the names of the havens, and hold them, for they are ours.
>
> Builders were our fathers, and no seafarers. They feared the open water, and built on the sheltered shores, where the mountains go down in long falls to the sea. Over the mountains they paved roads like veins. Ten havens they raised, the harbour-cities of the songs, and every wall of them was Stonewright work.
>
> *Then I read from the rolls:*
>
> Each haven hath the one first line: *This day was the first course laid, and a staff cut for the work.* Small is the clerks' hand, as if they were loath to waste the leaf. Ten havens, and ten staffs.
>
> *Then Kael spake again:*
>
> Another man's city I tell not. I call the haven, and its own man shall answer, and swear what he will do when it is come home. Oath-keeper am I. I hold them.

What the rewrite changes:
- *At a roll men stand* varies the refrain (craft rule 22) and explains the headnote's standing.
- The tower moment now comes first.
- The rolls are staged, not reported (never-list 5). The draft's *Then I read from the rolls:* was followed by commentary, not by a reading.
- *I hold them* answers *hold them*.

**One city line per man, the habit as a deed in the hall, one oath.** Most of the material is already in the draft. Three samples:

> **Tidesmeet, where the river meeteth the tide.**
>
> Tamm of the three spans. On both banks it stood, and three spans of stone bound it across the water.
>
> *Then Tamm set three stones on the hearthstone, in their order, as by his blanket every night.*
>
> One span is a plank. Three is a city. On its bank I will set them out again.

> **Holtward, the keep under the canopy.**
>
> Aske of the canopy. So close among the trees it lay that a traveller might pass under it and see no wall. No bough would we cut, and every autumn we swept the wall-walk of its leaves by hand.
>
> *By the hearth lay the night's wreck-wood. Aske laid his hand on every plank ere it went on the fire.*
>
> These were trees once. No axe shall come into Holtward while I live.

> **Highreach, the tower of the floating stones.**
>
> Lanner of the crags. So high were its crags that every course went up on cranes, and hung on the wind as hawks hang over a field. A wind I smell a day off. A boy of those crags can whistle one up ere he can walk. I will climb them again, and whistle for the wind.

Why these work:
- **Tamm.** The stones become something the hall sees. The draft's *Here is a stone of each, as I set them out by my blanket every night* was muddled.
- **Aske.** This hearth burns wreck-wood, so his habit happens in front of us. That removes the explaining sentence *On this mountain is no wood but wreck off the shingle*.
- **Lanner.** The smelling becomes his own habit, which III.2 pays: *A whole day ahead of them I smelled the grey on it.* The draft gave it to "a boy of those crags".

**Oaths the other men need, each one paid in g09:**
- **Corlen:** *The first boat of ours to come home, I will steer it in.* This drops the *-eth* from *cometh* and splits off the draft's 28-word *and*-chain.
- **Ulden:** *…and heard it hold. Under that roof I will sleep again.* (VI.1: *Tonight I sleep under mine own roof of stone.*)
- **Pellow:** *When Glasspire is come home, mine shall be the first eye on it.* (VI.1: *Pellow should have seen it first.* This is planted nowhere now.)
- **Marl** (*I am to lay her stone*), **Garvel**, **Harl** and **Hesk** already swear in effect. Change Lanner's *I mean to* to *I will*.

**Kael's close.** This replaces lines 73–77.

> Twelve of us answered under the banner. Ten have sworn tonight. Stannard of the ridge sat by and said naught, for the ridge was never a haven. Only he knocked once on the hearthstone with his knuckle. No stone will he trust till he have knocked on it.
>
> Ten havens have I told tonight, and ten oaths I hold. I have no haven.
>
> The Captain found me on a road.

*and told them twice* is cut. It used up VI.1's *I counted them twice, and it took not long*, which already echoes I.1. A third counting-twice in between flattens the ring.

**Net words.** About −40, so roughly 1,010, inside the 5–10% aim.

### 2 · III.2 has its weight in the wrong place: long exposition, a short halt.

**The numbers:**
- About 255 words of the tale come before the first fall: the peace, the sails, the envoy, the decks, the naming, the plainer sails, and *Now each man for his own city, in the order of the falling*.
- The halt (Rimewatch) is 105 words, smaller than Eldhythe's 120.

Craft §2.1 says the halt is the longest stretch and is told close. Here it is one fall among ten, a little slower.

**The opening, tightened.** About 195 words against 255. Every element is kept.

> Give me the stone. No, I will stand. This is no tale to sit for.
>
> Their names ye held in the autumn. Hold them now through the falling, and look not away, for we looked not away in the telling.
>
> In the last spring of the peace the swallows nested in the arches of the gates, and no man of the havens ever broke a nest. The havens had no fleet of war. Their walls were built for the high days and the weather. Their gates were wide, and stood open at night.
>
> Out of the grey, without warning, came the dark sails. No envoy came that any man could understand, but a knocking out of the grey, like thunder, on and on. The soldiers on this wall call it the drums. We looked for a captain on those ships, and a crew, and one man at one rope. No one stood on the decks. I swear to that.
>
> So we called it the ghost fleet, and its senders the Mystaeri, after the mist. A man must call his death by some name.
>
> Plainer were those sails than the sails that come against us now. Each came alone, and needed no more, for there was naught to stop them. No haven sent to another for help, for none had any help to send.
>
> *Then the stone went down the bench from hand to hand, Corlen first.*

**The halt, told close.** About 150 words against 105. It has a breath before the strike, one object that sounds, the strike alone in its paragraph, and a small aftermath. The taper the draft cut from III.1 comes back here as the one picture, and the good of the dead is remembered without a word about it (memory theme 4).

> **Rimewatch.**
>
> On the first ice of the autumn it was taken. The great hall burned on the mere with its lamps yet lit. Window by window the fire went, as my mother went at dusk with her taper. It burned as it had always shone.
>
> Into the smoke I went, to her window, and took down her lamp. It was lit.
>
> Then I turned for the shore. The ice was new, a night old. All the way across the mere it spake under me, far off and then near. Once the wind laid the flame flat, and I put my hand about it till it stood again. Behind me the roof went in. Before me there was no light but the one in my hand.
>
> The ice held.
>
> I carried the lamp out lit. Every dusk since, I have lit it on the north wall, and the night-watch steer by it.
>
> Someone has to keep a light.

The strike stays three shared words. VI.1's *The ice spake under him, as it spake the night he carried that lamp out of Rimewatch* and *That night it held* now answer a scene, not a sentence.

**Net words.** About −15, so roughly 1,140.

### 3 · Cut Harl's new *Aske may lay his hand on the planks. I say burn them.* (III.1, line 51)

This line is invented, and it is wrong in four ways:
- **It contradicts the scene.** Aske has just said every plank goes to the fire, so the planks are burned either way, and *I say burn them* means nothing.
- **It pre-empts III.2.** It uses up *I told him to burn it* a leaf early. The arc *the grey I will burn, and the sea too* (III.1) → *I told him to burn it* (III.2) → *I did not burn it* (VI.1) is a clean three, and this line turns it into four.
- **It invents a quarrel.** It sets two True Men against each other at the hearth, which nothing in the elements does.
- **It is a burning attitude toward the grey's wood, in a man's mouth.** It sits right where Jack tore the burning lore out. A reader who missed the cut of Tobe will hear *what the grey sends, burn it* again. That is "the kind of stuff that muddies the story."

Harl's entry ends better on his own temper: *…and the sea too, if it would take fire.*

### 4 · The banked hearth has been squeezed into a custom (III.2, line 177)

The draft line is *as women bank the hearth ere they leave their mothers' house*. With *the last night* and *as every night* gone, the image no longer says faithful care for a house you will never see again. It now sounds like a Shore rite, something women do on leaving home or at marriage. That is invented lore, made by cutting. Rewrite:

> In every haven the masters leaned its staff against the falling wall, as women on their last night in their mothers' house yet bank the fire, as every night. A staff leaveth not its work.

### 5 · The stone in III.2 does not add up, so the doom line wobbles

The draft passes the stone like this:
- *Kael put the stone into Corlen's hands* … *Corlen gave back the stone. It was the last time he held it.*
- Nobody else holds it until *Kael gave me the stone* and *Kael took it back*.

So why does Corlen alone get it? A careful reader trips here, and the doom line (*the last time he held it*) loses force because the rule behind it is unclear. Let it travel:
- open the falls with *Then the stone went down the bench from hand to hand, Corlen first.*;
- at Eldhythe: *Corlen gave the stone on to Tamm. It was the last time he held it.*;
- at Glasspire: *Then Pellow sat down with his glass in his lap, and held it not up. The stone he gave to me.*;
- *Kael took it back, and told the Road.* stays as it is.

III.1, a roll, keeps the stone in Kael's hands all night. The two nights then differ for a reason.

### 6 · The III.1 headnote is minutes, not a person (line 9)

There are three faults:
- *He bade each man stand to answer* is procedure.
- *the rolls … knew what no True Man could, I read it out* has a loose *it*.
- *reading out of my chest* reads, in English, as reading from her own body.

Rewrite (45 words):

> *Laid by the True Men, each for his own haven, as Kael the Counter called them up, and every man stood to answer as he had stood under the banner. {{Halyna}} had found the rolls in the chest I carried; what was in them and in no man's memory, I read out.*

*in no man's memory* is the memory theme, said lightly and in passing. The lock holds: {{Halyna}} discover, and Seren reads.

## The rest, numbered

1. **Corlen's seed is not whole** (line 31). His I.1 habit is *will not fire until the sea has finished*, and it is what kills him (D6: he held his guns quiet). *as to his guns* only implies it. Write: *To them he said what he saith to his gunners when they would fire: "Wait. The sea has not finished."* That also removes the stacked *doth ere* (two old words in one clause, §1.5).
2. **Corlen names himself *the eldest*** (line 29). His voice keeps himself out of the subject. Move it into Seren's stage line: *Then Corlen sat, that is the eldest of them, and wet a finger…*
3. **Garvel's 25-word sentence is the one line that edges toward parody** (line 47): *Every gun on this wall have I listened to ere it spake, as a mother to a cough, for guns that ring true speak true.* The preposition dangles after the inversion, the simile seems to make the mother listen "ere it spake", and a *for* explains it. Split it: *Every gun on this wall I listen to, as a mother to a cough. Guns that ring true speak true.* Keep *ring true*, because VI.1's *It rang true* pays it.
4. ***ere* comes four times in about 400 words of III.1's catalogue** (Corlen, Aske, Garvel, Lanner), three of them as *ere it/he* in neighbouring entries. That is costume (never-list 8). Keep two.
5. **The fronted run.** Even after item 1 of the worst problems, no more than one city line in three should open on a fronted place (*On… it stood*). Let some open on the man's memory (*Fifty years the evening tide…*, *My father ground it*).
6. **III.2 Ulden needs the plant VI.1 pays** (*lay with mine ear to the ground, as every night of the war*). III.2 gives only the Sandreach floor. Write *Every night since, I have slept on the open ground, with mine ear to it.* (line 145).
7. ***Now each man for his own city, in the order of the falling.*** (line 107) is a programme note. Cut it. The stone line in worst problem 5 does the work.
8. ***a month ere the banner*** (line 183) is an annal stamp. Cut it. *When Glasspire was gone* already dates it.
9. **The cloaks.** Make the night of the pass colder and give the kindness its own sentence: *On the night of the pass, the coldest of the road, many of the children died. Some of the old died with them. Their cloaks they had given the children.*
10. ***The wind cometh before the sails do.*** (line 161) mixes *cometh* and a modern pro-verb *do*. Write *Before the sails cometh the wind.*
11. ***night-watchmen steer*** (line 155) should be *night-watch*, to match VI.1's *the night-watch steered*.
12. ***A town is only the last course on the old*** (line 127) should read *but*. VI.1 has *but*, and *but* meaning *only* is a stock word (§2.1).
13. ***Till the Fall*** (line 55, Ulden) puts a period name of the annals in a man's mouth. Write *Till the havens fell*.
14. ***roofed so close in the trees*** (line 43) should read *among the trees*.
15. **Refrains repeated word for word.** *Give me the stone. No, I will stand.* is said bare at the head of both leaves, which sit a page apart in the Book. Vary it each time, as in worst problems 1 and 2 (craft rule 22).
16. **The True Men's sayings that use *has*** (*The sea has not finished*, *Someone has to keep a light*) break §1.2 unless they are fixed matter. The child in VI.1 repeats Hesk's line word for word. The orchestrator should add the True Men's sayings to §1.7, rather than have each writer choose.
17. **The staff law's wording.** I.4 (g03, line 40) reads *A staff is cut for its work, and leaveth it not. When the work is done, it is laid down in it.* The Epilogue (g11, line 132) pays the second half. III.2 should echo only the first half, as it does, and II.1 should use the same words. Do not let III.2 reach for *when the work is done*, because that is the Epilogue's.
18. **The Ebba line after the Amen** (line 83). V.5 (g08, line 232) carries the identical frame line, *Old Ebba began it, a breath before the rest.*, and its writer calls it "the one frame line after the Amen". Only one leaf may have it (voice §6). III.1 has the better claim, because it pays Marl's *She is there by the fire, nodding* in the same leaf. Let V.5 give its copy up, or let III.1 vary it (*Old Ebba of Fenholm began it. Marl said it after her.*). The orchestrator should choose.
19. **Pellow's *his supper among the rest*** now appears three times: III.1, V.5 (g08, line 192) and V.7 (g09, line 86). Plant it here, pay it once at his death (V.5's *and ye laughed at him, and so did I* is the right pay), and cut V.7's copy.
20. **The Argument is good:** one line, it spoils nothing, and *tell* carries its double sense. Keep it. If the orchestrator wants *walked away from it* less modern, *walked from it* does the same work.
21. **The epithet changed to *meeteth*** is right for the grammar. Check that no other leaf prints *where the river meets the tide* (VI.1 uses the bare call, so it is clear).
22. **No Plain Words twins are in g05.** That is not this critic's brief, but the Plain writer will need the oath frame and the stone business above, not the draft's.
