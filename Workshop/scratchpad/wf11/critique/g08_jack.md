# g08 · Jack's eye · V.2 The Night of the Naming · V.5 The Tides, as the Wall Counted Them

*Read against voice_v3 §10, craft §8, the three model leaves and the I.1 Plain model. Both leaves are stone leaves, so the wood-leaf questions do not apply. Counts come from `count_v3.py`. Both rewrites below were built into scratch copies and counted: `critique/_g08j/V2_rw.md` and `critique/_g08j/V5_rw.md`.*

## The verdict, in short

**V.2.** The night itself is the best thing in Book Five so far:
- the two ends of the vault, *the length of the vault*;
- the flame standing up straight;
- the two faces changing in the same moment;
- *It said* Burn, and the silence after it;
- *The broken grain met.*

Jack would lean in at all of these. He would not lean in at the first 190 words, which are an archivist's inventory: a count, where the planks came from, what the soldiers called them, and the Second Tide's paired signs. That is the "history text book" he named. The tale's question (*no one went down to ask them more*) comes at about word 190. The one thing the night breaks, the hearth's laughter at the "wrong fortress", is planted in the same breath it is paid, so the turn lands as a recap, not a blow. The halt also hides its own mystery: the silent plank's raw edge is never seen until dawn.

**V.5.** Kael is himself within three sentences, and the strip refrain builds as craft wants: told whole, then a clause, then paid. These are as good as anything in the models:
- Wick at the front with his knees to his chin;
- *His were.*;
- *a gun that was not there*.

But the climax of a 1,400-word leaf, the roll in the ward, is its thinnest passage: 33 words of summary. Three of the six counts end on a gloss or a moral instead of a deed. The turn puts an *-eth* on the hinge, and Halyna's line breaks Kael's own play on *called* and *sent*. Wick's one want is planted and never paid. The Ebba line after the Amen is stacking, and III.1 already uses it word for word.

Neither leaf is "blah" at the core. Both have joints that make it read clinical. Every fix below is a joint, not a beat. Jack's elements all stay.

**The custom (the brief's "V.2 leaned on the custom: mend it").** The writer's mend holds. There is no "old way", no saying, and no wood "saved from the custom". *Burn* is the Emberhythe hull's order and nothing more, and it sits clean beside V.6's *in your havens the word was* Burn and VI.1's *I did not burn it*. The mend did leave one scar, a garbled sentence (*The uncarved wood the soldiers' fires had, and the tide.*), fixed in §7 below. V.5 carries no custom.

## The worst problems, with rewrites

### 1 · V.2 opens as an inventory, and the laugh it breaks is planted at the moment it is paid

**The fault.** The first four paragraphs are register entries:
- *lay fifty-three planks of the grey sails by the end of the Hasty Tide*;
- who carried them;
- what was burned;
- *The soldiers called them the tablets*;
- what each gave;
- when the silent one came;
- the Second Tide's signs.

There is no hour, no one doing anything, and no stake. craft §2.1 wants the stakes shown "before [they are] in danger". In this tale the thing in danger is the hearth's comfortable belief that the grey sails are fools. The draft first mentions it after *Burn*, at line 58, and pays it in the next two lines (*They had not picked anything. / They had been sent.*). Planted and paid in one breath is a gloss, not a turn.

**The fix.**
- Open on the laugh, which gives an hour, a place, a sound and the seed of something wrong.
- Then go down the stair with them, and let the inventory come up as the lamp passes over the rows.
- End the inventory on a line that makes the night the question: *Till that night no one had gone down…*

Replace lines 9–21 (through *the length of the vault.*):

```text
Hold the stone, Seren.

All that first winter this fire laughed at the grey sails. "They have picked the wrong fortress," said Kael, and we laughed, and I with them.

One night of the second summer {{Halyna}} went down to the oldest room under this hall, and I came behind with the ink and the lamps. As cold it is in summer as in winter. The rows lay grey on grey, like a field the plough hath turned up stony.

Fifty-three planks lay there, and every one carved. The runners had carried them home two to a plank, from wherever the sea gave them up, the salt running down their arms. The soldiers' fires had the rest, and the tide. The wall called them the tablets. They were but planks.

Each but one had given {{Halyna}} one word, and each word I wrote on a leaf of its own. The one had given naught, though it was carved as finely as the rest. The sea brought it in weeks after the first.

The planks of the Second Tide bare two signs apiece, cut close, as names are cut close on an old lintel. On the shingle {{Halyna}} learned to hold two at once, and there were gaps in every telling. I have not mended them with guesses. Till that night no one had gone down to ask the fifty-three more.

The night was short and the rows were long, so they began at the two ends, Rhyna at the stair and Halvard at the far wall. Never ere then had I seen them work apart. They were not far apart: the length of the vault.
```

What the rewrite changes:
- **The logic slip is fixed.** The draft has *One of them was silent* and then *Each had given {{Halyna}} one sign and one word*. The rewrite says *Each but one*.
- ***The wall called them the tablets*** echoes V.5's *Treelings the wall called them*, and it stops three *soldiers* from falling in four lines.
- **The custom mend is now one plain clause.** *The soldiers' fires had the rest, and the tide.* leans on V.1's reason (hatred, and wood dear on the ridge) and adds nothing.
- **One element is folded.** *where the Rite was found* is gone. If I.4's link is wanted, restore it as six words after *the oldest room under this hall*.

At the turn, cut the recap (line 58, *All that first winter… and I with them.*). Let the pair follow *said it till it drowned.* directly:

```text
That was all the young wood knew: one word to a hull, and in it one order. Each hull said its word at our wall, and said it again, and said it till it drowned.

They had not picked anything.

They had been sent.
```

The laugh is now 850 words behind the reader, so the pair hits.

### 2 · V.2's halt hides its own mystery, and its longest sentence sits on the lift

**The faults.**
- **The edge.** Halvard takes up *the silent one*, and nothing more is said of it. At dawn Seren's eyes have *gone back to their two raw edges*, but the reader was never shown the second raw edge. The payoff (*The broken grain met.*) is planted only in V.1, for the first plank. The silent plank's half of the plant is missing.
- **The sound.** The silent plank *speaks*, and says Rhyna's words, for the first time in the Book. The draft lets that pass unmarked inside *Halvard said it with her*.
- **The order.** The first order is withheld from the scene (*It was a whole order*) and filed in the list later. The reader is told a whole order was spoken, and does not hear it.
- **The lift.** The 29-word lift (*Rhyna lifted her plank, and at the far wall Halvard lifted his, as the far corner of a net riseth with the near, for it is all one net.*) is the longest sentence in the leaf, and it sits at the halt. Voice §3.1 says the crisis stretch is told shortest.

Replace lines 23–31 (through *These are the orders as I wrote them:*):

```text
Rhyna took up the first plank of all, the wave that had said *shore*, with its one long edge split raw. Halvard took up the silent one, and its long edge was raw also.

I stood between them with the lamp. No wind cometh so far below, and the flame stood up straight in my hand.

Rhyna lifted her plank. At the far wall Halvard lifted his. So rise the far knots of a net with the near, for it is all one net.

Their two faces changed in the same moment.

Then Rhyna spake, and it was not a word. It was a whole order.

*To shore. As root to water, go. Wait on no bough.*

From the far end, with the silent plank in his hands, Halvard said it with her, word for word.

After that every plank spake. In the order the sea gave them up we had laid them, and that night each gave up its own. All night {{Halyna}} went along the rows toward each other, and I went between and wrote. By the middle rows my hand ached to the wrist, and I stopped not. Here are the rest, as I wrote them:
```

What the rewrite changes:
- **The net is set in the plural and inverted.** *So rise the far knots* drops *riseth*, which was the second *-eth* in two sentences. *Knots* chimes with Tarnel's *A net is only knots holding hands* (II.3) without citing it.
- ***with the silent plank in his hands*** is a fact, not a gloss. The reader sees the mystery, and dawn explains it without a word.
- **The first order moves into the scene,** so the list begins at *Break wall*, and its gloss line (*That was the wave…*) goes.
- ***After that every plank spake*** replaces *After that every plank gave up its whole order*. The draft said *gave up its… order* twice in two sentences. Now the play on *order* and *gave up* lands once, clean.

### 3 · V.5's halt is its thinnest passage

**The fault.** craft §2.3 sets V.5's halt in Count VI: the roll called, fourteen names and twelve to answer, then the belt. In the draft the roll is told in summary:

> *Tonight in the ward I called the roll of the True Men, fourteen names, and twelve answered. At two the ward was quiet. Hesk answered from the north wall, where his lamp was lit.*

That is 33 words for the scene the whole leaf climbs to. Craft §2.1: "The halt is the longest stretch of the tale." *At two the ward was quiet* is a count of a silence, not the silence. It is also the leaf's one great chance to mirror I.1's roll, where Kael counted twelve and *it took not long*.

**The fix.** Tell the roll. Name the two silences and the lamp, in roll order, and keep the outline's own line, *fourteen names, and twelve to answer them*. That line is better than *and twelve answered*, because it holds the dead inside the count.

Replace the two sentences after *A count is no count till it be named.*:

```text
A count is no count till it be named. Tonight in the ward I called the roll of the True Men: fourteen names, and twelve to answer them.

At Corlen of the harbour the ward was quiet. I let it be quiet the space of a breath, and called the next. At Hesk of the mere the answer came down from the north wall, where his lamp was lit. At Pellow of the spire the ward was quiet.
```

What the rewrite does:
- **It is the law of three, broken in the middle:** silence, then an answer from far off, then silence.
- ***The space of a breath*** is the hearth's wait for Ebba (I.5), made by Kael's own hand. Memory theme 3 is felt here, and nothing is said about it.
- **The cost is 32 words,** paid for by the cuts in §4, §5 and the list below.

Also fix the verbless line that follows the dead:
- *And the rest of the east cairns, every name, twice, at dusk among the stones.* switches from tonight's roll to the dusk without a verb.
- Write instead: *The rest of the east cairns I named at dusk among the stones, every name, twice.*

### 4 · Three counts end on a gloss or a moral

Craft rule 14: end on a picture, never on a moral. Each count is a small tale, and three of them explain their own last line.

| Count | The draft ends | Cut | It now ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| II | *"Change your shot." / It was his first call for the sky.* | the gloss (8 words). *Looked up, and not out to sea* already says it. | *"Change your shot."* |

### 5 · Wick's one want is planted and never paid

**The fault.** Count III plants Wick's want (*Naught he wanted, save to run a word to the Captain*), and I.3 plants it again (*Naught would he sooner do than carry a word to the Captain*). The cruelty of his death is that his word does reach the Captain, and someone else runs it. The draft says *three words* twice in four lines (*Three words Wick gave him.* · *Hale saith Wick's word was three words long, and it was enough.*), and never says that the want came true. g02's notes ask V.5 to pay exactly this.

Replace from *I did not see it.* through *So a boy died…*:

```text
I did not see it. Hale did. Wick gave him three words.

"They are looking."

His word came to the Captain, as he had wanted all the war.

Hale ran it.

So a boy died of a gun that was not there.
```

What the rewrite changes:
- ***Hale ran it.*** now stands alone as the cost.
- **The hinge is plain.** The inversion *Three words Wick gave him* becomes *Wick gave him three words*.
- **The length nets to zero.**

### 6 · V.5's turn puts an *-eth* on the hinge, and breaks its own play

**The faults.**
- **The hinge.** *The wood knoweth fear.* is "the first instant of a knowing" (voice §0 item 4, §1.6), and the hinge rule bars any *-eth* in a strike. Every other line of the turn is already plain: *Now I know. It cannot be called home. But it can be sent home.*
- **The play.** {{Halyna}} say *"The fleet cannot be called back"*, but Kael's turn and V.6 (*No voice among us can call us home*) both play on *called home* against *sent home*. With *back*, the echo misses.
- **The tag.** *a half each* is a tag. The two-ink layout already shows the halves.

The fix:

```text
In the Joined Tide {{Halyna}} had told me:

"The fleet cannot be called home,"

"for the wood obeyeth the wood."

I answered that we would send it home, and wist not what I meant. Now I know. It cannot be called home. But it can be sent home.

The wood can fear.
```

What the rewrite does:
- ***Can*** carries no ending.
- **The turn becomes a triple of *can*:** *cannot be called*, *can be sent*, *can fear*.
- **The line has its own paragraph,** as the turn deserves.
- **It pays Count II.** The sail went *grey-green to white, like a man's face*, and *It had not seen. It had felt.* The wood's fear was shown there, four counts before Kael says it. This is the leaf's best plant, and now the turn picks it up.
- **It sits against IV.4.** *Fear leaveth no mark on wood* stands there, and the reader can set the two side by side.

If the orchestrator wants craft's wording back, the HEAVY form must still be recast. *The wood knows fear* would mix *-s* and *-eth* in one mouth (§1.2).

### 8 · Stacked after the Amen

V.5 closes:

> *And the hearth said: We remember.*
>
> *Old Ebba began it, a breath before the rest.*

Three reasons to cut the Ebba line:
- **Voice §6 does not allow it.** It allows only IV.3's slow hearth, the hood, the waits for Ebba and VI.3's child, and says "Nothing else is stacked after the Amen."
- **It is a refrain that never changes** (craft rule 22). III.1 (g05, l.83) has the same line word for word, and the writer of III.1 flagged it there too.
- **It moves the last picture.** The leaf's last image should be the Captain at the end of the bench, with the cloak short of his belt. The Ebba line moves the eye off him.

Cut it here. If the orchestrator wants Ebba to begin one Amen outside IV.3, it should be III.1's, and only one.

## The rest

**V.2**
1. ***cometh so far below*, twice** (l.23 and l.64). Cut the second: *At full light I took up the first plank and the silent one.*
2. ***A mason of Emberhythe, the forge on the burning shore*.** The epithet glosses Emberhythe and tips *Burn* a beat early. III.1 and III.2 already carry the burning shore, and so does Harl's *I told him to burn it*. Write: *A mason had carried it up the mountain out of Emberhythe, charred black at one end.*
3. **The close repeats the turn.** *We had called the grey sails fools.* comes right after *They had been sent* and the laugh, so it is redundant. Cut it. Also write *At last I said this, and {{Halyna}} let it stand:* in place of *what I have written here*. The quoted speech then opens *"I think they were the youngest of the wood…"* and closes on *It is still growing.*
4. **The soldiers' names for the orders.** The Knowings (g11, l.144: *The seven of the young wood bear the soldiers' names, worn smooth with handling*, and its ask 8) depends on them, and v1.3 had them. The draft dropped them. Restore them in one line after the last gloss: *The soldiers took the head of each for its name:* To the shore. Break the wall. Wait in the grey. If they are not restored, g11 must drop its clause.
5. **The glosses run in one shape.** Six run *That was… / The… / Those… / Those… / Those…*. That is a register, and Jack hears registers. Optionally, break the last one, as the law of three wants: *Those scattered at the first loss. We had called that winning.* It is one shame line, and it carries the reader toward *They had been sent* without explaining it.
6. **Seren's turns.** These stay, and they are the measure for the rest of Book Five:
   - *a leaf of its own*;
   - *work apart / not far apart: the length of the vault*;
   - the play on *order* and *gave up*;
   - *I have not mended them with guesses*;
   - the withheld *I have not asked {{Halyna}} what it meaneth*.
7. **The memory theme is right and light.** It lives in the mason who *could not bear to bring naught of his city away*, and in the headnote's hand on the planks. Add nothing.
8. ***-eth* forms.** The draft has five. After the rewrites it has three (*hath*, *cometh*, *meaneth*). There is no *do*-negation and no contraction.

**V.5**

9. **The headnote.** *This time* has no anchor, and *as I mended the first* reads as "the first count". Write (46 words):

   ```text
   *Laid by Kael the Counter, a count at each Tide's end, for he would tell no Tide till it was over. In the sixth winter he brought me all six, and bade me mend them as I had mended his first. I have mended them less.*
   ```

10. **Corlen's mark is spelled two ways in the Book.** g08 and g11 (the Epilogue, l.114) write *The sea hath not finished*. g05 (III.1) keeps *has*, as "outside the dial". §1.7 does not list it, and §1.2 forbids mixing *-s* and *-eth* in one mouth. Rule *hath* Book-wide, and III.1 changes to match.
11. ***That is a True Man.*** reads oddly, as if the deed itself were a man. Use Kael's own words from I.1: *That is the whole of a True Man. It is also what killed him.* (+3 words). It pays I.1's creed at the death it cost.
12. ***Ruan of the spire hath his glass.*** Cut it (−7 words). V.5 comes before V.7 in Book order, and V.7 Part II closes on the glass changing hands (*So it went to Ruan… "Look close," he said.*). This line spends that payoff two leaves early. *Pellow looked, and he is under them too.* is the stronger end to the paragraph anyway.
13. **The last tag in the roll of the dead.** This is optional. *Pellow, that held his glass up to his supper* would replace *that ground our glasses*. Five tags would then end on the habit the hall laughed at (Count V), which is the good of the dead remembered, not his function (memory theme 4). *Our far-glasses he ground* stays in Count V.
14. **Similes.** Kael has three: *spilled kindling*, *like a man's face* and the loaf. Voice §4 gives him one picture of Seren's a tale. All three are R6b must-survives, and the face now plants *The wood can fear*, so keep them. Add none.
15. ***Tonight it cannot reach his belt.*** Accept it. g11 already matches it (l.284). *Tonight it is short of his belt* is the alternative, if *cannot* reads as the cloak trying.
16. **The thirds are flat** (9.8 / 10.1 / 9.9). That is Kael's evenness, and it is acceptable. But Count VI should feel the shortest. After §3, keep its new sentences short, and do not add any.
17. **Length.** The scratch rewrite lands at **1,372** (2% under the 1,400 ceiling). V.5 carries more elements than any leaf in the Book, so it may sit there for cause (§7). If the orchestrator holds it to 1,330, make these cuts next, weakest first:

    - *Now hear the dead of the wall, and hold them, for they are ours.* → *Now hear the dead of the wall, and hold them.* (−5).
18. **The Plain twins** (`drafts/_g08`).
    - `V5_plain.md` fails §11, and it is not a case for a ruling on the band:
      - 92.8% of the Book;
      - 25.5% overlap;

## What is right, so the reviser keeps it

**V.2**
- the flame standing straight;
- *Their two faces changed in the same moment.*, alone on its line;
- *It said* Burn., alone;
- *No one spake, and for a long while I wrote not.*;
- *said it till it drowned*;
- the pair *They had not picked anything. / They had been sent.*;

**V.5**
- *I count. That is what I am for.*;
- *God forgive us, we laughed.*;
- *It was the first strip.* with Seren's *It was not the last.*;
- *It had not seen. It had felt.*;
- the seed planted *to wherever our guns were quiet*, then *His were.*;
- Wick: *Ye knew Wick…*, his knees to his chin, the Title twice;
- *So a boy died of a gun that was not there.*;
- *never once our Captain*, and *I liked it less*;
- *a dead mind's hour*;
- *Six winters have I told the grey sails… But I will tell ours.*, the *tell* play at its best;
- the cloak at his knees, then short of his belt, then the bench.

**The grammar.** Both leaves are correct:
- no ending on the wrong person;
- no *ye* as an object;
- one *do*-negation, which is the sourcing line;
- no one-off antiquities and no tushery;
- pair-names with plural verbs;
- no singular *they*;
- no Christian mark;
- the warden is not named.

**Every lock in §10 item 10 holds.**
