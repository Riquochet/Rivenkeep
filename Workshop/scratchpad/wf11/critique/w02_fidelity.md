## A · Fixes to make in w02

**1. VI.2, Neivaere (l.275): restore the second half of the gloss, *the mirror*.**
- *What:* `We are **Neivaere**, the silvered.` → `We are **Neivaere**, the silvered, the mirror.`
- *Why:* this is v2.0's own-name gloss, and it is the only one of the ten that was cut, although the writer's notes say every gloss was kept. With Seren's lines gone, the glosses are now the only bridge from the wood's names to the havens. The cut name also carries weight elsewhere. VI.1 (`g09.md`) says the Silvered *broke like a mirror*, V.7 is *The Mirror in the Grain*, and this Throne's judgement is *Show them themselves.* Cost: +2 words.

**2. V.3, ring 2 (l.42): give back the fire's purpose, in V.5's exact words.**
- *What:* `One fire we threw at the stone that had spoken.` → `One fire we threw at the stone that had spoken, to see whether it would answer.`
- *Why:* R6b V.3 (e) makes Wick's death *the other side of the same shot*, left unsaid. V.5 Count III (`_g08/V5_book.md`) tells that shot as *It fired there, to see whether it would answer.* v2.0 used the same clause in V.3. The puzzle works when a reader hears one phrase from both sides of the wall. As drafted, only V.5 has it. The next sentence, *The stone answered not.*, then pays the clause directly. Cost: +6 words.

**3. V.3, the reading (l.9–16): cut it below a tenth of the tale.**
- *What:* the reading is 63 words in a tale proper of 583, which is 10.8%. Craft §5.1, which is binding, says *less than a tenth of the tale*. For comparison: V.4 is 8.7%, V.6 7.5%, VI.2 7.4%, and the IV.4 model 8.4%. Cut at least 6 words. One way that keeps the corrected image, the caught slip and the broken carving:
  - l.9 `*— Salt. Salt in every board. Cold, a long while.*` → `*— Salt in every board. Cold.*` (−4)
  - l.12 drop the opening `Turning.` (−1)
  - l.14 `*— you trust. Alone now, and slow.*` → `*— you trust. Alone, and slow.*` (−1)
- That gives 57 words of 577, or 9.9%.

**4. V.6, ring 2 (l.184): restore *after the first*.**
- *What:* `Each growing went out as tides go,` → `Each growing after the first went out as tides go,`
- *Why:* as drafted, the timing law takes in the green wood too. That contradicts the same ring's *At the edge of your sea it lay, while your children had children*, and V.4's growing held back until the last old voice went down (reckoning Fix E). v2.0 had *after the first* for exactly this reason. The draft also moved the tides passage in front of *The crudest came first*, so a reader now meets the law before the exception. Cost: +2 words.

**5. V.6, the reading (l.151–152): stop at the halt, and do not give away the turn.**
- *What:* `*— Sent. Sent, and none left to —*` / `*— call. None. Only the singing, and in it — [ ]*` says the turn, *No voice among us can call us home*, before the story reaches it. Voice §4 bars that in Halyna's reading (*Never: … the turn given away*). Craft §5.1 says the reading breaks off *at or near the tale's halt*, and the writer's own halt is *Your boats passed it in the breath, and saw naught.* Two possible lines, two words shorter, with one thought still broken across two inks:
  - `*— Boats, going over. Not seeing —*`
  - `*— naught. Only the singing, and in it — [ ]*`

**6. VI.2, ring 2 (l.243): restore the Thrones' command of the last hulls.**
- *What:* `On the sea they set us walking.` → `On the sea they set us walking, and the last of the growing in our keeping.`
- *Why:* v2.0's *with all the hulls of the growing in our keeping* is the only line that says the Thrones commanded the fleet. The headnote's *commanders of the armada* was cut too, as R6b (g) allows. Without one of them, nothing in the Book says why a fallen Throne frees its haven. *the last of* fits *The shore was not yet silent… they could not wait*. Cost: +9 words. VI.2 would then be at about 925 of 1,000, with fix 1 included.

**7. V.4, ring 1 (l.81): restore v2.0's change of tense.**
- *What:* `The silver elders keep all that is said beneath them, as they keep all they hear.` → `The silver elders kept all that was said beneath them, as they keep all they hear.`
- *Why:* in the present tense on both sides the line says the same thing twice. v2.0's past-then-present is the plant: they kept what was said at the vote, and they keep everything. The draft's own ring-3 payoff (*The silver elders keep them, as they keep all they hear, and so do we*) depends on that plant being heard as a fact about the council night.

**8. VI.2, Naelthar (l.273): make clear what is *lighter than iron*.**
- *What:* `Our kin were the cranes that lifted the floating stones. Lighter than iron they were, and they raised your towers into our own sky.` → `Our kin, lighter than iron, were the cranes that lifted the floating stones, and they raised your towers into our own sky.`
- *Why:* as drafted, *they* attaches most easily to the nearest plural, *the floating stones*. v2.0 and IV.1 (`g06.md`: *Harder than oak was their timber, and lighter than iron*) both say it of the black wood. The recast is about the same length.

## B · Fixes the orchestrator must route to other leaves (w02 can stay as written)

**9. VI.1 must use the same English Throne names as VI.2's glosses.**
- With Seren's line before each Throne cut (craft §2.3), VI.1's English Throne-names are the only thing that ties each own name in VI.2 to its haven. Two do not match in `g09.md`:
  - Fenholm: VI.1 *The Grain That Rots*, VI.2 *the rotting grain*.
  - Tidesmeet: VI.1 *The Bearer of Branches*, VI.2 *bearer of new branches* (v2.0's gloss).
- *Recommend* that VI.2 stay as written and VI.1 change to **The Rotting Grain** and **The Bearer of New Branches**. *The Grain That Rots* is also a bare *-s* form in a HEAVY leaf (voice §1.2). The rest match: Tide-Heart, Root-Heart, Stone-Grain, the Burning, the Deep-Beneath, the Long Winter, the Blood of the Sky, the Silvered.

**10. The soldiers' names for the Thrones are now in no leaf of the Book.**
- *The gap:* craft §2.3 cuts Leviathan, Hydra, Plague Herald, Ancient Treant, Colossus, Magma Titan, Sandworm, Frost Wyrm, Storm King and Crystal Lich from VI.2 on the premise that *the facing leaf holds them*. VI.1 (`g09.md`) does not, and no draft in `wf11/drafts` does. VI.2's *Our own names we had.* then has nothing to stand against, and the line is the point of the leaf: the mourner is the thing the soldiers named for a beast.
- *Recommend:* VI.1 gives each Throne its soldiers' name in one clause, at about 3 to 4 words each and about 35 in all. For example: *The Tide-Heart, that the soldiers called the Leviathan, sank into the sea it came from…*
- *Or* the orchestrator rules them to NOTES only. `wf10/notes_v2.md` already carries them.

**11. The wall's grain-names (Ironbark, Willow, Heartoak, Twistbough, Yew, Ashwood, Silverbark) and *treelings* are now gone from the Book.**
- *Where they went:* w02 dropped them from the V.6 and V.4 headnotes, for the 50-word cap and the no-gloss rule (voice §6, craft §3.2). That is a sound reason, but the outline §8 V.6 headnote and outline §9.2 had routed them there.
- *What is left:* only *Blackthorn* survives (IV.2, `w01.md`). *root-men* survives in V.5 (`_g08/V5_book.md`). *treelings* survives only in V.5's Plain.
- `notes_v2.md` carries all of them. The orchestrator should confirm NOTES is their home, so that the fold is a decision and not an accident.

## C · Optional (fidelity gains, nothing broken)

**12. *the grain holds nothing*, used twice (V.3 l.42 and VI.2 l.275).** Outline §8 VI.2 asked for *the grain holds nothing of that night*, and §9.1 lists it among the lines the wood says well, because it rhymes with the ring's *and the grain holds it still*. The writer changed both to *we hold naught*, to keep a *holdeth* away from the fixed *holds*. *the grain hath naught* would keep the rhyme without that clash:
- V.3: *Of what our fire found there, the grain hath naught.*
- VI.2: *Of that night the grain hath naught.*

**13. VI.2's headnote dropped *and the one outside knew it as the one within*.** This is the pair's shared knowing, which V.3's headnote (*knew it as he knew it*) and V.6's (*it came to them both*) both keep. It would fit within the 50-word cap if *At each haven, when its Throne was fallen,* became *At each fallen Throne,*. Leave it if the clause makes the sentence awkward.

**14. V.6's tempers (l.178).** The plural *some* (*some come again heavier…*) thins the *-eth* forms, but it blurs the fact that each temper belongs to one grain: nine grains, nine tempers. *Nine grains were chosen. The first was too young…* still carries the count. No change needed unless the orchestrator wants the one-grain-to-one-temper mapping to be audible.
