## Fixes, most important first

1. **Knot 2, the reason the seal was broken, is now in no leaf.** *(Must. It is a cross-tale seed.)*
   - **Where:** IV.2's headnote (l.82), or the foreword (`g01.md` l.112).
   - **What:** v2.0's IV.2 headnote said that the heart's shape showed {{Halyna}} what the mute stones beside the sealed bundle were. Outline §8 IV.2 marks this **[new]** with →IV.3, and puzzle §2.3 knot 2 calls it "the hinge between IV.1 and IV.3". w01 cut it, because a headnote may not carry a seal. The foreword now has only *till {{Idrenna}} gave leave to break it*, which gives no cause, so the knot is lost.
   - **The mend I recommend is in the foreword, l.112:** *One came out of a dark stone that lay under a seal in the chest I carried, till the wood told {{Halyna}} what the mute stones beside it were, and {{Idrenna}} gave leave to break it.* That adds 11 words to g01. It pays when the reader reaches IV.2's *A plea, on the mute thing*.
   - **IV.2 cannot carry it.** I tried. A two-sentence headnote of 50 words that holds Rhyna on the shingle, Halvard in the vault and Rhyna among the children, *as a wound is, and not as a tale*, and the mute stones runs to 55–59 words. It has to drop an outline keep to fit.

2. **Seren's saying from IV.2's headnote is lost from the Book.** *(Must: place it, or cut it with the orchestrator's word.)*
   - **What:** *The rolls forget, and can be made to lie; and the wood cannot lie, but cannot see fear.* Outline §8 IV.2 says "(keep)". w01 §5 item 3 sends it to the foreword, but `g01.md` did not take it, and no draft holds it.
   - **Why it matters:** V.7 II (`g09.md` l.120) quotes *"The wood cannot lie," … for so the Guest's gift first taught us*, and then turns it. At present only II.2's *tongue of truth* and IV.4's *Slow is its giving, and true* plant that line, and both do it only by implication.
   - **Mend:** put it in the foreword, beside the wood's sources (`g01.md` l.112), in HEAVY with no *-eth*: *The rolls forget, and can be made to lie. The wood cannot lie, but it cannot see fear.* The other home that fits is the close of IV.1, which is Seren's own tale from the rolls (`g06.md`).

3. **IV.6, l.281: a must-survive line was changed.** *(Should.)*
   - **What:** *Of the men in them we hold naught.* R6b (c) and outline §7.10 both quote *Of the men in them the grain holds nothing.*
   - **Why it matters:** the grain is the point of the line. The grain keeps every count and holds everything *still*, except these men. The line inverts the leaf's own ring. w01 made the change to avoid putting *holdeth* beside the fixed *holds*. Matching w02's V.3 is no reason to change it: that is a different wood speaking a different line.
   - **Mend:** *Of the men in them naught is held in the grain.* This has no *-eth*, it echoes *This is held in the grain.*, and it costs 2 words.
   - Set it as its own paragraph, as v2.0 did, after *both hulls broke and burned.* It is a strike line (craft §0: the great line alone on its paragraph).

4. **IV.6's headnote, l.238: the fixed line points at the wrong council.** *(Should.)*
   - **What:** *I, that heard Brenn's words, say of the council: They did not know it was ignorance.*
   - **Why it matters:** two leaves earlier, IV.3's annal says *The Council kept the letter, and forgat the Guest* (`g06.md` l.205). That is the Shore's Council. Read aloud, the lower-case *council* is heard the same way, so Jack's line seems to be said of the Shore's men. v2.0 avoided this with *the council that judged it*.
   - **Mend:** *say of the long-lived: They did not know it was ignorance.* The word count is the same, so the headnote stays at 50. *The long-lived* is the wood's own name for them in ring 3.

5. **IV.6, l.293: the peace dropped out with *holiest*.** *(Should.)*
   - **What:** v2.0 and the outline have *They saw their holiest peace answered with murder and flame.* w01 has *They saw the best they had answered…*
   - **Dropping *holiest* is right.** The arm-cut is the half of this rite, and craft §3.5 and §6 forbid calling it holy.
   - **But *the best they had* on its own** loses the fact that the gift was an offer of peace. A reader could also take it to mean Aelrhen himself.
   - **Mend:** *They saw their peace, the best they had, answered with murder and flame.* This adds 3 words and still answers IV.4's *we were his best*.

6. **IV.6's reading, l.246: the *we* in the grief line is unclear.** *(Should.)*
   - **What:** *— Grief. For this we have words.* This line carries the fold of the outline's keep, *grief is the one thing in the wood for which the shore has words enough*. That only works if *we* means {{Halyna}}, who speak for the Shore.
   - **Why it matters:** the line before it slides into the wood's *we* (*Blood in our — in its boards*), so the reader cannot tell which *we* this is.
   - **Mend:** *— Grief. For this we two have words.* This adds 1 word and uses the pair's own form (voice §1.1).

7. **IV.2's reading, l.88, half-mends a gap that should stay open.** *(Could.)*
   - **What:** *— One felt it. No — all. [⟦legend_iv2_gap1⟧]* The note at l.95 says the mark is one {{Halyna}} *could see, and could not yet tell*, and its English is *What one pillar felt, all the ranks felt*. The line in front of the mark already tells it, which goes against voice §5: a gap is never mended.
   - **Mend:** say less before the mark, for example *— One, and — [⟦legend_iv2_gap1⟧]*. Keep the key and the note word for word.

8. **IV.2, l.141: the lock line now reads differently.** *(Confirm only. No change in w01.)*
   - **What:** *The iron will tire; and when it stoppeth, the wood will come again.* The lock in outline §7.13 and §8 is *The iron tires; and when it stops, the wood comes again.*
   - **It is consistent with its neighbours.** It matches VI.3 (`g10.md` l.54) word for word, and V.4 (`w02.md` l.91, *when the iron stoppeth*).
   - **The sense shifts** from a law of the wood to a promise. VI.3's sliver pays a promise well.
   - **Recommendation:** keep it, and have the orchestrator confirm it as the new lock for all three leaves. The gnomic HEAVY form, *tireth … stoppeth … cometh*, would break the cap of one *-eth* to a sentence.

## The locks and rules: all hold

- **No custom of burning, and no echo of it.** The wood knows only *Then they put fire to us*. *A net they mean to burn* is the fisher's way, and answers IV.3's *torn net into the bilge*. *Wood can hide naught.* faces *Leave naught to show.* with no remark.
- **Brenn never strikes.** IV.4 and IV.6 name *the blows* with no hands.
- **▒▒▒▒.** The wood never names him: *the host*, *the master of that house*.
- **Pairs.** {{Halyna}} take plural verbs. *We two* appears in II.2's and IV.4's readings. There is no singular *they*, and *the carver … he spake* is right.
- **What the wood knows.** No Shore name or motive is in a story. Shore facts sit only in the headnotes.
- **Fixed matter is verbatim:** *Stop… Sky… Dying…*; the Guest's sentence; *They did not know it was ignorance.*; *They saw only an act of war.*; *This is held in the grain.* / *and the grain holds it still* / *And no one answered.*
- **No holy word, no Christian mark, no contraction.**
- **Datelines agree with the reckoning:**
  - II.2: Cam 27, the fifth winter, Deceit;
  - IV.2: whole at Cam 16, the third winter;
  - IV.4: Cam 18, the third spring;
  - IV.6: Cam 20, the third summer.
- **Seeds and payoffs across tales:**
  - *We went not down*, paid by V.4's *save once*;
  - the untold-name chip, filled with *Naelear* in VI.3;
  - *If any green be left of us* and *thin green thing*, which lead to the sliver;
  - *whither they went we wist not*, answered by IV.1, IV.5 and VI.1;
  - IV.6's two hulls, struck low where they rode heaviest, matching IV.5's *There were two boats* and *low … where the load rode heaviest*;
  - *the wood knoweth the way home*, which becomes Tarnard's line in VI.3;
  - the keel and bones *in its ribs*, matching V.4's *burned to the keel, with the bones of Aelrhen in its ribs*.
