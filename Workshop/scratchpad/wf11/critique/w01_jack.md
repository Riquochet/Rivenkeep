## The verdict

**IV.4 is the calibration leaf, word for word, and it stays.** It is the only one of the four that builds the way Jack asked.

**The other three are v2.0 with the dial turned up.** Jack's verdict on v2.0's tales was that they "don't build", and older grammar will not change that verdict.

- **II.2** keeps 65% of v2.0's four-word runs, **IV.2** keeps 59% and **IV.6** keeps 71%. They follow v2.0 sentence by sentence and in its order.
- By length, II.2's story is 3% shorter than v2.0's, IV.2's is about the same, and IV.6's is 11% shorter.
- The IV.4 model also kept 59% of v2.0, so overlap alone is no fault. The difference is that IV.4 also cut 21% and rebuilt its halt. These three leaves rebuilt very little.
- R6 called v2.0's wood prose "already short-sentenced", so the run-ons were never their problem. **Their problem is the build, and the build is what the draft left alone.**

## The worst problems, with rewrites

### 1 · IV.6: the burning boat turns in four words

Book Four is built on this image:
- Brenn's *Well out, the boat turned. Into the wind it went, and against the current*;
- the ship-masters' *It turned into the wind, and after it, weather*;
- the children's rhyme.

In its own leaf it gets four words, and then a gloss that says the next line before the next line can say it.

**Draft (w01 lines 269–273):**
> With poles they shoved us off. The current took us out toward the open sea, away from home.
>
> Against the current we turned, burning. No water could hold a keel that knew its way, and we sailed ourselves home.
>
> Even charred, even dying, the wood knoweth the way home.

What is wrong:
- **The halt is missing.** Craft §2.3 names this beat the halt, and §2.1 says the halt is the longest stretch of the tale, told close, in its shortest sentences. Here it is shorter than the council's list of the deed.
- **A lock is dropped.** Ruling 3 fixes *into the wind … and against the current*. IV.3 (`g06.md`) and IV.5 (`g07.md`) both keep the wind. IV.6 alone loses it.
- ***No water could hold a keel that knew its way*** and ***the wood knoweth the way home*** say the same thing twice.
- **The turn has no plant**, so it comes from nowhere.

**Rewrite.** One clause goes into ring 1, and the turn is rebuilt in ring 2:
> He went up from us to the door. All that morning we lay in the shallows, and waited to bear him home with the rite made. The tide came in under us, and turned, and he came not.

> With poles they shoved us off, and the current had us. Out it drew us, toward the open sea, away from home. The fire was in every board. He lay in us, and was cold.
>
> Then we turned.
>
> Into the wind we went, and against the current, burning.
>
> Even charred, even dying, the wood knoweth the way home.

What each change does:
- *The tide … turned* plants the turn, and the ebb is the current that takes them out.
- *and he came not* is a negation without *do*, and it is the "but" before *By the heels they drew him down to us*.
- *He lay in us, and was cold* is the one thing that sounds at the halt: a cold body in a burning hull. The wood knows cold. It pays the reading's *Heavy — / — and warm.*
- *Then we turned.* is the strike: three shared words, alone on the line.
- The next line is IV.3's sentence felt from inside the boat. Nothing points at the match. The gap between the two leaves belongs to the reader.

### 2 · II.2: the middle ring is a textbook, and the fault is confessed but never seen

Ring 2 runs 277 words. Ring 1, which holds the halt, runs 201, and ring 3, which holds the turn, runs 178. So the lecture is the longest stretch of the tale, which turns craft §2.1's proportion upside down.

What is in ring 2:
- **The lifespan as its own paragraph:** *We live twice as long as ye, and half again.* That gives a fact the weight §3.4 keeps for a great line.
- **The kinds of wood, as a list:** *Nor was the wood one wood. The twisted boughs we bent in our rites.*
- **The Ael'thar / Aelthar linguistics:** *Ael'thar we say in thunder, and carve it soft, Aelthar.* puzzle.md #14 decided this line: "keep 'the thunder is for the moment, the softness for always'; cut the rest". R6a (g) flagged it too. The cut was not made.

This is the history textbook Jack named, in its anthropology edition.

Ring 3 then confesses the fault in summary: *We saw your boats come a little way into the breath, and turn back with white faces.* That plural is the one moment that could show a people choosing to hide, and it passes in a clause.

**Rewrite ring 2.** Every element R6a (c) lists is kept, and the lecture goes. The pillars move to ring 3, where they stand at the edge the people watch from:
> All our long summers we led the wood. We pruned it, and led its roots to the water, and where we led it, there it grew. One life it was, in two kinds: we tended the wood, and the wood kept us, and kept its own will besides.
>
> Without the breath the wood is sick. Without the wood the breath cometh not again. Without either we fail, and slowly, for we live twice as long as ye, and half again: the eldest last, and the youngest first.
>
> Among the twisted boughs we made our rites. Under the silver elders sat the council, for none lie beneath a silver elder.
>
> Our speech is the storm in the high boughs, all cracks and breaks. What we carve in living wood is soft. The thunder is for the moment, the softness for always. What is carved is not heard. It is known: lay a hand on it, and ye know what the carver meant, whatever tongue he spake. So could no word be twisted.
>
> We wist not that there was any other way to be believed.

**Rewrite the opening of ring 3.** The pillars open it. Then the turn becomes a moment, built on Seren's look by three, whose third look is the one that matters:
> At the very edge, where the breath came down to your sea, stood our pillars in their long ranks. Between them they held the breath, as a mast holdeth a sail. We call them the Naelsaen, the mist-hearts: the anchors of our sky, and the only trees of ours ye ever found.
>
> From among them, all the summers of your havens, we watched your shore. We saw your havens raised. We saw your lamps lit at dusk. We saw your boats come a little way in, and turn back.
>
> Once a boat came farther than any. So near it came that we heard the oars stop. A man stood up in the bow and looked full at us.
>
> We wove the breath thicker.
>
> He saw but grey. His face went white, and the oars went back the way they came.
>
> We wist not why.
>
> Here is our fault, and the grain will not leave it out. We went not down to your shore. We hid, and were glad. So long hid we, and so well, that never once were we seen.

What this gains:
- *We wove the breath thicker.* now happens in front of the hearer, so *Here is our fault* names what we have just watched. That is craft §2.1's turn, which "comes out of what the hearer has already seen".
- *White faces* survives as one face.
- *We wist not why.* now carries irony and no Shore motive. The reader knows what frightened him, and the long-lived do not.
- *The only trees of ours ye ever found* now stands one breath before the people hide among them. The hall knows what was found at the edge, and nobody says so.
- No lore is added. This tells one of v2.0's boats, close.
- Ring 2 drops to 183 words, and the weight goes to the halt (220) and the turn (261).

**Bring the halt (ring 1) one step closer**, so the children are heard before the doom falls on them:
> At evening the light went low among the trunks. Then we sang to the pale saplings, and they answered, one stand and then the next, till the deep wood was one choir. Our little ones lay down among the roots to hear it. One by one their small voices left the song, and they slept in the singing.

*One by one* is how they will wither, and nobody says so.

**The question.** The draft has none to carry. *What was the grey?* is answered in the story's fourth sentence, *It was the air we breathed.* IV.4's model gives the leaf a listener's question, and II.2 can do the same in its headnote (50 words):
> *A child asked if the Guest's people had houses. Many nights {{Halyna}} held his gift, one at the hearth while the other slept, and ever the sleeper woke knowing. Into it his people had put the whole of home; for a home that is air, our tongue hath no word.*

It is a stone-people's question. *This is our home:* answers it at the close, and it faces II.1's Keep without a word of pointing.

### 3 · IV.2: the felling is one sentence, and the felled pillar has no life to lose

The sky's triple as a clock is the best structural idea in the file:
- *It was pale.* comes when the first pillar falls;
- *pale and hard* comes when the sons come;
- *pale and hard and wide* comes after *It came again.*

But the deed it measures goes by in one 24-word chain of *and*, at the very spot that should have the shortest sentences.

**Draft (lines 113–115):**
> The first of us they felled in a morning. To the last it held the breath, while the iron went in stroke on stroke, and every stroke we counted.
>
> Then the roots let go.

Memory theme 4 asks for one habit of the dead, and *the first of us* gives nothing to remember it by. *To the last it held the breath* is the right instinct, but it is abstract.

**Rewrite:**
> The first they came to stood nearest your shore. Of all the ranks it had marked the most of your hulls.
>
> At first light they laid the iron to it. Stroke on stroke it went in, and every stroke we counted. Ere noon it leaned. Yet it held the breath.
>
> Then the roots let go.

What each change does:
- It pays *every hull of yours that came nigh us we marked* and *The wood keepeth every count*, with no gloss.
- The good of the dead is a deed, and it is the deed that made it the first to fall.
- The halt drops to 7.9 words a sentence, and *Then the roots let go.* still lands in shared words.

**Ring 3 explains the grain's tell.** *Where we lost the most, thither we go.* is v2.0's *Such is our grain: it goes where it lost the most* in old dress. R6b (g) flagged it: "The tooth image alone carries it." Cut the sentence:
> When one of our hulls is struck, we go to the stroke, as the tongue to a broken tooth. It is in the rings.

### 4 · IV.6: five lines that leak

These are each small, but together they are why the leaf reads clinical after the turn.

**(a) *Into us they cast him, as men cast a net they mean to burn.*** (line 261)
- **It gives the men an intent to burn before the order.** In IV.3 they cast him in, and only then does ▒▒▒▒ say *"Burn it … Leave naught to show."* Jack's fix makes the burning the weak man's order. *A net they mean to burn* spreads it onto "men" in general.
- **It puts a Shore motive in the wood's mouth**, which craft §5.2 forbids.
- **It brings the custom in by the back door**, as men's usual way with a spoiled thing.

Rewrite:
> Into us they cast him, as men cast a torn net.

The net survives, as R6b (c) requires, and it rhymes with IV.3's *as a fisher casteth a torn net into the bilge*.

**(b) *Of the men in them we hold naught.*** (line 281)

R6b (c) lists *Of the men in them the grain holds nothing* as a line that must survive. The writer recast it to avoid a *-eth* near the fixed ring, but that *-eth* is the play. The grain that keeps every blow the Guest took keeps nothing of the axemen. This is memory theme 2 in a handful of words, and the coldest death line in the leaf. IV.6 has room for the ending, since it carries only two *-eth*. Rewrite:
> Of the men in them the grain holdeth naught.

*Holdeth naught* here answers *holds it still* at the close. Ruling 11 binds only the ring itself.

**(c) The council's list of the deed** (line 289):
> *They knew the gift set down, and the foot that put it in the dust. They knew the neck held gently, and the foreheads, and the blade. They knew the blows, the heels and the throwing, the fire and the poles.*

This is the deed told a third time, after IV.3 and IV.4, and the heels twice in this one leaf. The ledger in craft §2.2 gives IV.6 "the blows, with no hands named". Rewrite:
> They knew the gift, and the foot. They knew the neck held gently, and the blade, and the blows.
>
> Wood can hide naught.

**(d) *So the long-lived lay down their dead.*** (line 297)

This is an ethnographer's gloss at the grave, where the Book goes coldest (craft rule 12). It also breaks II.2's play. If the long-lived bury their dead among the silver elders' roots, then many lie beneath a silver elder. Cut it:
> Our keel they laid among the roots of the silver elders, with the bones of Aelrhen in its ribs.
>
> And the roots drank.

Now exactly one lies beneath a silver elder: the one who came to say a true thing. Nobody says so.

**(e) *They saw the best they had answered with murder and flame.*** (line 293)

This is a garden path. It first reads as "the best [that] they had answered". Rewrite:
> Their best they had sent, and they saw it answered with murder and flame.

It still answers IV.4's *we were his best*.

**II.2** came from the hardest telling, a home with no word in our tongue. Let the reading run out of words. The thought broken across both inks then acts out the headnote:

**IV.2** was "in them as a wound is". Let it count and repeat, and break the sky's triple before *wide* so that the story finishes it. The `legend_iv2_gap1` and `legend_iv2_gap2` keys stay where v2.0 has them:

**IV.6** is the leaf where "for this we have words". Its reading should run easy and break only at the fire. That break is the rain-butt, and *Hands burning — no. Boards.* is the right line for it. Make two changes:
- *Blood in our — in its boards.* becomes *— and warm. Into the boards. All of him.*
- *Proud of — of a name.* stammers for no reason. It becomes *A name, and proud of it.*

**Leave IV.4's reading alone.** It is the original of the slip, and these changes give it back its edge.

One more thing about IV.6's reading. As drafted it is a synopsis of ring 2: heels, thrown, blood, fire, poles, turning. The story then retells it line by line. The rebuilt turn in problem 1 gives the story things the reading never had: the tide, the cold body and the wind.

## The rest

1. **II.2's dateline** lacks *in*. Write *The fifth winter, in the Tide that Learned Deceit.*, as the other three and the samples have it. It comes to eight words.
2. **II.2 has no turn of Seren's own.** Every turn in it is from v1.3 or v2.0: *none lie*, *So long hid we, and so well*, *We had made sure of it.* (*So wither we.* is the writer's, and good.) The look by three in problem 2 gives her one (voice §10 item 8).
3. **II.2: *Naelsaen, the mist-hearts*** stands in the clear a few lines below the untold-name chip. A careful reader gets *nael-* for breath, or mist, before VI.3 gives *Naelear*. It is inherited from v1.3 and v2.0, and the puzzle kept it. **Orchestrator's call:** keep it, or give only *the mist-hearts* until VI.3.
4. **IV.2, the plea paragraph** (lines 129–131) has four fronted sentences in a row:
   - *So on the mute thing we cut…*
   - *In our own soft letters we cut it.*
   - *… that too we cut…*
   - *At the feet of the pillars … we set the stones.*

   Voice §1.5 forbids three in a row without weight, and this is where Yoda creeps in. Unfront one: *We cut it in our own soft letters.*
5. **IV.2: *They but cut it, and bare it away over the water.*** A *but* (meaning only) straight after the subject reads as a slip. Write *They cut it, and no more, and bare it away over the water.*
6. **IV.2's headnote** says Rhyna *had but its shape*, but the drawing's note says *{{Halyna}} could see*. Write *Rhyna took up this Blackthorn heart on the shingle, and {{Halyna}} had but its shape.* (50 words.)
7. **IV.2: two *X were we* in one leaf** (*Living pillars of the breath were we* · *A thin green thing were we*). The wood's object-first mark is in every leaf (*Eldest wood are we* · *Aelvaren were we* · *For that light were we made*). It teaches the register, but at one a leaf it stays a mark, and at two it becomes a tic. Unfront the first: *We were the living pillars of the breath.*
8. **IV.2: the `::: note` sits between the reading and the story**, and its English names the turn (*It did not answer.*) before the story reaches it. The text is v2.0's, and the key must stay. **Builder or orchestrator's call:** show the note's English only after the leaf is read, or set the block after *It did not answer.*
9. **IV.2: *The iron will tire; and when it stoppeth, the wood will come again.*** This agrees with `g10.md`, and it is the right HEAVY answer to three stacked *-eth* forms. It turns the proverb into a prophecy, and I would accept that. Make sure VI.3 quotes it exactly.
10. **IV.6: *Of us was left a keel and a few ribs*** puts a singular verb on a plural subject. Write *Of us were left*.
11. **IV.6's headnote** strings the facts on a semicolon, and *I, that heard Brenn's words, say of the council:* spends nine words of scaffold on Jack's seven. Write:
    > *Halvard lifted this heart in the vault; Rhyna, on the wall, thrust both hands into the rain-butt, and would not say why. The wood saith two, the ship-masters twenty, and I have kept both; of the council I say what the wood cannot: They did not know it was ignorance.*

    That is 50 words. *They did not know it was ignorance.* stays verbatim and in the headnote, as outline §IV.6 requires, and *what the wood cannot* gives *They* its referent.
12. **IV.6 is the lightest HEAVY leaf of the four**:
    - only two *-eth* forms;
    - one *wist*;
    - two negations without *do* (*Nor asked they*, *they wist not*).

    The rewrites add *he came not* and *holdeth naught* where the grammar does work. Do not sprinkle more.
13. **IV.6: *In its grief the old wood raged*** moves from *we* to *the old wood … its*. It is v2.0's, and it holds the burning at arm's length, so keep it.
14. **IV.4: no change.** It is the model, and its devices are now protected by the fixes to its neighbours (problem 5). It agrees with IV.3 (`g06.md`): the run of a day and a night, *old arms, two pairs* for {{Wendhessa}}, then *small arms, two pairs*, then one pair, for Tarnel and Seren.
15. **The memory theme** is light and felt in all four leaves, and preached in none:
    - II.2: *the grain will not leave it out* (theme 1) and *it remembereth this*;
    - IV.2: the counted strokes, and the axe in the rings (theme 4, which is now concrete);
    - IV.4: *his long listening*;
    - IV.6: the hand on the boards, the grief drunk with the rain (theme 3, as its dark mirror), and the grain that holds nothing of the axemen (theme 2).

    Add nothing more.
16. **The locks hold**, with one exception:
    - no custom, once (a) of problem 4 is mended;
    - ▒▒▒▒ is never named, and he is always *the host* or *the men of that house*;
    - no singular *they*;
    - no Christian mark;
    - Jack's two IV.6 lines are verbatim;
    - *and the grain holds it still* is exact in all four leaves.

    The one gap is IV.6's turn, which drops *into the wind* (problem 1).
17. **The writer's calls:**
    - **keep** *slept in the singing* in II.2, the best new clause in the file;
    - **keep** *his hand lay on our boards* in IV.6;
    - **keep** *Not fog* in {{Halyna}}'s mouth, without the *— no.* shape (problem 5);
