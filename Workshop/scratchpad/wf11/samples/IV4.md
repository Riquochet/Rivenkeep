### IV.4 · The Gift Held an Hour

*The third spring, in the Tide of Remembering.*

*On the night Brenn was read, a child asked what the Guest had given. This spring Rhyna held it an hour, and it came into her slow, as water over stone; Halvard, across the ward at the mortar, touched it not, and it was he that began the telling.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Old. Dark, and old. Stone — no. Not stone. A tree, once.*  
*— Hands, warm, a long while. Leaves. No — words, like leaves. One, and one, and one.*  
*— Three. Three words in him —*  
*— and naught to join them. We two have it.*  
*— A door. Our — the wood's kin, in the posts. Sawn. They answer not.*  
*— Kneeling. Set down. His best. A foot.*  
*— Dust. Laughter, through the dust.*  
*— A neck, held. Two brows, one —*  
*— the blade — [ ]*

This is held in the grain.

Eldest wood are we. A tree we were when the world was green to its edges. We fell, and the ages lay down on us, leaf on leaf, till time forgat us and we grew dark and hard. Ye took us for stone. We are not stone.

Wood that time forgat forgetteth not. Slow is its giving, and true. Of all the gifts the long-lived give, the dearest is a sliver of such wood. We were given once.

<!-- RING -->

Aelrhen he was named when he took his errand: bond-to-stone. A long life he listened across the breath, to the shore-men at their nets and at their walls. He gathered your words as a child gathereth fallen leaves, one and one and one. At the end he had three, and knew them for the right ones. Join them he could not. Ye join your words with small words, and small words make no sound across water.

But he had us. Whoso held us while a tree's shadow moved the breadth of its own trunk would know the whole of it. We were the sentence.

He chose the outermost of your holds, for its doors and its benches were of our wood. He took that for reverence. We knew it for what it was: our kin from the edge, sawn and polished. At the door he laid his hand on the post and greeted them. They answered not. They were dead.

He kneeled on the stones before the master of that house, and set us down. They that come under a roof bring their best, and we were his best. In the thunder of his own tongue he spake the name of the rite, *Ael'thar*, and after it his three words, in yours.

"Stop… Sky… Dying…"

A foot struck us into the dust by the wall. Laughter came to us through the dust.

He stopped not.

He rose, and took the back of the host's neck in his right hand, there where life goeth up into thought. A fledgling could have slept in that hand. The foreheads met, thought to thought, and he was silent.

With his left hand he set the blade to the host's arm.

And the host drew back. We know not why. Fear leaveth no mark on wood.

The blade that was to go a little way went deep, for the arm was moving. The host cried out, and his men came.

Then Aelrhen's blood ran over the stones of the step to where we lay, and with it came all of him: his long listening, and his last hour. So we know it as he knew it.

Between their hands he went down on his knees again, there where the rite began. He knew the blows as trees know the iron: one, and another. He lifted not his hand. The cut he would have made on his own arm was never made. He would ask no blood that he gave not. He gave it all, there on the step.

Never were the two arms laid together, shoulder to shoulder, that harm to the one should be harm to both. He died there, with the rite half made.

We lay in the dust and heard all of it, and no hand took us up. Of all things that ever lay at a shore-man's feet and looked like stone, we were the one that could have spoken to him.

And we were the one his foot found.

<!-- RING -->

In the morning a hand took us out of the dust, and put us away, and held us not.

Then a jolting in the dark, a day and a night. Then the dark, half a life, and never a hand. Then leaves about us, and cut stones cold beside us, and another dark as long. Then old arms, two pairs, slow, a long while; and fire, near and far. Then small arms, two pairs, going up, in the cold.

Then one pair, a long way.

None held us long, till thou heldest us while a tree's shadow moved the breadth of its own trunk.

Here is the sentence, as it was brought to the door:

*Stop the felling of the pillars at the edge of the grey, for they are not timber. The sky over us is their breath, and ours, and when you cut them it comes open, and our children cannot live in the open sun. We are here, behind the grey, and we have been here always, and we are not your enemies. Stop, for the sky is dying, and we are dying under it.*

That is all he came to say. We have carried it a long way, and the grain holds it still.

*And no one answered.*

### IV.4 · The Gift Held an Hour

*A child asked what the Guest had given, so this spring Rhyna held his gift for an hour. Halvard, across the yard at the mortar, never touched it, but he began the telling.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Old. So old, and dark. A stone — no. Not a stone. It was a tree.*  
*— Warm hands, for years. Leaves? No — words, like leaves. One, then one, then one.*  
*— Three of them. Three words inside him —*  
*— and nothing to tie them together. We two have it.*  
*— A door. Our — no, the wood's family, in the doorposts. Cut down. They don't answer.*  
*— He's kneeling. Putting it down. His best thing. A foot.*  
*— Dirt. Laughing, through the dirt.*  
*— A neck, held. Two foreheads, becoming one —*  
*— the knife — [ ]*

This is held in the grain.

We are the oldest wood, grown when the whole world was green, edge to edge. Then we fell, and age after age piled on us, until time forgot us and we went dark and hard. You took us for stone, but we aren't.

Time forgot us, but we do not forget. We give slowly, and only what is true. The long-lived have no finer gift than a sliver of wood like us, and we were given once.

<!-- RING -->

He took the name Aelrhen, bond-to-stone, when he took on his task. All his long life he listened across the grey to your fishermen and builders, collecting your words one at a time, the way a child picks up fallen leaves. By the end he'd gathered three, and he knew they were right. But he couldn't link them, because your language ties words together with little words, and little words don't carry over water.

He had us, though, and anyone who held us for as long as a tree's shadow takes to cross its own trunk would understand everything. We were the sentence.

He chose your farthest outpost because its doors and benches were our wood, and he took that as respect. We knew what it really was: our family from the edge, cut down and polished. At the door he touched the post to greet them, but they didn't answer, because they were dead.

He knelt before the master of the house and set us down. A guest brings the best thing he has, and we were his. In the thunder of his own language he named the ceremony, *Ael'thar*, then said his three words in yours.

"Stop… Sky… Dying…"

A foot kicked us into the dirt by the wall, and we heard laughing through the dirt.

He didn't stop.

He stood and cupped his right hand around the back of the host's neck, where life rises into thought, so gently a baby bird could have slept there. Their foreheads touched, mind to mind, and he said nothing.

With his left hand he put the knife to the host's arm.

And the host pulled away. We don't know why. Fear leaves no mark on wood.

The knife was meant to go in a little, but the arm moved, so it went deep. The host yelled, and his men ran up.

Then Aelrhen's blood trickled over the step and reached us, and all of him came with it: his long years of listening, and his final hour. That is how we know it the way he knew it.

While they held him, he knelt again where the ceremony had begun. He felt the blows the way trees feel the axe, one after another, and he never raised his hand. He had meant to cut his own arm next, because he would never ask for blood he wouldn't give, but he never got the chance. In the end he gave all of it, there on the step.

Their two arms were never pressed side by side, to promise that harm to one was harm to both. He died there, with the ceremony only half finished.

We heard it all from the dirt, and nobody picked us up. Of all the stony things that ever lay at your people's feet, we alone could have talked to him.

And we were the thing his foot found.

<!-- RING -->

Next morning a hand lifted us from the dirt and put us away, but never held us.

Then a bumpy ride in the dark for a day and a night, and half a lifetime of darkness with no hand at all. Then leaves wrapped us, with cold carved stones beside us, through another long dark. Then two pairs of old arms carried us slowly for a long time, past fires near and far. Then two pairs of small arms, climbing in the cold.

Then just one pair, for a long way.

Nobody held us for long, until you held us for as long as a tree's shadow takes to cross its own trunk.

This is the sentence he brought to the door:

*Stop cutting down the pillars at the edge of the grey, because they aren't just wood. The sky over us is their breath, and ours. When you cut them down, the sky opens up, and our children can't live in the open sun. We are here, behind the grey. We have always been here. We are not your enemies. Stop, because the sky is dying, and we are dying under it.*

That's all he came to say. We have carried it a long way, and the grain still holds it.

*And no one answered.*
