## BOOK TWO · THE BOOK OF THE BUILDERS

*Who we were, and who they were.*

### II.1 · Of the Keep That Was Forgotten

*Told by Seren Two-Inks. The founders' page inside it is their own, not mine. {{Halyna}} read it to me from the old writing, and I haven't dared to change a word of it.*

Hold the stone, Seren.

This is the Keep's story: how the founders kept it, and how we didn't. {{Aldwena}} carved its first page on slate in the First Days, in letters the size of barley grains.

> We two, who built the gate, wrote this.
>
> We found the ridge split from top to bottom, as if by a giant blade. In the split we built a stronghold of the ridge's own stone, cut by the quarry wardens. The first warden carved his name above the western stair and left room under it for his sons.
>
> With our four hands we two set the inner gate's capstone. Under it we cut our mark and vow, where nobody will see them unless the wall falls.
>
> This wall will stand. We two laid it, and we two answer for it, as long as stone is stone. It stands.

Their mark and vow are on the bottom of this stone. It takes two to turn it, but late at night, alone at the fire, I've reached under and traced the mark with one finger.

To the masters it's a Stonwryt, *a vow sealed in living rock, earned by toil and skill*, and gold can't buy one. Still, the havens' master pairs stamped theirs on coins, and to people the coins meant what the marks did: *trust, sealed in stone*. I have no mark, and I'll never cut one.

Under the first warden's name his sons carved theirs, one after another, and under the last the stone is smooth. Halvard Stone-Warden comes from that family. Rhyna of the Inner Gate comes from {{Aldwena}}'s. The two families were one long before those two shared a cradle.

Then the people moved to the warm harbours below, and the guild followed. Before leaving, the founders put the slates and the Keep's first staff into the black chest, like masons packing their tools at dusk. By the sea they built Eldhythe, the Eldest, though it's only the eldest of the havens. Nobody wrote down who left last, and after them the wind blew in under an unguarded gate.

It never fell. It was only left behind.

People forgot it until only the songs remembered it. No pair climbed up to *point* its walls, pressing new mortar into the joints. I love that word. So the rain got in, and then the frost and the ice.

Some winter, nobody knows which, the capstone slid off its two posts.

Nobody heard it fall.

But the whole coast heard the havens fall. After Glasspire, we climbed to the castle the songs promised would hold, and found only a shadow.

Forgetting did what no enemy ever could.

At dusk on the day we raised the banner, Halvard and Rhyna led the column through the inner gate. I followed with the water-skin, and I didn't know their names. Halvard swept the snow off the fallen capstone with his sleeve. A pale bloom covered its top, like frost on a gatepost.

They knelt, put their four hands under it, and turned it over like a boat the Tidesmeet fishermen gave up for lost.

It was theirs.

{{Aldwena}}'s mark and vow were there, still sharp, beside dents from the wardens' chisels. Rhyna touched her ancestors' mark. Halvard put his thumb in a dent his fathers' chisels made. The stone was whole. In the yard people kept coming in, their feet silent on the snow.

The stone kept its vow. We were the ones who didn't keep ours.

Before it got dark, they called the guild to its feet. After the Rite a new stone went up, and the True Men set the old one in the hollow of this hearth wall.

Whoever holds it holds the first builders' vow and their children's shame. Tonight it's under my two hands.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

### II.3 · Of the Bonded and the Theoliths

*That morning {{Idrenna}} sealed Enno and Della under the inner gate, the Keep's first sealing since the founders, and I held the lamp. That night {{Halyna}} told this to the children and had me write down who said what, because children like to know.*

*They put their hands on the stone and began:*

"We hold the stone together,"

"the way we hold everything."

<!-- MARKED -->

**Rhyna.** Come close, children in front, and the old folk can listen if they're quiet. This morning you saw Enno and Della kneel as two and rise as one, and now they're {{Enrella}}.

*When a little girl asked how it felt, Enno said, "It is like being one person," and Della finished, "with four hands and no excuses." They planned a one-door house with their mark under the first stone, because they couldn't wait for the last. Everybody laughed, and the girl asked when I'd be sealed. I didn't have an answer, so I kept writing.*

**Halvard.** Not all Shorelanders were Stonewrights, yet all Stonewrights were Shorelanders. The guild calls its children from the cradle, from any home, and once, the records say, from a jailer's house in Carnhold. Not one parent ever refused, because the guild guarded the land's faith as well as its walls.

**Rhyna.** We believe God made each pair one before either drew breath. The seers bend over each cradle with a lamp, but they don't choose. They only read what's written, and that's the Aetherbond. After a seer read us two, we shared one cradle, at his mother's house and mine by turns. We two have never slept under separate roofs. Remember this for later: the cradle shows the bond, but only the sealing makes it whole.

**Halvard.** {{Idrenna}}, by the door, were our Theoliths, our teachers. They taught us two to cut, lay and set stone, to pray, and to keep count in the old writing. They gave us only the vow's name, the Covenant of Completion, and you'll get no more until it's yours to understand. When we two get it right, {{Idrenna}} will nod.

*{{Idrenna}} sat with their white heads bent close, and didn't nod.*

**Rhyna.** At a sealing, the guild says out loud what the cradle first said. The masters make one name from the two, like two posts and a stone making a gate, and give them the mark the seers saw. A name for the mouth, and a mark for the stone. Only they may carve it, and no other pair may break it. So we two became {{Halyna}}, husband and wife, but a pair long before we were either.

*Halvard leaned towards the fire, and the children did too.*

**Halvard.** Tonight's ash is soft, and I'll only draw this once, so watch my finger. Here's our mark. We carved it under the new capstone the night of the Rite, where it stays hidden unless the wall falls. Look once.

*No child moved, and a coal shifted in the fire.*

**Rhyna.** Now I brush my palm across it, and it's gone. Ash blows away, but marks belong in stone, and stone lasts.

**Halvard.** You've seen us on the wall during the holding, working as one without a word. The old masters said a pair does twelve people's work. I'm Elder Halvard and she's Elderess Rhyna, and neither outranks the other. She never laid a stone second.

*A boy in front counted to twelve, and ran out of fingers.*

**Rhyna.** If I cut my hand in the south tower, he'd feel it on the north wall and know which hand. The old masters say that harm to one is harm to both.

**Halvard.** The Bonded are rarely apart and never far, and in all the guild's records no bond has ever broken. Separation, God did not permit.

*The fire had burned a finger lower. Enno and Della held hands, and the little girl slept against Della's knee.*

**Rhyna.** So Bonded pairs die together, but we two don't fear it. If one is killed, the other has no wound or illness. The heart breaks because it knows, and the body stops because its work is done. People say the longest anyone has lasted alone is eight days. The Stonewrights, who keep count, say hours. Many pairs have died a mile apart at the same moment, and each knew the other was dying. And each went too. When the left hand dies, does not the right one die too?

**Halvard.** One last thing, then bed. Sometimes one baby of a pair died before the sealing. The bond wasn't whole yet, so the other lived, unbonded. A bonded household takes that child in as a third. The unbonded have one name, and to the guild that means mourning. Still, the old masters said when God takes one, he leaves the other a job.

**Rhyna.** Our third is Seren, who held this morning's lamp and holds tonight's pen. Her cradle-mate was Tarnel, a boatbuilder's son from Tidesmeet. All along the road he tied a net for the mountain lakes, and taught her his knot. That night on the pass, the cold killed many children and some old people. Tarnel and Seren lay down under one cloak. She woke beside him.

**Halvard.** So she lived, and she is only one. Tarnel told her, "A net is just knots holding hands," and this Book is bound with his knot. She can't know the wood, because a knowing takes two, like a stone one hand can't lift. Nobody else's death can kill her, so she can go alone where we two can't and carry the Book on. The scribes before her were all pairs, and when a pair died, their book was shut in the vault. A book nobody reads aloud is just ink. Look, her pen hasn't stopped, because this Book has to outlive its tellers. That's why Seren writes it.

*I'd stopped writing at the cloak, but I dipped my pen and went on. {{Idrenna}}, by the door, nodded. Then, together, {{Halyna}} ended with the guild's oldest saying:*

Birth united them; death could not separate them.

We have laid this tale the way it was laid for us.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*
