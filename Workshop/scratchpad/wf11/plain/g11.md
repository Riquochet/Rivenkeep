## EPILOGUE

*What the grey sent back.*

### The Stone out of the Grey

*I wrote this on the page facing Kael's tale of the torn cloak. It stood empty until I drew the grain on it, with one line:* Not told yet. This is still too deep for {{Halyna}} to hold. *Nothing {{Halyna}} knew ever filled it.*

Hold the stone, Seren.

I write alone, in one ink, with nobody left who knows the wood. Their two inks sit dry on my table.

{{Halyna}} are gone.

That seventh winter they died the same night, as the Bonded do. Rhyna went first, asleep, and Halvard before the lamp went out, because he knew. They share one grave and one name under the inner gate's new top stone. The Title, their vow and their mark are cut on its underside, hidden until the wall falls.

*{{Orvenna}} sang at the grave, Ormund up to the comma and Penna the rest:*

> *Stone on stone, and the stone stays true.*  
> *Hand over hand, and the hand holds on.*  
> *Two at the gate when the grey sails gather.*  
> *Two at the gate, and the gate stands strong.*

{{Idrenna}}, old even when {{Halyna}} were children, have outlived them. {{Halyna}} carved Halvard's name on the western stair, under his fathers', before they sent their chisels into the grey. Below it the stone is blank, because his family is gone.

That night the Captain tied a hand's width of his half-cloak to a pike above the gate, the last of it. All winter he wore a plain grey cloak.

I didn't follow {{Halyna}}. They once said that alone I could go where they couldn't, and carry the Book on. Now the Covenant makes sense: when God takes one, he leaves the other a task.

This is mine.

I was scared to speak at my first tale, so {{Halyna}} put my hands on the stone. One said, "Hold the stone," and the other, "Hold it, and it will hold you." Now I say both halves alone.

One rainy morning in the seventh spring, an empty grey boat came in from the grey and beached itself.

The old ship-masters hung back, but the children went down together, and the first pressed both palms to the bow. A child once answered the green wood when we couldn't.

It was the same child.

Then Merrick came slowly down, the man who looked away from every burning ship.

Merrick put his hand on its side.

I went last, and the children parted like water before a boat. I knew its front post: the first heart's two halves, locked together, with {{Halyna}}'s mark over the join.

The mark of {{Halyna}} across the front post of the boat that came home: the same mark that was cut there when it was sent
:::

It had gone home, and it had come home. A stone lay where {{Halyna}} had set their chisels and the green sliver.

It was wood turned to stone, like the Guest's gift. The Rivenmen call it Myststone and say it remembers everything. Stone does not translate, but this was never only stone. Its carver learned the chisel late but well, young by their count, I think, and a man by ours.

The rain had stopped, the sea lay slate grey, and the tide drew out with a long sound.

I knew that chisel's bite.

I'd held the lamp for it twice, under the top stone and across this post. It was cut in our letters, so one person could read it alone, and I read it to the children without touching it.

::: native legend_stone_epilogue stone
The Stone out of the Grey: the Last Carver's letters, read with the eyes, cut across a grain that no one at this hearth has known
The letters are read right away, below. The grain is told only by this, written under the round in the wood's words:

*This is held in the grain. It is about one of the stone-folk. He went up alone, ahead of the rest, to the last stone still standing. He tore his sail, the red one, and set it on the stone. He cut marks on it, a hand's worth of them. And the stone-folk came to it, one and then many. Every branch that came against that stone saw it. What the branches saw went home through the roots, and the groves held it. One of us held your marks the longest. Home. Family. The one they kneel to. The open door. The stillness. They stood for these at the stone. We stood for the same things, on the other side of it. And the grain still holds it.*

And one line in the Book's own voice: *Known by no one at this hearth. Written down for whoever has learned the grain.*
:::

*In the memory of our home, our families...*

The cutting stops there, its last stroke still sharp. He never saw our cloak, since he never sailed, but he learned our letters. I call him the Last Carver, because I think he was the only one left.

It has a *the* before *memory*, which our Title never had. I read it like that, and no child laughed. I wonder if it stops there because his hand stopped. Nobody came with it, and maybe that's wise. We haven't earned a guest yet.

That spring the staff vanished from the gatehouse, and nobody saw the Captain take it. It left a clean line in seven winters of dust. If you doubt me, go and see the dust. The work is done. I know *the stairs that go down no more*.

I haven't gone down to look.

This Book stays in the archive chest with the silent stones, the Guest's gift and the Stone. Grief is an inheritance, paid out to the children. The torn cloak still flies over their one grave, and the sea hasn't finished.

I don't know what we'll send back. I only know it must be sent kneeling.

Every night the inner gate stands open for a guest, and a child keeps a light on the north wall.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

*And the Captain pushed his hood back.*

## APPENDIX

*Every carving the wood gave us, sorted by its root.*

### The Book of Knowings

*{{Halyna}} knew these carvings one at a time, from the wreck-wood and the Thrones, and I wrote each one here at the back. This half of the Book belongs to the fleet, because the wood made every carving and we made none.*

Every carving from the wood starts with one of ten strokes, its root. I learned their shapes by eye, on the shore and in the vault, before anything else. I named them, because a nameless shape slips away like a wet stone, and each name stuck once I'd said it three times.

The young wood cut only the root, and each Tide after added to it, so the oldest carvings are the young ones grown old. By the late Tides I could spot a root from the wall at a glance, but nothing more. The wood saves the rest for the Bonded, to be known, not read.

Here are only the first words of each carving. The rest, who waited for whom and when the heart would turn, is written in the tales. The young wood's seven keep the soldiers' names for them.

One more sign, *Burn*, came from Emberhythe in a mason's pack. It was cut in the Fall and never faced our wall. In the homecoming year the mason laid it where the Burning went out, and I don't count it.

The young wood gave seven carvings. The next three Tides gave eight each, and the Tide that Learned Deceit gave seven. The Last Tide cut nothing new, only whole tomorrows made of old carvings, each ending in a turn for home. The Thrones added eight Judgements. Two Thrones carried an older carving whole, because every other carving is written in the Wave and the Lone Stroke. I've set down all forty-six, and I knew none of them.

**The Last Note · Three Stones over the Hearth**

Hold the stone, Seren.

On this winter's first night, a child at the front asked what the three grey stones above the hearth say. In the havens every child, me included, was told they say nothing. I didn't say that. I said I didn't know.

Every night since, in the empty hall, I've held the lamp to the lowest stone. I wanted its sounds, not its meaning, because stone does not translate. The Mystaeri spelled our words by sound, in among their letters, and Brenn had given me three of our words.

Last night, alone by a low fire, I watched my lamp burn a finger's width lower. Then I found all three together, the sounds close but the symbols wrong.

::: native legend_three_stones chip
The lowest of the three grey stones over the Long Hearth: the curling letters along its edge, and among them three words of ours, cut by their sound
:::

*Stop… Sky… Dying…*

They were cut at the foot of the black pillars and sold off with the timber. At Eldhythe they spent three generations above a harbour-house fire. Under them, people laughed at the three axemen who came home on a spar, and ship-masters knocked their pipes out on the lowest. And nobody read them.

Nobody knocks a pipe on them here. I think I've read them. I say I think.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*
