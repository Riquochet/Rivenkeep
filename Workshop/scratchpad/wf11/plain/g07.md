### IV.5 · The Burning Boat

*Voss who asked told this as his grandmother Elspet told it to him at her fire on the dock at Eldhythe. The ship-masters wouldn't tell it, the children wouldn't stop singing its rhyme, and the shore laughed at it, but the sea didn't.*

Give me the stone. It's my grandmother's tale, not mine. Her brother gave it to her, and the sea gave it to him.

In this war's first fight I heard one of their ships burn and scream. The old sailors on the wall looked away, Merrick first, and Jory was whistling. A rhyme turned in me like a skipping rope, one I'd skipped to at seven and understood at forty.

As a boy I asked my grandmother why the ship-masters wouldn't hear it. She answered with a story, and you can judge it.

At twelve she looked after the fire at her mother's harbour inn. The ship-masters came in wet, the weather still in their beards. Three flat grey stones hung above the fire, with letters like bird tracks nobody could read. Only children asked, and the masters knocked their pipes out on the lowest.

When bold men got back from the grey with tales, she filled their cups and laughed. "The Mystlands turn honest men into liars." She said it about her brother Daveth, an axeman of the felling.

That summer he was on one of the last boats back from the edge, loaded low with black wood, and another followed. A burning boat came screaming at them out of the grey, into the wind and against the current, which no boat can do. It hit both boats low where the load was heaviest, their black wood caught fire, and rain fell on two crews.

Three came home.

Daveth floated home on a broken mast, the sea pouring off his coat onto the floor she'd swept. The three told it all autumn and never got in a boat again.

Drunk, the outpost men swore they'd pushed off a burning boat with a dead man aboard, and far out it turned. They swore to the scream above all, and that the grey man spoke three of our words, but no two agreed which.

She counted the boats on her fingers, as Daveth had for her, then laid her palm flat on the table.

There were two boats.

Now the ship-masters say twenty.

Everyone laughed at those three, and by day so did she, pouring for them below the lowest stone. At night she didn't.

Daveth drank on that tale and drowned in the harbour, a stone's throw from home.

So why wouldn't the ship-masters hear it? I've seen one leave his cup and walk into the rain rather than listen. Since then I've heard three of them in the passes, each setting his words down like a cup. The first said, "A small boat on fire, far out in the grey." The second said, "It turned into the wind, and weather came after." The third said, "It's the boat that comes for you when you die." Then the first said, "The shore can laugh, because it will never have to face it."

The cities wrote it down: an armed stranger, put down, his boat burned and sent drifting. Who argues with writing? But the children made a rhyme of it, and children will argue with anything. Jory and I turned the skipping rope because we were big and slow, and he turned it slower for the littlest.

*Then the children sang, as they still do:*

> *Grey boat, grey boat, out of the grey,*  
> *knelt at the warden's door one day.*  
> *A stone for a present, a cut for a kiss.*  
> *Who ever heard of a hello like this?*  
> *Burn it and push it and give it to the tide.*  
> *But the grey boat turned, and the grey boat cried.*

We never sang the last two lines, in case a ship-master heard.

> *Grey boat burning, and after it, the rain.*  
> *And the one it comes for won't come home again.*

Merrick heard us one evening and coiled our rope round his arm like a line he meant to keep. "Not that one," he said.

His father was Daveth.

Merrick, our oldest ship-master, sits near the door tonight. He hasn't gone out.

Jory whistled the tune on the east wall that whole first winter, never the words. He planned to drink at that fire again once the havens were ours. Every morning Merrick told him to stop, and he laughed. "It's only a rhyme."

That last morning I was beside him on the wall-walk while he whistled it slowly, the way he'd turned the rope for the little ones. The rhyme pulled tight in me, and the two lines we'd never sung came to me.

Merrick told him to stop.

I did not tell him to stop.

A fire-ship came onto the stones below us, burning and screaming.

The tune stopped.

His is the first cairn on the east face.

If my grandmother's house still stands, those stones hang over a cold hearth, with only rain for a roof.

We forget. We have always been good at that. But Mystwood remembers.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

## BOOK FIVE · THE BOOK OF THE TIDES

*The war: a doom set in motion by the dead, crewed by the living, against a people who had never known they were the foe.*

### V.1 · The First Glyph

*Told by Seren Two-Inks, who held the lamp. Sand from that beach is still caught in the binding of this Book.*

Hold the stone, Seren.

{{Halyna}} had the wood that night, all four of their hands on the grain, and I had the lamp in one of mine. It's been that way ever since.

After a month, we still didn't know what the grey sails were. The night we beat back their first attack, the sea brought us their grey wreck. Planks, ribs and a keel the size of a gate tumbled in the shallows.

The old sailors kept clear of it, but wood was scarce on the ridge, so the soldiers made fires of it on the beach. Harl of the burning shore snapped a rib across his knee and threw it on. "Burn it," he said. The Captain let most of it burn and stood among them, not moving.

The first fires were catching when Hale the runner came up the beach. He walked past the fires and the Captain to {{Halyna}} and said, "Marks on the wood."

When the tide turned, they went down, Halvard steadying Rhyna on the wet stones. I was their third, so I carried the lamp behind them. The men hauled a plank out of the waves. A single thin mark ran along its grain, and one long edge was raw, as though the rest had been torn away.

The only other things that might speak were the silent stones in the archive chest and a bundle {{Wendhessa}} had sealed. No pair would open it. Drunk axemen from the felling used to swear that black wood could put a thought into your hand if you kept it there long enough. Everyone had laughed at them, but there wasn't anything else to try.

So Rhyna sat beside the plank and pressed her palms flat to the grain, and Halvard knelt behind her with his hands over hers. Nobody spoke. The tide went out and came in, and the lamp burned a finger's width lower.

Then the knowing rose into Rhyna's palms. She said it burned, but like cold iron in midwinter, not like fire. The burning had a shape, a single stroke curling forward, like a wave just before it breaks. Before she'd said a word, Halvard looked up.

"Shore," he said.

Rhyna told him he was right. Halvard could never explain how he knew. It was as if someone had slipped the word into his mouth and left. They knew more than the word, but the rest was a forest's thinking and they only had a shore's words. They held on until the lamp was low, but they could only say one word.

When they'd stood up, stiff, others tried. Jory of Eldhythe, who's at the back of the hall tonight beside Voss, laughed with his hand on it. "Wet planks." He went back up the beach whistling the tune but never the words. Enno and Della, who climbed the mountain hand in hand, put their four hands on it. He said it was cold and she said it wasn't, and they were both right.

When they'd gone, I sat in Rhyna's place and put my own hand on the grain, alone. Something rose into it from the wood, much too big for me. I was a girl with a cup, and it was a river in flood.

I let go.

Halvard put the lamp back in my hand.

Before we climbed back up, I opened this Book on my knees. First I said *shore* three times, the way I test every word, to hear if it would hold. It held, short and square. Pages were precious then, but I wrote it in the middle of a clean one, and nothing else. I drew the sign on the facing page, but the ink wouldn't dry in the sea air.

On its page the word looked very small.

The fires were low behind us when we came up, and {{Halyna}} stopped at the gate.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*
