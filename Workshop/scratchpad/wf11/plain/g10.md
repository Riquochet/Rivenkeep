### VI.3 · The Knowing of the Green Wood

*I, Seren Two-Inks, held the lamp, and I told this on the night the boat went out, in the sixth autumn. {{Halyna}} stood at the stone with me, each with a hand beside mine, and we held it a long time before anyone spoke.*

<!-- TOKEN {LAST_THRONE_AT_HAVEN} -->

Hold the stone, Seren.

All day the tenth Throne, {LAST_THRONE_AT_HAVEN}, lay where it fell, a split black hill of timber taller than a harbour-house. Even dead, it scared the soldiers off. {{Halyna}} had known nine hearts since spring, all dead wood. Toward evening the Captain came down himself, stood a long time, hood up, and looked at {{Halyna}}.

At dusk I followed them in with the lamp, under dripping timbers. The heart lay in the oldest wood, hard, grey and still, with a sliver no bigger than a hand in a crack beside it.

It was green.

I lowered the lamp, not trusting the light. For six winters we'd searched wood and sea for orders, names and sails, but never for anything alive. And this had lived in a dead heart, like a seedling in a cracked wall.

Since the first winter {{Halyna}} had needed only one hand, but now they both touched it together. A drop hit the lamp flame and hissed.

Their faces changed as one. It wasn't an order or a wound. Rhyna spoke as if someone had put the word in her mouth and left.

"Home."

Halvard said she was right, but the word didn't come alone, and it didn't stop where we stop. They said it in one breath.

<!-- W -->
*We remember our home too.*

Rhyna cried, and Halvard didn't, because she was crying for them both. I held the lamp until she'd finished.

They gave me one more thing, tucked in the answer like a seed in a fruit. The wood's people call themselves *Naelear*, those of the breath, and *Mystaeri* is only the shore's word. I said their name three times before writing it, and it held.

<!-- RIPENS: II.2's untold name -->

Since the iron came, the black pillars hadn't grown a leaf, waiting for the sky to come back.

So it was a seed.

Rhyna carried it up to the Keep on moss, Halvard holding her elbow. A fisher-girl from the east keep, who lit the north lamp, ran up to ask where it was going.

<!-- HALYNA -->

"Home."

"And we two must find it a boat."

<!-- HALYNA END -->

The guild has no record of a Stonewright boat. The sea was something we walled out, never a road. But the havens' boatbuilders had carried their tools up the mountain too. Their oldest was Tarnard, Tarnel's father, and next day on the beach he laid the keel himself.

Harl of the burning shore shouldered the first plank out of the vault. He'd said *Burn it* about every plank in the war, but he laid this one at Tarnard's feet without a word. Tarnard put his palm on it and said, "Wood that knew the sea knows the way home."

Those planks had all spoken to us once, and now they'd speak for us. For the front post, Tarnard chose the two halves I'd set raw edge against raw edge, the *shore* plank and the silent one. {{Orvenna}} locked them like stone, and Ormund struck the last key once while Penna held it.

The broken grain met, and held.

That evening {{Halyna}} cut their mark over the join, one holding the chisel while the other struck, as my lamp burned a finger's width lower. No mark of ours had ever gone into wood.

The wood took it.

Rhyna pressed her palms to the cut, and Halvard covered them with his. Tarnard let me tie the bow with the knot Tarnel taught me on the Road, a knot for every mile. Tarnard stared at it a long time, then quietly repeated his son's saying. "A net is just knots holding hands."

It rode a rough sea off the point for a day without leaking. It was small, and it was sound.

At the evening tide the whole hearth came down, the old leaning on the young. {{Halyna}} put the green sliver on its moss in the bow, against the front post. Next to it went a Stonwryt coin of gold and black stone. It was trust sealed in stone, our dearest gift. They added their two chisels, which caught the last light.

<!-- HALYNA -->

"Send them with it,"

"because we two have cut our last mark."

<!-- HALYNA END -->

In silence, {{Halyna}} knelt at the bow on the wet stones. The whole hearth knelt behind them, and the soldiers put their pikes down. Kael the Counter, who won't sit to tell a tale, knelt too. Last in line, his hood still up, the Captain knelt.

That's him at the end of the bench, his knees still dark from the wet beach.

{{Halyna}} spoke from the front.

<!-- HALYNA -->

"One people can go this far alone: the gift and the kneeling."

"The rest needs the other."

<!-- HALYNA END -->

A wave ran up and soaked the keel. Then Harl stood, lifted the bow in both arms and walked it into the tide, and the fisher-children shoved it off. The girl who'd asked where it would go was in front, waist-deep in the cold water.

Far out on the outgoing tide, the boat turned. It went into the wind and against the current, into the grey.

The old ship-masters lined the beach, Merrick first, and for six winters they'd looked away from the sea whenever a ship burned.

They did not look away.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And no one answered.*

When the three of us let go, the hearth stayed silent a long time. The fire burned low, and nobody fed it. I think we were waiting for one old voice. Ebba of Fenholm is buried under the east cairns.

Then a child at the front spoke softly. It was the girl who'd been first into the sea, old Ebba's great-granddaughter.

"We remember."

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

*Then, for the first time, the hearth sang the way the wood sings. We call it* The Two Homes:

<!-- W: line 3 only -->
> *Our home was cut out of the mountain's bone.*  
> *We forgot it, and found it, and held it with blood.*  
> *Their home was breathed out of the hearts of the trees, and the axes came, and the breath got thin, and they haven't found it, and they can't hold it.*  
> *Stone keeps the promise, and the grain keeps the truth.*  
> *We remember our home. They remember theirs too.*
