### V.7 · The Mirror in the Grain

*I, Seren Two-Inks, told this on three nights, each one the night its carving was read. All three times I held the lamp down on the beach, and this is that lamp.*

**Part I · Three Roads, One Hour**

Hold the stone, Seren.

All that short night drums beat in the grey. Enno and Della sat up in their one-door house, which never rose past one row of stones. Their mark was under its first stone, cut with Enno's chisel and Della's mallet. They'd come into the yard hand in hand, and I came alone.

At dawn the fleet came three ways in one hour and broke through east and north. There were more gaps than pairs, so {{Orvenna}}, the wall-masters, quietly did what no masters had done.

They split up a pair, and Ormund sent Enno east while Penna sent Della north. Enno shouted, "It is like being one person," and Della, running, finished it: "with four hands and no excuses." The Bonded hardly ever part, and never far. A mile of wall is far.

Hale saw the shot that took the east wall and Enno with it, but I didn't.

At the north I held the mortar for Della. She turned the last stone over and cut their mark underneath, holding the chisel herself, and her hand didn't shake.

Then her mallet stopped in mid-air.

She looked east, then finished the last stroke, set the chisel by the mark, and laid her head down, cheek to the stone. The mortar was still soft under my thumb.

She was gone.

Hale brought the news from the east and didn't say it, the first runner ever to come too late. No other mark was ever finished by one hand, and until dark I searched it for Della's lone stroke.

You can't tell.

That night Stannard carried Enno the whole mile to her. {{Orvenna}} lowered the north stone onto them with their own four hands, mark down, and {{Idrenna}}, who had sealed them, prayed in two halves.

"God, you made these two one before either drew breath."

"One cradle from you, one stone from us, and you won't part them."

Ormund started the old joke over the stone, and Penna ended it. The Captain tied a strip above them, his first for Stonewrights, though they were his company too. Kael counted them and said, "One."

When the tide brought in the bearer's plank, Rhyna and I took the lamp down. Above, where Enno had knelt, Halvard mended the gap as the masons sang the gate song. No one sang either half of the first line, but all came in on the second as if it had been sung.

Rhyna touched the grain and went white, and up on the wall, trowel in hand, so did Halvard. Together they told me it was the whole fleet's orders, each ship tied to the next.

*Three roads, one hour, and east and north go as one. The south waits at the dread's edge until the south's reach goes east, and then it goes.*

For a long time neither spoke, and I asked what was wrong, though I think I knew.

"That's how we two work a wall."

"Each waits for the other's stroke without being told."

The carving called that Tide *Aelthae*, and that night I recognised the *ael* in it from *Ael'thar*. I've written the name, but I won't write what I thought.

The mortar was drying on Halvard's trowel.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

**Part II · What We Taught Them**

One spring morning a fleet charged the wall the old way, each ship alone, loud and straight. The whole wall laughed and fired every gun, Pellow's too. Pellow of the spire, our youngest, was always saying "Look close."

Hale was there when Pellow's spyglass showed a sail going pale, then another, and another.

"They are not green."

Hale ran with it, and Pellow, without orders, turned his guns on the grey and stood on the wall-top with his glass.

The Captain walked the wall, telling each True Man, "Shift your ground." Like a lamplighter, he didn't hurry, or the flame would go out.

The hunters came out of the grey for every gun that had fired, but the crews he'd reached survived. One at a time is slow.

Pellow's guns, farthest from him and nearest the grey, met the hunters first and alone, and the Captain didn't speed up.

Pellow lay dead on the wall.

His glass was by his hand, unbroken.

When the tide turned, {{Halyna}} touched a plank from that fleet.

<!-- W -->
*Come loud, alone and straight, like the young wood, and stop just before the fire. The hunters wait for the flashes.*

<!-- W: the italic line only -->
Under it: *they shoot first and far, and move any gun we find.*

"The wood can't lie," I said, but then I understood. It wasn't lying, only obeying an order to fool us.

<!-- HALYNA -->

"They learned it from us."

"We taught them, with every quiet gun."

<!-- HALYNA END -->

They had no word for a lie until we gave them one. Their name for us was carved small in it too: *Rhenear*, the stone-deaf.

At dusk we built Pellow's cairn, and the Captain tied a strip above it and offered Hale the glass and guns. "I'm not a True Man," Hale said. "I run."

Both went to Pellow's second-in-command, Ruan of the spire, and the Captain made him a True Man. Ruan raised the glass and looked past the cairn.

"Look close," he said.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

**Part III · The Plan That Carved Its Own Defeat**

In deep snow a bearer came, a whole tomorrow carved into its heart. We'd sunk it by midday, and the tide carried the heart in. {{Halyna}} had never read so long, and I filled the lamp twice before the end.

<!-- W -->
*If the bearer sinks, the next takes the flag, and if that one sinks, go home.*

They'd carved their own defeat into the plan.

<!-- HALYNA -->

"One hand did all that carving, not a council."

"A young one."

<!-- HALYNA END -->

But the last two words stopped us. To the young wood, *home* only meant where hurt things run, and this was carved in on purpose. We three looked at each other over the lamp, like wives when a long-sick man asks for his boots.

<!-- HALYNA -->

"They carve a tomorrow now, like us."

"Then let's build one they didn't carve for."

<!-- HALYNA END -->

On the beach the lamp went out, and I didn't fill it a third time.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

## BOOK SIX · THE BOOK OF THE HOMECOMINGS

*How the havens came home, and the war went home.*

### VI.1 · The Homecomings

*The True Men told this at each haven's fire on the night its Throne fell, and I wrote each part down right there. Kael told the beginning and the end at this hearth once the tenth haven was home, but first he counted my pages.*

Give me the stone — no, I'll stand. At winter's end we came down the old mountain roads, masons last, and found a Throne waiting in every ruined haven. Each man swore here for his haven, and I kept all ten oaths. Another man spoke for each who'd fallen. Hold on to the havens' names, because they're ours again.

**Eldhythe.**

*Voss, speaking for Corlen.*

The havens' first walls went up here, and stand once more. The Tide-Heart sank back to its own sea for good. Who ought to bring our first boat home? Corlen, but he lies under the east cairns, so I took the tiller. Like him, I steered her in slowly with a wet finger up, though the wind gave me nothing, then waited for the sea to finish. My grandmother's house was roofless, but the three grey stones still hung over its cold fireplace, the lowest scarred by ship-masters' pipes. They've hung over a dead fire long enough, so they're in my pack tonight.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

**Tidesmeet.**

*Tamm of the three spans.*

We hit all three heads in one holding, and not one grew back. The Bearer of New Branches sank where the middle bridge had been. Then I lined up my three stones, as I did every night by my blanket. One span is a plank, but three is a city.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

**Fenholm.**

*Marl the fen-born.*

A town's just new stone on old, we used to joke, and tonight we laughed again. The Grain That Rots sank in the fen, and our mortar stopped rotting. My grandmother carried her stone on her back out of the fen but died before it came home. I laid it on the old row as she wanted. If you'd asked if it would hold, she'd have said, "Perhaps." It hasn't sunk.

*At that fire too, everyone paused a breath for Ebba.*

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

**Holtward.**

*Aske of the canopy.*

The Root-Heart had rooted among the trees and wouldn't rise. I warned my men, "These were trees once, so mind your hands." Nobody raised an axe, and we let it stand: it had come home.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

**Carnhold.**

*Garvel of the smithies.*

Carnhold, which once rang all day, was silent, and the Stone-Grain crumbled into the first quarrymen's cairns. I hit the anvil once in a cold smithy, and it rang true. Whatever I make on it first won't be a gun.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

**Emberhythe.**

*Harl of the burning shore.*

The Burning died like a forge nobody feeds, and our mason laid his scorched plank on the black crust, the one marked *Burn*.

I did not burn it.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

**Sandreach.**

*Ulden of the galleries.*

The canyon shook for an hour as the Deep-Beneath burst out below the east keep and split. In the lowest gallery I put my ear to the floor and heard nothing, and tonight I'll sleep under my own stone roof.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

**Rimewatch.**

*Kael, speaking for Hesk.*

I don't tell other men's cities, but I must tell this one. The lake was white all morning, with the Long Winter waiting in it. Hesk stood at the edge in daylight, holding his mother's lit lamp. For six winters it had guided the night-watch from our north wall at dusk.

Before noon he walked onto the ice alone, and I counted each step. He stopped at two hundred and twelve, where the great hall stood. The ice groaned as it had when he carried the lamp out, and that night it held.

At midday the white cleared, and the ice dragged the Long Winter under.

The ice took Hesk too.

At dusk we found the lamp still burning on the ice — I swear to that.

He came home.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

***Added in Seren's hand:*** *That night the north wall was dark until a fisher-girl lit a lamp and said, "Someone has to keep a light."*

**Highreach.**

*Lanner of the crags.*

The Blood of the Sky dropped from its cloud for good. As I'd promised, I went up the high crag and whistled up a wind, and it brought no sails.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

**Glasspire.**

*Ruan, speaking for Pellow.*

In the black cellars the Silvered shattered like a mirror, every shard showing our own lamps, and Pellow ought to have seen it first. I looked close through his glass, and looked for him.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

*Kael took the stone again:*

Tonight I called fourteen names, and eleven answered. The yard went quiet for a breath at Corlen's name, and at Pellow's. When I called Hesk, someone lit a lamp on the north wall, but no answer came. I have no haven, because the Captain found me on a road. At all ten fires this year, whenever the answer came, I heard this hearth. Every haven is Rivenkeep — I swear to that.

Ten havens: I counted them twice, and it didn't take long.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*
