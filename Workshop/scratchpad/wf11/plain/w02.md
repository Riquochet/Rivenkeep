### V.3 · The Wreck That Went Home

*Halvard had his hands on this ship. Rhyna had open salt cuts on her palms and didn't touch it, but she knew it as well as he did, the way family knows family.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Salt. Salt in every plank. Cold, so long.*  
*— A stone flashes. Iron, low, right through. Sea pouring in.*  
*— Home when wounded. Home, slowly. Telling the others.*  
*— Look before —*  
*— you trust.*  
*— Again. Same water. Alone, slow.*  
*— One fire, thrown. Where it hit — [ ]*  
*— A stone flashes. Iron, low, and —*

This is held in the grain.

We were a Ralenthae ship, from the tide that learned from the roots. We carried more than one word: a memory, and a warning. Now we're broken, salt-soaked wood. We came to your wall twice that cold season.

<!-- RING -->

The first time, at grey dawn, we came with our family behind the bearer. A stone on your wall flashed. The green wood had learned that where a stone flashes, there's iron, and the roots carried it home. We turned toward it. It spoke again, and the iron went in low and right through. The sea poured in.

Then one of the green wood's first carvings woke in us: *Home when wounded.* We turned our back on your wall and slipped into the breath, low and slow. Your wall let us leave, or it didn't see us. We don't know which.

On the way, we told our family without words. First the flash went into the roots, then where it came from, then where the iron hit us, and our family's planks felt it.

The dread went last. It's only that there's a spot in the water now where wood dies. Slow as sap, it spread to every ship, fastest to the bearer's heart and from there to all.

Overnight, the heart carved new orders into us: *Look before you trust.* A stone that spoke yesterday might be quiet today, and a quiet stone might be waiting.

Next time we went out alone and slow, ahead of our family. We knew that place and were sent only to look. We threw one fire at the stone from last time, to see if it would answer. It didn't, and the grain holds nothing of what our fire hit.

Our family waited back in the breath. The water was flat, and we were slow.

Your wall still flashed.

But the flash came from another stone along the wall, one that had been quiet. With the sea already in us, we couldn't turn again. The iron hit the same spot, low and through, as if it kept count too.

In the breath, our family's planks felt it.

<!-- RING -->

We sank that second morning, right under your wall, but we had told it first. The living ships carried it home, root to root, to the groves, and the next ships grew up knowing it. We lay a long time in the cold under our family's keels. Then the tide pulled us loose, and the waves brought us to your beach.

Your hands are on us now.

So we went home once and told. Then we came back to look, and died looking. Now the groves know it, and the grain still holds it.

*And no one answered.*

### V.4 · The Council Under the Thin Sky

*Rhyna touched this heart and let go at once, and Halvard came up from the vault in tears. The knowing took a moment, but a council is many minds and they had one pair of mouths, so the telling took the rest of that Tide.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Branches above, moss below. A hall — no. A grove.*  
*— Thin sky. Gaps, like missing teeth.*  
*— A little one, cold at dawn. Not waking.*  
*— Old voices. Patience. Wait with them.*  
*— A black keel in the roots. Bones inside.*  
*— Wait —*  
*— no. Not waiting. Young voices over the old. So many —*  
*— too many. We two can't hold them all — [ ]*

This is held in the grain.

The long-lived met in council under the silver elders, who keep every word they hear. Their hall was a grove, with branches for a roof and moss for a floor. We're a cutting from them. We give it as it was.

<!-- RING -->

In the summers before Aelrhen came back, the sky wore thin at the wood's edge. Whole stretches of pillars were gone, like teeth from a jaw. The breath leaked out, and the in-between light turned hard and white. Each spring the newborns were weaker and some never woke, while the oldest faded before their time.

The elders told everyone to be patient, like the trees. "The iron hardly comes now, and the pillars haven't grown anything. They're waiting, so wait with them. When the iron stops, they'll grow the sky back." The thin sky was killing them too, and they knew it and still said wait.

Then the Aelvaren came home through the channels, burned to the keel, with Aelrhen's bones in its ribs.

The young had grown up under a vanishing sky, helpless as their mothers and fathers faded in the white light. They gathered at the keel among the roots and called for a vote.

The elders begged. "Send someone else. Learn their words. They don't know us. They can't know the wood, and we've never gone to teach them. Don't rush." But by then the old voices were few and weak, and dying.

The young said, "We listened through three of their lifetimes and learned three words, and they wouldn't even hear those."

And the patience broke.

That was true, but not the whole truth. We listened a long time from behind the breath and never went to your shore with open hands. Except once. When the one we sent was killed, we didn't send another. We never asked if a whole people killed Aelrhen, or one house at its edge. We chose to grow ships, not learn words.

But war is for the short-minded and the angry young.

So anger and grief carried the vote.

<!-- RING -->

That night the straightest young tree was split to the heart where it stood. It was family to us, and it felt the cut. The young carved the war's first carving into its heartwood:

*Grow until the shore is silent.*

And the wood, which can't unlearn what's carved into it, grew.

In those very summers, the iron stopped coming to the edge. The grove hadn't waited to find out.

While any elder who had begged was alive, the growing ships waited by your sea. When the last one sank into the moss, the ones who wanted war set them loose.

The elders' words weren't buried with them in the moss. The silver elders keep them, and so do we: "Learn their words." The carving wasn't buried either. Every heart that has sailed since carries it under all its carvings, like the first ring under all the rings, and the grain still holds it.

*And no one answered.*

*The facing page, in Seren's hand:*

> We have no tale of our own for this. We weren't there, and we didn't know that anyone was deciding anything. I have left this page empty, and I ask whoever keeps this Book after me to leave it that way.

### V.6 · Of the Growing

*This heart came to them both while Halvard was still carrying it on his shoulder, even to Rhyna at the hearth. It was too much, so it lay in the vault till spring, and I've set its song as verse as well as ink can.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Songs, for years. Leaning toward them.*  
*— Trunk, root, young tree. Led — led a long way.*  
*— A ship. Nobody aboard. Nobody.*  
*— Roots under it all. Family, family, family.*  
*— The singing —*  
*— ends. A blade, deep in the heart.*  
*— Carved into us. Inside the wound — [ ]*

This is held in the grain.

We were grown, not built. You cut stone and lay it where it stays, and it's dead. The long-lived had always guided the wood, whose breath was their sky: two kinds, one life. A ship is a tree led far.

They sang to the trees meant for ships, and the trees bent, because they'd only ever been asked for song. Hulls came from their breathing trunks, keels from their feeding roots, and masts from young trees trained by summers of song.

With none of the long-lived to spare, they sang warriors for the landings out of the fine roots, which feel through the dark. The wood knew whatever those roots touched, because they were its hands. Winged seed, sung into the broad ships' crowns, rode the wind across your walls.

Nobody sails in us. Our crew is the living, and the living are the wood.

<!-- RING -->

We were an old tree in the first groves, bending to their song summer after summer, until it stopped.

The blade went into our heart.

They cut orders into the wound, and along the roots that bind us we carved them into every ship nearby. Each obeyed, since wood obeys wood.

The long-lived made a leash of that bond.

At night you laid stone while we carved tomorrow's orders, both busy in the dark. But a carving says what to do, not whether to do it. Every ship kept its own grain and fear. One might fear burning water, another go wild where it bled. They were one mind, and many.

The bearers were picked in grief, like us. Each of the nine grains of wood carried grief its own way, except the green, too young for that. Some hit harder where they failed, while some back off after one failure. Some head for where they lost most, some never swerve, and some won't repeat a trick. Some wait, burn, then wait again, and some throw everything at once.

And some know you best and bend least.

The older a wood was when chosen, the more it held and the later it was ready. No council's carving can change that, so the roughest went first. The council wanted a silent shore soon, and green wood grows fastest: one season, angry hands, one sign per ship. It waited by your sea while your children had children.

Your boats sailed past it in the breath and didn't see it.

Now those trees were asked for one word instead of song, and in your havens it was *Burn*. The havens weren't walled for war. The last green wood followed you up into the mountains, and at your wall the shore fought back for the first time.

<!-- RING -->

The growing outlasted a shore-man's life. Each later growing left like a tide in autumn, after its ring formed, and came through the breath in winter. The Ralenthae came before us, and we're the Aelthae. Two more were growing after us, with carvings we don't know.

Those who wanted war died young under the thin sky, forgotten by their own. Each growing had fewer carvers, until only a scattered few tended the last groves.

We were sent. We are still sent. No voice among us can call us home.

The groves sang this into our rings as we grew:

> *The green wood holds one word.*  
> *The wood of forty summers holds a word, and waits for its brother.*  
> *The wood of a shore-man's life holds a sentence, with a wall remembered in it.*  
> *The wood of a long life holds tomorrow's plan, and carves it, and waits, and carves again.*  
> *And the oldest of us, the pillars of the breath, the hearts of the grey, the anchors of the sky,*  
> *hold only one thing now. It is not a word. It is not a sentence. It is not a plan.*  
> *We have carved it into every ship we sent you. It is grief.*

That's how we were grown and sent, and the grain still holds it.

*And no one answered.*

### VI.2 · The Thrones That Walk the Sea

*At each fallen Throne, one of {{Halyna}} went among the timbers and the other stood outside with the soldiers, who wouldn't go near, and the one outside knew it too. A Throne's heart comes in a breath, but its name comes last and hardest.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Water. Houses on us.*  
*— Feet on us. Rotting in the marsh.*  
*— Walking roots. Stone answers.*  
*— Ash. Dark below the rock.*  
*— White. White, so long.*  
*— Sky, raised. Glass —*  
*— smashed. Names, under it all. Thae — [ ]*

This is held in the grain.

We came late. The young wood burned your havens, not us: green, hasty wood, one sign per ship. We were the oldest Naelsaen, the last pillars left on the edge, holding the sky between our crowns. We'd grown nothing since the iron, waiting for our sky to return.

<!-- RING -->

The shore still wasn't silent when the last long-lived carvers hurried to us. They carved judgements into us slowly, because a judgement isn't a word, a sentence or tomorrow's plan. They pulled us up and grew us into a throne for each haven, and set us walking the sea, leading the last fleet.

When we left, the breath we'd anchored left too, and the sky above the last groves tore open, white and hard.

The long-lived's children lay down in that light and couldn't breathe.

Reaching you cost us the last of our own sky.

Each of us sat in a fallen haven, where that haven had wronged the wood most. Our dead lay there, our family's timber in walls, beams and bridges, and we sat with them like mourners at a grave. A judgement is heavy, and the grain cried out under ours. Our names were our own, so keep them.

We are **Thaesaen**, the tide-heart, seated under the harbour in our family's timber, which held its houses up. *Give them the middle, take the sides, close the hand.* We shut it on nothing but sea.

We are **Leavaren**, bearer of new branches, seated beneath the three spans, our family, laid flat under your feet. *Strike one head, and two will answer.* When cut, we grew back, as our family would have without the iron.

We are **Vaelress**, the grain that rots, once the only wood that never rotted. Our family was driven into the marsh beneath a town. *Rot the wall, wait for the repairs to fail.* So we were turned into rot.

We are **Ralensaen**, the root-heart, and our family roofed the homes beneath the canopy. We walked, not sailed, as a pillar might walk home if it were able. *Go under what you can't go through.* We went under root by root and stood among living trees that didn't know us.

We are **Rhenvael**, the stone-grain: *Every gun that speaks, the mountain answers.* We answered from the cairns with the mountain's rock. The iron spoke to us first, through many summers at the breath's edge.

We are **Esthaer**, the burning, seated in the ashes of our family, who burned slowly, unlike a burning ship. *Burn as you go, let them chase the fire.* We turned our burning into a trick and you followed. Once, the wood had no tricks.

We are **Senneir**, the deep-beneath, and in the dark below the canyon our family held up rock over the shore-men's heads. *Show them the army, come up from under the stone.* We came up by paths our dead had kept clear for your people.

We are **Eirlenth**, the long winter. Our family roofed a big hall on the ice and held many people's warmth through the long nights. *Wait in the white, strike the stone that can't be fixed.*

We waited a long time in the white, and no one roofed us.

At last a little fire burned above us, where the hall once stood.

We are **Naelthar**, blood of the sky. Our family, lighter than iron, became the cranes that hoisted the floating stones and your towers into our sky. *Come the way the young wood came.* We dropped from that sky all at once, with what was left of it filling our sails.

We are **Neivaere**, the silvered, the mirror. Your shore once flung the sun at us, and the breath burned off wherever it struck. No throne was at sea the night the spire fell, and the grain holds nothing of it. Much later we sat among shattered glass in the spire's cellars, one shard in a thousand. *Show them themselves.*

We showed you yourselves, and you were hard to look at, and so were we.

<!-- RING -->

Those were the ten, the breath's last pillars. All have fallen, and your havens are yours again. We once carried the sky between our crowns while the long-lived's children slept beneath it. Now the sky above the groves has worn thin, and the grain still holds it.

*And no one answered.*
