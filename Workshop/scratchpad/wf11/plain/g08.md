### V.2 · The Night of the Naming

*I held the lamp that night, and I told this at the hearth the next evening. The planks are still down in the lowest vault, and anyone who wants to can go and touch them.*

Hold the stone, Seren.

That first winter Kael said the grey sails had picked the wrong fortress, and this whole hearth laughed, me with them.

Two runners carried each carved plank home from the wrecks, salt water dripping off their arms. The soldiers' fires and the tide got the rest. The wall named them the tablets, but they were only planks. That first winter brought fifty-three, and all but one gave {{Halyna}} a word, and I gave each word its own page. The silent one, as finely carved as any, washed in weeks after the first.

The Second Tide's planks had two signs each, cut close like names over an old door. On the beach {{Halyna}} learned to hold both. Every reading had gaps, and I haven't filled them with guesses. Nobody asked the fifty-three for more until one night late in that Tide.

{{Halyna}} went down to the oldest room of all, under our feet, where the Rite was found. I followed with ink and lamps. Even in summer it's cold there, and the planks lay in long grey rows like a stony ploughed field. The night was short, so Rhyna began by the stair and Halvard by the far wall. I'd never seen them work apart, and even then they were only a vault's length apart.

Rhyna took the first plank, with its wave that said *shore*, and Halvard the silent one. Each was split rough along one long side. I held the lamp between them, its flame straight in the still air. When Rhyna lifted hers, Halvard lifted his, the way both ends of a net rise together, because it's all one net.

Their faces changed at the same moment.

Then Rhyna spoke, and it wasn't one word but a whole order.

<!-- W -->
*To shore. Go as a root goes to water, and wait for no branch.*

At the far end, holding the silent plank, Halvard said it with her word for word.

After that, every plank spoke. We'd laid them out in the order they came ashore, and now each gave us its own order. All night {{Halyna}} worked along the rows toward each other while I went between them, writing. By the middle rows my wrist ached, but I kept going. Here they are:

<!-- W: the italic carvings only -->
*Break the wall. First stone, only stone, so strike until it opens or the wood is ash.* The closed square is their sign for stone, the silent thing. Those ships hammered one wall until they sank, and we called them mad.

*Go until you are hit, and where the bark splits, turn away.* That one swerved off alone, and we said it was a coward.

*Thin wood goes before the bearer. Thin wood falls, and the bearer keeps up.* The front ships had orders to die first, and they died first.

*Strike where it flashed. Last flash, only flash.* They went for whatever gun fired last, and we thought it was hunger.

*Wait in the grey, as a seed waits out the frost, and when the stone is still, come.* They came once we'd stopped looking.

*Go home when wounded. One branch falls and all turn, each by its own water.* They scattered at the first loss while the wall cheered.

The soldiers named them by their first words: *To the shore. Break the wall. Wait in the grey.*

When {{Halyna}} met in the middle, grey light was coming down the stair, and one plank lay alone at the end. A mason from Emberhythe had brought it up with him after the Fall, one end burnt black. He didn't know what it was, but he couldn't bear to leave his city empty-handed. {{Halyna}} took it last, each with one hand on the wood, as the lamps grew pale.

It said *Burn*.

Nobody spoke, and for a long time I didn't write, until Halvard put it back alone at the end.

The young wood knew only one word for each ship, with one order inside it. Each ship kept saying that word at our wall until it drowned.

They hadn't picked anything.

They had been sent.

At full light I set the first plank beside the silent one, rough edge to rough edge, the edges I'd watched all night.

The broken grain met.

It fitted like two halves of a split log. I haven't asked {{Halyna}} what it means, and they haven't told me.

The three of us sat among the planks with the lamps still burning. Finally I said this, and {{Halyna}} didn't argue. "I think those ships were the wood's youngest, sent first because the young grow fastest. The worst of the wood isn't behind us. It's still growing."

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

### V.5 · The Tides, as the Wall Counted Them

*Kael the Counter wouldn't tell any Tide until it was over. In the sixth winter he asked me to polish all six the way I'd polished his first story, and I've polished these less.*

Give me the stone. No, I'll stand.

**I · The Hasty Tide**

I count. That's what I'm for.

Eight sails came the first morning, each alone, each hitting the first wall it reached until it sank. Their decks were empty, and root-men spilled off the transports like kindling. We called them treelings, as if that made them small. By noon their guns slowed, and "the fleet has spent its fire" became a saying.

The south-face breach fell outward, as the black chest's slates said, and nobody behind it died. I swear to that.

All winter my joke about the grey sails picking the wrong fortress went round with the cup. We laughed. God forgive us, we laughed.

Jory of Eldhythe, our east-wall whistler, laughed hardest, and he's first in the east cairns, our stone graves. A fire-ship got him, and that night our Captain cut a hand-wide strip off his half-cloak and tied it on a pike over the grave.

It was the first strip.

***Added in Seren's hand:*** *It wasn't the last.*

**II · The Second Tide**

This count was harder. They came in pairs, one to take our shot, one to land.

We hit one off the north point, and another a mile south turned, its grey-green sail going white like a face. It hadn't seen. It had felt.

Then the crown-hulls dropped winged seed wherever our guns went quiet. Sink a bearer and another takes the flag, and the fleet changes to match. I still tell young gunners: choose which heart you leave them.

Corlen of the harbour, oldest of the Twelve, wet a finger for the wind before every fight and said, "Wait, the sea hasn't finished." One seed-fall he held his guns to let a slow ship close, watching the water and nothing behind.

The seed came over the wall where the guns were quiet.

His were.

He died watching the water, with three of his crew, and that night the Captain cut the second strip.

Corlen did just what the Captain himself would have done. That's all a True Man is, and it's also what killed him.

Voss took Corlen's guns and was made a True Man, and next fight he wet a finger like Corlen. The Captain beside him looked up, not out to sea.

"Change your shot."

**III · The Tide of Remembering**

You all remember Wick, who ran everywhere, even here, and sat up front with the children, hugging his knees. He could run the wall-walk while you said the Title twice. All he wanted was to run a message to the Captain.

One night the Captain shifted the south gun forty yards. At dawn Wick was at the gun's old spot, watching the water, and Hesk's lamp still burned on the north wall.

A lone ship crept in and fired at the empty place, not the wall, to see if anything answered.

The shot smashed the wall-top and took Wick down with it.

Hale saw it, not me, and Wick had three words for him.

"They are looking."

His message reached the Captain, as he'd always wanted.

Hale ran it.

That night the Captain cut a strip for Wick, a boy killed by a gun that wasn't there.

We took them for beasts that learn from the stick, but they look before they trust. They learned our walls, but never once our Captain. After we began moving guns at night, the war got quieter, and I liked it less.

**IV · The Joined Tide**

All night drums went ship to ship in the grey. At dawn sails came down three roads, north, east and south. Three roads, one hour.

Lanner of the crags whistled for a wind, as crag-men do, until the Captain stood silent beside him.

"Loose."

An hour after sunrise Lanner's guns sank the bearer, but the fleet didn't falter. On the very hour its dead heart had carved, they swung into empty water on the east road, sails going pale a breath apart. They kept time for a dead mind, and only the bearer's own ships ran wild.

Counting along the wall before dark, I found Enno of the guild in the east-face breach and Della a mile north.

**V · The Tide that Learned Deceit**

Count five, and I don't like it. They came like the young wood, loud, alone, straight, and we laughed like that first winter. All our guns fired, and their hunters came for each. Fake straw ships took a different road every day, and root-men came where our guns slept.

But look closely, Pellow said. Straw rides high, leaves no wreck and never flinches at a shell. A fake retreat keeps neat spacing, and a green ship that goes pale isn't green.

Some of us didn't look, and they're in the east cairns. Pellow ground our far-glasses from his city's glass and held one up to everything, and you laughed at him, me too.

Pellow looked, and he's in there too.

Looking kept nobody alive. It kept the wall.

**VI · The Last Tide**

This winter a fleet that lost two bearers went home. It didn't scatter. It went.

In the Joined Tide {{Halyna}} told me:

"The fleet can't be called home,"

"because the wood obeys the wood."

I said we'd send it home, not knowing what I meant. You can't call it home, but you can send it home.

The wood can be afraid.

At winter's end ten great ships that weren't fleets settled into our havens, where they sit tonight. The wall calls them Mystarchs, the soldiers the Thrones.

After six winters of counting I know the grey sails' number, and I won't say it here.

But I'll count ours.

A count doesn't count until it has names, so tonight in the yard I called the True Men's roll, fourteen names for twelve voices. Corlen of the harbour got silence, and I let it hang a breath. Hesk of the mere called back from the north wall under his lit lamp, and Pellow of the spire got silence.

Hear the wall's dead, and keep them. They're ours. Jory, who whistled. Corlen, who waited for the sea. Wick, who ran everywhere. Enno and Della, {{Enrella}}, hand in hand. Pellow, who held his glass up to everything. For each, the Captain has cut a strip, like a poor man cutting the last loaf and keeping the thinnest slice.

At dusk I named the rest in the east cairns, twice each, among the stones. My voice shook at the end, and I won't pretend it didn't.

The first winter, his half-cloak reached his knees.

Tonight it can't reach his belt.

There he sits now, at the end of the bench.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*
