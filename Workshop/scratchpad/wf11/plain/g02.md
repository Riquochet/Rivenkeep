### I.2 · The Cry of the Bonded

*This is my own tale, the first I ever laid, and the hearth had heard only one before it. On the day I'm telling about, I was twelve years old and a child of the guild.*

I was scared to speak tonight, and {{Halyna}} put my hands on the stone.

<!-- HALYNA -->

"Hold the stone."

"Hold it, and it will hold you."

<!-- HALYNA END -->

So I'm holding it. At dusk on the day of the banner, the soldiers cheered while hundreds of us Stonewrights sat silent in the grass with our chisels.

I had no trade yet, so I carried the water. Over the mountains I'd walked and slept beside a guild boy who knotted nets as he went. I came into the yard alone. Soon after me, Enno and Della came in holding hands. They were fifteen, the only children's pair to get up the mountain whole, and they sat side by side and held on.

In twenty months we'd watched ten havens, our cities, die. We left each one with our chisels packed and never lifted a hand, because our faith said *Build, don't destroy. Protect, don't attack.* Mortar and chisels were all we had, and they weren't enough. Twelve generations of faith still hung on us like a wet cloak, and every elder had the same hard question. If walls built for peace must be held with blood, whose blood, whose hands?

Back then I only knew {{Halyna}} as two elders sitting by themselves under the broken inner gate. They didn't talk to anyone, because they didn't need to. Her family built that gate and his cut its stone, and they were husband and wife, one since birth.

Then the Captain came down off the wall. He wasn't tall, and he had coal-black fingers and half a cloak. I'd watched his banner go up without knowing what it meant for us. He looked from the elders to the broken gate, then asked one question, the only speech of his anyone here can repeat.

"Your walls are falling. Will you build, or will you watch and die?"

Nobody answered, and all I remember is the wind in the grass.

Then {{Halyna}} stood up.

They looked up at the torn cloak and bowed to it together. Their two chisels rose as one into the last light, and she began the cry, and he finished it.

<!-- HALYNA -->

"The mortar cries out from the ground. Rise, Stonewrights! Lay stone upon stone. Stand every wall to the very end."

"Rise! Rise! Rise!"

<!-- HALYNA END -->

It was one cry from two throats, and hers came first only because a cry has to start somewhere. Some tell it as call and answer, with silence in between.

There was no silence.

The guild rose like an arch when its wooden frame is knocked away, every stone leaning on the next. I stood with them, though I don't remember doing it. The soldiers took up our cry, and then the column did. Old stone doesn't shake, but I felt it shake under my feet.

That night, by this hearth's first fire in years, {{Idrenna}} made a deal with the Captain while I brought in the water. They spoke slowly, as old people do, and he didn't rush them.

The Stonewrights wouldn't carry weapons, and he didn't ask them to, but they'd build between volleys and mend walls under fire. The guild still puts it this way: *with the speed and precision that only Aetherbonded pairs could achieve*. {{Idrenna}} gave it to him in halves.

"Not the sword, but the stone."

"Not the killing, but the keeping."

The Captain bowed, and the guild has said it ever since.

Before the fire died, {{Orvenna}} cut the siege day into three, like a quarryman splitting stone along its crack.

"Every gun you haul is a row of stone we don't lay," they told him.

"So spend each gun the way a careful man spends silver."

*Then the masons sang, as they still do:*

> *Lay the stone while the sea is low.*  
> *Haul the guns while the guns are cold.*  
> *Hold the wall when the grey sails come,*  
> *and the wall will hold.*

If you see a Stonewright on the wall in a holding, look again. You'll see two.

Later, by hissing torches, we laid the war's first row across the broken inner gate, each stone over a joint below. {{Orvenna}} wanted the clearest voices for the havens' old gate-song, so Enno and Della stood up. They shared the lead, one singing up to the comma and one the rest.

*Then they sang it, with the guild joining in:*

> *Stone on stone, and the stone stays true.*  
> *Hand over hand, and the hand holds on.*  
> *Two at the gate when the grey sails gather.*  
> *Two at the gate, and the gate stands strong.*

I sang up to the comma. Then I sang the rest.

Enno and Della fell asleep against the new row as the torches burned down, still holding hands as they had on the mountain. I slept near the water buckets.

By morning the mortar had set.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

### I.3 · The Captain's Long Breath

*Hale the runner told this at seventeen, in his first weeks on the wall. He told it standing up, as fast as he runs, and I've written it down slower.*

Give me the stone, then. I'm no storyteller. I run.

Every haven had a runners' stone, and in our first week we swore the old oath on ours, a fallen block near the inner gate. "What I saw, I carry. I carry nothing else." Wick, our youngest and fastest, swore last, and he loves nothing more than carrying word to the Captain.

On the morning of the first attack, I was at Kael's guns, still our only guns. The Captain told Kael where to watch, what to load and when to hold. Then he went to the far end of the wall, and Kael put a hand on my shoulder.

"Only his own voice beside me can change that, lad, and nobody can stand in two places."

*Then the troops practised the three calls, as they still do:*

> *Move your spot, and watch new water.*  
> *Change your shot for the sea, the shore, the sky.*  
> *Hold, or fire, when the Captain is by.*

On the east wall, Jory of Eldhythe whistled a tune the old ship-masters hate. Merrick, the oldest of them, was coiling rope below and told him to stop.

"It's only a rhyme," Jory laughed.

Merrick didn't laugh.

The sails of my first holding came before mid-morning, too fast to count. In the laying, hours drag because stone is heavy. They drag worse in the hauling, but in a holding an hour goes by in a heartbeat.

Late in that holding, when the other ships were finished, Kael's lookout spotted a hull through one of Pellow's spyglasses. It had waited in the grey, as no ship had before, and now it came in late and alone.

The smoke hid it from the Captain, so Kael gave me three words: "Hull hangs back."

I ran it.

I run. Head down under the smoke, past the masons and the ropes, not speaking, not stopping, up into clear air above the gate.

The Captain is standing still.

What I remember first is everything on the wall moving except him. He stood facing the sea with one hand on the stone. He never turned round, but he heard every word.

He set off for Kael's guns without hurrying, with me at his shoulder, and the hour started to stretch. It didn't stop, only slowed, like a stone cart on a long hill, until a boy behind could count the spokes. I can't say it better.

Water poured off the late hull's bow. I counted the ripples on its grey sail the way you count rings in a sawn log, and each took a minute, I think. The smoke didn't move, the gulls hung, and he kept walking.

I had time to get scared, then time to feel ashamed of it, then time to stop.

He stopped beside Kael, who was quietly counting, and gave the call in his own voice.

"Hold."

The guns held while the masons below kept laying stone in pairs and never looked up. The late hull kept coming, until its timbers shone wet.

"Loose."

The hour let go of us slowly, like a held breath let out, and the guns fired. The hull broke apart right where the Captain wanted it, where it couldn't hurt anyone.

I don't know what the Captain was thinking. I know what he did.

That evening, walking the tide-line as runners do after a holding, I found marks cut into a plank in the shallows. I carried them to {{Halyna}} past the fires: "Marks on the wood." I ran it.

Coming back that night, I followed Hesk's lamp on the north wall, his mother's lamp, and knew the north was awake.

Along the wall the men say, "For the Captain, an hour lasts as long as a day." I think I started it, though Voss says it's older. They say the Rite gave it to him, and I did too, before I knew better. Kael fixed it, and I carry his words and nothing else.

"It's not the world that slows down, lad. It's the Captain who won't hurry. The moment he chose was the moment that came."

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*
