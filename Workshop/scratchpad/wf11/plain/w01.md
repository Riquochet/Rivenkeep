### II.2 · Of the Breath of the Wood

*A child asked if the Guest's people had houses. For many nights {{Halyna}} held his gift, one at the hearth and one asleep, and the sleeper woke knowing his people's whole home was in it, though we have no word for a home of air.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Old. Before anyone shaped stone.*  
*— Grey in the trees. Not fog. There's no word —*  
*— Breath. We two have it. Breath.*  
*— Sleeping trees breathe it out. We — no, they breathe it in.*  
*— A glow. Not day —*  
*— or dusk. In between. Soft.*  
*— Little ones, made for it.*  
*— Pale trees, singing. Their own name —*  
*— [ ]*

This is held in the grain.

We were old before your fathers first cut stone, older than all the lifetimes you can name. We who call ourselves [⟦legend_naelear_chip⟧]<!-- RIPENS: VI.3 --> and the wood lived in each other, with the breath between us.

::: note
The name the wood gives itself, not told yet
*Naelear*, those of the breath. Seren writes the word next to the sign, and the sign stays.
:::

It was what we breathed.

Our land ran flat and endless under the trees to the world's edge. The sleeping trees breathed it out, grey among the trunks. It fed them and us, so we tended it like a fire. The sun shone through it softly, neither day nor dusk. We were made for that light, never for the dark.

In the evening we sang to the pale young trees, and they sang back, grove after grove, until the deep wood was one song. The little ones listened from the roots and fell asleep in the singing, their voices dropping away one by one.

A fern from the deep wood withers on bare sand under the open sun. So do we.

In the years of the iron, we watched our children wither under it.

<!-- RING -->

Every long summer we led the wood's roots to water, and it grew where we took it. We and the wood were one life of two kinds, though it had its own will. We held ceremonies among the twisted branches and council under the silver elders, because nobody lies under a silver elder.

The wood gets sick without the breath, and the breath never returns without the wood. Without either, we fade slowly, youngest first and oldest last, since we live two and a half of your lifetimes.

When we speak, it's a storm in the treetops, all cracks and snaps, but all our carving is soft. Thunder is for now, softness for always. You don't hear a carving. You know it. Touch one and you'll know what the carver meant, whatever his language.

We didn't know there was another way to be believed.

<!-- RING -->

Our pillars stood in long rows where the breath met your sea, holding the breath up the way a mast holds a sail. We call them the Naelsaen, the mist-hearts. They anchored our sky, and you never found any other trees of ours.

Every summer we watched from among them as your havens rose and your lamps came on at dusk. Your boats came a little way in, then turned around.

Once a boat came nearer than any, close enough that we heard its oars stop. A man at the front stood up and stared at us.

We wove the breath thicker.

He saw only grey. His face went white, and the oars took him back.

We didn't know why.

Here is our fault, and the grain won't leave it out. We never came down to you. We hid gladly, so long and so well that no one ever saw us. We had listened to you since your first haven. We made sure you never heard us once.

This is our home: the flat wood under the breath, the in-between light, the young trees singing, the silver elders at council. And at the world's edge, the pillars, holding the breath up.

If any green is left of us, it remembers this, and the grain still holds it.

*And no one answered.*

### IV.2 · The Axe in the Grain

*Rhyna picked this heart off the beach, but {{Halyna}} had only its shape until it came whole this winter, to Halvard in the vault and to her among the children. Telling it took three nights, because it was a wound in them, not a story.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Rows. Tall, dark-barked. Holding the breath down.*  
*— Oars, so many. Then iron, into the bark.*  
*— One, and — [⟦legend_iv2_gap1⟧]*  
*— Blows. Every one counted.*  
*— Each one falls. Sky shows through. Pale —*  
*— then hard. Shoots, cut down again.*  
*— Soft letters, cut in the silent thing —*  
*— and it didn't [⟦legend_iv2_gap2⟧]*

::: note
Two of the wood's marks that {{Halyna}} could see but couldn't tell yet
The first is the pillars, many of them, with one memory cut through all of them to the heart: *What one pillar felt, all the rows felt, root to root.* The second is the mouth, drawn as a mirror image, and tied back across the rings to the plea cut on the stone: *It did not answer.*
:::

This is held in the grain.

We stood in long rows along the breath's edge, tall and dark-barked, holding it down the way roots hold a riverbank. Nobody thinks about breath until it's taken, so we never did. We were its living pillars, there for nothing else, and that was enough.

We kept watch on your shore and marked every boat that came near. Every scrape was a cut, every trespass a wound, and the wood never loses count.

<!-- RING -->

When your havens were at their height, the iron came in many-oared boats, and the shore-men waded out of the surf.

What one pillar felt, all the rows felt, root to root, out to where the wood ended.

They began with the pillar nearest your shore, which had marked more of your boats than any. From first light we counted every blow. By noon it leaned, but it still held the breath.

Then its roots let go.

The sky showed through where it had stood, like daylight through a torn sail. It was pale.

One fell, then another. Every summer the shore-men returned, until the first of them were old and sent their sons. By then the sky was pale and hard.

The stumps sent up straight black shoots, and within a few summers young pillars rose where the old ones fell. The iron returned for them. We grew more, and it came back.

Then it was pale and hard and wide.

The young ones of the long-lived turned their faces from the open sky and cried without knowing why.

We carved, as we always did when a thing had to be understood, but the shore-men never touched the wood to know it. They only cut it and shipped it away.

So we carved our plea into the silent thing, the stone your builders love, the stone of houses and walls. We thought any people would understand what was carved on the thing they loved. We used our own soft letters, adding any of your words we'd caught across the water, spelled as near their sound as we could. Then we left the stones by the pillars still standing.

Everything we make speaks, so we believed everything made must speak. That's what a child believes. Stone has no mind.

It did not answer.

The shore-men carried the stones off with the timber, and we didn't know where. In the end the stumps along the edge grew nothing. The breath thinned, and we held in our sap and waited.

The iron will get tired. When it stops, the wood will return.

<!-- RING -->

We who are telling you this never became a pillar. We were the last thin green shoot of a stump cut low where the first iron bit. The whole axe was in our rings. The iron left us alone, since we weren't thick enough to make a pole.

After forty summers the council of the long-lived came looking for hearts to carry their carvings. They didn't take the straightest or the oldest. They took us, because of the axe in our rings.

Strike one of our ships and we go to the blow, the way a tongue goes to a broken tooth. It's in the rings. We always go to the same place: the iron at the breath's edge, every summer, and the sky breaking through, and the grain still holds it.

*And no one answered.*

### IV.4 · The Gift Held an Hour

*When Brenn's words were read, a child asked what the Guest had given. This spring Rhyna held it an hour and it came into her slowly, like water over stone, but Halvard, at the mortar across the yard, began the telling without ever touching it.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Old. Dark. A stone — no. It was a tree.*  
*— Warm hands, for years. Leaves? No — words, like leaves.*  
*— Three. Three words inside him —*  
*— and nothing to tie them. We two have it.*  
*— A door. Our — no, the wood's family, in the posts. Sawn. They don't answer.*  
*— Kneeling. His best, set down. A foot.*  
*— Dirt. Laughing, through the dirt.*  
*— A neck, held. Two foreheads, one —*  
*— the blade — [ ]*

This is held in the grain.

We are the oldest wood, once a tree, when the whole world was green. We fell, and ages piled on us like leaves until we turned hard and dark. You took us for stone, but we aren't. Time forgot us, but we don't forget, and we give slowly, and only the truth. The long-lived have no gift more precious than a sliver of wood like us, and we were given once.

<!-- RING -->

When his task began he was named Aelrhen, bond-to-stone, and spent his long life listening over the breath to your fishermen and builders. He gathered your words one by one, like a child gathering leaves, until he had the right three. But he couldn't link them, because you link words with little words, and those don't carry over water. He had us, though, and anyone holding us until a tree's shadow moved the width of its trunk would know it all. We were the sentence.

He chose your farthest outpost because its doors and benches were our wood, which he took for respect. We knew they were our family from the edge, cut and polished. He touched the doorpost to greet them, but they didn't answer. They were dead.

He knelt before the master of the house and set us down. A guest brings his best, and we were his best. He spoke the ceremony's name in his own thundering language, *Ael'thar*, and then his three words in yours.

"Stop… Sky… Dying…"

A foot kicked us into the dirt by the wall, and we heard laughing through it.

He did not stop.

He rose, and with his right hand he cupped the host's neck from behind, where life climbs into thought. A baby bird could have slept in his hand. Their foreheads met, mind to mind, in silence.

His left hand laid the blade on the host's arm.

And the host pulled back. We don't know why, because fear leaves no mark on wood.

The arm moved, so the blade went deep, not a little way, and the host's cry brought his men. Aelrhen's blood ran down the step to us, bringing all of him, his years of listening and his final hour. That's how we know it as he did.

Held by the men, he knelt again where the ceremony began, felt the blows as a tree feels the axe, and never raised a hand. The cut meant for his own arm was never made. He'd never ask for blood he wouldn't give. He gave it all on that step. Their arms were never pressed together, shoulder to shoulder, so that hurting one would hurt both. He died with the ceremony half done.

We heard everything from the dirt, and no one lifted us. Many stone-like things have lain at a shore-man's feet, but only we could have talked to him.

And we were the one his foot found.

<!-- RING -->

In the morning a hand put us away, but didn't hold us. Then a bumpy day and night in the dark, and half a lifetime with no hand. Then leaves around us, cold cut stones beside us, and another long dark. Then two pairs of slow old arms for years, and fire, near and far. Then two pairs of small arms, climbing in the cold.

Then one pair, for a long way.

Nobody held us that long until you did. Here is the sentence he brought to the door:

*Stop cutting down the pillars at the edge of the grey, because they aren't just wood. The sky over us is their breath, and ours. When you cut them down, the sky opens up, and our children can't live in the open sun. We are here, behind the grey. We have always been here. We are not your enemies. Stop, because the sky is dying, and we are dying under it.*

That's all he came to say. We have carried it a long way, and the grain still holds it.

*And no one answered.*

### IV.6 · The Voyage of the Aelvaren

*Halvard lifted this heart in the vault while Rhyna, up on the wall, pushed both hands into the rain barrel and wouldn't say why. About the long-lived I'll say what the wood can't: They did not know it was ignorance.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Small. Grey. Light on the water. Proud of its name.*  
*— A hand on the boards, the whole way. Shallows. Waiting.*  
*— Heels on stones. Thrown in. Heavy —*  
*— and warm. Soaking in. All of him.*  
*— Grief. We two have words for this.*  
*— Fire. Hands on fire — no. Boards on fire.*  
*— Poles. Out to sea, away from —*  
*— Turning. Against the — [ ]*

This is held in the grain.

Other grain holds it too. The ship that brought this heart wasn't there, but it grew from what we were, so it knows.

We were the Aelvaren, the Bearer of the Binding, a small boat of the old wood, grey and light, and proud of our name. We carried Aelrhen through the thin breath to your shore, his hand on our boards all the way. He went up to the door, and all morning we waited in the shallows to take him home after the ceremony. The tide rose and fell under us, and he didn't come.

<!-- RING -->

Men from that house dragged him back by the heels and threw him in like a torn net. That morning his hand had rested lightly on us. Now his blood soaked in.

So it all came to us.

Then they set us on fire. Grieving, the old wood raged, burned higher than their flames, and screamed, because wood can.

They pushed us off with poles. The current carried us out to sea, away from home, with every board burning and his body cold inside us.

Then we turned.

Burning, we headed into the wind and against the current. Wood knows its way home, even charred and dying.

Twice, where the breath runs thin, shore-men's ships blocked us, heavy with iron and our family, cut and stacked in their holds. We rammed them burning, where the load sat heaviest. The iron had cut into us, and now we cut into the iron. Our family caught our fire, and both ships broke up and burned.

The grain keeps nothing of their crews.

Under the thinnest breath we reached the old channels, secret water among the roots where no shore-man's boat has gone, and ours knew the way. The fire slowly died as the breath closed over us. Only our keel and some ribs were left, with Aelrhen's bones inside.

<!-- RING -->

That night we came out onto the pale sand, small and black, and lay still under the silver elders. The council of the long-lived came down and knew us, because they'd sent us.

Many hands touched our keel at once and knew it all: the gift, the foot, the gentle hand on the neck, the blade, the blows. Wood can't hide anything.

They had sent peace and their best, and it was answered with murder and fire. They knew it was murder, and it was. But they didn't know why the host's arm pulled away. And they didn't ask.

All they saw was an act of war.

They laid our keel among the silver elders' roots, with Aelrhen's bones in its ribs.

And the roots drank.

From that grove the knowing spread root to root to every grove. Each young tree meant for a ship drank it with the rain. So every ship that came against your wall carried our grief, and the grain still holds it.

*And no one answered.*
