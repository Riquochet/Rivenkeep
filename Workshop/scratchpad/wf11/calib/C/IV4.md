### IV.4 · The Gift Held an Hour

*The third spring, in the Tide of Remembering.*

*On the night Brenn was read, a child asked what the Guest had given. This spring Rhyna held it an hour at the hearth. Halvard, at the* lodh *across the ward, never touched it, and began the telling. The last of it is about me. I have let it stand.*

<!-- READING · {{Halyna}}, in two inks, alternating line by line; whose is whose is not marked -->

*— Old. Dark. Older than — no. A tree, once. Green to the edge.*  
*— We two* keth *it. Hands. Long hands, carrying. Over water.*  
*— Leaves. No — words, like leaves. One, and one, and one. Three.*  
*— A door. Our — the wood's kin, in the posts. Sawn. Dead.*  
*— Kneeling. Set down. His best. A foot.*  
*— Dust. Laughing, through the dust.*  
*— A hand on a neck. Gentle. Two heads. Then —*  
*— drew back. Why? We two* keth *no why. Blood. Warm. All of him, coming.*

<!-- STORY -->

This is held in the grain.

Eldest wood are we. A tree we were when the world was green to its edges. We fell, and the ages lay down on us, leaf upon leaf, till time forgot us and we grew dark and hard. You took us for stone. We are not stone.

Wood that time has forgotten forgets not. Slowly it gives, and only what is so. Of all the gifts a child of the long-lived can give, a sliver of such wood is the dearest. We were given once.

<!-- RING -->

One of the long-lived took the name Aelrhen when he took his errand. Bond-to-stone, it means. A long life he listened across the breath to the shore-men, at their nets and at their walls. He gathered your words as a child gathers fallen leaves, one and one and one.

Three he had at the last, and he knew them for the right ones. To join them he knew no way. Your tongue joins its words with small words, and the small words make no sound across water.

But Aelrhen had us. Whoso held us while a tree's shadow moves the breadth of its own trunk would know the whole of it. We were the sentence.

He chose the outermost of your holds, for its doors and its benches were of our wood, and he took that for reverence. We knew it for what it was: our kin from the edge, sawn and polished. At the door he laid his hand on the post and greeted them. They answered not. They were dead.

On the stones before the master of that house he knelt, and set us down. Whoso comes under a roof brings his best. We were his best. The name of the rite he spoke in the thunder of his own tongue, *Ael'thar*, and after it his three words in yours.

"Stop… Sky… Dying…"

A foot struck us into the dust by the wall. The men of that house laughed.

He stopped not. He rose, and took the back of the host's neck in his right hand, there where life goes up into thought. A fledgling could have slept in that hand. The foreheads met, thought to thought, and he was silent.

With his left hand he set the blade to the host's arm.

And the host drew back. We know not why. Fear leaves no mark on wood.

The blade that should have gone a little way went deep, for the arm was moving. The host cried out, and his men came.

Over the stones of the step his blood ran to where we lay, and with it came all of him: his long listening, and his last hour. So we know it as he knew it.

He went down on his knees again, where the rite begins. He knew the blows as a tree knows the iron: one, and another. He lifted not his hand. The cut he would have made on his own arm was never made. Never met the two arms, shoulder to shoulder, that harm to the one should be harm to both.

He died there, with the rite half made.

Of all things that ever lay at a shore-man's feet and looked like stone, we were the one that could have spoken to him.

And we were the one his foot found.

<!-- RING -->

In the morning a hand took us out of the dust, and put us away, and held us not.

Then a jolting in the dark, a day and a night. Then the dark, half a life, and never a hand. Then leaves about us, and cut stones cold beside us, and another dark as long. Then old arms, two pairs, slow, a long while, and fire near and far. Then small arms, two pairs, going up, in the cold. Then one pair, a long way.

None held us so long, till you.

Here is the sentence, as it was brought to the door:

*Stop the felling of the pillars at the edge of the grey, for they are not timber. The sky over us is their breath, and ours, and when you cut them it comes open, and our children cannot live in the open sun. We are here, behind the grey, and we have been here always, and we are not your enemies. Stop, for the sky is dying, and we are dying under it.*

That is all he came to say. A long way have we carried it, and the grain holds it still.

*And no one answered.*
