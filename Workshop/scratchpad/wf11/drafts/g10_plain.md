### VI.3 · The Knowing of the Green Wood

*Told by Seren Two-Inks, who held the lamp. Tonight {{Halyna}} stood at the stone with me, each with a hand next to mine, and for a long time none of us spoke.*

<!-- TOKEN {LAST_THRONE_AT_HAVEN} -->

"Hold the stone, Seren."

The tenth Throne was {LAST_THRONE_AT_HAVEN}. All day it lay where it had crashed, a split black heap of timber taller than a harbour-house. Even dead, it frightened the soldiers off. Since spring {{Halyna}} had known nine hearts, all dead wood.

Toward evening the Captain came down himself, stood beside it a long while, hood up, and looked at {{Halyna}}. At dusk I followed them in with the lamp, water dripping above us. The heart was deep in the oldest grain, grey, hard and still. Next to it, in a crack, there was a sliver the length of a hand.

It was green.

I didn't trust my eyes, so I lowered the lamp to it. For six winters we'd looked for orders in the wrecks, names in the hearts and sails in the grey, but never for anything alive. This had grown inside a dead heart, like a seedling in a cracked wall. The groves used to sing, *The green wood holds one word.*

One hand had been enough for {{Halyna}} since the first winter, but this time both of them touched it at once. A drop fell into the flame and hissed.

Their two faces changed as one.

It wasn't an order or a wound. Rhyna spoke as if someone had put the word in her mouth and left:

"Home."

Halvard said she was right. But there was more, and it went past where we stop. Together they said:

<!-- W -->
*We remember our home too.*

Rhyna cried and Halvard didn't, because her tears were for both of them. I held the lamp till she stopped. Then they handed me something else, folded into the answer like a seed in a fruit: what the wood's people call themselves. *Naelear*, those of the breath. I said it three times before I wrote it, and it held. *Mystaeri* is only our shore word.

<!-- RIPENS: II.2's untold name -->

The great trees hadn't put out a leaf since the iron axes came. They'd been waiting for the sky to come back, so this was a seed.

Rhyna carried it up to the Keep on moss, Halvard steadying her elbow. A fisher-girl from the east keep ran over to ask where it was going.

"Home."

"And we two have to find it a boat."

We Stonewrights never built boats: the sea was something to wall out, not a road. But the havens' boatbuilders had carried their tools up the mountain, and next day their oldest, Tarnel's father Tarnard, laid the keel himself.

Harl of the burning shore carried the first plank out of the vault. All war long he'd said *Burn it* about every plank, but he set this one at Tarnard's feet without a word. Tarnard laid his palm on it.

"Wood that knew the sea knows the way home."

Every plank came from the vault. All but one had once spoken to us, and now they'd speak for us. For the front post Tarnard used the two whose broken edges I'd matched, the one that said *shore* and the silent one. {{Orvenna}} locked them like stone, Penna holding the last key while Ormund gave it one blow.

The broken grain met, and held.

That evening {{Halyna}} carved their mark across the join, one holding the chisel, one hammering. No mark of ours had ever gone into wood. I held the lamp as it burned a finger's width lower.

The wood took it.

Rhyna put her palms on the cut, with Halvard's hands over hers.

"It means in wood what our vow means in stone,"

"harm to one is harm to both."

Tarnard left the front tie for me. I used the knot Tarnel taught me on the Road, a new one every mile. His father studied it a long time and quietly said his son's saying:

"A net is only knots holding hands."

With every joint locked like stone, the boat rode a rough swell off the point all day and let in no water. It was small, and it was sound.

The whole hearth came down for the evening tide, old people on young arms. {{Halyna}} set the green sliver on its moss at the front, against the locked post. Beside it they put a Stonwryt coin of gold and black stone: trust, sealed in stone. It was the best we had, as the dark wood was the Guest's best. Then they added their two chisels, which caught the last light.

"Let them go with it,"

"because we two have cut our last mark."

Nobody spoke. {{Halyna}} knelt on the wet pebbles, and everyone knelt behind them. The soldiers put their pikes down. Kael the Counter, who stands to tell a story, knelt too, and last in line, hood up, so did the Captain. He's at the end of the bench now, knees still dark from the beach. {{Halyna}} spoke from the front:

"That's as far as one people can go alone: the gift and the kneeling."

"The rest needs the other."

A wave wet the keel. Harl got up and walked the boat into the tide. The fishermen's children pushed it off, with the girl who'd asked in front, waist-deep in the cold sea.

Far out on the falling tide, it turned into the wind, against the current, and went into the grey.

This time it carried a gift, not a grave.

Merrick stood at the head of the old ship-masters. For six winters they'd turned from the sea whenever a hull burned. Nothing frightens them more than a grey boat heading out against the wind.

They didn't look away.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And no one answered.*

After we three let go of the stone, the hall was silent a long time. The fire burned low, and nobody got up to feed it. I think we were leaving room for the voice of Ebba of Fenholm, who lies under the east cairns. Then, softly, a child at the front spoke: the girl who'd waded into the sea first, old Ebba's great-granddaughter.

"We remember."

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

*Then for the first time the hearth sang the way the wood sings. We call it* The Two Homes:
