### V.7 · The Mirror in the Grain

*The fourth summer, the fifth spring, the sixth winter.*

*Laid by me, Seren Two-Inks, on three nights, each the night its carving was known. On all three I held the lamp on the shingle, and it is this lamp.*

**Part I · Three Roads, One Hour**

Hold the stone, Seren.

All that short night the drums knocked in the grey, like mallets in a far quarry. Through it sat Enno and Della in their house of one door. Their mark lay under its first stone. Enno had set the chisel, and Della struck. One course high it stood, and higher it stood never.

Hand in hand had they come into that courtyard, where I came in alone.

At dawn the fleet came by three roads in one hour, and broke the wall east and north. There were more breaches than pairs. {{Orvenna}} the wall-masters, that never raise their voices, did without a word what no masters ere then had done.

They sent a pair apart.

Ormund gave Enno the east breach, and Penna gave Della the north. At the stair-head Enno called back, "It is like being one person," and Della threw him the rest, running: "with four hands and no excuses."

Seldom apart are the Bonded, and never far. A mile of wall is far.

In the east a shot took the wall, and Enno with it. I did not see it. Hale did.

At the north I was beside Della with the mortar. The last stone she had turned over, to cut their mark beneath it. She set the chisel herself, and struck, and her hand shook not.

Then the mallet stopped in the air.

She looked east. Then she bent again, and cut the last stroke, and laid the chisel by the mark, and lay down with her cheek against the stone.

I put my thumb to the mortar, as masons do. It was still soft.

She was gone.

Hale came at a run with the word from the east, and said it not. Never before had a runner come too late.

Theirs is the only mark in the annals that one hand finished. By the last light I looked long for the stroke Della cut alone.

The mark of {{Enrella}}, whole, on the underside of the north stone, as Seren looked upon it by the last of the light

Ye cannot tell.

At nightfall Stannard carried Enno the whole mile, and laid him by her. With their own four hands {{Orvenna}} set the north stone over them, mark down. {{Idrenna}}, that had sealed them, prayed over it in two halves:

"God, thou madest these two one ere either drew breath."

"One cradle they had of thee, and one stone of us; and thou wilt not part them."

Over the stone Ormund began the old jest, and Penna finished it. Then the Captain bound a strip on a pike over them, his first for Stonewrights. They were his company too. Kael stood by it and counted them.

"One," he said.

At the tide's turn the bearer's plank came in. Rhyna went down to it, and I with the lamp. On the wall above, where Enno had kneeled, Halvard mended the breach while the masons sang the song of the gate. No one took the first voice, before the comma or after it. On the second line the rest came in, as though the first had been sung.

Rhyna laid her hand on the grain, and went pale. Above, Halvard went pale in the same breath, trowel in hand. He came down, and they told it: the whole fleet's order, hull bound to hull.

<!-- W -->
*Three roads, one hour. Let the east and the north go as one. Let the south stand at the edge of the dread until the reach of the south has walked east; then let the south go.*

Long neither spake. I asked what ailed them, though I think I knew.

<!-- HALYNA -->

"It is how we work a wall."

"The one waiteth for the other's stroke, and need not be told."

<!-- HALYNA END -->

The carving named that Tide *Aelthae*. That night I knew its first half. *Ael* I had written once before, in *Ael'thar*. I have written the name, and I will not write what I thought.

On Halvard's trowel the mortar was drying.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

**Part II · What We Taught Them**

On a grey spring morning a fleet ran at the wall in the old way, each hull alone and loud and straight. Then laughed the whole wall, and every gun on it spake, Pellow's with the rest.

Pellow of the spire, the youngest, had two words for every man on the wall.

"Look close."

What followed, Hale saw, and not I. When his guns had spoken, Pellow lifted his glass, and a sail went pale in it. Then a second. Then a third.

"They are not green."

Hale ran it. Behind him Pellow turned his guns on the grey, unbidden, and stood on the parapet with his glass.

The Captain went along the wall, and at each True Man's shoulder gave the call: "Shift your ground." He hurried not, as lamplighters hurry not, lest the taper die.

Out of the grey came the hunters, to every gun that had spoken, and would not leave it. Each battery he had reached lived. One at a time is slow.

Pellow's was the farthest from him, and the nearest the grey. To his they came first, and it met them alone. The Captain quickened not his pace.

Pellow lay dead at the parapet.

His glass lay by his hand, and it was whole.

At the tide's turn {{Halyna}} laid hands on a plank of that fleet.

<!-- W -->
*Come as the young wood came: loud, alone and straight. Stop one breath short of the fire, and let the hunters wait upon the flashing.*

<!-- W: the italic line only -->
Under it was cut: *they strike first and far; they move the guns we find.*

"The wood cannot lie," I said, and then I saw it. The wood lieth not. It was told to make us believe a lie, and it obeyed.

<!-- HALYNA -->

"They learned it from us."

"We taught it them with every silent gun."

<!-- HALYNA END -->

They had no word for a lie. We gave them one. Small in that carving was cut their word for us: *Rhenear*, the stone-deaf.

At dusk we raised Pellow's cairn, and the Captain bound a strip over it. Then he held out the glass to Hale, and the battery with it.

"I am not a True Man," Hale said. "I run."

So they went to Ruan of the spire, his file-leader. He put the glass to his eye, and looked out past the cairn.

"Look close," he said.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

**Part III · The Plan That Carved Its Own Defeat**

In snow to the knee came a bearer, with a whole morrow carved in its heart. We sank it ere noon, and the tide brought the heart in.

It was the longest knowing {{Halyna}} ever held. Twice I filled the lamp ere they had told it all, and at the end was this:

<!-- W -->
*When the bearer falls, the second takes the standard; when the second falls, go home.*

Into the plan they had cut their own defeat.

<!-- HALYNA -->

"There is one hand in all that carving, and not a council's."

"And the hand is young."

<!-- HALYNA END -->

Yet it was the last two words that held us. To the young wood, *home* had been but whither hurt things run. Here it was cut in on purpose.

We three looked at one another across the lamp, as wives look up when men long in fever ask for their boots. That night, for the first time, we saw the enemy hope.

<!-- HALYNA -->

"They carve a morrow now, as we do."

"Then let us build a morrow they did not carve for."

<!-- HALYNA END -->

The lamp went out on the shingle, and I filled it not a third time.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

## BOOK SIX · THE BOOK OF THE HOMECOMINGS

*How the havens came home, and the war went home.*

### VI.1 · The Homecomings

*The year of the homecomings, at ten fires.*

*Laid by the True Men at each haven's fire, on the night its Throne fell; I set each down beside it. Kael laid the first words and the last at this hearth, when the tenth was home, and counted my leaves ere he would begin.*

Give me the stone. No, I will stand.

At winter's end we went down by the old roads over the mountains, the True Men and their troops before, the masons behind. All the way the snow-water ran beside us. In every haven a Throne sat among the fallen walls, and waited.

At this hearth each of us had told his haven. Oath-keeper am I, and I held all ten. Where a man was fallen, another spake for him.

Hear now the names of the havens, and hold them, for they are ours again.

**Eldhythe.**

*Voss, for Corlen.*

Here were the first walls of the havens laid, and here they stand again. The Tide-Heart sank into the sea it came from, and rose not. Who should bring the first boat home? Corlen should. He lieth under the east cairns. So the tiller came to mine hand.

I wet a finger and held it up, as he did, though the wind told me naught. I brought her in on the evening tide, slow. Then I waited, as he would, till the sea had finished.

On the quay my grandmother's house stood without a roof. Over the cold hearth the three grey stones were yet in the wall, and the lowest scarred where the ship-masters knocked their pipes. They are in my pack tonight. Long enough have they hung over a dead fire.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

**Tidesmeet.**

*Tamm of the three spans.*

Three heads it had, and three we struck in the one holding, and none grew back. Where the middle span had stood, the Bearer of Branches went down. On the bank I set out my three stones in their order, as by my blanket every night of the war. One span is a plank. Three is a city.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

**Fenholm.**

*Marl the fen-born.*

A town is but the last course on the old, we used to say. Tonight we said it again, and laughed. Like a log gone soft the Grain That Rots went down into the fen, and the rot went out of our mortar. My grandmother carried the last course of our house out of the fen on her back, and lived not to see it home. I laid her stone on the old course, as she bade me. Had ye asked her would it hold, she had said, "Perhaps."

It hath not sunk.

*At that fire, too, the hearth waited a breath for Ebba.*

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

**Holtward.**

*Aske of the canopy.*

Among the trees we found the Root-Heart standing still. It had put down roots, and would not rise. "These were trees once," I told my men. "Mind your hands." Not an axe was lifted. We left it standing. It had come home.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

**Carnhold.**

*Garvel of the smithies.*

Carnhold, that rang all day, was quiet. Stone into stone the Stone-Grain crumbled, and the cairns of the first quarrymen took it. In a cold smithy I struck the anvil once, to hear it. It rang true. The first thing I make on it shall not be a gun.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

**Emberhythe.**

*Harl of the burning shore.*

Our mason went down to the black crust, where the Burning went out like a forge left untended. On it he laid his charred plank, the plank that said *Burn*.

I did not burn it.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

**Sandreach.**

*Ulden of the galleries.*

An hour the canyon shook, when the Deep-Beneath burst up under the east keep and broke. When it was still, I went down to the lowest gallery, and laid mine ear to the ground. I heard naught. Tonight I sleep under mine own roof of stone.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

**Rimewatch.**

*Kael, for Hesk.*

Another man's city I tell not. This one I must.

All that morning the mere lay white, and in the white the Long Winter waited.

Hesk stood on the shore with his mother's lamp lit in his hand, in full day. Six winters he had lit it at dusk on our north wall, and the night-watch steered by it.

Ere noon he went out on the ice alone. From the shore I told his steps. At two hundred and twelve he stopped, where the great hall had stood. The ice spake under him, as it spake the night he carried that lamp out of Rimewatch.

That night it held.

At noon the white lifted, and the ice took the Long Winter down.

The ice took Hesk too.

At dusk we went out along the break with ropes, and found his lamp on the ice, burning. I swear to that.

He came home.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

***Added in Seren's hand:*** *At the Keep that night no lamp burned on the north wall. Then a child set one there, and lit it, and said, "Someone has to keep a light."*

**Highreach.**

*Lanner of the crags.*

The Blood of the Sky came down out of its cloud, and went not up again. Then I climbed the high crag, as I said I would, and whistled for the wind. The wind came. It brought no sails.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

**Glasspire.**

*Ruan, for Pellow.*

In the dark of the cellars the Silvered broke like a mirror, and every piece of it showed us our own lamps. Pellow should have seen it first. I have his glass. I looked close, and I looked for him.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

*Then Kael laid his hands on the stone again:*

Tonight in the ward I called the roll. Fourteen names, and eleven to answer. At Corlen's name the ward was quiet a breath, and at Pellow's. At Hesk's a lamp was lit on the north wall, and no answer came down.

Of no haven am I, for the Captain found me on a road. This year I sat at ten fires. At every one, when the answer came, it was this hearth I heard. Every haven is Rivenkeep. I swear to that.

Ten havens. I counted them twice, and it took not long.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*
