### V.3 · The Wreck That Went Home

*Deep in the third winter.*

*Halvard's hands were on this hull; Rhyna, with the salt-cuts open in her palms, touched it not, and knew it as he knew it. As it came to her, {{Halyna}} say, so went the hull's hurt among its kin.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Salt. Salt in every board. Cold, a long while.*  
*— A stone, flashing. Iron, low and through. The sea in.*  
*— Home when wounded. Home, slow. Telling its kin.*  
*— Look before —*  
*— you trust.*  
*— Again. The same water. Alone, and slow.*  
*— One fire, thrown. Where it fell — [ ]*  
*— A stone, flashing. Iron, low and —*

This is held in the grain.

We were a hull of the Ralenthae, the tide the roots taught. More than a word was cut in us: a memory, and a caution. Now are we broken wood, and the salt is in every board.

Twice in one cold season came we to your wall.

<!-- RING -->

The first time, at the grey of morning, our kin were about us, and the bearer at our head. A stone of your wall flashed. The green wood had learned that, and the roots had carried it home. Where a stone flasheth, there the iron is.

We turned toward the stone, and it spake again.

The iron went into us, low and through. In came the sea.

Then an older carving woke in us, one of the first the green wood was given: *Home when wounded.* Our stern we turned to your wall, and went back into the breath, low and slow. Your wall let us go, or did not see us go. We know not which.

As we went, we told our kin, for between wood and wood there need be no words. First went the flash out of us into the roots. Then went the place of it. Then went the place where the iron had found us. Our kin felt it in their own boards.

Last went the dread. The dread is but this: one place in the water is become a place where wood dieth. Slow as sap it went to every hull of that coming, quickest to the heart of the bearer, and from the heart to all.

In the night the heart carved the morrow into us: *Look before you trust.* A stone that spake yesterday may be silent today. A silent stone may be waiting.

On the next coming we went out before our kin, alone and slow. We knew that place. To look were we sent, and for naught else.

We threw one fire at the stone that had spoken, to see whether it would answer. Of what our fire found there, the grain hath naught. The stone answered not.

Behind us our kin stood off in the breath, and waited. Flat lay the water, and we were slow.

Your wall still flashed.

It flashed not from the stone that had spoken. It flashed from a stone along the wall, that had been silent.

With the sea in us already, we could not turn a second time. The iron found us where it had found us before, low and through, as though it too kept a count.

In the breath, our kin felt it in their own boards.

<!-- RING -->

On that second morning we went down in the lee of your wall. But ere we went down, we had told it. The hulls that lived bare it home, root to root, to the groves where the next hulls stood growing, and they grew knowing it.

Long we lay in the cold, and the keels of our kin went over us. Then the tide worked us loose, and the surf brought us to your shingle.

Your hands are on us now.

So we went home once, and told; we came back to look, and died looking. The groves have it now, and the grain holds it still.

*And no one answered.*

### V.4 · The Council Under the Thin Sky

*The fourth winter, in the Joined Tide.*

*Rhyna laid a hand on this heart, and took it away at once; Halvard came up from the vault with his face wet. The knowing took a moment, the telling the rest of that Tide, for a council is many minds, and they had one pair of mouths.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Boughs for a roof, moss under. A hall — no. A grove.*  
*— The sky thin. Gaps in it, like teeth gone.*  
*— A small one, cold at morning. Not waking.*  
*— Old voices. Wait. Wait with them.*  
*— A keel, black, in the roots. Bones in the ribs.*  
*— Wait —*  
*— no. Not wait. Young voices, over the old. Many —*  
*— too many. We two cannot hold them all — [ ]*

This is held in the grain.

Under the silver elders sat the council of the long-lived, in a hall that was a grove. No roof it had but the boughs, nor floor but the moss. The silver elders kept all that was said beneath them, as they keep all they hear.

A cutting of those elders are we. As it was, we give it.

<!-- RING -->

In the summers ere Aelrhen came home, the sky was thin along the edge of the wood. Gone were the pillars at the edge, in long gaps, like teeth from a jaw. Through the gaps the breath ran out. The between-light went hard and white.

Frailer every spring came the newborns of the long-lived. Some did not wake. The eldest dwindled, their years not full, their strength running out like water from a cracked jar.

The elders counselled the patience of the trees. "The iron cometh seldom now," said they. "The pillars have put up naught. They are waiting. Wait with them, and when the iron stoppeth, they will grow the sky back."

Of the very wound for which they counselled patience, the elders were dying. They knew it, and counselled patience still.

Then came the Aelvaren home by the channels, burned to the keel, with the bones of Aelrhen in its ribs.

The young of the long-lived had grown up under a vanishing sky. Their mothers and fathers they had watched fade in the white light, and could not help them. Into the grove they came, where the keel lay among the roots. Over it they put the question.

The elders rose and pleaded. "Send another. Learn their words. They know us not. The wood they cannot know, and never have we gone down to teach them. Make no haste."

But few were the old voices by then, and faint: the voices of the dying.

The young answered: "We listened three of their lives and learned three words, and they would not hear even those."

And the patience broke.

Their answer was true, and not the whole of the truth. We that heard it know the difference. More could we have learned, for we live long. From behind the breath we listened, and never once went down to the shore with our hands open. Save once. When the one that went was killed, we sent not a second.

Whether a whole people had killed Aelrhen, or one house at its edge, we asked not. Nor asked we who else dwelt along that shore, in the ten havens, that had never heard our name. We chose to grow hulls, and not to learn words.

But war is for the short-minded and the angry young.

So the vote was carried, by the weight of anger and grief.

<!-- RING -->

That same night the straightest young tree was laid open to the heart, where it stood. It was our kin, and it felt the blade. Into its heartwood the young cut the first carving of the war:

*Grow until the shore is silent.*

And the wood, that cannot unlearn what is carved in it, grew.

In those same summers the iron stopped coming to the edge. The grove had not waited to see it.

While one elder that had pleaded yet lived, the growing lay ready at the edge of your sea, and went not. When the last went down into the moss, they that had cried for war cut it loose.

The elders' words went not into the moss with them. The silver elders keep them, as they keep all they hear, and so do we: "Learn their words."

Nor went the carving down. All the hearts that have sailed since bear it. Under all their carvings it lieth, as the first ring under all the rings; and the grain holds it still.

*And no one answered.*

### V.6 · Of the Growing

*The fourth spring.*

*Halvard bare this heart up on his shoulder, and ere he set it down it came to them both, though Rhyna sat at the hearth. Too great it was, and lay in the vault till spring; its song I set as verse, as near as ink will go.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Singing. A long while, singing. Bending to it.*  
*— Trunk, and root, and sapling. Led — led far.*  
*— A hull. No one in it. No one at all.*  
*— Roots under all, binding. Kin, and kin, and kin.*  
*— The singing —*  
*— stops. A blade, in the heart.*  
*— Cut into us. In the cut — [ ]*

This is held in the grain.

We were grown, not built. Stone ye cut and lay. Where ye laid it, there it lieth, and it is dead.

From the first the long-lived guided the wood, and the wood breathed out the sky they lived by: one life, in two kinds. A ship is but a tree led far.

To the trees that were to be hulls the long-lived sang. The trees bent to the singing, for naught had ever been asked of them but song. Hulls they sang from the breathing trunks, keels from the roots that fed them, masts from saplings trained by many summers of song in the wind.

Of the fine roots, that find their way in the dark, they sang the warriors of the landings, for none of the long-lived could be spared. What those roots touched, the wood knew. They were the wood's hands. In the crowns of the broad hulls they sang the winged seed, that went up on the wind over your walls.

No one saileth in us. We are crewed by the living, and the living are the wood.

<!-- RING -->

A tree we were, and old, in the first groves. Many summers they sang to us, and we bent. Then the singing ceased.

The blade went into our heart.

In the wound they cut the orders. Through the bond of roots we carved them on into every hull about us. Every hull obeyed, for the wood of the hull and the wood of the command were one. The wood obeyeth the wood.

The long-lived made that bond into a leash.

In the night, while ye laid your stone, we carved the morrow. Ye and we were both at work in the dark.

Yet the carving said what, and not whether. Each hull kept its own grain, and its own dread. One might balk at burning water, another turn savage where it bled: one mind, and many.

In grief were the bearers chosen, and we were chosen so. The grief is in our grain. Nine grains there were, and the first was too young for any temper.

Of the rest, some come again heavier where they failed, and some turn from the first failure. Some go where they lost the most, and some never turn aside. Some play no trick twice. Some wait, and burn, and wait again. Some throw all they have at once.

And some know you best, and bend least.

As many years as a wood had when it was chosen, so much could it hold, and the older it was, the later it came ready. Against that law no council can carve. One season, one sign. Forty summers, a phrase. A shore-man's life, a sentence, and in the sentence a memory.

The eldest pillars hold a judgement.

So the crudest came first. The council would have the shore silent soon. Green wood is quickest grown: one season, angry hands, one sign to a hull. At the edge of your sea it lay, while your children had children.

Your boats passed it in the breath, and saw naught.

Naught but song had ever been asked of those trees. Now one word was asked of them, and in your havens the word was *Burn*.

The havens had no walls for war. The last of that green wood followed you into the mountains. There, at your wall, for the first time, someone struck back.

<!-- RING -->

Through many summers the growing went on, longer than a shore-man's life. Each growing after the first went out as tides go, at the leaf-fall, when its ring was laid, and came out of the breath with the winter. Ralenthae was the tide the roots taught. Aelthae is ours. Two more were growing behind us, and what is cut in them, we know not.

They that cried for war died ere their years, under the thin sky, and their own forgat them. At every growing the carvers were fewer, till none were left but scattered souls, tending the last groves.

We were sent. We are still sent. No voice among us can call us home.

Over us the groves sang this as we grew, and it is in our rings:

> *The green wood holds one word.*  
> *The wood of forty summers holds a word, and waits for its brother.*  
> *The wood of a shore-man's life holds a sentence, and a wall remembered in it.*  
> *The wood of a long life holds the morrow, and carves it, and waits, and carves again.*  
> *And the eldest of us, the pillars of the breath, the hearts of the grey, the anchors of the sky,*  
> *hold one thing only now, and it is not a word, and it is not a sentence, and it is not a morrow,*  
> *and we have carved it into every hull we sent you, and it is grief.*

So we were grown, and so we were sent; and the grain holds it still.

*And no one answered.*

### VI.2 · The Thrones That Walk the Sea

*Through the sixth year, haven by haven.*

*At each fallen Throne one of {{Halyna}} went among the timbers, and the other stood at the edge with the soldiers, that would not go nigh; and the one outside knew it as the one within. A Throne's heart is known in a breath, its name last and hardest.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Water. Houses, heavy on us.*  
*— Feet, walking on us. Rot, in the fen.*  
*— Roots, walking. Stone, answering.*  
*— Ash. Dark, under the rock.*  
*— White. A long while in the white.*  
*— Sky, lifted. Glass —*  
*— broken. Names, under all. Thae — [ ]*

This is held in the grain.

Late came we. It was not we that burned your havens. That was the young wood, green and hasty, one sign to a hull.

The eldest of the Naelsaen were we, the last pillars standing at the edge, with the sky held up between our crowns.

Since the iron we had put up no growth. We were waiting for the sky to come again.

<!-- RING -->

The shore was not yet silent. Then came to us the last of the long-lived that could carve, and they could not wait. Slowly they cut judgements into us, for a judgement is not a word, nor a sentence, nor a morrow.

Out of the earth they drew us, and grew us into thrones, one for each of your havens. On the sea they set us walking, and the last of the growing in our keeping.

When we went, the breath went with us, for we had been its anchors. Over the last groves the sky came open, hard and white.

The children of the long-lived lay down in the light, and could not breathe.

To come to you, we spent the last of our own sky.

Each of us took its seat in a fallen haven, there where that haven had most wronged the wood. Our dead were there: the timber of our kin, in walls and beams and bridges. We sat among them, as mourners sit by a grave.

Heavy to carry is a judgement, and under it the grain cried out.

Our own names we had. Hold them, for they were ours.

We are **Thaesaen**, the tide-heart. Beneath the harbour we sat, among the timber of our kin, that held up its houses. Our judgement was *Give them the middle; take the sides; close the hand.* We closed it, and it held but the sea.

We are **Leavaren**, bearer of new branches. The three spans were our kin, laid flat, and your feet walked on them. Under them we sat. *Strike one head; two will answer.* Where we were cut we grew again, as our kin would have grown, had the iron let them.

We are **Vaelress**, the grain that rots. The one wood in the world that rotted not were we. Into the fen our kin were driven, and a town stood on them. *Rot the wall; wait for the mending to fail.* So we were made into rot.

We are **Ralensaen**, root-heart. Our kin were the roofs of the houses under the canopy. We came not as hulls come, but walked, as a pillar would walk home, if it could. *Go under what you cannot go through.* Root by root we went under, and stood among the living trees, and they knew us not.

We are **Rhenvael**, stone-grain. *Every gun that speaks, the mountain answers.* With the mountain's own stone we answered, from among the cairns. The iron had spoken to us first, summer after summer, at the edge of the breath.

We are **Esthaer**, the burning, and ash was our seat: the ash of our kin, that had burned slow, and not as hulls burn. *Burn as you go; let them chase the fire.* A trick we made of our own burning, and ye followed. Yet once there were no tricks in the wood.

We are **Senneir**, the deep-beneath. In the dark under the canyon our kin held up the rock over the heads of the shore-men. *Show them the host; come up beneath the stone.* Up beneath it we came, by the roads our dead had held open for your people.

We are **Eirlenth**, the long winter. Our kin roofed a great hall on the ice. All the long nights the warmth of many came up into them. They held it. Our judgement was *Wait in the white; strike the stone that cannot mend.*

We waited in the white a long while, and no one roofed us.

At the last a small fire stood over us, where the hall had been.

We are **Naelthar**, blood of the sky. Our kin, lighter than iron, were the cranes that lifted the floating stones, and they raised your towers into our own sky. *Come as the young wood came.* Down out of that sky we came all at once, with the last of it in our sails.

We are **Neivaere**, the silvered, the mirror. Long ere we came, the sun was thrown at us from your shore. Where it fell, the breath burned away. No throne sailed the night the spire fell. Of that night the grain hath naught.

Long after came we, and sat in the cellars of the spire, among the broken glass, one shard among a thousand. Our judgement was *Show them themselves.*

We showed you yourselves, and ye were hard to look on; and so were we.

<!-- RING -->

These were the ten, the last pillars of the breath. All are fallen now, and your havens are your own again.

Once we held the sky up between our crowns, and under it the children of the long-lived slept. The sky over the groves is thin, and the grain holds it still.

*And no one answered.*
