## BOOK THREE · THE BOOK OF THE HAVENS

*What we had, and how we walked away from it.*

### III.1 · Of Their Raising

*The first autumn of the siege.*

*Laid by the True Men, each for his own haven, as Kael the Counter called them, and every man stood to answer, as under the banner. {{Halyna}} had found the rolls in the chest I carried. What was in them and in no man's memory, I read out.*

Give me the stone. No, I will stand. At a roll men stand.

At first light I went up the east tower. From there that coast may be seen yet, as far as the haze. Not one hearth on it sendeth up smoke.

Hear now the names of the havens, and hold them, for they are ours.

Builders were our fathers, and no seafarers. They feared the open water, and built on the sheltered shores, where the mountains go down in long falls to the sea. Over the mountains they paved roads like veins. Ten havens they raised, the harbour-cities of the songs, and every wall of them was Stonewright work.

*Then I read from the rolls.*

Each haven's roll beginneth with the one line. *This day was the first course laid, and a staff cut for the work.* Small is the clerks' hand, and patient, as if they were loath to waste the leaf. Ten havens, and ten staffs.

*Then Kael spake again:*

Another man's city I tell not. I call the haven, and its own man shall answer, and swear what he will do when it is come home. Oath-keeper am I. I hold them.

**Eldhythe, the Eldest Haven.**

Corlen of the harbour. Fifty years the evening tide brought the boats home past my quay, slow, to the warmest harbour-houses on the coast. The first boat of ours to come home, I will steer it in.

*Then Corlen sat, eldest of the Twelve, and wet a finger and held it up, as at every holding on the wall. There is no wind in this hall. The young men at the back laughed. To them he said what he saith to his gunners when they would fire: "Wait. The sea hath not finished."*

**Tidesmeet, where the river meeteth the tide.**

Tamm of the three spans. On both banks it stood, and three great spans bound it across the water.

*Tamm set three stones out by the fire, in their order, as by his blanket every night.*

One span is a plank. Three is a city. On the bank where they stood, I will set them out.

**Fenholm, the town that was always sinking.**

Marl the fen-born. Our town stood on pilings, and sank a hand's breadth a lifetime, and every generation laid a new course on the old. A fen-man that cannot laugh at that had best go and live on a hill.

Old Ebba, my grandmother, laid the last course of our house. A stone of it is in my pack, and I would carry her too, if she would let me. She is there by the fire, nodding. I am to lay her stone on the old course.

**Holtward, the keep under the canopy.**

Aske of the canopy. It lay so close among the trees that a traveller might pass under it and see no wall. No bough would we cut, and every autumn we swept the wall-walk of its leaves by hand.

*By the hearth lay the night's wreck-wood. On every plank of it Aske laid his hand, ere it went on the fire.*

These were trees once. No axe shall come into Holtward while I live.

**Carnhold, the stronghold of the cairns.**

Garvel of the smithies. We hewed it among the cairns of the first quarrymen, and a man could tell by the ring which of its forges was at work. Every gun on this wall I listen to, as a mother to a cough. Guns that ring true speak true. But at an anvil of Carnhold I will make a thing that is not a gun, and hear it ring.

**Emberhythe, the forge on the burning shore.**

Harl of the burning shore. It stood where the mountain smoketh into the sea, and the ground was so warm that our children ran barefoot in the snow. My shore I will have again, and the grey I will burn, and the sea too, if it would take fire.

**Sandreach, the citadel carved in the canyon wall.**

Ulden of the galleries. Sandreach had no harbour. We called it a haven all the same, for it sheltered us. Dug it was, not built, gallery below gallery into the red rock. Every night till the havens fell I slept with the whole mountain for my roof, and mine ear to the floor, and heard it hold. Under that roof I will sleep again.

**Rimewatch, the watch on the frozen mere.**

Hesk of the mere. All winter its great hall stood out on the ice, and the town feasted under its roof, and was warm. Every dusk a taper went from window to window, and the fishers out on the ice steered home by the lamps. My mother kept those lamps. One of them is lit on the north wall tonight. On the ice I will light it again.

**Highreach, the tower of the floating stones.**

Lanner of the crags. So high were its crags that every course went up on cranes, and hung on the wind as hawks hang over a field. A wind I can smell a day off. A boy of those crags can whistle one up ere he can walk. I will climb the highest again, and whistle for the wind.

**And Glasspire, the last and proudest.**

Pellow of the spire. Seven generations raised it, and it caught the sun like a blade of ice. At the top stood the great glass, that threw the sun a day's sail out into the grey. My father ground it. What I carried of it up the mountain in straw, I have ground into our far-glasses. When Glasspire is come home, mine shall be the first eye on it.

*Then Pellow, youngest of the Twelve, held his glass up to the fire, as he holdeth it up to everything, his supper among the rest. To the children at the front he said, "Look close."*

Ten cities, ten harbours, ten kindreds that called themselves one nation.

Twelve of us answered under the banner. Ten have sworn tonight. Stannard of the ridge sware naught, for the ridge was never a haven. He knocked once on the hearthstone with his knuckle. No stone will he trust till he have knocked on it.

Ten havens have I told tonight, and ten oaths I hold. I have no haven.

The Captain found me on a road.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

*Old Ebba of Fenholm began it, a breath before the rest.*

### III.2 · Of Their Fall

*The second winter, in the Second Tide.*

*Laid by the True Men again, each for the fall of his own city, on a night of hard frost when the guns were cooling and the mortar was not yet set. Kael the Counter called them, and I spake for the guild.*

Give me the stone. No, I will stand. This is no tale to sit for.

Their names ye held in the autumn. Hold them now through the falling, and look not away, for we looked not away in the telling.

In the last spring of the peace, twenty months ere the banner, the swallows nested in the arches of the gates. No man of the havens ever broke a nest. The havens had no fleet of war. Their walls were built for the high days and the weather. Their gates were wide, and stood open at night.

Out of the grey, without warning, came the dark sails. No envoy came that any man could understand, but a knocking out of the grey, like thunder, on and on. The soldiers on this wall call it the drums. We looked for a captain on those ships, and a crew, and one man at one rope. No one stood on the decks. I swear to that.

So we called it the ghost fleet, and its senders the Mystaeri, after the mist. A man must call his death by some name.

Plainer were those sails than the sails that come against us now. Each came alone, and needed no more. No haven sent to another for help, for none had any help to send.

*Then the stone went down the bench from hand to hand, Corlen first.*

**Eldhythe.**

On the first morning of that spring the dark sails came in at the harbour mouth, on the flood. From dawn to dusk the harbour burned: the boats at their moorings first, then the harbour-houses, where the ship-masters of all the coast had sat at the fire.

*Then he waited, as at his guns, and the hall waited with him.*

At evening the wind laid the smoke across the city, so that the city saw not its harbour go. Three months it held, and on the last evening the tide came in over the ash, slow, as it always came, and the sea had not finished.

*Corlen gave the stone on to Tamm. It was the last time he held it.*

**Tidesmeet.**

Three days it took. From the high road we watched its three spans go down into the river, and heard them go. The upper went, and then the lower. The middle stood longest, and we waited for it, and it went. That night I climbed down in the dark, and took a stone of each.

**Fenholm.**

In one night it went under, as if the fen had only been waiting. A town is but the last course on the old, we used to say, and laugh. That night no one laughed. My grandmother carried her course-stone out on her own back, and would not give it me till the fen had closed over the last course.

**Holtward.**

It burned in its trees, and the leaves came down burning on the wall-walk we had swept. I laid my hand on the last tree standing. Its bark was as warm as a living hand.

**Carnhold.**

It burned in its smithies. In the street of the smiths I heard the hammers stop, one forge and then the next. The last anvil rang a while alone.

Then it did not ring.

**Emberhythe.**

Five months it held on its burning shore, and the column wintered with us, warm on the bare ground. When it fell, a mason of ours carried a charred plank out in his pack. I told him to burn it.

**Sandreach.**

Gallery by gallery it was taken from us. Each night I lay with mine ear to the floor, and heard the gallery above me go ere any runner came. At the last we came out in the dark at the canyon's foot. Every night since, I have slept on the open ground, with mine ear to it.

**Rimewatch.**

On the first ice it was taken. The great hall burned on the mere with its lamps yet lit. Window by window the fire went, as my mother's taper went at dusk. It burned as it had always shone.

Into the smoke I went, to her window, and took down her lamp. It was lit.

Then I turned for the shore. The ice was new, a night old. All the way across the mere it spake under me, far off and then near. Once the wind took the flame down to the wick, and I put my hand about it till it stood again. Behind me the roof went in. Before me there was no light but the one in my hand.

The ice held.

I carried the lamp out lit. Every dusk since, I have lit it on the north wall, and the night-watch steer by it.

Someone has to keep a light.

**Highreach.**

Before the sails cometh the wind. A whole day ahead of them I smelled the grey on it, and there was naught to do with that day but go. Down the crag-stairs we came in the dark, with the children on our backs. Behind us the cranes stood burning on the crags.

**Glasspire.**

At the first snows it went down in one night into its own cellars, spire and glass together. The dark beneath it lieth open yet. Some that fled say a shadow stood off in the fog that night, greater than any hull. I was on the last stair of the spire, with the glass coming down about me in pieces, and the snow with it.

I looked close. I saw nothing.

*Then Pellow sat down with his glass in his lap, and held it not up. The stone he gave to me.*

On the last day of every haven, when a mason's hands could do no more, the guild went out by the landward gate. Chisels they carried in their packs, and naught beside. Last went the masters. They had cleaned their trowels, as after every day's work, and wrapped them in sacking against the road.

The Stonewrights did not fight. They simply walked; and I was among them.

The staffs they carried not. The masters leaned each haven's staff against its falling wall, as women bank the fire on their last night in their mothers' house, as every night. A staff is cut for its work, and leaveth it not.

Ten staffs there were, and not one came up the mountain.

*Kael took it back, and told the Road.*

When Glasspire was gone, the column turned up the mountain. Haven by haven it had gathered, till behind it no haven was left. A month were we in the passes.

On the night of the pass, the coldest of the road, many of the children died. Some of the old died with them. Their cloaks they had given the children.

In the morning the snow fell yet. We rose out of it, as many as could rise, and walked.

At the foot of the passes I counted us, and I counted us at the top. I will not tell you the difference.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*
