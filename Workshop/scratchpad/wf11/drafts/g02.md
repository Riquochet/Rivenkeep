### I.2 · The Cry of the Bonded

*The first winter, after the first assault.*

*Mine own, and the first tale that ever I laid; this hearth had heard but one before it. On the day I tell of I was twelve winters old, and a child of the guild.*

Tonight I was afraid to speak, and {{Halyna}} laid my two hands on the stone.

<!-- HALYNA -->

"Hold the stone."

"Hold it, and it will hold thee."

<!-- HALYNA END -->

So I hold it.

At dusk on the day of the banner the guild sat in the grass of the old ward, some hundreds of us, with our chisels in our laps. Thin as ash lay the last light on the snow. On the wall the soldiers shouted for the banner. In the grass we said naught.

There sat I, that had no craft yet but the carrying of water. Over the high passes I had walked beside a boy of the guild, that knotted a net as he went. At night I slept at his side.

I came into that courtyard alone.

A little after me came two children with their hands joined, Enno and Della, of fifteen winters. Of the children's pairs, none came up the mountain whole save those two. They sat down together, and held on.

Ten havens had we watched die in twenty months. From each we walked away with our chisels in our packs. No hand had we lifted, for our creed forbade it: *Build, do not destroy. Protect, do not attack.* Mortar and chisel were our only arms, and they had not been enough.

The faith of twelve generations sat on us yet, like a wet cloak. Every elder there turned over the same hard thing, unspoken: that walls raised for peace must at the last be held with blood. And if that were so, whose blood? Whose hands?

{{Halyna}} I knew not then, save as two elders that sat apart under the broken inner gate, and spake to no one, for they had no need. Her line had laid that gate. His had cut its stone out of the mountain. Husband and wife they were, and one since the cradle.

Then came the Captain down from the wall. I had seen the cloak go up, and wist not what it was to us. Not tall was he. The coal-black was yet on his fingers, and the half of a cloak on his back. He looked at the elders, and past them at the broken gate, and asked one question. It is the only speech of his that any at this hearth can say over:

"Your walls are falling. Will you build, or will you watch and die?"

No one answered him. I remember the wind in the grass.

Then {{Halyna}} stood up.

To the torn cloak they looked up, and bowed to it in the same breath. Up went their chisels as one.

Their two chisels took the last of the light.

She began the cry, and he ended it:

<!-- HALYNA -->

"The mortar cries out from the ground. Rise, Stonewrights! Lay stone upon stone. Stand every wall to the very end."

"Rise! Rise! Rise!"

<!-- HALYNA END -->

One cry it was, in two throats. Hers came first for no cause but that a cry must begin somewhere. Some tell it that she called and he answered, with a silence between.

There was no silence.

Then stood the guild, like an arch when the frame is struck from under it, every stone on its neighbour. I stood with them, and remember not the standing. We took up the cry, and the soldiers on the wall took it from us, and the column from the soldiers.

Old stone trembleth not. Under my feet I felt it tremble.

That night this hearth had its first fire in more years than any could tell. By it {{Idrenna}} made the terms with the Captain, and I carried in the water. Slow they spake, as the old speak, and the Captain hurried them not.

The Stonewrights would bear no arms, and he asked it not. But they would build between the volleys, and mend a breach under the guns, with the speed and precision that only Aetherbonded pairs could achieve. {{Idrenna}} gave it him in two halves:

"Not the sword, but the stone."

"Not the killing, but the keeping."

The Captain bowed his head. It is the guild's word now.

Ere that fire burned down, {{Orvenna}} split the siege day in three, as quarrymen split a block along its own seam. To the Captain they said:

"Every gun ye haul is a course we lay not,"

"so spend a gun as a careful man spendeth silver."

*Then the masons sang, as they sing it still:*

> *Lay the stone while the sea lies bare,*  
> *haul the gun while the guns are cold,*  
> *hold the wall when the grey sails come,*  
> *and the wall will hold.*

And wherever ye see a Stonewright on the wall in the holding, look again. Ye will see two.

Late that night, by torches that hissed in the falling frost, we laid the first course of the war in the gap of the inner gate. Every joint we broke over the joint below. For the old gate-song of the havens {{Orvenna}} asked the clearest voices. Up stood Enno and Della, and took the first voice between them, the one to the comma and the other the rest.

*Then they sang, and the guild after them:*

> *Stone upon stone, and the stone keeps faith;*  
> *hand over hand, and the hand holds true;*  
> *two at the gate when the grey sails gather,*  
> *two at the gate, and the gate stands through.*

I sang to the comma, and then I sang the rest.

When the torches were stubs, Enno and Della lay asleep against the new course, their hands yet joined, as they had come up the mountain. I lay down a little way off, by the water-pails, and slept.

By morning the mortar had set.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

### I.3 · The Captain's Long Breath

*The first winter, in its second month.*

*Laid by Hale the runner, that was seventeen and in his first weeks on the wall. He told it standing, and as fast as ever he ran; I have set it down slower.*

Give me the stone, then. I am not a teller. I run.

Every haven had its runners' stone. Ours we made the first week, of a fallen block by the inner gate, and on it we sware the old oath:

"What I saw, I carry. I carry nothing else."

Wick sware it last, the youngest of us and the swiftest. Naught would he sooner do than carry a word to the Captain.

At the grey of the morning of the first assault I stood by Kael's guns, that are yet all we have. The Captain gave Kael his mind: where to watch, what shot to lay, and when to hold. Then went he on, to the far end of the wall. Kael laid his hand on my shoulder.

"Naught may change that mind but his own voice at my side, lad, and a man can stand but in one place."

So we runners are his eyes, where the smoke lieth between him and the water.

*Then the troops drilled the three calls, as they drill them still:*

> *Shift your ground, and watch new water;*  
> *change your shot for the sea, the shore, the sky;*  
> *hold, or loose, when the Captain is by.*

The drill done, Jory of Eldhythe leaned on the parapet of the east wall and whistled a tune that the old ship-masters hate. Merrick, eldest of them, coiling rope at the stair-foot, bade him stop. Jory laughed down at him.

"It is only a rhyme."

Merrick did not laugh.

Ere the morning was half gone, the sails of my first holding came out of the grey faster than I could tell them. In the laying the hours go slow, for stone is heavy, and in the hauling slower. In the holding an hour is gone in a heartbeat.

Late in the holding, when the rest were spent and the masons were come out on the breach, Kael's lookout saw a hull in a far-glass of Pellow's grinding. It had hung back in the grey, where none had hung before. Now it came in, late and alone.

Between the Captain and that hull lay the smoke. Kael gave me three words.

"Hull hangeth back."

I ran it.

I run. Under the smoke, head down, past the masons, past the ropes. To no man speak I, and I stop not. Up into the clear, above the gate.

The Captain standeth still.

That is what I remember first. Everything on that wall was moving but him. One hand he had on the parapet, his face to the sea. He looked not round at me. Yet he heard me to the last word.

Then he walked toward Kael's guns at his own pace, and I at his shoulder. As he walked, the hour began to stretch.

It stopped not short. It slowed, like a stone-cart on a long hill, till a boy behind might count the spokes. I have no better way to say it.

I saw the water sheet off the late hull's bow. I counted the ripples going down its grey sail, as ye count the rings on a cut log: one, and then the next. Each, I think, was a minute. The gulls hung. The smoke stood. On he walked.

I had time to be afraid. Then I had time to be ashamed of it. Then I had time to stop.

At Kael's shoulder he stopped. Kael was counting under his breath. Then the Captain gave the call, with his own voice.

"Hold."

The battery held. Below us the masons laid on, two and two, and looked not up. The late hull came on through the smoke, and on, till I could see the wet on its timbers.

"Loose."

The hour let us go, slow, like a held breath going out. The guns went. Out in the smoke the hull broke up, in the one stretch of that water where it could hurt no one, where the Captain had meant it to break.

I know not what the Captain thought. I know what he did.

That evening I walked the tide-line, as we runners do after every holding. In the shallows I saw marks cut in a plank, and carried them up past the fires to {{Halyna}}:

"Marks on the wood."

I ran it.

That night I found my way back by Hesk's lamp, his mother's lamp, on the north wall. By it I wist the north was awake.

Now the men say it along the wall: "For the Captain, an hour lasteth as long as a day." I think it began with me, though Voss will have it older. They say it as though the Rite had given it him. So said I myself, and I know better now. Kael mended it, and I carry it as he said it, and nothing else:

"It is not the world that sloweth, lad. It is the Captain who will not hurry. The moment he chose was the moment that came."

This I lay as it was laid for me.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

### 6 · The custom, and the locks

- **No custom of burning, and no echo of one.** The tune *that the old ship-masters hate* is plain fear (IV.5 grounds it in Daveth). *Up past the fires* is bare, and V.1 (g07) now gives the fires their plain cause.
- **Pair grammar.** Every verb after a pair-name is plural or past: *{{Halyna}} laid*, *stood up*, *they looked up, and bowed*, *{{Idrenna}} made*, *Slow they spake*, *{{Orvenna}} split*, *they said*, *{{Orvenna}} asked*. Pair-speech stands on two bare lines.
  - *She began the cry, and he ended it* names two deeds of two persons. It is not a *he and she* tag.
  - There is no *the two of them* and no singular *they*.
  - Enno and Della are not yet sealed, so they have no pair-name.
- The Captain is unnamed, and speaks only the question and the calls.
- {{Halyna}} discover, cry and are cried after. {{Idrenna}} and {{Orvenna}} make the terms and the day. There are no Christian marks.
- **Asides to the hall:** one in I.2 (*look again. Ye will see two.*), none in I.3.
- **No native blocks.** book_v2 has none for I.2 or I.3, so no key was lost.
- **Idlan and Denna, Ormund and Penna.** I.2 no longer names them. They were register appositions, and the Jack's-eye critic asked that they go.
  - Ormund and Penna are first named by deed in V.7 (g09), *With their own four hands {{Orvenna}} set the north stone… Over the stone Ormund began the old jest, and Penna finished it.* After that they appear in VI.3 (g10) and the Epilogue (g11). Each time they stand next to {{Orvenna}}, so the reader joins them.
  - Idlan and Denna are named in no other draft. If the orchestrator wants them in the Book, their place is the foreword's one line on the four pairs (g01, line 104), as one clause, not an apposition at I.2's terms.
