### VI.3 · The Knowing of the Green Wood

*The sixth autumn, when the boat went out.*

*Laid by Seren Two-Inks, that held the lamp. Tonight {{Halyna}} stood with me at the stone, a hand each beside mine, and a long while we held it so ere any of us spake.*

<!-- TOKEN {LAST_THRONE_AT_HAVEN} -->

"Hold the stone, Seren."

All that day lay the tenth Throne where it fell, black of grain and split open, a hill of timber higher than a harbour-house. It was {LAST_THRONE_AT_HAVEN}. The soldiers stood well off, for they feared it even dead.

Nine hearts had {{Halyna}} known since the spring. Each was dead wood.

Toward evening came the Captain down to it himself, and stood long beside it with his hood up. Then he looked to {{Halyna}}.

At dusk they went in, and I behind them with the lamp. Overhead the water dripped. In the oldest of the grain lay the heart, grey and hard and still.

Beside it, in a crack of the heartwood, lay a sliver no longer than a hand.

It was green.

I brought the lamp down to it, for I trusted not the light.

Six winters had we searched the wrecks for orders, and the hearts for names, and the grey for sails. Not one of us had looked for a thing that lived. Yet this had lived in the dark of a dead heart, as seedlings live in the crack of a wall. *The green wood holds one word*, the groves sang.

One hand only had {{Halyna}} needed on any wood since the first winter. All the same they put out a hand each, and touched it in the same moment.

A drop fell from the timbers into the flame, and hissed.

Their two faces changed as one.

Not an order was it, nor a wound. Rhyna spake, as though someone had put the word into her mouth and gone away.

"Home."

Halvard said that she was right. But the green wood gave not the word alone, and it stopped not where we stop. They said it together, in one breath:

<!-- W -->
*We remember our home too.*

Rhyna wept, and Halvard did not, for she was weeping for them both. I held the lamp till Rhyna had done.

Then gave they me the other thing, folded in the answer as a seed is folded in a fruit: the name the wood's own people give themselves. *Naelear*, those of the breath. Three times I said it over ere I wrote it, and it held. *Mystaeri* is but the word of the shore.

<!-- RIPENS: II.2's untold name -->

Since the iron came, not a leaf had the pillars put up. They had waited for the sky to come again.

So it was seed.

Up to the Keep Rhyna bare it on moss, with Halvard's hand under her elbow. Into the ward ran a fisher-girl of the east keep, and asked whither it would go.

"Home."

"And we two must find it a boat."

In all the annals of the guild there is no Stonewright's boat. The sea was ever a thing we walled against, and never a road.

But up the mountain had the boatwrights of the havens carried their adzes, as the masons their chisels. On the morrow they came down to the shingle. The eldest of them was Tarnard, Tarnel's father. The keel he laid himself.

Out of the vault Harl of the burning shore bare the first plank on his shoulder. *Burn it*, he had said of every plank of the war. This one he set down at Tarnard's feet, and said naught. Tarnard laid his palm flat on it.

"Wood that knew the sea knoweth the way home."

Of the vault's planks they built it. All but one had spoken to us once, and now they would speak for us.

For the stem Tarnard took the two I had laid raw edge to raw edge: the one that said *shore*, and the silent one. {{Orvenna}} keyed them as stone is keyed. Penna held the last key, and Ormund struck it, once.

The broken grain met, and held.

That evening {{Halyna}} cut their mark across the join, the one setting the chisel and the other striking. Never ere then had a mark of ours gone into wood. I held the lamp, and it burned down a finger's width.

The wood took it.

::: native legend_stonwryt_halyna chip
{{Halyna}}'s Stonwryt, cut across the join of the stem, where the two halves of the first heart are keyed: the mark that Halvard drew once in the ash
:::

Rhyna laid her palms on the cut, and Halvard his hands over hers.

"It is in wood what our vow is in stone,"

"harm to one is harm to both."

The lashing of the bow Tarnard left for me. I tied it with Tarnel's knot, that he taught me on the Road, a knot to a mile. Long looked Tarnard at it. Then he said his son's saying, low:

"A net is only knots holding hands."

Every joint of the hull had {{Orvenna}} keyed as stone is keyed, that no sea should work it loose. A day it rode off the point in a hard swell, and took no water. It was small, and it was sound.

At the evening tide came the whole hearth down, the old leaning on the young. {{Halyna}} laid the green sliver in the bow on its moss, against the keyed stem. Beside it they set a Stonwryt coin, part gold and part black stone: trust, sealed in stone. It was our dearest gift, as the dark wood was the Guest's. Then they laid their two chisels by it, and in the bow the chisels took the last of the light.

"Let them go with it,"

"for we two have cut our last mark."

No one on the shingle spake. {{Halyna}} kneeled on the wet stones before the bow, and behind them the whole hearth kneeled. The soldiers laid down their pikes. Kael the Counter, that will not sit to tell a tale, kneeled with the rest. Last in the line, with his hood up, kneeled the Captain.

There he sitteth now, at the end of the bench. The wet of the shingle is dark yet on his knees.

From the front {{Halyna}} said:

"So far can one people go alone: the gift, and the kneeling."

"The rest needeth the other."

A wave came up the shingle and wet the keel. Then rose Harl, and took the bow in his two arms, and walked it down into the tide. The fisher-children pushed it off. Foremost, to her waist in the cold sea, was the girl that had asked whither it would go.

Well out on the ebb, the boat turned. Into the wind it went, and against the current, and into the grey.

This time it carried a gift, not a grave.

Along the shingle stood the old ship-masters, and Merrick first. Six winters had they turned their faces from the sea when a hull burned. A grey boat going out against the wind is the thing they fear most on all the water.

They did not look away.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

*And no one answered.*

When we three took our hands from the stone, the hearth was silent a long while. The fire burned down, and none rose to lay wood on it. We waited, I think, the width of one old voice. Ebba of Fenholm is under the east cairns.

Then a child near the front spake, not loudly. It was the girl that went first into the sea. Old Ebba was her great-grandmother.

"We remember."

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

*Then for the first time the hearth sang in the wood's way. We call it* The Two Homes:

<!-- W: line 3 only -->
> *Our home was hewn from the mountain's bone;*  
> *we forgot it, and found it, and held it in blood.*  
> *Their home was breathed from the hearts of the trees, and the axes came, and the breath grew thin, and they have not found it, and they cannot hold it.*  
> *Stone keeps the vow, and the grain keeps the truth.*  
> *We remember our home. They remember theirs too.*
