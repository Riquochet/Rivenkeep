### V.2 · The Night of the Naming

*Late in the second summer.*

*I held the lamp that night, and laid this at the hearth on the evening after. The planks I tell of lie yet in the lowest vault, and whoso will may go down and lay a hand on them.*

Hold the stone, Seren.

All that first winter this fire laughed at the grey sails. "They have picked the wrong fortress," said Kael, and I laughed with the rest.

One night late in the Second Tide, {{Halyna}} went down to the oldest room under this hall, where the Rite was found. I came behind with the ink and the lamps. As cold it is in summer as in winter. The rows lay grey on grey, like a field the plough hath turned up stony.

Fifty-three planks of the first winter lay there, and every one carved. The runners had carried them home two to a plank, from wherever the sea gave them up, the salt running down their arms. The soldiers' fires had the rest, and the tide. The wall called them the tablets. They were but planks.

Each but one had given {{Halyna}} one word, and each word I wrote on a leaf of its own. That one had given naught, though it was carved as finely as any. The sea brought it in weeks after the first.

The planks of the Second Tide bare two signs apiece, cut close, as names are cut close on an old lintel. On the shingle {{Halyna}} learned to hold two at once, and there were gaps in every telling. I have not mended them with guesses. Till that night no one had gone down to ask the fifty-three more.

The night was short and the rows were long, so they began at the two ends, Rhyna at the stair and Halvard at the far wall. Never ere then had I seen them work apart. They were not far apart: the length of the vault.

Rhyna took up the first plank of all, the wave that had said *shore*, with its one long edge split raw. Halvard took up the silent one, and its long edge was raw also.

I stood between them with the lamp. No wind cometh so far below, and the flame stood up straight in my hand.

Rhyna lifted her plank. At the far wall Halvard lifted his. So rise the far knots of a net with the near, for it is all one net.

Their two faces changed in the same moment.

Then Rhyna spake, and it was not a word. It was a whole order.

<!-- W -->
*To shore. As root to water, go. Wait on no bough.*

From the far end, with the silent plank in his hands, Halvard said it with her, word for word.

After that every plank spake. In the order the sea gave them up we had laid them, and that night each gave up its own. All night {{Halyna}} went along the rows toward each other, and I went between and wrote. By the middle rows my hand ached to the wrist, and I stopped not. Here are the others, as I wrote them:

<!-- W: the italic carvings only -->
*Break wall. First stone, only stone. Strike till it opens or wood is ash.* That was the closed square, their sign for the mute thing, stone: the hulls that struck one wall till they sank.

*Go until struck. Where bark splits, turn aside.* The lone hull that swerved, and was not followed.

*Before bearer, thin wood. Thin wood falls; bearer keeps pace.* The cheap hulls at the head of the column. They were told to die first, and they died first.

*Where it flashed, strike. Last flash, only flash.* Those bent toward whichever gun had last spoken.

*Wait in grey, as seed waits out frost. When stone is still, come.* Those hung in the grey, and came when we had done with looking.

*Home when wounded. One bough falls; all turn, each by its own water.* Those scattered at the first loss.

The soldiers took the head of each for its name, in plainer words: *To the shore. Break the wall. Wait in the grey.*

When {{Halyna}} met in the middle, the grey of morning was coming down the stair. One plank more lay at the end of the last row, by itself. A mason of Emberhythe had carried it up the mountain after the Fall, charred black at one end. He could not bear to bring naught of his city away, and what it was he knew not.

{{Halyna}} held it last, a hand of each on the grain, while the lamps went pale.

It said *Burn*.

No one spake, and for a long while I wrote not. Then Halvard laid it back at the end of the last row, by itself.

That was all the young wood knew: one word to a hull, and in it one order. Each hull said its word at our wall, and said it again, and said it till it drowned.

They had not picked anything.

They had been sent.

At full light I took up the first plank and the silent one. All night mine eyes had gone back to their edges. I laid them side by side, raw edge to raw edge.

The broken grain met.

It met as the halves of a split log meet. I have not asked {{Halyna}} what it meaneth, and they have not told me.

Then we three sat down among the planks. The lamps burned on, and no one put them out. At last I said this, and {{Halyna}} let it stand:

"I think those hulls were the youngest of the wood, sent first, for the young grow quickest. The worst of the wood is not behind us. It is still growing."

This I lay as it was laid for me.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*

### V.5 · The Tides, as the Wall Counted Them

*At each Tide's end, for six winters.*

*Laid by Kael the Counter, that would tell no Tide till it was over. In the sixth winter he brought me all six himself, and bade me mend them as I had mended his first; I have mended them less.*

Give me the stone. No, I will stand.

**I · The Hasty Tide**

I count. That is what I am for.

Eight sails the first morning, and not one kept station with another. Each struck the first wall it touched till it sank.

No man stood on the decks. Off the transports the root-men came like spilled kindling. Treelings the wall called them, as if a name could make them small.

By noon their fire slackened, and we learned to say, "The fleet hath spent its fire."

A breach on the south face fell outward, as the slates of the black chest said, and all behind it lived. I swear to that.

All that winter I said at this fire that the grey sails had picked the wrong fortress. Every night it went round the benches with the cup, and every man drank to it. We laughed. God forgive us, we laughed.

Jory of Eldhythe, that whistled on the east wall, laughed the loudest. His name is first under the east cairns.

A fire-ship took him. That night the Captain cut a hand's breadth from his half of the cloak, and bound it on a pike over his cairn.

It was the first strip.

***Added in Seren's hand:*** *It was not the last.*

**II · The Second Tide**

The second count, and a harder.

In pairs they came, the front to take the shot and the back the ground.

Off the north point a hull was struck. A mile south, another's sail went grey-green to white, like a man's face, and it turned.

It had not seen. It had felt.

Then came the crown-hulls, and their winged seed, to wherever our guns were quiet.

When we sank a bearer, another took up the standard, and the fleet took on the new bearer's temper. Choose which heart ye leave them.

Corlen of the harbour, eldest of the Twelve, held a wet finger to the wind before every holding. "Wait," he would say. "The sea hath not finished."

In a seed-fall a hull came slow on his water. By his own judgement he held his guns quiet, to let it close. He had eyes for the water, and for naught behind him.

The seed came over the wall where the guns were quiet.

His were.

He died at the parapet, watching the water, and three of his crew with him. That night the Captain cut the second strip.

Corlen did what the Captain would have done. That is the whole of a True Man. It is also what killed him.

Voss took his battery, and was raised a True Man. At the next holding he wet a finger, as Corlen did. The Captain at his shoulder looked up, and not out to sea.

"Change your shot."

**III · The Tide of Remembering**

When Brenn had been read, the runners added four words to their oath: *Not as Brenn carried.* Wick, the youngest yet, sware it loudest.

Ye knew Wick. He ran everywhere, even to this hearth, and sat at the front where the children sit, his knees to his chin. The wall-walk he could run in the time it took to say the Title twice. Naught he wanted, save to run a word to the Captain.

By night the Captain had the south gun moved forty yards. At grey morning Wick stood at the old gun-place, to watch the water. On the north wall Hesk's lamp burned yet.

One hull came, alone and slow, sounding. Not to the wall. To the place where the gun had been. It fired there, to see whether it would answer.

The shot took the parapet, and Wick with it.

I did not see it. Hale did. Wick gave him three words.

"They are looking."

His word came to the Captain, as he had wanted all the war.

Hale ran it.

That night the Captain cut Wick's strip. So a boy died of a gun that was not there.

We had thought them beasts, that learn by the stick. They look before they trust. Our walls they knew, and never once our Captain. After that we moved our guns by night, and it was a quieter war, and I liked it less.

**IV · The Joined Tide**

All the night before, the drums went in the grey, hull to hull. At first light the sails came on three roads at once, north and east and the river mouth. Three roads, one hour.

Lanner of the crags whistled for the wind, as crag-men do. The Captain stood at his shoulder, and was still. Lanner stopped whistling.

"Loose."

An hour after dawn Lanner's battery sank their bearer.

The fleet faltered not. At the hour the dead heart had carved, they turned in on the east road, into empty water, one sail pale and then the next, a breath apart. They kept a dead mind's hour. Only the bearer's own went wild.

Ere dusk I walked the wall to count. Enno of the guild I found in the east breach, and a mile on, in the north, Della. With that hour I count them.

I will not say more.

**V · The Tide that Learned Deceit**

The fifth count, and I like it not.

They came as the young wood came, loud and alone and straight, and we laughed as we laughed the first winter. Every gun spake, and the hunters came for every gun that had spoken. Straw they sent down a new road each day, hulls that were not hulls, and root-men down the road where no gun woke.

But look close, as Pellow said. The straw left no wreck, nor flinched at a shell. A true rout scattered, and a false one kept its spacing. A laden ship rode low. The young wood never blanched, and a green hull that blanched was no green hull.

Some of us looked not, and they are under the east cairns.

Pellow held his glass up to everything, and ye laughed at him, and so did I. Our far-glasses he ground, out of the glass of his city.

Pellow looked, and he is under them too.

Looking kept no man alive. It kept the wall.

**VI · The Last Tide**

This winter each fleet came with a morrow carved in it, as we carve ours, and a turning for home at the end. When its bearer fell, and the second, it went home. Not scattered. Went.

In the Joined Tide {{Halyna}} had told me:

"The fleet cannot be called home,"

"for the wood obeyeth the wood."

I answered that we would send it home, and wist not what I meant. Now I know. It cannot be called home. But it can be sent home.

The wood can fear.

At this winter's end came ten great hulls that were not fleets, and sat down in our havens. The wall named them for beasts. They sit there tonight.

Six winters have I told the grey sails. I know their number, and I will not tell it at this hearth.

But I will tell ours.

A count is no count till it be named. Tonight in the ward I called the roll of the True Men: fourteen names, and twelve to answer.

At Corlen of the harbour the ward was quiet. I let it be quiet the space of a breath, and called the next. At Hesk of the mere the answer came down from the north wall, where his lamp was lit. At Pellow of the spire the ward was quiet.

Now hear the dead of the wall, and hold them, for they are ours. Jory, that whistled. Corlen, that waited on the sea. Wick, that ran everywhere. Enno and Della, {{Enrella}}, hand in hand. Pellow, that held his glass up to everything. For each of them the Captain hath cut a hand's breadth, as poor men cut the last loaf, their own share thinnest.

The rest of the east cairns I named at dusk among the stones, every name, twice. My voice was not steady at the last of them, and I will not feign it was.

His half of the cloak hung to his knees the first winter.

Tonight it cannot reach his belt.

There he sitteth now, at the end of the bench.

This I lay as it was laid for me.

*And the hearth said: We remember.*
