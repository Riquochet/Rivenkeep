### II.2 · Of the Breath of the Wood

*The fifth winter, the Tide that Learned Deceit.*

*A child asked whether the Guest's people had houses. Many nights {{Halyna}} held his gift, one at the hearth, the other asleep, and ever the sleeper woke knowing; into it his people had put the whole of home, and for a home that is air our tongue hath no word.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Old. Ere stone. Ere any stone was cut.*  
*— Grey among the trunks. Not fog. There is no word —*  
*— Breath. We two have it. Breath.*  
*— Trees asleep, breathing it out. We — they breathe it in.*  
*— A light. Not day —*  
*— and not dusk. Between. Soft.*  
*— Little ones, made for it. Only for it.*  
*— Pale trees at evening, singing. And a name, their own —*  
*— [ ]*

This is held in the grain.

Ere your fathers cut their first stone, we were old, by more lives of yours than ye have names for. We who call ourselves [⟦legend_naelear_chip⟧]<!-- RIPENS: VI.3 --> lived in the wood, and the wood in us, and between us was the breath.

::: note
The name the wood gives itself, not yet told
*Naelear*, those of the breath. Seren sets the word beside the sign, and the sign stays.
:::

It was the air we breathed.

Flat lay our land under the trees, past all measuring, to the far edge of the world. In their long sleep the trees breathed, and their breath lay down grey among the trunks. It fed the trees, and it fed us, and we tended it as ye tend a hearth.

Through the breath the sun came softened: the between-light, neither day nor dusk. For that light were we made, and never were we children of the dark.

At evening the light went low among the trunks. Then we sang to the pale saplings, and they answered, one stand and then the next, till the deep wood was one choir. Our little ones lay down among the roots to hear it. One by one their small voices left the song, and they slept in the singing.

Ferns of the deep wood, cast on bare sand, wither in the open sun. So wither we.

In the years of the iron we saw the open sun wither our children.

<!-- RING -->

All our long summers we led the wood. We pruned it, and led its roots to the water, and where we led it, there it grew. One life it was, in two kinds: we tended the wood, and the wood kept us, and kept its own will besides.

Without the breath the wood is sick. Without the wood the breath cometh not again. Without either we fail, and slowly, for we live twice as long as ye, and half again: the eldest last, and the youngest first.

Among the twisted boughs we kept our rites. Under the silver elders sat the council, for none lie beneath a silver elder.

Our speech is the storm in the high boughs, all cracks and breaks. But soft is all we carve in living wood. The thunder is for the moment, the softness for always.

What is carved is not heard. It is known. Lay a hand on the carving, and ye know what the carver meant, whatever tongue he spake. The wood cannot lie.

We wist not that there was any other way to be believed.

<!-- RING -->

At the very edge, where the breath came down to your sea, stood our pillars in their long ranks. Between them they held the breath, as a mast holdeth a sail. We call them the Naelsaen, the mist-hearts: the anchors of our sky, and the only trees of ours ye ever found.

From among them we watched your shore, summer on summer. We saw your havens raised. We saw your lamps lit at dusk. We saw your boats come a little way in, and turn back.

Once a boat came farther than any. So near it came that we heard the oars stop. A man stood up in the bow and looked full at us.

We wove the breath thicker.

He saw but grey. His face went white, and the oars went back the way they came.

We wist not why.

Here is our fault, and the grain will not leave it out. We went not down to your shore. We hid, and were glad. So long hid we, and so well, that never once were we seen.

Since your first haven we had listened to you. Ye had never once heard us. We had made sure of it.

This is our home: the flat wood under the breath, the between-light, the pale saplings singing at evening, the silver elders at council. And at the edge of the world, the pillars, with the breath held up between them.

If any green be left of us, it remembereth this; and the grain holds it still.

*And no one answered.*

### IV.2 · The Axe in the Grain

*The third winter, in the Tide of Remembering.*

*Rhyna took up this Blackthorn heart on the shingle, and {{Halyna}} had but its shape. This winter it came whole to Halvard in the vault, and to her among the children; the telling took three nights, for it was in them as a wound is, and not as a tale.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Ranks. Tall, dark of bark. Holding the breath down.*  
*— Oars. Many oars. Then iron, in the bark.*  
*— One, and — [⟦legend_iv2_gap1⟧]*  
*— Strokes. Counted. Every stroke, counted.*  
*— Each fell, and the sky came through. Pale —*  
*— and pale, and hard. Shoots. Cut, and cut again.*  
*— Soft letters, on the mute thing —*  
*— and it did not [⟦legend_iv2_gap2⟧]*

::: note
Two marks of the wood's shape that {{Halyna}} could see, and could not yet tell
The first is the pillars, many, with a memory cut through them to the heart: *What one pillar felt, all the ranks felt, root to root.* The second is the mouth, mirrored, and tied back across the rings to the plea cut on the stone: *It did not answer.*
:::

This is held in the grain.

At the edge of the breath we stood in our long ranks, tall and dark of bark. We held the breath to the earth, as roots hold a bank.

Of that we thought not, for none think of breath till it be taken. We were the living pillars of the breath. For that alone we stood, and it was enough.

From the edge we watched your shore, and every hull of yours that came nigh us we marked. Each brush was a cut, and each trespass a wound. The wood keepeth every count.

<!-- RING -->

In the high summer of your havens came the iron, in boats with many oars. Up out of the surf waded the shore-men.

What one pillar felt, all the ranks felt, root to root, to the far edge of the wood.

The first they came to stood nearest your shore. Of all the ranks it had marked the most of your hulls.

At first light they set the iron to it. Stroke on stroke it went in, and every stroke we counted. Ere noon it leaned. Yet it held the breath.

Then the roots let go.

Where it had stood, the sky came through, as daylight through a rent in a sail. It was pale.

One fell, and another. Summer after summer the shore-men came, till they that first brought the iron were old, and sent their sons. Then it was pale and hard.

The stumps put up shoots, straight and black, and in a few summers a young pillar stood where the old one fell. The iron came back for the young ones. We put up more. It came again.

Then it was pale and hard and wide.

The young of the long-lived turned their faces from the open sky, and wist not why they wept.

We carved, as ever we had carved when a thing must not be mistaken. But never laid the shore-men a hand on wood to know it. They cut it, and bare it away over the water.

So on the mute thing we cut our plea: on the stone your builders love, of which ye make your houses and your walls. For a people, we thought, must know best what is cut in the thing it loveth.

We cut it in our own soft letters. Where we had caught a word of yours across the water, that too we cut, as near its sound as our letters would go. At the feet of the pillars yet standing we set the stones.

All the made things of ours speak, and so we believed that all made things speak. It was a child's belief. Stone hath no mind.

It did not answer.

With the timber the shore-men took the stones away, and whither they went we wist not. At the last, along the whole edge, the stumps put up nothing, and the breath grew thin. We held our sap, and waited.

The iron will tire; and when it stoppeth, the wood will come again.

<!-- RING -->

In the years of the iron we that tell you this were never a pillar. A thin green thing were we, at the edge.

Where the iron first went in, a stump was cut low, and we were its last shoot. In our rings lay the whole of the axe. The iron passed us over, for we were too thin to make a pole.

Forty summers had we stood when the long-lived of the council came seeking hearts, to bear their carvings. Not the straightest young tree chose they, nor the eldest. They chose us, for the axe in our rings.

When one of our hulls is struck, we go to the stroke, as the tongue to a broken tooth. It is in the rings.

What we go to is ever the same. It is the iron at the edge of the breath, summer after summer, and the sky coming through; and the grain holds it still.

*And no one answered.*

### IV.4 · The Gift Held an Hour

*The third spring, in the Tide of Remembering.*

*On the night Brenn was read, a child asked what the Guest had given. This spring Rhyna held it an hour, and it came into her slow, as water over stone; Halvard, across the ward at the mortar, touched it not, and it was he that began the telling.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Old. Dark, and old. Stone — no. Not stone. A tree, once.*  
*— Hands, warm, a long while. Leaves. No — words, like leaves. One, and one, and one.*  
*— Three. Three words in him —*  
*— and naught to join them. We two have it.*  
*— A door. Our — the wood's kin, in the posts. Sawn. They answer not.*  
*— Kneeling. Set down. His best. A foot.*  
*— Dust. Laughter, through the dust.*  
*— A neck, held. Two brows, one —*  
*— the blade — [ ]*

This is held in the grain.

Eldest wood are we. A tree we were when the world was green to its edges. We fell, and the ages lay down on us, leaf on leaf, till time forgat us and we grew dark and hard. Ye took us for stone. We are not stone.

Wood that time forgat forgetteth not. Slow is its giving, and true. Of all the gifts the long-lived give, the dearest is a sliver of such wood. We were given once.

<!-- RING -->

Aelrhen he was named when he took his errand: bond-to-stone. A long life he listened across the breath, to the shore-men at their nets and at their walls. He gathered your words as a child gathereth fallen leaves, one and one and one. At the end he had three, and knew them for the right ones. Join them he could not. Ye join your words with small words, and small words make no sound across water.

But he had us. Whoso held us while a tree's shadow moved the breadth of its own trunk would know the whole of it. We were the sentence.

He chose the outermost of your holds, for its doors and its benches were of our wood. He took that for reverence. We knew it for what it was: our kin from the edge, sawn and polished. At the door he laid his hand on the post and greeted them. They answered not. They were dead.

He kneeled on the stones before the master of that house, and set us down. They that come under a roof bring their best, and we were his best. In the thunder of his own tongue he spake the name of the rite, *Ael'thar*, and after it his three words, in yours.

"Stop… Sky… Dying…"

A foot struck us into the dust by the wall. Laughter came to us through the dust.

He stopped not.

He rose, and took the back of the host's neck in his right hand, there where life goeth up into thought. A fledgling could have slept in that hand. The foreheads met, thought to thought, and he was silent.

With his left hand he set the blade to the host's arm.

And the host drew back. We know not why. Fear leaveth no mark on wood.

The blade that was to go a little way went deep, for the arm was moving. The host cried out, and his men came.

Then Aelrhen's blood ran over the stones of the step to where we lay, and with it came all of him: his long listening, and his last hour. So we know it as he knew it.

Between their hands he went down on his knees again, there where the rite began. He knew the blows as trees know the iron: one, and another. He lifted not his hand. The cut he would have made on his own arm was never made. He would ask no blood that he gave not. He gave it all, there on the step.

Never were the two arms laid together, shoulder to shoulder, that harm to the one should be harm to both. He died there, with the rite half made.

We lay in the dust and heard all of it, and no hand took us up. Of all things that ever lay at a shore-man's feet and looked like stone, we were the one that could have spoken to him.

And we were the one his foot found.

<!-- RING -->

In the morning a hand took us out of the dust, and put us away, and held us not.

Then a jolting in the dark, a day and a night. Then the dark, half a life, and never a hand. Then leaves about us, and cut stones cold beside us, and another dark as long. Then old arms, two pairs, slow, a long while; and fire, near and far. Then small arms, two pairs, going up, in the cold.

Then one pair, a long way.

None held us long, till thou heldest us while a tree's shadow moved the breadth of its own trunk.

Here is the sentence, as it was brought to the door:

*Stop the felling of the pillars at the edge of the grey, for they are not timber. The sky over us is their breath, and ours, and when you cut them it comes open, and our children cannot live in the open sun. We are here, behind the grey, and we have been here always, and we are not your enemies. Stop, for the sky is dying, and we are dying under it.*

That is all he came to say. We have carried it a long way, and the grain holds it still.

*And no one answered.*

### IV.6 · The Voyage of the Aelvaren

*The third summer, in the Tide of Remembering.*

*Halvard lifted this heart in the vault; Rhyna, on the wall, thrust both hands into the rain-butt, and would not say why. The wood saith two, the ship-masters twenty, and I have kept both; of the long-lived I say what the wood cannot: They did not know it was ignorance.*

<!-- READING -->

*— Small. Grey. Light on the water. A name, and proud of it.*  
*— A hand on the boards, all the way over. Shallows. Waiting.*  
*— Heels on the stones. Thrown in. Heavy —*  
*— and warm. Into the boards. All of him.*  
*— Grief. For this we two have words.*  
*— Fire. Hands burning — no. Boards. The boards burning.*  
*— Poles. Out, to the open, away from —*  
*— Turning. Against the — [ ]*

This is held in the grain.

And not in this grain only. The hull that brought it to you was not there. Yet it knoweth, for it grew out of what we were.

Aelvaren were we, the Bearer of the Binding, and proud of the name: a small boat of the old wood, grey and light. Through the thin breath we bare Aelrhen to your shore, and all the way his hand lay on our boards.

He went up from us to the door. All that morning we lay in the shallows, and waited to bear him home with the rite made. The tide came in under us, and turned, and he came not.

<!-- RING -->

By the heels they drew him down to us, the men of that house. Into us they cast him, as men cast a torn net.

That morning his hand had lain light on our boards. Now his blood ran into them.

So we had all of it.

Then they put fire to us. In its grief the old wood raged, and burned higher than their fire, and screamed, for wood can.

With poles they shoved us off, and the current had us. Out it drew us, toward the open sea, away from home. The fire was in every board. He lay in us, and was cold.

Then we turned.

Into the wind we went, and against the current, burning. Even charred, even dying, the wood knoweth the way home.

Twice on that road, where the breath is thin, hulls of the shore-men lay across our way. Heavy they rode, with the iron in them, and our kin cut and stacked in their holds.

We went into them burning, low, where they were heaviest. The iron had gone into us; now we went into the iron.

Our kin in their holds took our fire, and both hulls broke and burned.

Of the men in them naught is held in the grain.

Under the thinnest of the breath we found the old channels, the hidden waters among the roots. Never went a keel of the shore-men there. Ours knew its way. Slowly the fire went out of us under the closing breath. Of us were left a keel and a few ribs, and in them the bones of Aelrhen.

<!-- RING -->

In the night we came up out of the channels, black and small, onto the pale sand. Under the silver elders we were still. The long-lived of the council came down to us, and knew us, for they had sent us.

They laid their hands on our keel, many hands together. They knew the gift, and the foot. They knew the neck held gently, and the blade, and the blows.

Wood can hide naught.

Their peace they had sent, and the best they had. They saw it answered with murder and flame. They knew a murder, and it was one. But why the host's arm drew back from the blade, they wist not. Nor asked they.

They saw only an act of war.

Our keel they laid among the roots of the silver elders, with the bones of Aelrhen in its ribs.

And the roots drank.

From that grove the knowing went to every grove, root to root, to the far edge of the world. Every young tree that was to be a hull drank it with the rain. So every hull that came against your wall bare our grief in it; and the grain holds it still.

*And no one answered.*
