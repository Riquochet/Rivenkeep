### The Stone out of the Grey

*I wrote this on the page facing Kael's torn cloak. It had been empty since the first night, with wood grain drawn on it and one line:* Not told yet. {{Halyna}} can't hold anything this deep yet. *None of {{Halyna}}'s knowings ever filled it.*

Hold the stone, Seren.

I write alone now, in one ink, with no one to know the wood, and their two inks are dry.

{{Halyna}} are gone.

They died together that seventh winter, as the Bonded do, Rhyna asleep and Halvard before the lamp burned out, because he knew. They share one grave under the inner gate's new top stone, one stone, one name. The Title and their mark and vow are cut face-down above them, hidden till the wall falls.

*{{Orvenna}} sang, Ormund up to the comma, Penna after it:*

> *Stone on stone, and the stone stays true.*  
> *Hand over hand, and the hand holds on.*  
> *Two at the gate when the grey sails gather.*  
> *Two at the gate, and the gate stands strong.*

{{Idrenna}}, already old when {{Halyna}} were small, have outlived them. Halvard's is the last name over the western stair, and none of his family is left to add one. That night the Captain tied a hand-wide strip of his cloak on a pike above the gate. It used up the last of his half, and he wore plain grey all winter.

I didn't go with {{Halyna}}. They once told me I could go alone where they couldn't, and keep this Book going. I understand the Covenant now. When God takes one, he leaves the other one a job to do.

This is mine.

When I told my first tale, I was too scared to talk, so {{Halyna}} pressed my hands to the stone.

"Hold the stone."

"Hold it, and it will hold you."

Now both halves are mine to say.

One drizzly morning that seventh spring, a little grey boat slid up the beach out of the grey, empty. The old ship-masters held back at the top, but the children rushed down, and the first girl there pressed both palms to the boat. Once, when none of us grown-ups could answer the green wood, a child had.

It was the same child.

Then Merrick came slowly down, the man who always looked away when a ship burned.

Merrick put his hand on the boat.

I came last, and the children parted for me like water around a hull. I knew it by its front post, its two halves locked like stone, with {{Halyna}}'s mark over the seam. It had been home and come back.

::: native legend_stonwryt_halyna chip
The mark of {{Halyna}} across the front post of the boat that came home: the same mark that was cut there when it was sent
:::

Where {{Halyna}} had put the green sliver and their chisels, there was a stone that wasn't quite stone. It was wood turned to stone, grain and all, like the Guest's gift. We call it Myststone and say it remembers everything. Stone does not translate, but this was always more than stone. Its carver learned the chisel late but well, young for his people, I'd guess, and grown by ours.

Nobody spoke. The rain had passed, and the slate-grey tide made a long sound going out.

I knew that chisel. I'd held the lamp for it twice, under the gate's top stone and on this front post.

I didn't need my palms. It used our letters, so one person could read it alone, and I read it aloud to the children.

::: native legend_stone_epilogue stone
The Stone out of the Grey: the Last Carver's letters, read with the eyes, cut across a grain no one at this hearth has known
The letters are read at once, below. The grain is told only by this, written under the round in the wood's words:

*This is held in the grain. It is about one of the stone-folk. He climbed up alone, ahead of the others, to the last stone still standing. He tore his sail, the red one, and set it on the stone. He cut marks on it, a hand's worth of them. And the stone-folk came to it, one and then many. Every branch that came against that stone saw it. What the branches saw went home through the roots, and the groves held on to it. One of us held your marks the longest. Home. Family. The one they kneel to. The open door. The stillness. They stood for these at the stone. We stood for the same things, on the other side of it. And the grain still holds it.*

And one line in the Book's own voice: *Known by no one at this hearth. Written down for whoever has learned the grain.*
:::

<!-- MIXED: stone letters on wood that became stone; the stone face with the wood tint -->
*In the memory of our home, our families...*

The carving breaks off there, its last stroke still sharp. He never sailed, so he never saw our cloak, yet he learned our letters. I call him the Last Carver, since I think he was the last. Its one extra word, *the*, before *memory*, is like the Guest's three words, right and truly meant by someone still learning how we talk. Maybe it stops because his hand did.

It came alone, which may be wise, since we haven't earned a guest. But the inner gate stays open every night, and a child keeps the north wall's lamp lit.

Before spring ended, the staff vanished from the gatehouse, and nobody saw the Captain carry it off. It left a clean line in seven winters of dust. If you doubt the tale, go and look at the dust. His work is done, and I know *the stairs that go down no more*.

I haven't been down to look.

I keep this Book in the archive chest, with the mute stones, this Stone and the Guest's gift. Grief gets handed down to the children. The torn cloak still flies above their one grave, and the sea hasn't finished.

I don't know what we'll send back. I only know it has to be sent kneeling.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*

*And the Captain pushed his hood back.*

### The Book of Knowings

*I wrote this at the back of the Book, one carving at a time, as {{Halyna}} knew each one from the wreck-wood and the Thrones. It's the fleet's own half of the Book, because the wood cut every carving in it, and we cut none.*

Every carving begins with a root, one stroke, like a wall's first row, and there are ten. I learned them by eye, on the tide-line and in the vault. I named them myself, since a nameless shape slips away like a wet stone. I said each name three times before writing it, and each one stuck.

The young wood carved only roots. Each Tide after added to them like a tree's rings, so the oldest carvings are the youngest grown up. Later I could spot a root from the wall as fast as a wreck rolls over. The rest only comes to the Bonded, by touch.

Here are only the heads, each carving's main order, youngest first. The rest is in the tales. The young wood's seven go by soldiers' names, smooth as an old mallet's handle.

*Burn* came in a mason's pack from Emberhythe. It was carved in the Fall but wasn't used on our wall, and I haven't counted it. In the homecoming year the mason laid it on the black crust where the Burning died.

The young wood made seven, the next three Tides eight each, and the Tide that Learned Deceit seven. The Last Tide built whole days of orders from old heads, each ending with a turn home. The Thrones added eight Judgements and carried two older carvings whole, since all the rest are written in the Lone Stroke and the Wave. I've written down all forty-six, and I didn't know a single one.

**The Last Note · Three Stones over the Hearth**

Hold the stone, Seren.

This winter a child at the front asked what the three stones over the hearth say. In the havens children were told "nothing", and so was I. I said I didn't know.

Every night since, I've held the lamp to the bottom stone for its sounds, since stone does not translate. The Mystaeri spelled some of our words by sound, and Brenn gave me three.

Last night, as the lamp burned a finger's width lower, I found all three together, sounds nearly right, letters wrong.

::: native legend_three_stones chip
The lowest of the three grey stones over the Long Hearth: the curling letters of the edge, and among them three of our words, carved by their sound
:::

*Stop… Sky… Dying…*

They were carved at the foot of the black pillars and sold with the timber. For three generations they hung over a fire at Eldhythe. Under them the shore laughed at three axemen saved by a broken mast, and ship-masters knocked their pipes on the lowest. Nobody read them.

Nobody knocks pipes on them here. I think I've read them. I say I think.

I have laid this tale the way it was laid for me.

*And everyone at the hearth said: We remember.*
