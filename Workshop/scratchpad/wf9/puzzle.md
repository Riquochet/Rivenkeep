# THE PUZZLE PIECES

*How the tales of the Legends of Rivenkeep lock together: what each tale seeds, where each seed is paid, what Halyna find, and what has to move. This is a design map for reshaping v1.3.0 under Jack's notes of 2026-09-30. It is not Book text. Lines quoted from the Book come from `wf7/book/legends_v13.md`. Any new line in this map is a sample of tone for the rewriter, not final wording.*

**Read in full:** legends_v13 (all of it), the Story Bible, Fiction Hooks, Revision Plan v1.1 §1–2, Tongues §0–1. **Searched:** the Legends Plan and the admiral doc.

**Marks.** **[must]**: the tales do not fit without it, or one of Jack's rulings requires it. **[should]**: recommended, and strong. **[may]**: optional, and Jack's call. An arrow (→) names a new home for something.

## 0 · THE SHORT VERSION

1. **One motif holds the pieces together: the half that waits for its other half.** The bond makes two into one. The Guest's rite was only half made. The cloak was torn down the back. The first heart split into two planks. The Last Carver stopped his Title in the middle of a line. *Halyna* is one name made of *Hal-* and *-yna*. Every spread of the Book is a stone leaf and a wood leaf. Use this as the test for every tale: a piece is locked when its other half turns up somewhere else in the Book.
2. **Halyna find, Seren writes, and others act.** Halyna make every great find: the fallen capstone, the black chest, the oils, the seal they break, the wood's knowings, the green sliver. Seren writes the tales from those finds. The deeds pass to others. The eldest bonded pair, **Idrenna**, make the terms and do the anointing. The **True Men** tell the havens. **Kael and the guild** speak the naming between them. **Tarnel's father** builds the boat, and **the children** push it off. Of the stone leaves, Halyna keep only **II.3**, told to the children in their own "we two". Every leaf that now speaks of Halvard and Rhyna in the third person passes to Seren, which fixes the third-person problem at the root.
3. **The chest Seren carried is the Book's spine-object.** It holds the rolls, with the warden's report copied into them. It also holds a bundle sealed with an Eldhythe pair's Stonwryt, and inside the bundle are Brenn's confession wrapped round the gift-stone, and the mute stones. She and Tarnel carried it between them as far as the passes. At the end it holds the Book, the gift and the Stone out of the Grey, side by side.
4. **The Stonwryt becomes each bonded pair's own sacred mark.** The seers read it at the cradle, as the written form of a bond God made before birth. A pair may cut it only once they have earned the right. They cut it under every work they lay, seal with it, and mint coin under it. Four marks recur: **Aldwena's** (the tale-stone and the lid of the black chest), **Halyna's** (drawn in the hearth-ash for the children, cut under the new capstone, cut across the stem of the sending boat, cut on their grave), **the Eldhythe pair's** (the seal on Brenn's bundle) and **Seren's**, which is no mark at all, because the unbonded have none.
5. **The green sliver is the first growth a pillar has put up since the iron came.** Three tales promise it already (III.2, IV.2, V.4), and VI.1 never cashes the promise. The sending carries the seed of their sky home to be planted.
6. **The sending boat is built round the two halves of the first heart, keyed together, with Halyna's Stonwryt cut across the join.** It is the first word the Rivenmen ever cut in wood, and so the first thing of theirs the Mystaeri could ever *know*. Halyna lay their two chisels beside the sliver (recommended). **The boat that comes back in the Epilogue is the same boat.** Seren knows it by its stem, and the Last Carver cut his Stone with Halyna's chisels.
7. **The burning has a reason.** The shore has an old way: *what the grey sends, the fire sends back.* The warden used that custom to hide a murder. The same custom runs from IV.3 through IV.5 and V.1 to VI.1, where a boat goes out unburned, and to the Epilogue, where the children reach the grey boat before anyone can burn it.
8. **The Aelvaren rammed two ships of the felling, and the ship-masters say twenty.** The few who lived started the rumours. The fear they brought home helped stop the axes, in the same summers that the council voted for war. So the Guest's errand was granted by his death, and the war began anyway.
9. **Brenn was the outpost's runner, and he carried the lie to the Council.** Hale's law, "A runner carries what is seen", is the law Brenn broke. The Captain does not call by horn, which could not carry that far. He calls with his own voice, at the battery he stands beside. That is why he can call only one battery at a time, and why the runners exist: they bring him what is seen, so that he knows where to stand.
10. **Every "you call it…" leaves the wood leaves.** The wood cannot know the shore's names: the fear-names, the grain-names, the Mystlands, the root-men, the Bonded. Seren supplies them in her headnotes. Wherever the two sides disagree she writes her old formula: *I have kept both.*
11. **Time.** Each Book opens with an **Argument**. Each headnote begins with a time-stamp in the hearth's own reckoning: *before the banner* or *after the banner*, then the Tide.
12. **Halyna's name is written under one stroke, a lintel.** The English always shows that the name belongs to two people ("the two of them", "he and she", "both"), and Halyna speak of themselves only as "we two".

## 1 · THE RULES THIS MAP HOLDS TO

- **The wood knows only the wood's side** (Jack, 2026-09-30). A wood leaf holds only what that wood could have known: what was cut into it, what it touched, what it felt, and what came to it through the roots. It never knows the shore's names, thoughts, laws or rumours. **Seren combines the histories.** She does it in her headnotes and frames, and in the facing stone leaf, never in the wood's mouth.
- **Halyna are the discoverers of the stories Seren writes.** They must not be the actors of every deed (§5).
- **The seers read a bond that already exists.** God made it before birth. The seers do not choose (II.3, Jack).
- **The first grain stays unmarked:** the Stone's rings, facing I.1, with Seren's line beneath them.
- **The common origin of the two tongues is never told.** Shared roots, letters and ideas may hint at it. The parting of the peoples stays untold, and no cross-shaped sign is ever a holy sign.
- **The Book is a standalone story.** Everything about what was done to it, and how it unlocks in the game, goes to the separate **NOTES** document. That includes the editor's note, *How the Legends Enter the Game* and the "unlocked by" lines in the native folds.
- **Jack's fixed lines stay verbatim** (§9).

## 2 · WHERE EVERY PIECE SITS IN TIME

### 2.1 The hearth's reckoning (the device) [must]

Jack's note: *"Before the destruction? After? Before the banner is lifted? How far afterwards."* The hearth answers in its own terms. **The banner is the hinge.** The Rivenmen count from the day the cloak went up, and a fixed time-stamp opens every headnote and every Book's Argument.

- **Before the banner**, by ages: *the First Days* (the founding of the Keep) · *the Havens* (twelve generations) · *the Harvest* (two generations of felling) · *the summer of the Guest* (late in the harvest) · *the Forgetting* · *the Fall* · *the Road*. Seren counts distance in generations of the shore: "three generations before the banner".
- **The banner**: the day the column came up the mountain. The cloak, the cry and the terms all fall on this one day and night.
- **After the banner**: *the Forty Days* (the time before the first assault) · then the six Tides by name, each also given as "the Nth year of the banner" · *the Going-Down* (the Thrones and the homecomings) · *the Sending* · *the Years After*.
- **Wood leaves** count in summers and rings, and never know the banner. Seren's headnote converts their count into the shore's reckoning. This is part of how she combines the two sides.

*Sample stamp (tone only):* *Of the summer of the Guest, three generations before the banner. Read aloud in the Tide of Remembering, the third year of the banner.*

### 2.3 The knots this spine unties [must]

1. **The hearth leaf must be found before the first tale is laid.** In v1.3 the leaf turns up after the Rite (I.4), yet I.2 is laid "the night after the first assault". So the Rite has to come **before** the first assault, which is why the Forty Days exist. It also explains why the wall calls him *Commander* from the very first battle.
2. **Why IV.3 waits until the Tide of Remembering when the chest has been at the Keep since day 0.** The confession and the stone lie in a bundle **sealed with another pair's Stonwryt**, and no pair breaks another pair's mark. Halyna leave the seal alone until IV.2's first shape (*"we cut on [mute thing]… it did not [answer]"*) shows them what the mute stones beside the bundle are. Then Idrenna, the eldest of the guild, give leave. This seal is the hinge between IV.1 and IV.3.
3. **Halyna can read the slates but cannot sound them.** No one alive can sound the Hal. Halyna read it for its meaning, because the Theoliths taught them the old hand "to keep the count of the stones" (II.3). Idrenna's eyes are too old for small letters cut by lamplight, so it is Halyna who read the slates.
4. **One chest has three names, and there is a second chest.** "The Stonewrights' chest" (IV.1), "the archive chest" (IV.3) and "the chest I carried" (the foreword) are one object. Keep two names only, **the archive chest** (Seren's) and **the black chest** (the founders', in the shaft), and never let them blur.

## 3 · ONE NAME FOR TWO PEOPLE: THE DEVICE

Jack: *"some way in the text to show that Halyna is a compound name that is the single name for both people. Something simple… I also don't want to think this is a transgendered 'they/them' situation."* And: *"'So it was with Halvard and Rhyna'… they are telling the story. This is just a little off."*

### 3.1 On the page: the lintel [must]

- **Every pair-name (*Halyna*, *Aldwena*, *Idrenna*) is set under one thin stroke the width of the name**, as a capstone lies across two posts. The builder does this automatically for the three names (`<span class="pair">`, a top border in the ink colour), so the source text needs no markup. The plain-text fallback has no stroke, and the English carries the meaning there.
- **It is already seeded twice.** II.3 says the name is made "as a gate is made of two posts and the stone laid across them", and the native fold on the tale-stone says the vow is "signed under one lintel with the pair-name of *Aldwena*". The device draws what the Book already says.
- **Seren states it once, in the foreword, as her own custom:** she writes their one name under one stroke, as a lintel lies across two posts, because it is one name and it is the name of two. It is said nowhere else.
- **In the course-hand** (the *Original* tab), a pair-name stands between its mortar line and a second line above it, like a gate, and its verb takes the **dual** (Orrowen *-ana*; *Tuma Halyna*, "they two remember"). The English "the two of them" and "we two" translate that dual. This explanation goes in NOTES and never in the Book.

### 3.2 In the English: always two [must]

1. **"Halyna" always takes a plural verb.** Never write "Halyna is", "Halyna was" or "Halyna has".
2. **Show two people at least once in each tale, and at the first mention.** Use "Halyna, the two of them", "Halyna, he and she" or "Halyna both". Inside a paragraph, the first pronoun after the name should be *the two of them*, *both*, *he and she*, or one of their cradle-names. A bare *they* is fine only once the paragraph has already shown two people ("one of them… the other…").
3. **Prefer images of two over pronouns:** *their four hands*, *both their chisels*, *his hand and hers*, *one began and the other finished*.
4. **Wherever one of them acts, use the gendered singular** (*Rhyna… she*, *Halvard… he*). Each of these reminds the reader that Halyna are a man and a woman.
5. **Say "husband and wife" early**, once, in I.2 (it is there already), and once again in II.3.
6. **Never write** *themself*, *their own self*, or any construction that makes Halyna one person.

### 3.3 Halyna speak of themselves as "we two" [must]

- **The rule.** When Halyna speak or tell, they say **"we two"**, "the two of us" or "us two", and **never** "Halyna", "Halvard and Rhyna" or "they" about themselves. When one speaks of the other, it is "he" or "she", or the cradle-name.
- **It is older than they are.** The vow under the tale-stone, the oldest sentence in the Book, says *"We two laid it, and we two answer for it."* Every bonded pair has spoken this way since the founding. That needs no explaining, because the reader has already seen it on the first spread.
- **Lines to change:**
  - **I.4 headnote:** cut "They speak of themselves by name and from far off, as the annals speak of the dead…". It becomes moot anyway, because Seren now lays I.4 (§5.3).
  - **II.3**, Rhyna: "So it was with Halvard and Rhyna, who have never…" → *"So it was with us two, who have never in our lives slept a night under different roofs."*
  - **II.3**, Rhyna: "In that way Halvard and Rhyna were sealed, and named Halyna, and by that name you know us." → *"In that way we two were sealed, and named Halyna…"*
  - **II.3**, Rhyna: "So Halvard is Rhyna's husband and Rhyna is Halvard's wife" → *"So he is my husband and I am his wife, and we were one pair long before we were either."*
  - **II.3**, Halvard: "You have seen Halyna on the wall" → *"You have seen us two on the wall."* Also, "So he is Elder Halvard and she is Elderess Rhyna" → *"So I am Elder Halvard and she is Elderess Rhyna, and neither is the elder of the other."*
  - **II.3**, Rhyna: "If Rhyna cuts her hand in the south tower, Halvard feels it on the north wall" → *"If I cut my hand in the south tower, he feels it on the north wall, and knows which hand."*
  - **I.4, II.1, III.1, IV.1, V.1 and VI.1:** these were in third person throughout. Moving them to Seren's voice (or to the True Men's, for III.1) fixes them, and Seren speaks of Halyna in the third person with the §3.2 rules.
  - **Two-voice speech lines** ("one of them said, and the other finished") stay as they are. Seren is the one reporting them.

## 4 · THE PIECES: SEED AND PAYOFF

Each entry gives where the piece stands **now** (v1.3), the **gap**, and **the lock**: where it is seeded, where it is paid off, and what has to change.

### 4.1 The tale-stone and the granite-bloom

- **Now.** The foreword sets the old capstone in the hearth niche with Aldwena's Stonwryt, which pays off in II.1, where Halyna turn it over at dusk. "Whoever holds it tells."
- **Gap.** It gives nothing to any later tale. It is also too heavy to pass from hand to hand: "a stone that takes two" to turn.
- **The lock.**
  - [should] **The granite-bloom for the anointing is scraped from its upper face** (§4.5). The face that lay to the sky through all the forgotten years grew the bloom. II.1 seeds it in one clause (the bloom pale on the fallen stone), and I.4 pays it off.
  - [should] **Its weight.** A teller *lays both hands on it* where it sits by the fire. "Hold the stone" is the hearth's way of saying it, and "sets it back" becomes "takes their hands from it". This is a small fix for realism; it is Jack's call.
  - [must] **Aldwena's mark on its underside is the same mark Halyna find cut in the lid of the black chest** (I.4). They know whose chest it is before they open it. Drawn natively, the player sees the same mark on the first spread and again in I.4 without a word said.

### 4.2 The Stonwryt: each bonded pair's own sacred mark

- **Jack:** *"The mark is the Stonwryts. A symbol every bonded pair owns and holds sacred."*
- **Now.** II.1 calls it "a vow sealed in living rock, earned by toil and skill" (Jack's words). I.4 has Halyna cut "a Stonwryt… for his work… the first the guild ever cut for a soldier". VI.1 sends the coin.
- **Gap.** If the Stonwryt is a pair's own mark, a soldier cannot have one. Halyna's own mark never appears at all. And the chest lid "with the mark of the first builders" is left unexplained.
- **The lock** [must]:
  - **What it is.** When the seers read two names together at the cradle, what they read is the pair's mark, the written form of the bond God made before birth (II.3, Jack's ruling). The guild keeps the mark until the sealing. At the sealing a pair receive two things: **a name for the mouth and a mark for the stone.** The mark is theirs from the cradle, but **the right to cut it under a wall is earned by toil and skill** (Jack's words, kept). No two pairs' marks are alike, and no pair ever cuts another's. A pair cut their mark under every work they lay, as their promise that it will hold. They seal with it, and coin minted by master-pairs carries the minting pair's mark, which is where the coin gets its name: "trust, sealed in stone" (kept). The unbonded have none.
  - **Not a holy sign.** A pair hold their mark sacred, but it is not a sign of the faith, and no mark may be drawn in a cross-shape that could read as one.
  - **Where the four marks appear:**

| Mark | Seeded | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| **Aldwena's** | the foreword (tale-stone, drawn) · II.1 (defined: the founders cutting it) | I.4 (the chest lid) · *Read Down the Lintel* (at the head of the lintel's names, [may]) |
| **Halyna's** | **II.3: they draw it in the hearth-ash for the children** ("you may see it once, for after this it will be under stone"), drawn natively | I.4 (cut under the new capstone, unseen) · **VI.1 (cut across the join of the sending boat's stem)** · **Epilogue (on the returned boat, and on their grave)** |
| **the Eldhythe pair's** | IV.3 (the seal on Brenn's bundle, drawn broken) | (none: this mark marks a breaking) |
| **Seren's (none)** | II.3: "the unbonded keep one name all their lives" | Epilogue: she signs the Book with her one name, beneath Halyna's mark. [may] It is the mark the seers once read for her and Tarnel, found in the cradle-roll in her own chest and never cut (§4.8) |

  - **I.4 changes.** Under the new capstone **Halyna cut their own Stonwryt**, their vow as the pair who raised that gate, and beside it, for the first time under any capstone, **words that are not a vow: the Title, in the Captain's own unsteady strokes, copied stroke for stroke from the cloak**. That is the guild's mark for a soldier's work. Cut the sentence "the first the guild ever cut for a soldier". → NOTES: MAP §4.1's "the Captain's Stonwryt under the new capstone" becomes *Halyna's mark and the Title under the new capstone*.
  - **Why it pays.** The Title is cut in stone twice in the Book: once by Halyna, hidden under the gate, and once by the Last Carver, sent across the sea and stopped halfway (§4.20). Halyna's grave lies under their own vow (§4.4).
- [should] **Kael's oath-ending *Ston.*** is the last word of Aldwena's vow (Hal *STONOS*, "it stands"). Kael ends his oaths with the founders' last word without knowing it. Nothing needs saying; the *Original* tab shows it.

### 4.3 The black chest and the Rite of the Cornerstone texts

- **Now.** I.4: a chest of black stone at the foot of the shaft, holding the slate leaves of the Rite and the secrets of building for battle. The last leaf, in a smaller hand, holds the hearth custom.
- **Gap.** We are never told why the chest was there, why it was left, or what else was in it. "The first builders of the Keep had known war" hints at something and then stops.
- **The lock:**
  - [must] **What the chest holds:** (a) the slates of the Rite: the words, the three oils and where each is got, and **the law of the staff** (§4.6); (b) the slates of building for battle: walls sloped back to turn a shot, crooked gates, courses laid so a breach falls outward; (c) **the first staff**; (d) **three stoppered stone flasks, dry for generations**, which is why the oils must be made (§4.5); (e) **the founders' own annal of the raising**, which becomes the core of II.1; (f) **the hearth leaf**, at the very bottom.
  - [must] **Why it was there.** When the first builders went down to raise Eldhythe, they laid the Keep's things in the deepest vault, because *a staff does not leave its work*. The Keep was not stripped and abandoned. It was set in order and left, and then forgotten around its own chest. The line "It had never fallen. It had only been left." (II.1) gains a second meaning.
  - [should] **How Rhyna knew where to look.** It was a saying of her line, Aldwena's line, a cradle-rhyme no one had understood, which turns out to be directions to the foot of the shaft. That gives the rhyme as a find and keeps "she knew where to look, and he went down". [may] The rhyme could be a short verse, two lines of the hearth's best quality.
  - [should] **"The first builders had known war."** Keep it as a hint the Book never answers: *against what, the slates do not say.* Do not explain it, and do not tie it to the parting (Jack: it stays untold).
  - [should] **Payoff of the battle-slates.** In V.5, Kael's count includes a breach that **falls outward into the ditch "as the slate said it would"**, and the men behind it live. The slates found on the third night save lives years later, which the hearth can see.
  - [should] **Who finds the hearth leaf.** Choose one:
    - **(a)** Halyna, as now, but the "smaller hand" is a child's: the founders' youngest apprentice wrote the hearth's custom down.
    - **(b)** **Seren herself**, sent down with the lamp to lay the slates back, finds it.

    (b) is the one find that is Seren's own, and it gives her the vocation she keeps for the rest of the Book. Recommended: (b), with the child's hand from (a), so that the youngest apprentice of the first days and the youngest of the last both keep the hearth.
  - [must] **The wreck-planks are later laid in this same vault** (V.2, "the lowest vault, where the Rite was found"). The founders' vault comes to hold the enemy's words. → V.2 gets one clause, and nothing more.

### 4.4 The new capstone and the grave under the gate

- **Now.** The new capstone is cut and dressed, the Rite is done over it, it is raised that night, and Halyna are buried under it (Epilogue).
- **The lock:**
  - [must] **Under it lie Halyna's mark and the Title** (§4.2). In the Epilogue, Halyna lie "in one grave, with one stone, and one name on it". **The stone over them carries, unseen on its underside, the vow they cut on the night of the Rite.** They answer for the wall with their bodies, as their vow said they would.
  - [must] **"Two at the Gate" pays off here.** *"two at the gate when the grey sails gather, / two at the gate, and the gate stands through"*: the song the guild sang on the first night is, by the end, their epitaph. Seren need not say so. [should] At the Epilogue's burial, the hearth sings it.

### 4.13 The burning, and the custom of burning

## 6 · THE WOOD HOLDS ONLY WHAT THE WOOD COULD KNOW

### 6.1 The rule, and the seam

- **A wood leaf may hold:** what was cut into it; what it touched and felt; what came to it through the roots; what its makers put into it (the gift); its own counts in summers and rings; and the Mystaeri's own judgements of themselves.
- **It may not hold:** any name the shore gives (the Mystlands, the Mystholders, the fear-names, the grain-names, the root-men, the Bonded); what the shore thought, feared, wrote or rumoured; and any rule of the game.
- **The seam is Seren's.** Her headnote, the line she sets before an entry, or the facing stone leaf supplies the shore's side. Where the two disagree, her formula (*I have kept both*) does the combining in the open.
- **Three things the wood says well:** "we do not know why", "fear leaves no mark on wood", and "we did not know where they went". Keep them all; they are the rule made audible.

### 7.1 The Books' Arguments [must]

Each Book opens with 3 to 5 sentences in Seren's ink, with its time-stamp. It takes the context the tales now stop to explain.

- **Book One · The Wall:** *the day of the banner and the Forty Days after it.* The column, the cloak, the cry, the Rite, the naming. Laid in the first year of the banner.
- **Book Two · The Builders:** *the First Days, long before the havens*, and the wood's home, older still. The Keep left, not fallen. What a bonded pair is, as Halyna tell it to the children.
