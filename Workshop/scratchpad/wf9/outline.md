## 0 · THE SHORT VERSION

1. **The frame.**
   - The tales are laid at the Long Hearth after each assault.
   - **Seren Two-Inks** sets every tale in measured Orrowen, a line just short of song. The Book is the English of her verse, and Plain Words unwinds it.
   - The hearth answers every stone tale with ***We remember.***, as a congregation says Amen. **Old Ebba of Fenholm** said it first.
2. **The clock** (§3).
   - Every date counts from **the banner**: the dusk the Captain lifted the torn cloak.
   - The siege lasts **six winters, with a Tide each winter**.
   - The Rite falls on **the fourteenth day**, once the oils are made (the hearth calls the whole stretch **the Fourteen Nights**).
   - The havens come home **in the sixth year**, Halyna die in **the seventh winter**, and the Stone comes in **the seventh spring**.
   - Every Book opens with a one-line **Argument**, and every tale carries a two-clause **dateline**: when it was laid, and when its events happened.
3. **The order** (§3.4).
   - The Book begins at the banner, reaches back (Builders, Havens, Wound) and comes forward again (Tides, Homecomings).
   - Two changes from v1.3:
     - **Book Three now holds only the havens' raising and fall.**
     - **The Homecomings and the Thrones move to Book Six, the Book of the Homecomings, where they face each other.**
   - The play order is the hearth's order, the nights on which the tales were laid. It is listed in §3.5.
4. **Halyna discover, Seren writes, and other hands act** (§5.3).
   - Halyna keep the cry, the finds, the making of the oils, the reading aloud of Brenn, the knowings, their own mark, the telling of II.3 and the kneeling.
   - **Idrenna** (the eldest pair) make the terms and anoint the Captain.
   - **Orvenna** are the wall-masters.
   - **Enrella** are the young pair, sealed in the first summer.
   - **Wendhessa** heard Brenn's confession.
   - **The True Men** tell their own havens.
   - **Seren** tells every stone tale that Halyna used to tell about themselves.
5. **One name for two** (§4).
   - Every pair-name is printed under a thin stroke, like a lintel over two posts. Seren says why, once, in the foreword.
   - A pair-name always takes a plural verb.
   - Halyna say *we two* of themselves, and never their own names.
   - A single *they* is never used for one person.
6. **The dead** (§6).
   - Twelve deaths (D1 to D12), and every one is planted earlier:
     - the Guest (beaten to death and thrown broken into his boat);
     - the axemen of two rammed boats;
     - the warden and Brenn;
     - Tarnel and Wendhessa (the night of the pass);
     - Jory, Corlen, Wick, Enno and Della (Enrella), Pellow, Old Ebba, Hesk;
     - Halyna, in peace.
   - The Captain lives. He cuts a strip from his half of the torn cloak for each of the dead.
7. **The runners and the calls** (§7.6).
   - The horn is gone. The Captain calls **with his own voice, standing at the battery's side**, so he can call only one battery at a time.
   - **The runners are his eyes.** They carry what is seen to wherever he stands, breaches to the masons, and marked planks to Halyna.
   - Brenn, the outpost's runner, once carried a lie. The wall's runners swear by the oath he broke.
8. **The Wound, reshaped** (IV.3 to IV.6).
   - The killing is brutal, and the body is thrown into the boat like a torn net.
   - The coast's old custom gives the burning its reason: *what the grey sends, the fire sends back, or it comes again.*
   - The burning boat rams **two** timber-boats of the felling. Three men live, and they start the rumour. The ship-masters say **twenty**.
   - The fear helps stop the axes in the same summers the grove votes for war.
9. **The Stonwryt is each pair's sacred mark** (§7.2).
   - The seers read it at the cradle as the written form of a bond God made before birth.
   - It is given at the sealing, and a pair earn the right to cut it under their own work.
   - Four marks recur: Aldwena's, Halyna's, Enrella's, and Wendhessa's on the seal.
10. **The finds** (§7):
    - **the black chest**, holding the Rite, the battle-slates, the first staff, three dry flasks and the founders' annal;
    - **the oils** Halyna had to make;
    - **the staff**: one of generations of staffs, and the first, left behind by the law that *a staff does not leave its work*;
    - **the archive chest** Seren and Tarnel carried a handle each;
    - **the sealed bundle**, which holds Brenn's confession wrapped round the gift;
    - **the two halves of the first heart**, which become the stem of the sending boat;
    - **the green sliver**, the first growth a pillar has put up since the iron.
11. **The grain knows only the wood's side** (§9). Every shore name, every shore thought, and the whole of the Shoreland history come from Seren's headnotes and the stone tales.
12. **Explanation moves out of the story** into scene, speech, song, the Book's Argument, or the NOTES document (§8, under each tale).
13. **Every verse is kept**, and Jack's fixed lines stand word for word (§10). A few new short verses are offered as [should] or [may].
14. **The NOTES document** (§11) takes everything about how the Book was made and how it enters the game.

## 2 · THE FRAME

### 2.1 The hearth

- **The place.**
  - The Long Hearth is in the hall behind the inner gate, under the wall where the torn cloak flies.
  - A tale is laid in the hours after an assault, "when the guns are cooling and the mortar has not yet set" (keep).
- **The tale-stone.**
  - It is the old capstone of the inner gate, set in a niche of the hearth-wall, with Aldwena's mark and vow on its underside.
  - It takes two to turn. The teller lays both hands on it in its niche [may].
  - "Give me the stone" is how a teller asks for the turn. "Hold the stone" means *tell*. Seren's own phrase, "hold the lamp", means *see*.
- **A tale is *laid*, not told.** "A tale that is only told is gone by morning, and a tale that is laid is part of the wall" (keep).
- **The Captain** sits at the end of the bench with his hood up. **When the dead are named, he puts the hood back** [should]. He speaks only to say that it was not like that.
- **The custom is the founders'.** Seren found it on the hearth leaf at the bottom of the black chest (I.4).
- **The Invocation is spoken before the first tale of every night.**
- **The fixed formulas.** Repeat them exactly, every time; the listener learns them as a congregation learns a liturgy.
  - A stone teller's close: *This I lay as it was laid for me.* (Halyna, together: *This we lay as it was laid for us.*)
  - Its answer: *And the hearth said: We remember.*
  - A wood leaf's frame: it opens *This is held in the grain.*, closes *and the grain holds it still.*, and is followed by *And no one answered.*
  - The epithets: Kael the Counter, oath-keeper of the True Men · Hale the runner · Voss who asked · Seren Two-Inks · Halyna, the Bonded of the Keep · Halvard Stone-Warden · Rhyna of the Inner Gate · each True Man's "of the …" · the Kneeling Guest · the Aelvaren, the Bearer of the Binding.

### 2.2 *We remember*: the hearth's Amen (J16)

- **What it is.**
  - After every stone tale, the whole hearth says it together: ***We remember.*** It is Jack's line, and it never varies.
  - It is said the way a congregation says Amen: the hearth's yes to a tale laid true.
  - It comes from the first words of the Title, which the Captain wrote in coal from this hearth.
  - In Orrowen it is *Tumar*, which is also how Orrowen says yes. That stays in NOTES.
- **Where it began [should].**
  - On the night Kael laid I.1, he took his hands from the stone and no one knew what to say.
  - Old Ebba of Fenholm said it, from the first words of the Title, and the hearth took it from her.
  - After that she began it most nights, a breath before the rest.
  - After she dies (end of the sixth winter), the hearth waits a breath for her, then says it without her.
  - Her great-granddaughter is the first to say it to the wood (VI.3).
- **Never after a wood leaf until VI.3.** A wood leaf ends *And no one answered.*, and Seren writes the silence down.
- **The one name the hearth does not remember** is the warden's, ▒▒▒▒.
  - When Halyna read the confession aloud and reach a blot, they stop and say nothing, and the hearth hears a silence where the name was.
  - After IV.3 the hearth answers *We remember*. It is the first time the hearth says it over a killing done by our own people. (It has already remembered the felling, IV.1, and the forgetting, II.1.)
- **At a death**, the tale that tells it ends with the answer, and the Captain's hood goes back.
- **It is never a joke, never a Whisper, and never in store art.**

### 2.4 Seren, the poet-scribe, and the two tongues (J12)

- **Who she is.**
  - She was twelve at the banner, unbonded, and Halyna's third.
  - Her name means "a single sorrow that overcomes".
  - She holds the lamp. By custom the unbonded hold the lamp at sealings and knowings [should].
- **Her craft is words.**
  - She does not only record. Every tale laid at the hearth she sets that night into **measured Orrowen**: lines just short of song, laid as a mason lays a course, so many stresses to a line with the joints broken, so that the line stands when it is said aloud.
  - She **says each word aloud three times before she writes it, to hear whether it will hold**. That is her version of Stannard's knuckle on a stone [should].
  - She **enjoys the tongue**. The foreword says so in one plain sentence of her own, and the joy shows wherever she lingers on a word (*ael*, *Aelthae*, the one word in the middle of a page).
- **She does not change a teller's words.** Kael "would not let me change a word of it, and I have not" (I.1, keep). She sets his words in her lines. The words are his and the measure is hers.
- **Her habits.**
  - She keeps Tarnel's netting-needle in the Book as its marker.
  - She binds the leaves with the knot he taught her [should].
- **The three layers of every tale** (in-world, and as the team's workflow):
  1. **Orrowen, in Seren's ink**: the Book as she wrote it (the *Original* tab).
  2. **The Book**: the English, a translation that keeps her measure, her refrains and her line.
  3. **Plain Words**: the same tale unwound into easy modern prose, paragraph for paragraph.
- **The team's workflow.** Following Jack ("Give the stories feeling, then translate them into the other language"):
  - the poets write the English with full feeling first;
  - the Tongues team then sets it in measured Orrowen;
  - then it is unwound into Plain Words.
- **The two inks.** They now mark Halyna's paragraphs in II.3 and in the wood tales, and the two halves of every line Halyna speak between them. She will not mark whose ink is whose (keep): "You cannot tell."
- **The Orrowen measure** (for the Tongues team; the details go to NOTES):
  - a line is a *course* of three to five stressed words;
  - bonded speech comes in paired half-lines;
  - songs rhyme by their **closing consonants**, as the True Men's lay already does in the Orrowen (Jack: keep);
  - Kael's oaths end *Ston.*;
  - the chisel's economy in cut stone (word-stones only), while the ink hand writes every small word.

### 2.5 The form: an epic told at a hearth (J1)

**From the Odyssey (the format).**
- Books, each headed by its **Argument**, with the Invocation as the proem.
- **In medias res**: the Book opens on the banner and reaches back.
- **Catalogues**: the roll of the Twelve (I.1), the havens (III.1 and III.2), the Thrones (VI.2), the cargo count (IV.1) and the roll of the dead (V.5 Count VI).
- **Fixed epithets and formula lines**, repeated without variation.
- **Speeches set off** with a formula ("one of them said, and the other finished").
- **Songs** set into the tales.
- **The law of the guest** (IV.3, IV.4).
- **Two homecomings**: ours and theirs (Book Six).
- **Recognition by token**: the long sleeves, the boat's stem, the chisels.
- **A bard inside the story**: Seren.
- **Listeners who interrupt**: a child's question opens IV.4's holding; the children in II.3.
- **One extended simile at most at each impact moment** ("as when…"), drawn from the world: stone, mortar, nets, the sea, fever, bread.

**From Tolkien (how the world is built). Take the methods, never the sentences.**
- **Annal distance**, broken by sudden close scenes.
- **Genealogies**:
  - Aldwena, then Rhyna; the quarry-wardens, then Halvard;
  - Elspet, then Daveth, then Merrick;
  - Ebba, then Marl, then the child;
  - Tarnard, then Tarnel.
- **Many names for one thing**: Mystholders and Naelsaen; Mystarchs, Thrones and their true names; the fog, the grey and the breath.
- **Found documents**: the founders' annal, the rolls, the report, Brenn's confession.
- **Belief told as what the Stonewrights hold.**
- **The long defeat**: each victory shadowed by its cost.
- **Eucatastrophe**: the green sliver.

**The line on the page.**
- **[should]** The stone tellings are set in **measured lines**: unrhymed long lines of about five to seven beats, one or two clauses to a line, in verse paragraphs. **An impact line is short and stands alone.**
- Seren's headnotes and datelines, the annals, the rolls, the report and the warning stay in **plain prose**. This is the cool annal voice against the measured tale.
- The fallback is measured prose paragraphs, as in v1.3.
- **The wood tales are never set in lines.** They stay in rings of plain full sentences, with the grain's formulas. The more the stone sings, the more the grain should sound like the wood (J23).

### 2.6 The impact rules (every tale)

1. **Each tale has one to three ★ impact moments**, shown up close and in scene, and each is preceded by a quiet beat: the wind in the grass, the lamp burning down a finger's width. The draft names them.
2. **The blow is short**: a short sentence, or a short line, after long ones. ("We obeyed." · "It was green." · "One," he said.)
3. **Nothing is explained within a breath of an impact moment.** The explanation goes earlier, into a speech, the headnote, the Argument or NOTES (§8, under each tale).
4. **Every tale has a speech.** Most have a refrain.
5. **A person who will die is seen at least twice before**, doing their habit and wanting their want (§5.2).
6. **A death is followed by three things:** someone else says the dead person's line; the hearth answers; the Captain's hood goes back.
7. **Every tale stands alone** at about 300 to 900 words. IV.3, V.7 Part I, VI.3 and the Epilogue may run longer.
8. **The Book only adds.** Where a later fact belongs under an earlier tale, it is a short line *Added in Seren's hand*, never a rewrite. Several of the deaths use this device (§8).

## 3 · THE CLOCK AND THE ORDER (J3)

### 3.1 The hearth's reckoning

- **There is one hinge: the banner.** The Rivenmen count from the dusk the Captain lifted the torn cloak (deep winter, with snow on the hearth).
- **Distances before the banner** are counted in generations of the shore, about twenty-five years each ("three generations before the banner").
- **Inside the siege, the count is by winters.** Each siege year runs from its winter to the next, so "the second spring" is the spring after the second winter.
- **The wood counts in summers and rings and never knows the banner.** Seren converts its count in her headnote; that is part of how she combines the histories (J24).
- **The Mystaeri live two and a half of our lives.** One of their lives is about three of ours.

## 4 · ONE NAME FOR TWO PEOPLE: THE HALYNA DEVICE AND THE PRONOUN RULE (J2, J13)

### 4.1 On the page: the lintel [must]

- **The stroke.** Every pair-name is set under one thin stroke the width of the name, as a capstone lies across two posts. The pair-names are Halyna, Aldwena, Idrenna, Orvenna, Enrella and Wendhessa.
  - The builder sets it automatically. The source text needs no markup.
  - In plain text the stroke drops, and the English rules below carry the meaning.
- **The Book has already seeded it.** II.3: the name is made "as a gate is made of two posts and the stone laid across them". The tale-stone's vow is "signed under one lintel". The device draws what the Book already says.
- **Seren says it once, as her own custom, in the foreword** (sample of tone): *"I write their one name under one stroke, as a lintel lies across two posts; for it is one name, and it is the name of two."* It is said nowhere else.

### 4.2 In the English: always two [must]

1. **A pair-name takes a plural verb**, as a couple's name does: *Halyna are*, *Halyna were*. Never *Halyna is* or *Halyna was*.
2. **At the first mention in every tale, show two people**: "Halyna, the two of them" · "Halyna, he and she" · "Halyna both" · the cradle-names.
3. ***They* only after two have been shown** in that paragraph ("one of them… the other…"). Prefer images of two: *their four hands* · *both their chisels* · *his hand and hers* · *one began and the other finished*.
4. **When one of them acts, use the gendered singular and the cradle-name**: *Rhyna… she* · *Halvard… he*. Each one reminds the reader that Halyna are a man and a woman, husband and wife.
5. **Say "husband and wife" twice**: in I.2 (it is there already) and in II.3.
6. **Never** *themself*, *their own self*, or any construction that makes Halyna one person. **No singular *they* is ever used for anyone.**
7. **Use the pair-name less in scenes.** It is the guild's name for them and the hearth's. In a scene, *he and she*, the cradle-names and their two hands do the work.

### 4.3 When Halyna speak: "we two" [must]

- **The rule.** Halyna speak of themselves as **"we two"**, "the two of us", "us two", and never as *Halyna*, *Halvard and Rhyna* or *they*. When one speaks of the other: *he*, *she*, or the cradle-name.
- **The custom is older than they are.** The oldest sentence in the Book, the vow on the tale-stone, says *"We two laid it, and we two answer for it."* Every bonded pair has spoken so since the founding. The reader has seen it on the first spread, so it needs no explaining.
- **The lines that change in II.3** (samples):
  - "So it was with Halvard and Rhyna, who have never…" → *"So it has been with us two, who have never in our lives slept a night under different roofs."*
  - "In that way Halvard and Rhyna were sealed…" → *"In that way we two were sealed, and named Halyna, and by that name you know us."*
  - "So Halvard is Rhyna's husband…" → *"So he is my husband and I am his wife, and we were one pair long before we were either."*
  - "You have seen Halyna on the wall" → *"You have seen us two on the wall."*
  - "So he is Elder Halvard and she is Elderess Rhyna" → *"So I am Elder Halvard and she is Elderess Rhyna, and neither is the elder of the other."*
  - "If Rhyna cuts her hand…" → *"If I cut my hand in the south tower, he feels it on the north wall, and knows which hand."*
- **Every stone tale that Halyna told of themselves in v1.3 is now Seren's** (I.4, II.1, IV.1, V.1, VI.3). Seren may speak of them in the third person, by the rules of §4.2. That fixes the problem at the root.
- **Two-voice lines stay** ("one of them said, and the other finished"). Seren is the one reporting them.
- **In the Orrowen** a pair-name stands under a lintel line in the course-hand, and its verb takes the dual (*-ana*). The English *we two* and *the two of them* translate that dual. NOTES only.

## 5 · THE CAST

### 5.1 The laws of the cast

1. **A want, a habit, a line.** Everyone named has all three. The want is what a death cuts off or a homecoming pays. The habit is what the listener sees each time the person comes in. The line is what the hearth says back after they are gone.
2. **Deaths are fixed points in the war** (§6). No death depends on how the game is played.
3. **Halyna discover, Seren writes, and other hands do the work** (§5.3).
4. **The Captain** is never named, never tells, and lives.
5. **No one sails in the grey fleet.** The ship is the commander.
6. **The warden's name** is always exactly four marks, ▒▒▒▒, in stone. In wood he never had one: he is "the master of that house", "the host".

## 7 · THE THINGS AND THE CUSTOMS THAT LOCK THE TALES TOGETHER (J11)

*The puzzle pieces, held as objects. None of them is explained in the Book. Each sits in two or more tales and waits for the listener to see it.*

### 7.1 The motif that tests every lock

**A half waiting for its other half.**
- The bond makes two into one.
- The Guest's rite was left half made.
- The cloak was torn down the back.
- The first heart split into two planks.
- The Last Carver stopped his Title in the middle of a line.
- *Halyna* is one name made of two.
- Every facing spread pairs a stone tale with a wood tale.

A piece is locked when its other half turns up somewhere else in the Book.

### 7.2 The Stonwryt: each bonded pair's sacred mark (J8) [must]

- **What it is.**
  - When the seers read two names together at the cradle, what they read is the pair's mark: the written form of the bond God made before either drew breath.
  - The guild keeps the mark until the sealing. There a pair receive **a name for the mouth and a mark for the stone**.
  - The mark is theirs from the cradle. **The right to cut it under a wall is earned by toil and skill** (Jack's words, kept in II.1: "a vow sealed in living rock, earned by toil and skill").
  - A pair cut their mark, with the vow beside it, under every work they answer for. They seal with it. Coin minted by master-pairs carries the minting pair's mark, which is where the coin gets its name ("trust, sealed in stone", kept).
  - No two marks are alike, and **no pair ever cuts or breaks another pair's mark**.
  - The unbonded have none.
- **It is held sacred, but it is not a sign of the faith.** No pair's mark is drawn as a cross (J25).
- **The four marks:**

### 7.3 The black chest

- **What it holds:**
  - (a) the Rite's slate leaves: the words, the three oils and where each is got, and **the law of the staff**;
  - (b) the battle-slates: walls sloped to turn a shot, crooked gates, courses laid so a breach falls outward into the ditch;
  - (c) **the first staff**;
  - (d) **three stoppered stone flasks, dry for generations**;
  - (e) **the founders' annal of the raising**, in the Hal, in Aldwena's "we two";
  - (f) at the very bottom, **the hearth leaf**, in a child's hand.
- **Why it was there.** When the founders went down to raise Eldhythe, they set the Keep's things in order in its deepest vault, *for a staff does not leave its work*. The Keep was not stripped and abandoned. It was set in order and left, and then forgotten around its own chest. That is the second meaning of "It had never fallen. It had only been left." (II.1).
- **Halyna can read the Hal for its meaning, but cannot sound it.** The Theoliths taught them the old hand "to keep the count of the stones" (II.3).
- **"The first builders had known war."** This stays a hint the Book never answers: *against what, the slates do not say.* It is never tied to the parting (J25).
- **The payoff.** In V.5 Count I a breach falls outward into the ditch *as the slate said it would*, and the men behind it live.
- **The same vault later holds the enemy's words:** the planks (V.2).

### 7.5 The staff, and the generations of staffs (J10) [must]

- **There are generations of staffs, and this one is the first.**
  - Every great work had its own staff, cut when its first stone was laid.
  - **The law of the staff**, on the slates: *a staff is cut for its work, and does not leave it; when the work is done, the staff is laid down in it.*
  - Jack's description is kept word for word, with one clause added: *"…borne by every overseer of a great Stonewright work since the founding; and this was the first of them."*
  - **Its wood is ridge-cedar, under the polish and the ashlar cap of Jack's description, and older than the pride.** (Do not call it "plain"; Jack's words say polished.) [may] The later staffs of the Harvest years were cut from the black wood.
- **Why it was left behind:** by law, when the founders went down. Not by neglect.
- **The havens' staffs.** III.2 says that when the Stonewrights walked out of each falling haven, *they left each haven's staff in its falling walls, for a staff does not leave its work.* All ten were lost. The Captain holds the only one left.
- **Why he leans it in the gatehouse.** The work is the wall and the war, and the gatehouse is the work's heart. A staff does not go into battle, and he would not carry it if it did. Law and humility are both true, and the dust keeps its meaning ("go and look at the dust on it", kept).
- **The payoff [should] (Epilogue).**
  - The staff is gone from the corner. No one saw him take it.
  - *The slates say that when the work is done, the staff is laid down in it. I think I know where it lies, and I have not gone down to look.*
- **[may]** One homecoming finds a lost staff: Ruan, in Glasspire's cellars. One at most.

### 7.6 The runners, the calls, and Brenn (J7, J19) [must]

- **The horn is gone.** The guns eat a horn's voice, and a horn cannot say which battery it means.
- **The Captain calls with his own voice, standing at the battery's side.**
  - A man can stand in only one place, so **he calls one battery at a time**.
  - In the holding he walks the wall at his own pace, to wherever the next moment will be, and the hour goes long around him as he goes. That is how he reaches a battery in time.
  - Kael keeps it from being magic: *"It is not the world that slows, lad. It is the Captain who will not hurry. The moment he chose was the moment that came."*
- **The True Men command their batteries by knowing his mind.** Before the sails come he tells each one where to watch, what shot to lay and when to hold. In the holding each fights his battery by his own judgement.
- **The three calls** stay word for word: *Shift your ground* · *Change your shot* (for the water, or the shore, or the sky) · *Hold, or loose*. [should] They are the True Men's morning drill, a three-line chant that Hale hears every dawn (new verse).
- **The runners are the Captain's eyes.** They carry what is seen, and nothing else, to three places:
  - to wherever the Captain stands: what a battery's lookout sees through Pellow's far-glass that the Captain cannot (a hull hanging back, a sail going pale, a hull riding low, root-men in a gully). **His calls are only as good as their word.** Hale's work is to find him;
  - to the masons: where the wall is broken;
  - to Halyna: a plank with marks on it. After a holding the runners walk the tide-line, so it was a runner who first saw the marks (V.1).
- **Why the runner runs past the other batteries to the Captain.** Each True Man fights his own water by the mind the Captain gave him at dawn, and only the Captain, who sees the whole wall, may change that mind. So what is seen goes to him, and the change comes from his own mouth, one battery at a time. A word shouted along the wall would be lost in the guns, or turn into rumour on the way. (This is the in-world reason; say it once, in Hale's mouth in I.3, in a sentence.)
- **A runner's word is short:** three or four words, like a chisel-cut. *"Hull hangs back."* · *"Marks on the wood."* · *"They are looking."* · *"They are not green."*
- **The runners' oath**, an old oath of the havens' runners: *"What I saw, I carry. I carry nothing else."*
  - Every haven had a runners' stone. In the first week at the Keep the runners set one in the ward, a fallen block by the inner gate, and Hale swore on it there.
  - Brenn swore it at fifteen on the runners' stone at Eldhythe, and broke it.
  - The morning after IV.3, the wall's runners swear it again and add four words: ***"Not as Brenn carried."***
- **The locks.** Hale keeps the law; Brenn broke it. The Guest's three words were carried by no one; Wick's three words were carried and understood.

### 7.10 The ramming (J21, J22) [must]

## 9 · THE GRAIN HOLDS ONLY WHAT THE WOOD COULD KNOW (J24)

### 9.1 The rule

- **A wood tale may hold:**
  - what was cut into the wood;
  - what it touched and felt (weight, feet, fire, water, cold, blows);
  - what came to it through the roots;
  - what a child of the long-lived knew in his last hour, if his blood ran into it;
  - what its makers put into it (the gift);
  - what the wood's hands, the root-men, touched;
  - its own counts, in summers and rings;
  - the Mystaeri's own judgements of themselves.
- **It may not hold:**
  - any name the shore gives: the Mystlands, the Mystholders, the fear-names, the grain-names, the root-men, the Bonded, the warden's name;
  - what the shore thought, feared, wrote, rumoured or knew;
  - any rule of the game.
- **The seam is Seren's.** She combines the histories in her headnotes, in the line before each Throne, and in the facing stone tale, never in the wood's mouth. Where the two sides disagree, she says so in the open: *I have kept both.*
- **Three things the wood says well,** which keep the rule audible: *"we do not know why"* · *"fear leaves no mark on wood"* · *"we did not know where they went"*.
- **The first spread's grain stays unmarked** until the Epilogue. It is the Stone's own rings, with Seren's line beneath them.
