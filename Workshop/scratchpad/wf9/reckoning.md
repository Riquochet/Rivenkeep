## *A master timeline for the Legends of Rivenkeep*

*For Jack, and for the reshaping that follows his notes of 2026-09-30. Built from the Book (Legends v1.3.0, `wf7/book/legends_v13.md`; "L123" below means its line 123), the Story Bible, the Legends Plan (§12, its old chronology), the Revision Plan v1.1, the Fiction Hooks, the Admiral doc (the Tides, the carvings, the Thrones) and the Tongues doc §1 (lore only). ## 0 · THE SHORT VERSION

- **One axis.** Every date is counted from the day the Captain lifted the torn cloak. That day is **the banner**, written **B0**. Years before it are written **B−75** and so on. Inside the siege, years are counted by winters: **W1** is the first winter of the siege (the winter of the banner), **W2** the second, and each siege year runs from its winter to the next.
- **The Book already keeps a calendar, but it is never stated.** Every season and month the current headnotes and counts name fits one rule: **a Tide each winter.** The Hasty Tide fills the first year, the Second Tide comes with the second winter, the Tide of Remembering with the third, and so on. Eight anchors in the text all fit, and none breaks it (§1.3). I recommend making it the law of the Book. It gives the siege **six winters**, the homecomings in the sixth year, Halyna's death in the seventh winter, and the Stone in the seventh spring.
- **The deep past holds together** on the Plan's old count, sharpened. The felling runs from B−120 to B−70. The Guest comes in the last summers of it, at B−75. The council votes on the night the Aelvaren comes home. The green wood lies in the grey for some seventy years. The Fall burns the ten havens over twenty months, from B−2 to B0.
- **Eight muddles break the clock** (§5, A1–A8), and two dozen smaller ones only blur it. The worst:
  - the hearth's custom is found after the Rite, but three tales are laid before anyone dates the Rite;
  - the green wood's seventy-year wait has no cause;
  - the Law of the Years counts a tree's age in two different ways;
  - the Thrones' coming is never dated, yet "the old songs" know them;
  - two stone leaves know things a Tide before the wood has told them;
  - several wood leaves use the Rivenmen's own names, which the wood cannot know;
  - the burning of the boat has a reason that contradicts the report.
- **The dating system** (§1) can be followed without a table, and **each Book gets an argument** (§6).
- **§7 places your other notes on the clock:** Brenn's ride, the ramming, the oils, the staff, the runner, the warden's name, and the deaths.

## 1 · THE DATING SYSTEM

### 1.1 The axis

- **B0** is the day of the banner: dusk, in deep winter, with snow on the hearth (I.1, L166). The Rivenmen would count it so, every year of the war from the banner, as the banner is from the Title.
- **A Shoreland generation** is about twenty-five years.
- **A Mystaeri life** is two and a half of ours ("We live twice as long as you and half again", II.2, L436), so one of their lives is about three of ours ("We listened three of their lives", V.4, L1061).

### 1.3 Inside the siege: a Tide each winter

- **The rule.** Each winter a Tide comes out of the grey.
  - The first winter brings the last of the green wood, the Hasty Tide.
  - The second winter brings the Second Tide.
  - Then come the Tide of Remembering, the Joined Tide and the Tide that Learned Deceit.
  - The sixth winter brings the Last Tide, with the Thrones behind it.
- **Why the wood keeps that rule** (true to the wood, and nothing new). A growing is ready when its ring is laid, and a ring is laid by summer's end. So each growing goes out at leaf-fall and comes out of the grey with the winter. The older the wood, the longer it took to lead into a hull, so each growing came ready a year after the last. The reader needs none of this. The rule is enough, and it can be said once, in V.6.
- **What the reader says:** *the third winter of the siege, in the Tide of Remembering*; *the second spring*; *the fifth autumn*. The Tide and the winter always agree, so either one dates a tale.
- **The anchors already in the text, all kept:**
  1. "at the end of the first month" (V.1, L916), which falls inside Cam 1;
  2. "the first spring" (I.4, L308), Cam 2;
  3. "the first summer" (II.1, L378), Cam 3;
  4. "the first year" (II.3, L458), Cam 4;
  5. "the first autumn" (III.1, L503), Cam 5;
  6. "in the winter after" (III.1, L503), Cam 8, the second winter;
  7. "the third month of that Tide" (V.5 II, L1103), Cam 9, which is where the Admiral doc puts the first answering sail;
  8. "the second month of this Tide" (V.5 III, L1117), Cam 16, which is where the Admiral doc puts the first look.

  At six to eight campaigns a year, a campaign is one to two months, and every anchor lands in its campaign.

### 1.4 The dateline under every tale (for the reader's document)

Under each title there are two short clauses in small capitals, always in the same order: **when it was laid**, then **when its matter happened**, with the distance said once.

- *Laid in the first spring of the siege. Of the first month at the Keep.* (I.4)
- *Laid in the second spring of the siege, in the Second Tide. Of the days of the felling, five generations to three before the banner.* (IV.1)
- *Known in the third spring of the siege, in the Tide of Remembering. Of the last summer of the felling, seventy-five years before the banner.* (IV.4)

A wood leaf's dateline belongs to Seren's headnote and never to the wood's voice, for the wood counts in summers and rings and never in shore years. This answers your "Before the destruction? After? Before the banner is lifted? How far afterwards?" at the head of every tale, without explaining inside the tale.

### 1.5 Optional: a count for the reader

The standalone reader's document could end with one page in Seren's hand, *The Count of Winters*, kept from Kael's counts and the rolls. It would give one line to each age and one to each winter, and hold only what the Rivenmen know. Do not call it "the Tale of Years", which is Tolkien's appendix title.

### 2.8 The siege, year by year

**Year 1 · the first winter · the Hasty Tide · Cam 1–6**

## 5 · THE MUDDLES

*A breaks the clock or the knowledge, B blurs it for a reader, and C is small. Each entry gives what the text says, why it does not fit, and the fix, with the cheapest fix first and the recommended one marked.*

### A · They break the clock

**A1 · The hearth begins after the Rite, but three tales are laid before anyone dates the Rite.**
- **What the text says.** The custom of laying a tale is found in the last leaf of the chest "until the Rite was done" (I.4, L330). The tale-stone is set in the niche only "when the Rite was done" (II.1, L402). Yet:
  - I.1 is laid on "the first night this hearth asked for a tale" (L160);
  - I.2 "on the night after the first assault the Rivenmen held" (L233);
  - V.1 "at the end of the first month" (L916).
- **Why it fails.** Nothing dates the Rite. Your note that Halyna had to make the oils puts days or weeks between the chest (night 3) and the Rite.
- **Fix A (recommended).** The Rite comes on about the fourteenth day, when the oils are made. The Naming comes the week after (I.5, L346), and the first assault after the Naming (§2.7). No word of I.4 changes. I.2's headnote, "the first assault the Rivenmen held", then becomes literally true, since they were named Rivenmen days before. The wall's orders also say "Commander" from the first assault.
- **Fix B.** Hold the Rite until the first spring, perhaps because the granite-bloom must flower. Then the hearth-leaf must be found on night 3 with the chest, and the old capstone carried into the hall at once. That costs a change to I.4 (L330) and to II.1 (L402).

**A2 · The green wood waited seventy years, and nothing says why or what sent it.**
- **What the text says.** V.6: "young wood grows quickest: one season… It lay at the edge of your sea while your children had children" (L1193); "the armada sailed generations before it arrived" (L1195). Yet the green wood's whole nature is "Wait on no bough" (V.1 facing leaf; V.2, L969).
- **Why the wait is needed.** The seventy years are load-bearing:
  - Brenn's forty years;
  - "The Shorelanders of the final days remembered nothing at all";
  - Voss's grandmother;
  - the late humility.
- **Fix E (recommended).** The vote carried the growing, but while one of the elders who pleaded still lived, the council would not cut the sending. "The elders who had pleaded went down into the moss one by one" (V.4, L1077) is already the hinge. One clause adds the rest: when the last of them went, the young cut the sending, and the green wood came out of the grey. The wood can know it, and nothing on the Shoreland side has to be revealed. The irony fits the puzzle: the patience the young scorned bought the shore seventy years, and the shore spent them forgetting. It also explains "No voice among us can call us home" (L1217).
- **Fix G (compatible with E).** Glasspire's great glass, finished in the forgetting (III.2, L623: "where it fell the breath burned away"), was the last wound that broke the last patience.

**A3 · The Law of the Years counts a tree's age in two ways.**
- **The conflict.**
  - IV.2's heart is a Second Tide hull ("forty summers") grown from "the last shoot of a stump cut low where the iron first went in" (L716), so it sprouted around B−120. Counted at its sailing in W2, it is about 120 summers old.
  - V.6's heart is "grown in the first groves their council set" (L1167), which reads as planted at the vote. Counted at its choosing, it would be a seedling, which is green wood and not a Joined Tide.
- **Fix (recommended).** A wood's years **at the choosing** set what it can hold. Read "grown in the first groves" as the grove where it was led into a hull, not where it was planted. Then IV.2 is forty summers at the vote, which fits, and V.6's tree was an old tree led in the first groves, which also fits. This also explains why the late Tides came last: old trees take longest to lead (§2.4). Say it once in V.6, in one clause, or leave the reader to feel it.

**A4 · The Thrones' coming is never dated, yet "the old songs" know them.**
- **What the text says.** III.2 says they came "when the groves had sent all they had" (L593), which is after the Last Tide sailed. I.5, at the end of Cam 32, says one sits in every haven (L362). But III.2's headnote says "The old songs name them the sovereign commanders of the entire armada" (L583). And I.5's old woman speaks of "the last Mystarch" (L360) at an undated time.
- **Fix (recommended).** The Thrones come at the end of the sixth winter, as the Last Tide's fleets go home. The wall sees them come, and the fear-names are given then.
  - In III.2, "the old songs" becomes "the songs the wall made of them".
  - Voss's question to the old woman is set after the Thrones came.
  - The sliver then grows inside a Throne during its months in the haven (§7.9).

**A5 · Two stone leaves know what the wood has not told yet.**
- **IV.1 (Cam 10, the second spring).** It says the stones "were warnings. We know that now", that the Mystaeri cut Shoreland words "by their sound", and that they trusted the stone "as their wood carries it" (L660). All of that is known only from IV.2 whole (Cam 16, the third winter; L708–710) and from IV.4.
  - **Fix:** in the second spring, IV.1 holds only what the rolls show. Those sentences are added in Seren's hand when IV.2 comes whole, as a ripening, which the Book's laws allow.
- **IV.3's closing annal (Cam 15).** It calls the arm-mark "the only mark the Kneeling Guest's rite ever made … the half of a holy thing" (L774). Halyna learn it was a rite, the Ael'thar, only from IV.4 (Cam 18).
  - **Fix:** the same ripening, or word it from Brenn's account alone: "the only mark the stranger's blade ever made".

**A6 · Wood leaves that use the shore's own names (your rule: "The grain should only be what Seren has learned from it").**
- **Fails:**
  - "you call it Blackthorn" (IV.2, L720);
  - "Your names for the grains will serve" and "You call them the root-men" (V.6, L1189 and L1201), since Seren and the wall coined those names during the siege;
  - "whom you named the Leviathan" and "the grain you call Heartoak" (III.2, all ten entries, from L605);
  - "as you two feel each other's hurt across a city" (V.3, L1019): the wood cannot know the bond.
- **Fails on the facts:**
  - Thaesaen knows the stones "hung over the trade-tables, and were priced" (L605), while IV.2 says "we did not know where they went" (L710);
  - Rhenvael knows the felling iron "was beaten out" at Carnhold (L613);
  - Esthaer knows of the forges and the charcoal at Emberhythe (L615).
- **Passes.** II.2's "You call them the Mystholders" and "the Mystlands" pass, because the Guest heard them across the water.
- **Fix.**
  - Move every shore name into Seren's headnote or gloss, and let the wood name only by its own words or by what it is ("the grain that goes where it lost the most").
  - For the Thrones' knowledge, give it a route the wood has. The root-men are the wood's hands, and what they touched in the Fall the wood knows, including the stones on the harbour-house walls. Or move those facts to III.1, in the stone's voice.

**A7 · The siege's length is never stated.**
- **What the text says.** "The whole war", "the long years of the siege" (L1339), and "the winter after the tenth Throne" (L1400), but no count.
- **Fix:** six winters, said once and plainly by Kael in Count VI ("I have counted the grey sails six winters"), and carried by the datelines (§1.4).

**A8 · Burning the boat "so there would be nothing to show" contradicts both the deed and the report.**
- **What the text says.** IV.3, L760. But a boat burning on the tide is a beacon, and the warden's own report says *Vessel burned and released* (L764). You asked for a reason.
- **The options, all on day 1:**
  - (a) **Fear of the wood.** The boat came with no wind, and the mist-cloth moved when he did not. The men would not touch it, and the coast burns what it fears.
  - (b) **A message sent back (recommended, with (a) as the men's reason).** The warden meant it to be seen from the grey: whoever sent this should watch it burn. Then the report is honest about the fire, and the lie is "hostile intent". The burning becomes the shore's own act of war, and the council, which "saw only an act of war" (L900), saw it truly from its side.
  - (c) **To hide the deed.** This is the current reason, and it fails as above.

### B · They blur the clock for a reader

**B1 · I.2's voice looks back across years.** It is laid weeks after the arrival, yet it says:

- "It has been the guild's word ever since" (L261);
- "this hearth has names for its hours" (L263).

**Fix:** trim those three phrases, or say "tonight, when I was afraid to speak".

**B2 · I.1's "the pennant on every keep of ours is crimson" (L198)** is said when there is one keep and one battery. **Fix:** "on this keep, and on every keep we raise".

**B3 · I.3 looks back on the first winter from later**: "I said it so myself, the first winter. I know better now" (L298), and "In those first days" (L288). But the game lays it in the first winter. **Fix:** "my first weeks on the wall" and "in these first weeks"; date its laying "at the end of the first winter".

**B4 · The confession is read in the first winter but laid in the third, and no one says why.** V.1 (the first month) already quotes "three words and no sentences" (L938), so Halyna had read Brenn by then. IV.3 is laid only in the Tide of Remembering. **Fix:** one clause in IV.3's headnote. Halyna read it in the first winter and would not lay it until the hearth could bear it, and until the wood could answer it, for "a tale told from one side of a wall is half a tale". It also matches the Whispers' arc: courage first, then humility, then guilt.

**B5 · "In the years the havens came home" (III.1, L566).** The homecomings fill one year (VI.1 is "not long after the tenth", and Halyna die "the winter after"). **Fix:** "in the year".

**B6 · "Those who cried for war grew old, and died, and were forgotten" (V.6, L1215).** The Mystaeri live two and a half times as long, so the angry young of the vote would still be in their prime at the Fall. **Fix:** one clause, since II.2 and V.4 already have the reason: under the thin sky the long-lived died before their years (L428, L1051).

**B7 · The burning boat's tale has the wrong source (your note).** IV.5 sources it to "the men of the outermost outpost" (L842). IV.6 says the shore hulls "turned from us, and ran" (L892). **Fix:** the Aelvaren rams the two hulls (§2.3), and the few survivors start the tale in Eldhythe's harbour-houses that same season. Voss's grandmother heard it from them. The wood says "twice" (true), and the ship-masters say twenty.

**B8 · Brenn's next morning (L770) must become his ride to the Council (your note).** **Fix:** the next morning he picks up the stone and sets out with the report: the gift and the lie in one pack (§7.3). IV.4's "a man of the house came back alone and picked us out of the dust, and put us away" (L822) gains a road.

**B9 · The staff was "borne by every overseer of a great Stonewright work since the founding" (I.4, L324)**, yet it lies in the Keep's chest. If every overseer bore it, it could not have lain at the Keep since the leaving. **Fix (your question answered):** there are generations of staffs. Each great work had its own, made in the likeness of the first. A staff is laid down with its work's Rite when the work is finished, so the first one lay in the Keep's chest. The havens' staffs were lost in the Fall. Halyna found the original.

**B10 · One column or many?** II.1 says "the folk who lived fled up the mountain roads" as the havens fell (L396), but I.1 has one column coming in at dusk after a month's walk (L166). And the long retreat's length is never given: I.2 says "we had walked away from each" (L237). **Fix:**
- The long retreat is the whole Fall, twenty months haven to haven.
- The column gathers as it goes and turns up the mountain only after Glasspire.
- II.1's line means that column.
- The Keep stands above Eldhythe, the haven its masters founded, which is why the column walked a month back to it.

**B11 · The place of the war is never stated.** The tales say "under this wall" and "the shingle below this wall", while I.5 has keeps across ten haven-lands (L362), and the battles are fought along that line. How does far wreck-wood reach the vault? **Fix (one sentence in V.2, prop.):** every plank that bore a carving was carried home to the Keep, wherever it came ashore. The optional line of keeps is in §2.11.

**B12 · Which chest?** IV.1's rolls "came up the mountain in the Stonewrights' chest" (L641), but Seren's chest "held nothing of worth" (L776) and held only the mute stones, the gift and the confession. **Fix:** two chests. The rolls came in the masters' packs, and Seren's chest held nothing anyone valued.

**B13 · The horn (I.3, L286).** You note that a horn cannot carry that far. On the clock: in the first winter there is one battery on one wall, within earshot. From the second year the line of keeps runs across ten haven-lands, and no horn or runner reaches. So the True Men must fight by knowing the Captain's mind, and the Captain calls only the battery he stands beside, or goes to. The runner's purpose grows with the line (§7.5).

### C · Small

- **C1 · V.1's "Battle orders were carved into the living wood, we know now" (L924).** In the first month they knew one word. That the marks were orders is V.2's knowledge, in the second autumn. **Fix:** "we know now" is acceptable only if V.1 is felt as laid later. Otherwise write "we did not yet know they were orders".
- **C2 · V.2's "They were the youngest, sent first because the young grow quickest" (L995)** asserts V.6's law in the second autumn. **Fix:** make it Seren's guess ("I think they were the youngest"). V.6 confirms it in the fourth spring.
- **C3 · II.3's "the bond is ordained in the cradle" (L467)** must follow your ruling: the bond is made by God before birth, the seers only read it in the cradle, and it is made whole at the sealing. The life of a pair runs: made before birth → read in the cradle → taught by the Theoliths → sealed, with the pair-name and their own Stonwryt → their works → death together.
- **C4 · I.2's "I had been called to the guild already" (L237)** conflicts with II.3, where every guild child is called in the cradle (L465). **Fix:** "I was of the guild".
- **C5 · V.6's "with one sign in it, *Burn*" (L1195)** conflicts with V.2, where the Keep's green wood holds seven other signs (L969–981). **Fix:** "one sign to a hull, and in the havens it was *Burn*".
- **C6 · The Hasty carvings speak of walls and flashing guns (V.2, L969–981).** By the reckoning they were cut in the season after the vote, three generations before anyone struck back. **Fix:** none needed if read as the work of watchers who had seen the havens' walls and their harbour guns flash for generations. Say nothing.
- **C7 · I.4's "go and look at the dust on it" (L328)**, laid in the first spring, means a few weeks of dust. It is fine, and grows truer every year.
- **C8 · The Epilogue's "the years after"** has no number. **Fix:** "in the fourth year after the homecomings" (§2.10).
- **C9 · The Invocation's "Their fathers forgot a wrong" (L144).** The felling lies three to five generations back. It is poetic, and fine, so leave it.

## 6 · THE ARGUMENTS OF THE BOOKS

*One line under each Book's title, as the verse Odysseys head their books, saying where and when the Book stands.*

- **BOOK ONE · THE BOOK OF THE WALL.** *At Rivenkeep, in the first month of the siege, a broken people finds a ruin, lifts a banner, raises its builders, anoints its Captain and takes the Keep's name.* (I.5 is laid six winters on, on the eve of the going-down; its dateline says so.)
- **BOOK TWO · THE BOOK OF THE BUILDERS.** *Long before the banner, before the axes and before the havens: how the first builders raised the Keep and their children forgot it, how the mist-folk lived in the breath of their wood, and how the guild makes two into one.*
- **BOOK THREE · THE BOOK OF THE HAVENS.** *From the raising of the ten havens through twelve generations, to their burning in the two years before the banner, and on to the sixth year of the siege, when ten Thrones sat in the ruins and the Rivenmen went down to bring the havens home.*
- **BOOK FOUR · THE BOOK OF THE WOUND.** *In the days of the felling, five generations to three before the banner: the axes at the edge of the grey, the pleas cut in stone that no one could read, the Guest killed at the warden's door, and the burning boat that went home in fury.*
- **BOOK FIVE · THE BOOK OF THE TIDES.** *From the vote under the thin sky, seventy-five years before the banner, to the sixth winter of the siege: how the wood was grown and sent, a Tide each winter, and how each side learned the other at the wall.*
- **BOOK SIX · THE BOOK OF THE GREEN WOOD.** *In the sixth autumn of the siege, after the tenth Throne fell: a green sliver in a dead heart, the hearth's own words given back, and a boat sent out kneeling.*
- **EPILOGUE.** *The seventh winter and spring, and the years after: the tellers dead, one scribe left, and a stone out of the grey.*

## 7 · YOUR OTHER NOTES ON THE CLOCK

*Each note below is given its place in time, and the puzzle-piece it makes with another tale.*

1. **Brenn, the runner, and his ride to the Council.** It happens on day 2, the morning after the killing. He picks the stone out of the dust and rides the coast road to Eldhythe, with the report in one hand and the gift in his pack. He gives the Council the lie, and keeps the truth for forty years.
   - *Joint:* this is why the wall's law is that **a runner carries what is seen, and never what he is told to say**. A runner once carried a lie. Hale is the answer to Brenn.

2. **The ramming, and the rumours.** It happens on day 1, at evening, at the edge of the grey. The victims are best made **the last timber-boats of the felling**, lying at the pillars.
   - *Joints:*
     - IV.1's rolls can show two hulls struck off a cargo-list as "lost at the edge".
     - No crew will row to the pillars after it, so the felling ends by fear and fickleness, with "no one decided it" still true.
     - V.4's "in those same summers the iron stopped coming" becomes the bitterest line in the Book: the Aelvaren's fury helped end the very axes the Guest came to stop, and the grove had already voted.
     - The survivors reach Eldhythe within the month, so Voss's grandmother heard them first-hand, as a young woman keeping the fire.
     - The ship-masters' "twenty" against the wood's "twice" is your two tellings of one thing.

3. **"The stone and the lie in one pack" (IV.3 and IV.4).** IV.4's road for the gift becomes: "a man of the house… carried us on a long road, and put us away, and did not hold us".

4. **The oils, the staff and the mark.**
   - The oils take about a fortnight to make, which sets the Rite on day 14 (A1).
   - The black chest's "mark of the first builders" (L316) should be **Aldwena's Stonwryt**, the same mark as on the capstone. So the pair who laid the gate also laid the chest. That is why Rhyna "knew where to look" (L314): the chest was her own line's. With your ruling that the Stonwryt is a symbol every bonded pair owns, it is their own mark that leads her down.
   - The staff in the chest is the first staff, laid down with the Rite when the Keep was finished (B9). The Captain leaning it in the gatehouse corner that same night now echoes the old custom without anyone saying so.

5. **The runner's purpose.** In the first winter there was one battery, within earshot. As the line of keeps lengthens across the war, no horn reaches, so the Captain's word can only travel in his True Men's heads, and the runners carry what is seen to whichever of them must act. Where the line is long the runner matters most, so the runner's tale should belong to the later years too. (The horn itself is a staging call for the I.3 rewrite.)

6. **The warden's name, ▒▒▒▒.** It was cut from the rolls and from Brenn's written words by the Stonewrights of Eldhythe at B−35, when they sealed the chest. So wherever Brenn named him, the reader sees **▒▒▒▒**, and that is the guild's own hand at work, forty years after.

7. **The killing, the body, the boat.** All of it is on one day. A brutal death needs no more time, but the burning needs a reason (A8).

8. **The deaths, and the time to love the dead.**
   - **The deaths already on the clock:**
     - the Guest (B−75) and the drowned of the two boats (B−75);
     - the warden (before B−35) and Brenn (after B−35);
     - the elders who pleaded, one by one;
     - the havens' dead (the Fall);
     - Tarnel, in the passes, two weeks before the banner;
     - Voss's friend (the first winter);
     - the men under the east cairns (the fifth year);
     - the Mystaeri children in the light (the end of the sixth winter);
     - Halyna (the seventh winter).
   - **What the clock offers.** No True Man and no named soldier dies yet. The clock gives six winters at the hearth, so a death in the fourth to sixth year lands after the reader has known the dead for years. Each True Man's homecoming falls in the sixth year, at the haven he lost twenty months before the banner. The twenty months of the Fall also give Tarnel time to be seen alive before the passes.

9. **The sliver, one season old.** It grew in the dark of the tenth Throne's heart in the months that Throne sat in its haven, so it is green wood, and "the green wood holds one word". The Book never has to say this; the dates make it true.

10. **"Halyna discover; Seren writes."** On the clock, each source has a finder:
    - Halyna: the capstone (day 0) and the chest (night 3);
    - Seren: the chest she carried (from the summer Eldhythe fell);
    - Hale: the first marks on the wreck (day 24);
    - a mason of Emberhythe: the plank;
    - Voss: the rhyme;
    - the children: the Stone out of the Grey.

    Halyna know the wood. Seren puts the histories together, which is why only a stone leaf, in her hand, can join the ramming to the end of the felling (joint 2).

11. **The sliver's boat and the Stone.** The boat goes out in the sixth autumn, and the Stone comes in the seventh spring. The Last Carver's answer took one winter to cut, in a chisel he had learned from the Title that every hull had seen flying since the first winter.
