# THE LEGENDS OF RIVENKEEP · THE CAST, AND WHO DIES

*A design note for reshaping the tales after Jack's notes of 2026-09-30. It is not part of the Book and touches nothing in the simulation. It covers who is in the cycle, what the listener must come to love or fear in each of them, who dies, when and how, what was planted so the death lands, and who remembers them at the hearth. It also gives the runners a purpose and replaces the Captain's horn.*

---

## 0 · The short version

- **The cast grows outward from Halyna.** Halyna are now the *discoverers*: they find things and they know the wood. Seren makes the tales out of what they find. The wall's other work goes to other hands:
  - two more bonded pairs on the wall: **Orvenna**, the wall-masters, and **Enrella**, the young pair sealed during the siege;
  - **Idrenna**, the Theoliths;
  - the fisher-folk;
  - the Twelve;
  - the runners.
- **Fourteen deaths, and every one is planted earlier.**
  - The Guest, beaten to death and thrown broken into his boat.
  - The axemen whom the burning boat rammed.
  - The warden and Brenn, offstage.
  - The night of the pass, which kills Tarnel and the archivist pair **Wendhessa**.
  - Jory, the first man under the east cairns.
  - **Corlen of the harbour**, killed in the first seed-fall.
  - Wick the runner, killed at the moved gun.
  - **Enrella**, who die together on the wall, a mile apart and a breath apart, in the Joined Tide.
  - **Pellow of the spire**, killed while looking.
  - Old Ebba, who first said *We remember*.
  - **Hesk of the mere**, killed at his own homecoming.
  - Halyna, who die in peace.
  - The Captain lives.
- **The runners are the Captain's eyes, and the horn is gone.**
  - The Captain calls one battery at a time from his tower with **the calling-pike**: one pike, one cloth, and three ways of flying it.
  - Each True Man reads the pike through a far-glass.
  - The runners carry three things and nothing else: what is seen, to the tower; the breach, to the masons; and the marked plank, to Halyna.
  - They carry it all on an oath that Brenn, the outpost's runner, broke.
- **The dead keep speaking in other mouths.** For every death, someone else says the dead person's line afterward. That is how the hearth remembers. It is also how most of the puzzle pieces lock (§6).

## 1 · The laws of the cast

1. **A want, a habit, a line.** Every named person has all three.
   - The *want* is what the death cuts off or the homecoming pays.
   - The *habit* is what the listener sees each time the person comes in.
   - The *line* is what the hearth says back after they are gone.
2. **Deaths are fixed points in the war.** Each death has a set place in the fiction and is told in a leaf that opens at a campaign milestone. No death depends on how the player plays. None is told before the player has met the thing that caused it (the Book's law 3: after, never before).
3. **Halyna discover, Seren writes, and other hands do the work.**
   - What Halyna found is listed under their entry (§3.5).
   - Their hands keep only the acts that are theirs by right: the cry, the terms, the Rite, the knowing and the kneeling.
   - Everything else is done by the guild's other pairs and by the Rivenmen.
4. **Halyna never speak of themselves in the third person.**
   - In their tellings they say *we*.
   - When a tale needs one of them alone, the teller of that paragraph says *I* and names the other: "I rose from my blanket, and Halvard rose in the same breath."
   - *So it was with Halvard and Rhyna* becomes *So it has been with us.*
   - Neither of them is ever *they* alone. *They* means the pair, and it always follows the pair-name or *the two of them*.
5. **The warden's name is ▒▒▒▒ in every telling.** It is always four marks, whatever the name's length.
   - Stone leaves blot it.
   - Wood leaves never had it: there he is "the master of that house" and "the host".
6. **The Captain** is never named, never tells a tale, and lives.
7. **Every death has a stone telling, and the hearth answers it: *We remember.*** The one person the hearth refuses to remember is the warden.
8. **A wood leaf holds only what the wood could know.**
   - The gift knows the Guest's last hour because his blood ran into the dust where it lay.
   - The Aelvaren knows his broken body and the two hulls it rammed. It knows nothing of the men in those hulls.
   - The Shorelanders' side of every death comes from stone.
9. **Jack's fixed lines are untouched:** the Title, the cry, *We remember our home too.*, and the hearth's *We remember.*

## 3 · The cast

Each entry gives: **who** they are · what the listener must come to **love or fear** · their **want** · their **habit** · their **line** · where they **stand in the tales** · their **fate**.

### 3.1 The Captain (never named)

- **Who.** The captain of a small company, who wrote the Title in coal on his torn cloak. The Rite made him Commander. At the hearth he is still the Captain. He is the player.
- **Love.** He did not want to lead, and he stands still while everything else moves. He carries the dead with him, literally (below).
- **Fear.** His mind can kill.
  - The True Men fight by knowing his mind. Corlen died doing exactly what the Captain would have done.
  - The listener should feel how heavy every call is. That weight is the meaning of *strategy, not skill*.
- **Want.** At first, for someone whose place it was to lead to take up the leading. None did. Now, to bring them all home, and to set the staff down for good.
- **Habits.**
  - He stands still.
  - He has coal on his fingers and mortar on his hands.
  - **He wears the other half of the torn cloak.** He tore the cloak down the back, bound one half to the pike, and put the other half back on. For each of the named dead of his company he cuts a hand's breadth from it and binds it to the pike on their cairn.
    - Kael's line for it: "His half of the cloak hung to his knees the first winter. By the Last Tide it did not reach his belt."
    - That image counts the deaths without a number, and it puts a crimson strip on every cairn as the cloak's colour is on every keep.
  - At the hearth he sits at the end of the bench with his hood up. When the dead are named, he puts the hood back.
- **His lines.** Only the Title, which he wrote, and the question he asked: *"Your walls are falling. Will you build, or will you watch and die?"*
  - At the hearth he speaks only to say that it was not like that.
  - He says nothing at the cairns. The strip is his speech.
- **Lines about him that people quote.**
  - "There was no greater man."
  - "For the Captain, an hour lasts as long as a day."
  - "He will tell you it was not like that. It was like that."
- **In the tales.** Every Book. He gives the calls from the tower (§3.3).
- **Fate.** He lives. In the Epilogue the last strip of the cloak goes to Halyna's grave (optional, D12).

### 3.3 The runners, and the calling-pike

Jack's notes: "The runner needs a purpose. A horn cannot carry that far." The earlier ruling stays: runners carry intel, never commands; the True Men command the batteries by knowing the Captain's mind; and the Captain calls one battery at a time.

**Why there is no horn.** The guns eat a horn's voice, and a horn cannot say which battery it means. A crimson cloth can be read a mile off in smoke, by a man who is watching for it through a glass.

**The tower.**
- The Captain calls from his tower: at the Keep, the gate-tower over the inner gate, under the torn cloak.
- Along the line of keeps, whatever keep he stands on is his tower, and the calling-pike goes up with him.

**The calling-pike.**
- It is one pike, lower than the Title's, and the Captain holds it himself.
- It carries one battery's cloth at a time. That is why he calls **one battery at a time**: one pike, one cloth, one mind. He will not give the calling to another hand, for the call is his mind, and a mind is one.

**The three calls, as the cloth is flown.**
1. ***Shift your ground.*** He lays the pike toward the water the battery must watch, and the cloth streams that way.
2. ***Change your shot.*** He dips the cloth to the parapet for the water, holds it level for the shore, and raises it high over his head for the sky.
3. ***Hold, or loose.*** The cloth is furled tight to the pike to hold, and shaken loose to fly to loose.

**The far-glasses.**
- Pellow ground them for the Twelve in the first winter, from the glass of Glasspire carried up the mountain in straw.
- Every True Man, or the tower-eye of his battery, keeps one eye on the tower between shots.

**Command Time.** When the Captain lifts the pike, the hour stretches (I.3). Nothing else about it changes.

**The cloths, and the wall's sign of mourning.**
- Each cloth is cut from its True Man's own cloak, crimson, with his sign in white.
- When a True Man falls, his cloth goes to the man who takes his battery.
- At dusk after that holding, the Captain flies the dead man's cloth furled. Every battery that reads it stays silent until he lowers it. That is how the wall mourns.

**What the runners carry.** What is seen, and nothing else, to three places:
- **To the tower:** what each battery's lookout sees that the tower cannot. That means the shingle under the smoke, a hull hanging back behind another, a sail going pale, a hull riding low, root-men in a gully, and a breach.
  - The Captain sees the sea and the sails. The runners are his eyes for everything else, so **his calls are only as good as their word.** The listener must feel that.
- **To the masons:** where the wall is broken.
- **To Halyna:** a plank with marks on it.
  - After the holding the runners walk the tide-line. It was Hale who came up saying there were marks on the wood (V.1).
  - So the runners are the hinge between the wall and the Knowing.

**The runners' oath.**
- *"What I saw, I carry. I carry nothing else."*
- It is an old oath of the havens' runners, and Brenn swore it at fifteen (D4).
- After his confession is read (IV.3), the wall's runners swear it again and add four words: *"Not as Brenn carried."*

**A runner's word is short.** It runs three or four words, like a chisel-cut: *"Hull hangs back."* · *"Third rides low."* · *"They are looking."* This answers the Guest's three words, which no one carried. It also fits a chisel tongue's economy.

#### Hale (Private Hale, Hale the runner)

- **Love.** He is earnest and fast, and he tells only what he saw.
- **Fear.** Being too late.
- **Want.** To be fast enough, every time.
- **Habit.** He says what he saw in the words he saw it, no bigger and no smaller.
- **Lines.**
  - "I am not a teller. I run."
  - **"I ran it."** This is his refrain, and it comes back at every death he carries.
- **His arc, in deaths.**
  - **Year 3.** He carries Wick's last three words to the Captain.
  - **Year 4.** He runs from the east breach to the north to tell Della, and finds her already gone. It was *the only time a runner came too late, and it was no runner's fault*: the bond is faster than any runner.
  - **Year 5.** He carries Pellow's word in time, and the wall lives. He refuses Pellow's cloth.
- **Fate.** He lives. At the end, he is the one who swears the new runners.

#### Wick

- **Who.** The youngest runner, 13 in the first winter. He is thin and quick, so the wall calls him Wick.
- **Love.** He runs everywhere, even to the hearth, where he sits on the floor at the front.
- **Want.** To run for the Captain himself.
- **His line.** **"They are looking."** These are his last three words (D7).
- **Fate.** He dies in the Tide of Remembering.

### 3.4 The six companies: Voss and Jory

#### Voss (Corporal Voss, Voss who asked)

- **Who.** About 40 in the first winter. His grandmother Elspet raised him in a harbour-house on the quay at Eldhythe.
- **Love.** He keeps his question until it is answered. He keeps a dead friend's name.
- **Want.** An answer that is his own.
- **Habit.** He asks.
- **Lines.**
  - "We do not fight because we are right. We fight because our children are behind these walls."
  - "But Mystwood remembers."
  - Optional, for I.5: *"I have no children of my own. That is why I said our."*
- **In the tales.**
  - He asked "why we fight for ice and stone" on the night he buried Jory. Stannard answered him.
  - He tells IV.5 and I.5.
  - He takes Corlen's cloth in the Second Tide (recommended) and speaks the Eldhythe Homecoming for him.
- **Fate.** He lives.

#### Jory

- **Who.** A soldier of the six companies, of Eldhythe, and Voss's friend since the quay. The two of them turned the rope while the little ones jumped.
- **Love.** He whistles, and the old ship-masters hate it.
  - The tune is the quay-rhyme of the grey boat. The listener does not learn that until IV.5.
  - Merrick tells him to stop, and he laughs.
- **Want.** To drink again at Elspet's fire.
- **His line.** **"It's only a rhyme."** It lands when the ship-masters' couplet is heard in IV.5: *and the one it comes for will not come home again.*
- **Fate.** He dies in the first winter, the first name under the east cairns (D5).

### 3.5 The guild

#### Halyna (Halvard and Rhyna, the Bonded of the Keep)

- **Who.** About 62 in the first winter. They are the elders of the guild at the Keep, sealed long ago.
  - His fathers cut the capstone of the inner gate.
  - Her forebears, Aldwena, laid it.
- **The name.**
  - It is one name for two people, a husband and a wife, *as a gate is made of two posts and the stone laid across them*.
  - The cast keeps a rule for it: *they* and a plural verb go only with the pair-name or with *the two of them*. Prefer *both*, *he and she*, and *each*. Neither of them is ever *they* alone.
  - How the name is shown on the page belongs to the naming note. The Tongues doc's sealing lintel over the written name is the simple visible sign.
- **Love.**
  - They are one without speaking: neither calls the other.
  - They are humble: "a tale is not a boast."
  - Rhyna weeps for both of them.
- **Fear.** Their death. After Enrella, the listener knows exactly what the law of the bond will do to them, and dreads every scene where one of them is hurt.
- **Want.** To find what the guild forgot, and to tell the wood's half, so the tale is whole.
- **What Halyna discovered** (Jack: "They should be the discoverers of the stories that Seren writes"):
  - the old capstone lying in the gateway, which they turned over;
  - the black chest in the deepest vault, with the Rite on its slate leaves;
  - **the receipt for the three oils, and the oils themselves, which they had to make** (Jack: "That is part of what Halyna found"):
    - granite-bloom gathered on the quarry ledges of the ridge in the first spring, where Halvard's fathers knew it grew;
    - cedar pressed from the fallen roof-trees of the hall;
    - and the last of the guild's myrrh (optional): it had been carried up the road for the dead, whom the frozen pass would not let the guild anoint. So the Captain was anointed for the burden of command with the myrrh of the road's dead;
  - the staff, and whatever the staff note decides about why it was left;
  - the last leaf in the chest, which gave the hearth its custom;
  - the archive rolls, and Brenn's confession, both in the chest Seren carried;
  - the knowing of the wood, from the first glyph to the green sliver.
- **What other hands do now.**
  - **Orvenna** cut and dress the new capstone, share out the siege day, and key the sending boat's joints.
  - **Idrenna** seal Enrella.
  - **The fisher-folk** build the sending boat.
  - **Harl** carries it down to the tide.
  - **Halyna keep:** the cry, the terms, the Rite, the Captain's Stonwryt, the knowing, and the kneeling.
- **Habits.** One begins and the other ends. They lay one hand each on the wood. They have never slept a night under different roofs.
- **Lines.**
  - "Hold the stone." / "Hold it, and it will hold you."
  - "A tale told from one side of a wall" / "is half a tale."
  - "They learned it from us." / "We taught it them with every silent gun."
- **Fate.** They die in peace, in the winter after the tenth Throne falls (D12).

#### Seren (Seren Two-Inks)

- **Who.** About 12 in the first winter. She is unbonded and Halyna's third. Her name means "a single sorrow that overcomes".
- **Love.**
  - She loves words. Jack: "Her craft is the crafting with words." Her Orrowen runs just short of song, and the Plain Words translation unwinds it.
  - She is honest: "I have not mended it with guesses."
  - She is alone: among the guild, one name is a mourning.
- **Want.**
  - To set the Book down so that it outlives its tellers.
  - Unspoken: the life she did not get. She held the lamp at Enrella's sealing.
- **Habits.**
  - She says a word aloud three times before she writes it, to hear whether it will hold. This is her craft's version of Stannard's knuckle on the stone.
  - **She holds the lamp.** By custom the unbonded hold the lamp at sealings and at knowings. "Hold the stone" means tell; "hold the lamp" means see.
  - She keeps Tarnel's netting-needle in the Book as its marker, and binds the leaves with the knot he taught her.
- **Lines.**
  - "Hold the stone, Seren. I tell myself so every time."
  - "The wood does not lie. It was told to make us believe a lie, and it obeyed."
  - "We have not yet earned a guest."
  - "I know only that it must be sent kneeling."
- **Her role.** She combines the histories. The stone's side and the wood's side meet only in her hand, and she makes the lays.
  - Optional: the words of *Two at the Gate* are hers, made the first night out of the cry. Enno and Della sang them first.
- **Her griefs, in order.**
  - Tarnel, on the road.
  - Enrella. She was carrying mortar for Della at the north breach and saw her go (D8).
  - Halyna.
- **Fate.** She lives, and writes the Epilogue alone.

#### Tarnel, and his father Tarnard

- **Tarnel** ("little net").
  - **Who.** A fisher's son of Tidesmeet whom the guild called. He was laid in one cradle with Seren.
  - **Love.** He knotted a net as he walked the passes, to fish the mountain tarns when they got there, and he taught Seren the knot.
  - **His line.** *"A net is only knots holding hands."*
  - **Fate.** He died of the cold on the night of the pass, and she woke beside him (D3).
- **Tarnard** (name proposed). Tarnel's father and the eldest of the boatwrights. He lays the keel of the sending (VI.1).
  - Seren ties the bow lashing of that boat with Tarnel's knot. It takes one sentence, and it closes Tarnel's thread.

#### Idrenna (Idlan and Denna), the Theoliths

- **Who.** The oldest pair in the guild, about 88 in the first winter. They taught Halyna as children, and later Enrella.
- **Love.** They sit by the door and nod when a tale is told right.
- **Fear.** Outliving their pupils. They outlive both pairs.
- **Want.** To seal one more pair. They get Enrella.
- **Habit.** They speak in halves, like every pair, but slowly.
- **Line.** "Every wall begins with one stone and one prayer." / "We laid the prayer first."
- **At the deaths.**
  - They say the prayer for the dead in two halves over Enrella's stone.
  - They sit by Halyna's grave all of the day after.
- **Fate.** They live past the end of the Book.

#### Aldwena (Aldun and Bena), the first builders

- **Who.** The bonded pair who laid the capstone of the inner gate in the Age of the Keep. Rhyna is of their line.
- **Love.** Their vow, cut on the tale-stone that every teller holds: *"This wall will stand. We two laid it, and we two answer for it, while stone is stone. It stands."*
- **Optional.** The last leaf in the chest, "in a smaller hand than the rest", is Bena's. The hearth's custom is theirs, so the Book's line runs Aldwena, then Halyna, then Seren.
- **Fate.** Long dead. Optional: Halyna lie beside them under the inner gate (D12).

#### Orvenna (Ormund and Penna), the wall-masters

- **Who.** A bonded master-pair of about 45, who share out the laying.
- **Love.** They are steady and plain, and never raise their voices on the wall.
- **Fear.** Their own choice. In the Joined Tide they send Enrella apart.
- **Want.** A finished wall: *"A wall is never finished. It is only stood."*
- **Habit.** They sing *Two at the Gate* at the laying, one to the comma and the other the rest.
- **Line.** "Put the stone down," / "and pick the next one up. That is the whole war." This line was a mason's Whisper, and now it is theirs.
- **What they do.**
  - They share out the siege day (I.2).
  - They cut the new capstone (I.4).
  - They send the pairs to the breaches.
  - They lay Enrella's stone over them, mark down.
  - They key every joint of the sending as stone is keyed (VI.1).
- **Fate.** They live.

#### Enrella (Enno and Della), the young pair

- **Who.** Bonded children who came up the mountain **together**, 15 in the first winter.
  - They are Seren's opposite: both of them lived through the road, and they came into the courtyard hand in hand.
  - In the first autumn they were sealed by Idrenna, the first sealing at Rivenkeep since the founding (II.3).
  - Orvenna's prentices.
  - Theirs were the first voices of *Two at the Gate*.
  - They tried the wood after Halyna, and knew a little (V.1).
- **Love.** Laughter: they finish each other's jokes, and the antiphon becomes a game. They are young.
- **Want.** A house in the ward with one door. They laid its first course, and it stands one course high.
- **Line.** **"It is like being one person" / "with four hands and no excuses."**
- **Their Stonwryt.**
  - Jack: "A symbol every bonded pair owns and holds sacred."
  - They received it at the sealing and cut it together under the first stone they laid as one: one set the chisel, the other struck.
- **Fate.** They die together on the wall in the Joined Tide (D8).
- **Name check.** *Arvira* was considered and dropped: it is a minor Star Wars character. *Enrella* traces to nothing fictional; it turns up only as a rare real given name and surname.

#### Wendhessa (Wendel and Tessa), the archivists of Eldhythe

- **Who.** The bonded pair at the temple of Eldhythe who heard Brenn, wrote his confession down from his mouth, and sealed it in the archive chest with the stone. They told no one.
- **Fear.** The guild's share of the silence ("we filed the warning away").
- **Want.** Unknown. They carried the chest out of the Fall themselves and never said why.
- **Line.** None. Their silence is their line.
- **Fate.** On the night of the pass they lay down in the snow under one cloak and did not rise. The chest was between them, and the masons gave it to the youngest apprentice because it held nothing of worth (D3).

### 3.6 The wound

#### Aelrhen, the Kneeling Guest

- **Who.** One of the long-lived. He took the name *Aelrhen*, "bond-to-stone", when he took his errand.
  - The Tongues doc puts it at the same root as Rhyna's name. That is left unsaid, as Jack wants.
- **Love.**
  - His patience: he listened across the grey for a life and gathered three words "as a child gathers fallen leaves".
  - His gentleness: he took the neck "as gently as one lifts a fledgling".
  - **His last act: he knelt again, to begin the rite again** (D1).
- **Want.** To be heard, and to make the Ael'thar.
- **Line.** *"Stop… Sky… Dying…"*
- **Fate.** He is beaten to death on the warden's step, dragged by the heels, and thrown broken into his boat (D1).

#### The Aelvaren, the Bearer of the Binding

The boat is a character.
- **Love.** It was proud of its name, and it waited in the shallows to carry him home with the rite made.
- **Fear.** Its fury. Burning, it rams the axe-boats at the edge of the grey (D2).
- **Its want and its fate.** To carry him home, and it does: it beaches beneath the silverbarks. Every hull since has drunk its grief.

#### The warden ▒▒▒▒

- **Fear and contempt.** He held his post by his father's name on the Trade Council, not by his own. He had never had a wound.
  - The listener must fear **what fear does in a man with power**.
- **Want.** To be his father's son in the cities again, and to be thought great.
- **Habits.** He set the black wood everywhere at the outpost, even in his bed. He wore long sleeves for the rest of his life.
- **Lines.** *"Seize him."* and *"Kill him."* His report: *Unknown intruder. Hostile intent. Neutralized. Vessel burned and released.*
- **Fate.** He died old and rich around year 84, in his bed of black wood.
  - Brenn confesses because the warden is dead and Brenn is the last of the twelve: "there is no one left to ask but God."
  - The Stonewrights of Eldhythe cut his name from the rolls, and from the confession wherever Brenn spoke it.
  - He is the one man the hearth will not remember.

#### Brenn, the man who picked up the stone

- **Who.** At 19 he was the outpost's **runner**, sworn at fifteen on the runners' stone at Eldhythe.
  - The warden gave him the lying report, and he ran it to the Council at Eldhythe in a day and a night.
  - The Council's clerk asked him, *Is it so?* He did not say yes. He said, *It is written.*
- **Love and fear.** Both, by design.
  - He struck the Guest too. He says so, and he counts the blows.
  - He picked the gift out of the dust and kept it forty years without ever holding it.
- **Want.** To set the stone down, and to be forgiven.
- **Lines.**
  - "I laughed. I have not laughed at anything since."
  - "We obeyed."
  - **New:** *"I carried the lie in a day and a night. I carried the truth forty years, and gave it to no one."*
  - **New:** *"I did not say it was so. I said it was written. I have had forty years to see what a small cleverness that was."*
- **Fate.** He died within the year of his confession (D4). The wall's runners swear by his broken oath.

#### The outpost's twelve, and Tobe

- "There were twelve of us and the warden." All of them are dead by the time Brenn confesses.
- **Tobe** (name proposed) is the oldest of them, a man who fished the edge of the grey for thirty years. He speaks the coast's custom that gives the burning its reason:
  - *"What comes out of the grey goes back to the grey burning, or it comes again."* (D1)

### 3.7 The grandmothers and the ship-masters

#### Elspet, Voss's grandmother

- **Who.** She kept the fire in a harbour-house on the quay at Eldhythe. The three mute stones hung over that fire.
- **Love.** She knocked her pipe on the lowest of the stones. She said the Mystlands made liars of honest men, and **she said it of her brother.**
- **Want.** That her brother had been lying.
- **Habit.** She laughed with the shore by day, and did not laugh at night.
- **Line.** "The Mystlands make liars of honest men."
- **Fate.** She died before the Fall. Voss remembers her (IV.5).

#### Daveth, her brother

- **Who.** An axeman of the felling, one of the few to survive the ramming (D2). He came home on a spar.
- **What he did.** His tellings started the rumours.
- **Fate.** He drank on his tale and was drowned in the harbour within a few years. He is Merrick's father.

#### Merrick, the eldest ship-master at the Keep

- **Who.** Daveth's son and Elspet's nephew, about 70 in the first winter. He was the ship-master who took the rope out of the children's hands on the quay when they sang the couplet, and did not say why.
- **Love.** He is ashamed of his fear, and stubborn in it. In the end he is brave.
- **Want.** Never to see the grey boat.
- **Habit.** He turns his face from the sea when a hull burns. He tells Jory to stop whistling.
- **Line.** *"Not that one."* (He says it of the couplet.)
- **His arc.**
  - In VI.1 he watches the sending go out and does not look away.
  - Optional, in the Epilogue: he goes down to the grey boat behind the children, the first ship-master ever to touch one.
- **Fate.** He lives.

#### The ship-masters, together

- **Who.** A chorus. They will not tell the burning-boat tale.
  - Daveth said two boats were rammed. The ship-masters say twenty.
- **How their fear gets started.** Daveth's tellings are the start of it, so the ship-masters' tellings agree because they are all of one thing.
- **Fate.** They watch the sending in VI.1.

#### Old Ebba, Marl's grandmother

- **Who.** Of Fenholm, the town that was always sinking, about 88 in the first winter.
- **Love.** **She said *We remember* first.**
  - On the first night the hearth asked for a tale, Kael set the stone back in its niche and the hall was silent. Ebba said *We remember*, from the first words of the Title, and the hearth took it from her.
  - Jack: "'We remember' is like 'Amen'." She is the one who first said the Amen.
- **Want.**
  - To go home. She is the old woman of I.5 who still murmurs "the Shorelands".
  - To see her course-stone laid. She carried it from the top course of her house, the course her generation laid, and gave it to Marl.
- **Habit.** She begins the answer most nights, a breath before the rest.
- **Line.** *"We remember."* (She said it first.)
- **Fate.** She dies in her sleep in the winter of the Last Tide, the winter before the going-down, and never sees a haven come home (D10). Her great-granddaughter, a fisher-child of the east keep, is the child who first answers the wood in VI.1.

### 3.8 The other side

#### The Last Carver

- **Fear first.**
  - His is the "one hand… young" that V.7 Part III knows in the late heart.
  - He cut the late Tides' carvings, the deceits among them. The carving that killed Pellow was his.
- **Love after.**
  - He learned the Rivenmen's letters from a Title he never saw: the hulls saw it, and what they saw went home through the roots.
  - He cut it in stone, the one medium his people knew would not carry a meaning, so that the stone-folk could read it alone.
  - His small wrong word, *the*, is the Guest's three words over again.
- **Want.** To be read.
- **Habit.** The chisel, which he learned late and learned well.
- **Line.** *"In the memory of our home, our families…"*
- **Fate.** Unknown.
  - The cutting stops in the middle of the Title with the last stroke still sharp. Seren wonders whether it stopped because his hand did.
  - Nothing is told. It is the one fate the Book leaves open, and that is a mercy.

## 4 · Who dies

### 4.1 The design

**The shape of the deaths.**
- **The first wrong:** the Guest.
- **The price of each act of the war:** one of the Twelve, and one other.
- **The bond's own law, shown three ways:**
  - on the road, in the cold (Wendhessa);
  - on the wall, in war, a mile apart (Enrella);
  - in peace, after the war (Halyna);
  - and Tarnel, whose bond was not yet whole, so that Seren lives.
- **Memory passing:** Old Ebba.
- **A bitter homecoming:** Hesk.
- **The Captain lives,** and carries all of them on his back.

**One of the Twelve to each act of the war.**
- **Corlen**, in the Second Tide: the price of the Captain's mind.
- **Pellow**, in the Tide that Learned Deceit: the price of looking.
- **Hesk**, at the Thrones: the price of coming home.

**How the deaths are told.**
- **The dead keep speaking in other mouths.** After each death, someone else says the dead person's line (§4.2, last column).
- **Every death has a stone telling,** and the hearth answers it, *We remember.*
- **No death is a lesson about the next battle.** Each comes after the player has met the thing that caused it. Corlen dies in the Second Tide's first seed-fall, so the seed is never introduced by a death. The death only tells what the seed costs.

### 4.3 Each death

#### D1 · Aelrhen, the Kneeling Guest (felling year about 45; IV.3, IV.4, IV.6)

Jack: "The Guest killing should be brutal. His broken body should be thrown on the boat. The burning of the boat must have a reason."

**The stone side, in Brenn's mouth (IV.3).** These beats are new; the rest of the confession stays.
- The cut goes deep. The warden screams, *"Seize him."* They seize him. He does not fight.
- **He goes down on his knees again on the step.** Brenn did not understand it. The wood does (below).
- The warden screams, *"Kill him."* The outpost had no swords worth the name, so they used boat-poles, staves, and stones from the shingle.
- Brenn's own share:
  - *"I struck him with the pole I pushed the boats off with. Twice. I have counted them every night since."*
  - *"He did not lift his hand. If he cried out, it was in his own tongue, and I took it for the wind."*
  - *"When he was still, the warden was still screaming, and some of us went on, because he was still screaming."*
  - *"Both his arms were broken, the arms of his rite. I saw it after."*
- The body: *"We took him by the heels down the shingle, and his head went on every stone. We threw him into his boat as you throw a torn net into the bottom of a boat, to be burned."*
- **The reason for the burning.** There are two, and they are not the same reason.
  - **Fear.** The warden held his arm and screamed that the blade was poisoned, that the thing had come out of the grey to curse him. Old Tobe said what every man on that coast would have said: *"What comes out of the grey goes back to the grey burning, or it comes again."*
  - **Concealment.** The warden wanted nothing left to show.
  - **The irony, which no one states.** The coast's custom against the grey made the ghost the ship-masters fear. It did come again, as a fleet.
- *"We shoved it off with the same poles."* The burning and the scream of the wood are kept.
- **Then Brenn runs the report** (D4).
- Wherever Brenn names the warden, the Book shows ▒▒▒▒: *"▒▒▒▒ kicked it aside."*

**The wood side, from the gift (IV.4).**
- The gift lay in the dust by the wall. His blood came across the stones of the step to it, *and his last hour with it*. This is the same law by which the Aelvaren "had all of it". So the gift knows his death **as he knew it**.
- Fear leaves no mark on wood, so it knows nothing of the men's fear. It knows his intent:
  - *"He went down on his knees again, for that is where the rite begins, and he meant to begin it again."*
  - *"He knew the blows as a tree knows the axe: one, and another. He did not lift his hand, for the rite is not stopped once it is begun, and his half was not done."*

**The wood side, from the boat (IV.6).**
- *"He came back to us broken. They threw him into us as men throw a net they mean to burn, and his blood ran into our boards."*
- The rest of the leaf (the fire, the turning, the channels, the council) is kept. The ramming is added (D2).

**Who remembers him.**
- The wood, which every hull since has drunk.
- Brenn, for forty years.
- Halyna, whose kneeling in VI.1 is "the first step of the Guest's rite, and the only step one people can take alone".
- The hearth, which says *We remember* after IV.3. It is the first time the hearth remembers a wrong that was ours.

#### D2 · The axemen of two boats (felling year about 45; IV.6, IV.5)

Jack: "The ship that carried the dead body should ram the Shorelanders' ships in its fury. It may have only been 2 boats, but the ship-masters say 20. The sailors who survived the ramming started the rumours. Not many of them survived though."

- **The wood (IV.6)** holds only what the wood could know. This replaces "turned from us, and ran":
  > *Twice on that road we met hulls of the shore-men at the edge of the grey, low in the water with the iron and the cut wood of our kin; and we went into them, burning, as the iron had gone into us, and they broke. Of the men in them the grain holds nothing.*
  - So they were **the axe-boats of the felling**, laden with Mystholder timber. The wood knew them by the iron and by its kin.
- **The stone (IV.5, Voss).**
  - Elspet's brother Daveth was an axeman on one of the two boats. He and two others came home on a spar out of more than twenty men.
  - His tellings started the rumour. The ship-masters have grown it to twenty boats.
  - Daveth drank on it and drowned in the harbour within a few years.
  - Elspet told it more than all the other tales. The old line "so I think she half believed it" becomes *"for her brother was in it."*
  - Merrick is Daveth's son. He is the ship-master who took the rope out of the children's hands, and he is sitting at this hearth.
- **A puzzle piece (optional; IV.1, one clause).** The axemen would not go out to the edge after the year of the burning boat, though no one wrote down why.
  - So the "fickleness" that ended the felling had a fear in it.
  - V.4's *"In those same summers the iron stopped coming to the edge. The grove had not waited to see it"* now lands twice: the Aelvaren's fury helped stop the iron, and the grove voted for war anyway.
- **Who remembers them.** Merrick and Voss. The ship-masters' turned faces.

#### D3 · The night of the pass: Tarnel and Wendhessa (about year 120; II.3, IV.3, Epilogue)

- **The night.** The coldest night of the road, high in the passes. The road killed many children that night, and some of the old.
- **Tarnel.**
  - He and Seren slept under one cloak, as cradle-mates do. She woke beside him.
  - His net, half-knotted, is in her pack. She binds the Book with its knot.
- **Wendhessa.** The archivist pair of Eldhythe were found a little way off under one cloak, together, as the Bonded go, with the black chest between them.
  - The masons gave the chest to the youngest apprentice because it held nothing of worth.
  - The annal in IV.3 now runs: *"Wendhessa, who heard him, carried the chest out of Eldhythe themselves and told no one what was in it. On the night of the pass they lay down in the snow under one cloak and did not rise."*
  - So **Seren lost her bond-mate and was given the chest in the same morning.** The guild's long silence ends with her.
- **Optional.** The guild had no ground to bury them in and no myrrh to spare on them: the last flask went to the Captain at the Rite (§3.5).
- **Who remembers.**
  - Seren, in II.3, the Epilogue, and the knot.
  - Tarnard, at the keel of the sending.

#### D4 · The warden, and Brenn (about years 84 and 85; IV.3)

- **The warden** died in his bed, which was of the black wood, "last winter", in Brenn's words. That death is why Brenn confesses now.
- **Brenn's run.** This is the runner's sin, and the reason the wall's runners swear by him.
  > *The warden gave the letter to me, for I was the post's runner. I had sworn at fifteen on the runners' stone at Eldhythe: what I saw, I carry, and I carry nothing else. I ran it to Eldhythe in a day and a night. The Council's clerk read it and asked me, Is it so? I said, It is written.*
- **Brenn's death.** He died within the year, "as a man sets down a load". The annal gives it one line. The guild buried him; it cut the warden.
- **At the hearth (Seren's line after IV.3's annal).** *"When it was laid, Hale the runner went out of the hall, and the runners with him. In the morning they swore their oath again, and added Brenn's name to it."*
  - Wick is one of those who swear that morning (D7).

#### D5 · Jory (siege year 1, the first winter, about Cam 3; V.5 Count I, IV.5, I.5)

- **Planted.** In I.3 he whistles the quay-rhyme on the wall-walk, and the old ship-masters tell him to stop. In the battle messages of Cam 1–2 he is a wall-hand's voice.
- **The death.** It comes in the Hasty Tide, when the young wood struck the same wall again and again until it sank. Jory was on the east wall, and a fire-ship's fire took him.
  - That morning he had been whistling. Merrick told him to stop. He laughed and said *"It's only a rhyme."*
- **Kael (V.5 Count I).** *"We laughed. God forgive us, we laughed. Jory laughed loudest, and his is the first name under the east cairns."*
- **Voss (IV.5, in the Second Tide).** Jory turned the rope with him on the quay, and the couplet comes after. *"He whistled it on the east wall the morning he died, and Merrick told him to stop, and I did not."*
- **Voss (I.5).** *"I had buried a friend under a cairn on the east face, because the ground would not take a spade. His name was Jory."* That night he asked his question, and Stannard answered it.
- **Who remembers.** Voss, and the first of the east cairns.

#### D6 · Corlen of the harbour (siege year 2, Cam 7; V.5 Count II, the Eldhythe Homecoming)

- **Planted.**
  - His roll clause, *who will not fire until the sea has finished*.
  - His wet finger before every holding.
  - His want, said at the hearth in the first winter: to steer the first boat into Eldhythe when it comes home.
  - III.1 Part One (Cam 5), on Eldhythe's harbour-houses.
  - His battle messages in Cam 1–6, every one patient.
- **The death.**
  - It is the first month of the Second Tide, the first time a crown opens.
  - Corlen holds his battery by his own judgment, to let a hull come close, because that is what the Captain would have done.
  - The seed goes up. The wind chooses the keep, and the seed goes where the guns are quiet. His are.
  - He dies watching the water for the hull he was waiting for, and his death comes from the sky. Three of his gun-crew die with him.
- **That night.** The Captain cuts the first strip from his half of the cloak. At dusk he flies Corlen's cloth furled, and the wall stands silent. Voss takes the cloth.
- **The next holding.** The first *sky* call: Corlen's cloth, raised high over the Captain's head, to Corlen's battery under Voss.
