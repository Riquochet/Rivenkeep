1What This Doc Is

The mini-games are a major part of the game — the "candy-crush" of Rivenkeep — and they get designed to the same standard as everything else. This doc is the starting design: the requirements, the skill-building structure, and the good bones pulled out of the GDD's Skill-Building Game Packs. It is a brief, not the finished design (see Open / Pending).

Why they exist — the on-ramp to the north star

Rivenkeep's north star is strategy is the fun — but that fun is only reachable by a player who can read the fleet, and reading is a learned skill. The mini-games are how a raw player builds that skill until the strategy game becomes intuitive. They are recorded as a supporting law in The Why (§4, "skill is taught, not assumed"): a player who can't read the mind can't enjoy the strategy, so the game must build the reader. They also defend two of the Why's standing tensions — they grow the reasoning that stops retry becoming brute-force, and they train the legibility-reading the system leans on as it combines.

2The Non-Negotiables

Jack's requirements — the fixed points the full design must honour:

  • They build ALL the skill needed to intuit the game. Every drill teaches a real skill the campaign will demand — never a throwaway diversion.
  • Built entirely from the real game's mechanics. Same walls, cannons, ships, terrain, wind, enclosure as the campaign. (This is the GDD's own aligned principle: "no mini-games outside the game's mechanics." Kept — the name "mini-games" is Jack's; the substance is skill-drills inside the real game, not separate match-3-style toys.)
  • Fun, with ZERO downside. No punishment, no loss states that take something away. Players should want to replay them over and over — this is the retention engine, the candy-crush hook.
  • Endlessly replayable. A drill you return to for its own sake, not a checkbox you clear once.
  • Designed to full standard. Design-first, in depth, verified against game theory and real games, honest about seams — never an afterthought.

3The Skill Ladder — The Core Structure

The mini-games assemble a player's skill bottom-up, in four rungs (Jack's structure):

RungTeachesWhy it comes here
1 · Parts of a phaseOne isolated skill inside one phase — wall placement, piece efficiency, reading wind, cannon targeting, reading a ship's movement.Introduce → Isolate: a single new thing at a time (the Why's teaching law). The atoms of play.
2 · A full phaseA whole phase end-to-end — a complete Build, or a complete Fight — combining the parts into one coherent phase.Combine the atoms into the first whole unit of the loop.
3 · Two phases togetherThe seams between phases — Build→Deploy, or Deploy→Fight — where a decision in one phase pays off (or bites) in the next.The seams are where real strategy starts: planning for what comes next. This is the rung the current packs under-serve (see below).
4 · A single full runOne complete Build → Deploy → Fight loop — the whole game in miniature.The graduation drill: the player now runs the entire loop, ready for the campaign.
The ladder mirrors the Why's Introduce → Isolate → Combine

This is the same law the campaign obeys, pointed at teaching the player instead of escalating the enemy: never more than one new thing at a time, challenge from combining the understood. The mini-games teach the mechanics in isolation and in small combinations; the campaign then teaches the minds those mechanics face.

4The Starting Packs — Seeded From the GDD

The GDD already sketches seven "Game Packs." They are a strong seed — each teaches a real skill in the real mechanics — and they map (imperfectly) onto the ladder. Kept as the starting set, to be systematised in the full design:

Efficient EnclosureEnclose a target % with a limited piece budget, no timer.Piece efficiency, coverage per piece.1–2 · Build part → full Build

Cannon EnduranceDeploy cannons, survive multiple Fight waves without rebuilding (cannon health carries).Placement optimisation, type selection, cannon preservation.3 · Deploy→Fight seam

Gap to close in the full design — the ladder isn't systematic yet

The seven packs are a grab-bag, not a clean ladder. Rung 1 (parts) is well covered on the Build side but thin on the Fight/Deploy side; there is no deliberate Rung 2 "one full phase" drill distinct from the parts; and Rung 3 "two phases" is only implied (Castle Defense, Cannon Endurance). The full design should lay the packs onto the four rungs deliberately — a clear progression a new player climbs — rather than a themed rotation. The rotation/cadence (how packs are surfaced) is a separate concern from the ladder (what they teach).

5The No-Downside Reframe — The #1 Design Decision

A live contradiction with the GDD, to resolve

The GDD's current packs are "complete-or-lose": you climb levels 1→5, and a loss ends the run (Level-1 loss = no reward). That is a real downside — losing costs you the run — and it directly conflicts with Jack's rule that the mini-games have zero downside and make players want to replay endlessly. The reframe is the first thing the full design must settle.

Direction (proposed, to design out): make the drills progress-only. A run never takes anything away — you always keep your best result, and replaying can only match or improve it. "Levels" become a personal-best climb (beat your own mark), not a lose-and-restart gauntlet. Failure just means "that attempt didn't beat your best — try again," with no loss of standing, currency, or streak. This keeps the tension that makes a climb fun (can I do better?) while removing the sting that makes players stop. The exact shape — endless-climb vs fixed rungs, how "better" is scored per pack — is for the full design.

6What Lives Here vs Elsewhere

The GDD's "Daily Challenges" section bundles three different things. Only the first is this doc's:

  • The drills / skill-building — HERE. The pack designs, the ladder, the teaching goals, the no-downside model.
  • Leaderboards & scoring → the Victory doc. Battle Score (ships% + area% + cannon%), sub-scores, regions, per-level/per-campaign/friends boards — these are the victory/score system, not mini-game design. (Note: the GDD says daily challenges do not feed the leaderboard — consistent with no-downside.)
  • Streaks, reward packages, package timers, Stonwryt payouts → the economy / meta-progression doc (queued). The 14-day cycle, streak shields, package-timer retention loop, and currency rewards are economy systems that wrap the drills; they must honour the no-downside rule but they are not the drills.

Keeping these separate is deliberate: the drill is the skill-builder; the economy decides how it is rewarded and surfaced. Designing them apart keeps each legible.

7Open / Pending

Sequencing — why the full design waits

The mini-games are built from the phases (parts → full phase → two phases → full run), so they can't be finished until the Build phase and the unified Fight phase are locked. This doc captures the requirements and the seed now; the full design follows those two.

  • The no-downside reframe (§5) — settle the progress-only model and how "better" is scored per pack.
  • Systematise the ladder (§4) — lay the packs deliberately onto the four rungs; fill the Fight/Deploy "parts" gap and the explicit "full phase" and "two phases" rungs.
  • Surfacing & cadence — daily-gated (the GDD's 14-day rotation) vs always-available climb; how the ladder is presented so a new player naturally walks up it.
  • Onboarding vs endless — do the drills double as the first-launch tutorial, or sit alongside it? (The GDD's tutorial is a separate video-guided demo.)
  • Numbers — all per-pack difficulty levers (time limits, piece budgets, wave counts, wind strength) are the layer above the design, deferred.
