# THE LEGENDS OF RIVENKEEP: PLAN OF THE CYCLE

*The architecture for rewriting the Legend of Rivenkeep as a cycle of tales. Drafted 2026-09-25 from story_bible.md and fiction_hooks.md, and aligned with admiral_framework.md §9 and §12 to §15. Nothing here touches the simulation. Every surface is cosmetic and sits off the sim path.*

**Canon used throughout (Jack's rulings).**
- The enemy people are the **Mystaeri**. The defenders were the **Shorelanders**, and they are reborn as the **Rivenmen**.
- **Elder Halvard** (Old Norse *Hallvarðr*, "rock-guardian") and **Elderess Rhyna** are Aetherbonded. The tales always name them together, and they always act together.
- The Captain stays unnamed. The player is the Captain.
- The Stonewrights' faith stays. It is voiced by the people who hold it, never by the narrator.

---

## 0 · THE SHAPE ON ONE PAGE

- **What it is.** *The Book of the Riven Stone* is the Rivenmen's book of tales, told at the Long Hearth of Rivenkeep in the hours after an assault. Seren Reader-of-Wood sets it down.
- **What is in it.** There are **six Books** holding **24 tales**, plus an Invocation and an Epilogue. The tales run 300 to 900 words each, and the multi-part tales stay within that limit per part.
- **Two kinds of tale.** **Stone leaves** are the Rivenmen's own tales: plain, oath-heavy and earthy. **Wood leaves** are "as the Mystwood remembers": the Mystaeri side, read from wreck-wood, from the emissary's petrified gift and from the hearts of the Thrones. They are rolling, patient and sorrowful.
- **Seven paired tellings** put the same event on facing leaves. A wood leaf sits blank, showing only grain, until Halvard and Rhyna can read deep enough to fill it. So the player can see from the start the shape of what they do not yet understand.
- **Play order is not story order.** As in the Odyssey, the cycle opens at the wall in the middle of things (the Torn Cloak) and reaches back. The cosmology arrives last, as memory.
- **The spine is one exchange.** After every stone tale, the hearth answers with the Rivenmen's formula, taken from the Title of Liberty: ***"We remember."*** The hearth never answers a wood leaf. It does not know how. At the very end, the green sliver of living Mystwood gives the hearth its own words back, with one word added: ***"We remember our home too."*** The whole Book is set up so that the word *too* lands.
- **The Ender's Game arc** has its own tales. The Night of the Naming shows that the young wood came first. The Wreck That Went Home shows the wood learning. The Tides, as the Wall Counted Them is the stone telling of the fleet, and Of the Growing is its wood telling. The Mirror in the Grain is the fleet becoming like the Rivenmen. Together they map one-to-one onto the Admiral's eras (E1 to E5 and the Thrones).

## 1 · THE FRAME: THE BOOK OF THE RIVEN STONE

### 1.1 Who tells, where, and why

- **Where.** The tales are told at the **Long Hearth** in the hall behind the inner gate of Rivenkeep, beneath the wall where the torn cloak flies.
- **When.** They are told in the hours after an assault, while the guns cool and the mortar sets. In game terms that is the debrief, and the interstitial between campaigns.
- **Why they tell.** *They forgot once.* Their fathers forgot a keep, and it crumbled. Their fathers' fathers forgot a wrong, and it came back across the sea under grey sails. The telling is how the Rivenmen repent of forgetting. So they tell what they did as well as what was done to them.
- **Why the wood's side is in it.** The Bonded insisted: "a tale told from one side of a wall is half a tale."
- **Where the custom came from.** The Bonded found it in the same vault as the Rite of the Cornerstone (tale I.4). The first Stonewrights of the Keep "laid a tale" at the hearth each night, as a course is laid on a wall.
- **The old frame, kept.** The source imagined the legend "set down in runes upon enduring granite". That survives as a vow. Seren's Book is ink, and the Bonded mean to cut it into the granite of the hall "when there is peace enough to cut it".

### 1.2 The customs of the hearth

1. **The tale-stone.** The teller holds the old capstone of the inner gate. It fell in the forgotten years. Halvard's forebears cut it and Rhyna's forebears laid it, and their Stonwryt is on its underside (tale II.1). "Whoever holds it tells."
2. **Laying a tale.** A tale is *laid*, not told. When it is done, the teller sets the stone back in its niche in the hearth-wall.
3. **The answer.** After every stone tale the hearth answers: **"We remember."** This is the one fixed formula of the Book. It comes from the Title's first words ("In memory of…").
4. **The silence.** Wood leaves are not told with the stone. The Bonded hold the wood itself, with her palms on the grain and his hands over hers. When a wood telling ends, the hearth is silent, and Seren writes the silence down: *"And no one answered."* The only exception is the last.
5. **The listener.** **The Captain never tells a tale.** He sits apart with his hood up and hears his own deeds told, as the wandering hero hears the singer at the feast. He is the player, so his voice is the player's. His only words in the whole Book are the Title, which he wrote rather than spoke, and the one question he asked the Stonewrights (I.2).

### 1.4 The Book as an object (the codex design)

- **Spreads.** A paired event is a two-page spread: the stone leaf on the left and the wood leaf on the right. An unpaired tale is a single leaf.
- **Blank leaves.** An unread wood leaf shows Mystwood grain and one line: *"Unread. The Bonded cannot yet hear this deep."* The blank leaf facing **I.1 The Torn Cloak** is visible from the first launch and fills only at the Epilogue.
- **Ripening.** A wood leaf may first appear in fragments (italic, with gaps marked **[ ]**) and fill in later when the Bonded read deeper (§7.4). The Book never removes words. It only adds them.
- **Two inks.** In the Bonded's antiphonal tellings, alternate sentences are set in two inks (a grey and a rust) and no names are marked. Seren's note on the first such leaf: *"When the Bonded tell, one begins and the other ends, and they would not have me mark which. I have used two inks, and marked nothing."*
- **Headnotes.** Every tale opens with an italic headnote of one to three sentences from Seren: the teller, the source, the date in the siege, and whether the carving was broken. *"Where the carving was broken I have said so, and have not mended it with guesses."*
- **The appendix: the Book of Readings.** This is the Admiral's §9d codex, bound in as the Readings of Seren. It has one entry per carving met (45 in all), grouped by the ten growth lines and filling from root sign to capstone. Each entry gives the carved line, the root sign, Seren's gloss and the plain gloss. The stratagem ladder is the fleet's own half of the Book.
- **The name swap.** As wood leaves fill, the Book replaces the Rivenmen's names with the wood's own in the wood leaves. The Mystholders become the **Naelorn**, and the Thrones get their true names. The last swap comes at the end: the Mystaeri's own name for themselves (proposed, **Naelaer**, "those of the breath") appears for the first time in VI.1.

### 1.5 The Invocation (first launch, before the first tale)

> **IN MEMORY OF OUR HOME, OUR FAMILIES, OUR GOD, OUR FREEDOMS, OUR PEACE.**
>
> Sit, if there is room, and stand if there is not. The guns are cooling, and the mortar will not set before dark.
>
> Here is the hearth, and here is the stone that fell from the inner gate. Whoever holds it tells.
>
> We tell these tales because we forgot once. Our fathers forgot a keep, and it crumbled. Their fathers forgot a wrong, and it came back to us under grey sails. So we tell all of it: the building and the breaking, and what we did as well as what was done to us.
>
> Some of these tales are ours, and they are cut plain, like the stones of the wall. Some are the wood's, and the wood tells slowly and will not be hurried. Where the two do not agree, we have kept both.
>
> Hold the stone. Lay the tale. When it is laid, we answer as we always answer.
>
> *And the hearth said: We remember.*

That makes 24 tales: 16 stone and 8 wood. The Invocation and the Epilogue are frame pieces and are not counted.

**The six Books, and why they are ordered so.** They are ordered by subject, as the Silmarillion is. Play unlocks leaves across all six at once, as the Odyssey tells its wanderings out of order.
- **Book One · The Book of the Wall:** how we came to stand.
- **Book Two · The Book of the Builders:** who we were, and who they were.
- **Book Three · The Book of the Havens:** what was lost, and what sits in it now.
- **Book Four · The Book of the Wound:** the wrong, told from both sides.
- **Book Five · The Book of the Tides:** the war, and how the wood learned.
- **Book Six · The Book of the Green Wood:** the turn.

Book Six has a single tale, as the last book of such a collection often does.

### 2.2 BOOK ONE · THE BOOK OF THE WALL

#### I.1 · The Torn Cloak
- **Teller:** Sergeant Kael. The sceptic's testimony is worth more than a believer's. **Form:** tale, followed by *The Lay of the Torn Cloak* (§6 V1).
- **Unlock:** first launch, right after the Invocation. The prologue shows the Invocation and the climb-and-writing passage (about 200 words). The whole leaf is in the Book. Its facing leaf stays blank until the Epilogue.
- **Establishes:**
  - The game begins *here*, in the middle of things. The Keep is in ruins, the people are breaking apart, and the grey sails are behind them.
  - Who the Captain is, and what the True Men are.
  - That the Title of Liberty is the Rivenmen's founding word.
  - That the **crimson pennant** on every keep is the colour of the torn cloak (proposal; it ties in the art direction's "crimson pennant flags").
- **Carries:**
  - the last column of refugees at dusk, and the ruin, briefly (the history is in II.1);
  - the fracture: "Some shook. Some wept. Some fell and would not rise." Some cried surrender, some flight, some to hide;
  - the Captain: no general, no politician, no nobleman, no Stonewright; captain of a small company;
  - the True Men, "soldiers who kept their oaths, who built what they promised, who fought only when the cause was just", found one by one and bound by trust;
  - his humility and his waiting for a greater leader, while "the generals were slain, the politicians forgotten, the nobles scattered, and the Stonewrights despaired";
  - the climb alone, the cloak torn, the pike, the words written in his own hand;
  - the True Men answering first, then the soldiers, then the refugees: "not by command";
  - the banner "above the last unbroken wall".
- **Beats:**
  1. Kael swears to plain truth on the lintel of the western stair.
  2. The column comes in, and the heap of stone where the castle should be.
  3. The list of what the Captain was not.
  4. The three cries: surrender, flight, hide. **"The True Men looked to their Captain. The Captain looked at the wall."**
  5. The climb, the tearing and the writing. Kael was the first to read the words, and reads them out.
  6. Who answered, and in what order.
  7. The close, with Kael nodding across the fire: *"He is sitting there now. He will tell you it was not like that. It was like that."*
- **Verbatim:** the Title; "The True Men looked…"; "Some shook…".

#### I.2 · The Cry of the Bonded
- **Teller:** Seren, who was a girl in the courtyard that day. **Form:** tale with the building-song *Two at the Gate* (§6 V2).
- **Unlock:** the debrief of the player's first battle. The player has just defended walls the Stonewrights laid.
- **Establishes:**
  - The Stonewrights, their creed and their refusal of arms.
  - The Bonded as the guild's leaders. Here the epithets and lines are first given: **Rhyna of the Inner Gate**, whose bonded forebears laid its capstone, and **Halvard Stone-Warden**, of the quarry-wardens of the high ridge.
  - The pact of the Keep.
  - The hearth's name for a siege day's three working hours: **the laying, the hauling and the holding** (Build, Deploy, Fight). Every gun the Captain moves is a course of stone not laid, which is the in-world reason for the Deploy cost.
- **Carries:**
  - the Stonewrights, who had watched ten havens die;
  - "the faith that had sustained them through twelve generations", and the hard truth "that walls built for peace must sometimes be defended with blood";
  - the creed: **Build, do not destroy. Protect, do not attack.** No Stonewright ever bore a weapon, and "mortar and chisel were their only arms";
  - the Captain's question;
  - the bow, the two chisels lifted as one, the cry and the answer;
  - the guild's shout, "until the walls of Rivenkeep itself seemed to tremble";
  - the terms: no arms, ever, but building between volleys and mending breaches "with the speed and precision that only Aetherbonded pairs could achieve";
  - the Stonewright Post, which in the fiction is always a bonded pair at work.
- **Beats:**
  1. The courtyard in despair.
  2. The Captain comes down from the wall and asks his one question: **"Your walls are falling. Will you build, or will you watch and die?"** It is the only speech of his anyone can repeat.
  3. The Bonded bow in the same breath, for the bonded do not wait on each other, and their chisels rise together.
  4. **The cry.** Rhyna: **"The mortar cries out from the ground. Rise, Stonewrights! Lay stone upon stone. Stand every wall to the very end."** Halvard answers: **"Rise! Rise! Rise!"** Seren's line: *"I have heard it told that she called and he answered. That is not how it was. It was one cry in two throats, and hers came first only because a cry must begin somewhere."*
  5. The shout and the trembling walls.
  6. The terms: **"Not the sword, but the stone. Not the killing, but the keeping."**
  7. The three hours.
  8. The song.
- **Verbatim:** the challenge; the cry and the answer, unaltered; "Not the sword…".
- **Note:** the story bible's B9 #2 suggested splitting Jack's cry between the two voices. This plan does **not** do that, so Jack's words stay whole. The togetherness is carried by the single breath, the lifted chisels and "one cry in two throats". The antiphony lives in the song instead.

#### I.3 · The Captain's Long Breath
- **Teller:** Private Hale. **Form:** a hearth-saying, explained.
- **Unlock:** the first debrief after the player has started an order in a Fight, which triggers Command Time. If another leaf is due at the same debrief, this one waits for the next.
- **Establishes:** the fiction for Jack's slow-time ruling, as something the men *say* about the Captain. It has no mechanical meaning. The tale also carries vision test 3 (strategy, not skill) in its moral: *"It is not that he is quick. It is that he will not be hurried."*
- **Carries:**
  - "Every cannon is positioned by the Commander's orders" (the Whispers frame);
  - the siege day of eighteen hours;
  - the holding (the Fight).
- **Beats:**
  1. Hale's first holding. The hours go by like breaths, and the grey sails come on fast.
  2. He runs a message to the Captain and finds him still, with his hand on a battery's rail, watching one hull.
  3. Around the Captain the hour stretches. Hale can count the ripples on a grey sail.
  4. The Captain gives the word. The world comes back to its pace, and the hull dies where he meant it to.
  5. The saying: **"For the Captain, an hour lasts as long as a day."** (At 18 real seconds per game-hour, that is literally true.)
  6. Kael's correction from across the fire: *"It is not the world that slows. It is the Captain who will not hurry. The moment he chose was the moment that came."* This is the determinism law told as character: the stamp is the tick, not the wall-clock.
- **Rule:** no numbers, no promise of a mechanic, and no text during a live Fight. It is a label and nothing more (fiction_hooks F9; admiral §12).

#### I.4 · The Rite of the Cornerstone
- **Teller:** the Bonded, in two inks. **Form:** tale.
- **Unlock:** Cam 2 complete.
- **Establishes:**
  - Rivenkeep's oldest rite, and the Captain made Commander.
  - The origin of the hearth custom, found in the same texts (§1.1).
  - That the first builders of the Keep knew war, which is the scandal of the Rite texts: "the secrets of building for battle".
- **Carries:**
  - the deepest vaults and the texts of the Rite, "the rite by which the first Stonewrights had consecrated the leaders of Rivenkeep to build Rivenkeep itself";
  - **found together**: Rhyna knew where to look, and Halvard went down, and neither had said a word (the shared thought);
  - the Rite performed "together", before all, in the shadow of the Title;
  - **the three oils**: cedar for endurance, granite-bloom for strength, and myrrh for the burden of command;
  - **the staff**: "a rod of polished wood capped with the finest polished ashlar, borne by every overseer of a great Stonewright work since the founding";
  - the title "Commander of Rivenkeep";
  - a new Stonwryt cut into the new capstone for his work (this links to II.1);
  - his humility: he never carried the staff into battle, never wore the oils again, and led from the wall "mortar upon his hands and a soldier's plain cloak upon his back".
- **Beats:** the vault → the texts → the rite → the oils and the staff → where the staff is now (leaning in the corner of the gatehouse, where he left it) → the hearth custom found → **"In his own heart he was still only the Captain."**
- **Verbatim:** the oils and the staff as quoted, and the closing line.

#### I.5 · The Naming of the Rivenmen
- **Teller:** Corporal Voss. He asked "why we fight for ice and stone", and here he answers it. **Form:** tale.
- **Unlock:** the Cam 32 completion interstitial, on the eve of the Finale (the defiance beat).
- **Establishes:**
  - A people remade.
  - The land's new name. This is said once, deliberately, to settle the story bible's A11 collision.
  - The vow to go home, and why the Rivenmen now go *out* to the havens where the Thrones sit.
- **Carries:**
  - **"The Commander fought. The Stonewrights built."** From that union a people was remade;
  - the whole land named Rivenkeep, "from the high ridge to the farthest fallen harbour";
  - the vow on the banner to reclaim every stone;
  - the eldest still murmur "the Shorelands", a name "to return to, perhaps, when the last wall stood and the last Throne lay broken";
  - the Rivenmen, "the people of the riven stone, the keepers of the keep that would not fall";
  - the line of keeps across all ten haven-lands, each flying crimson;
  - the convention that the tellers call their forebears *Shorelanders* and themselves *we*.
- **Beats:**
  1. Voss's old question.
  2. The naming, as he remembers it, the week after the Rite.
  3. The double name.
  4. The vow.
  5. The old ones' word.
  6. *"Tomorrow we go down to the havens."*

### 2.3 BOOK TWO · THE BOOK OF THE BUILDERS

#### II.1 · Of the Keep That Was Forgotten *(stone leaf of the pair "Homes")*
- **Teller:** the Bonded, in annal register. **Unlock:** Cam 3 complete.
- **Establishes:**
  - Rivenkeep as the first work of the first Stonewrights, "in the age before the havens". This fixes the story bible's A12 #3: Eldhythe is only the eldest of the *havens*.
  - The Bonded's line, which resolves the unclear "ancestor-grandfather and mother". **Rhyna's** bonded forebears laid the capstone of the inner gate. **Halvard's** forebears, the quarry-wardens of the high ridge whose names are cut in the lintel of the western stair, cut that same stone. So the gate was two lines made one.
  - The Stonwryt.
  - The forgetting.
  - Where the hearth's tale-stone came from.
- **Carries:**
  - the founding, the high ridges, and "the castle that had never fallen";
  - **the Stonwryt**: "a vow sealed in living rock, earned by toil and skill", carved into every foundation, and never bought;
  - **the Stonwryt coin**: part gold and part black stone, minted only by bonded master-couples "in workshops no outsider had ever entered", **"trust, sealed in stone"**;
  - the Keep abandoned for the lowlands, until it was "more myth than memory";
  - the flight after the Fall;
  - what they found: "a shadow", broken gates, crumbled walls, and **"What man could not destroy, time had withered"**, for "forgetting had done what no enemy ever could";
  - the capstone found fallen in the gateway, whole, with the forebears' Stonwryt on its underside.
- **Beats:** the founding annal → the vow → the coin → the going-down → the forgetting → the return → the capstone. The close: *"The stone kept its vow. It was we who did not keep ours."* They lay a new capstone together and set the old one at the hearth.

#### II.2 · Of the Breath of the Wood *(wood leaf of the pair "Homes")*
- **Teller:** as the Mystwood remembers.
- **Read from:** the petrified gift, at its deepest hearing. Petrified Mystwood "speaks truth slowly, requiring long contact". After many nights of holding, and only once the Bonded can read the *aim* of a carving, the gift gives up more than its errand: it gives the whole wood's memory of home, for it is the oldest wood.
- **Unlock:** the first debrief of Cam 27 after a carving of the Tide that Learned Deceit is read (fallback: Cam 27 complete). This is "the enemy understood as a people, just as they learn to lie."
- **Ripens:** one word stays **[unread]** until VI.1: *"we who call ourselves [ ]"*.
- **Establishes:**
  - The Mystaeri as a people, and the mist as breath.
  - Mystwood as a tongue of truth.
  - The home the wood remembers, which lays the ground for "We remember our home too".
  - The Mystaeri's own fault, confessed, because the wood cannot lie. They wove the veil thicker to hide, and hid so long that the shore-men never learned there was anyone to wound.
- **Carries:**
  - the mist, "a living barrier, ancient, deliberate, and breathing";
  - "measureless flat forestlands that stretched to the world's far edge";
  - "older than the builders by many centuries", and a life "twice and half again" as long;
  - kinship with the Mystwood: the trees breathe out the mist, and the mist sustains trees and folk;
  - "a gentler radiance". They are not of the dark but of the between-light. The open sun withers them "as it withers a deep-forest fern dragged into open desert";
  - without the mist the wood sickens, and without both the folk perish: "The mist was not a wall… It was the air they breathed";
  - **the kinds of Mystwood**: twisted boughs for ceremony, pale saplings for song, silver-barked elders for council, and the black pillars at the edge, whose own name is the Naelorn, "mist-hearts";

### 2.5 BOOK FOUR · THE BOOK OF THE WOUND

*The Book is ordered as a mystery: the rumour comes first (IV.5, at Cam 12), then the confession (IV.3, Cam 15), then the wood's truth (IV.4, Cam 18, and IV.6, Cam 20). The harvest pair comes before all of it (Cam 10 to 16).*

#### IV.1 · Of the Felling of the Black Pillars *(stone leaf; required pair 1: the harvest and the warnings in stone)*
- **Teller:** the Bonded, reading from the archive rolls that came to the Keep in the archive chest. Stone annal, in the register of loss. **Unlock:** Cam 10 complete ("we did this": humility).
- **Establishes:**
  - The first cause of the war, as the Shorelanders' own record keeps it.
  - That the Stonewrights warned, and then filed the warning away with the stones they could not read. The guild's own share of the fault.
  - The long forgetting.
- **Carries:**
  - the fog in the oldest archives, "written down only as a question, asked in the night-watches and never answered" (the source's "blackish veil… as wonderings", recast);
  - "the high summer of their pride", when the Stonewrights' counsel seemed "a voice from humbler days";
  - **two generations** of Trade Council expeditions, felling the pillars "for the buttresses of fortresses, bridge-beams", and for halls that did not need them. "Decadence" is cut as narration and shown instead;
  - the timber "harder than oak, lighter than iron, proof against rot";
  - the fog thinned "like a dying enchantment", and the Council named it fortune;
  - **the warning: "These trees grow in strange soil. The carvings we find beside them bear a tongue we cannot read. Where wisdom is absent, let restraint be our guide."**;

God forgive us, we laughed."* That is the warden's sin, echoed.

  - **III, Remembering.** *"They came to see whether the gun was still there."* The fleet looks before it trusts.

  - Throughout: the **ghost fleet**, with no one on its decks; **"the drums"**, which are the knocking of the wood along the root-bond; the lean of the hulls toward the shore or away (the motion signature); "the fleet has spent its fire" (ammunition as the clock); the root-men who come off the landing ships.

- **Teller:** as the Mystwood remembers. **Form:** a telling with *The Song of the Years* (§6 V4). It absorbs fiction_hooks F8 and story_bible Tale 12.

- **Unlock:** Cam 24 complete.
- **Establishes:**
  - **The fleet was grown, not built**, and commanded by carving: *the wood obeys the wood*.
  - **The Law of the Years** (renamed from "the Law of the Rings"; see §10). A sapling-hull holds one sign. A hull of forty summers holds a phrase. A hull grown through a whole life holds a sentence. The eldest pillars hold a judgement. **That is why the crudest came first.**
  - The young wood that gathered in the grey for a generation before the Fall. That explains "sailed generations before it would arrive" and "came without warning". After the Fall, each Tide sailed as soon as it was grown.
  - There is no crew. The fleet is "crewed by the living" *wood*. This settles A12 #4 and #22.
  - The warriors of the landings are grown of root: **the root-men** (A12 #5; proposal).
  - Every hull is grown for its shore. This resolves A12 #17: root-hulls that crawl on land, bladder-sails that ride the sky-currents, hulls that swim fire in a skin of wet bark, and hulls that go white in the snow. There are no wagons, camels or propellers.
- **Carries:**
  - "The fleet was grown, not built": hulls from "breathing trunks", keels from root-systems, masts from "saplings trained by decades of windsong";
  - **the Heartwood** (*Ornvael*) and the **root-bond** (*Aelralen*) made into a chain of command;
  - *the carving says what, not whether*: each hull keeps its own fear, which is why the fleet is one mind and many;
  - **the grains**, the tempers of trees chosen in grief: Greenwood (none yet), Ironbark (stubborn), Willow (cautious), Blackthorn (vengeful), Heartoak (proud), Twistbough (cunning), Yew (patient), Ashwood (rash), Silverbark (wise);

  - "The growing took decades", and "those who cried for war grew old and were forgotten";
  - the Tides' carved names: *Leathae, Vaelthae, Ralenthae, Aelthae, Rhenthae, Eirthae*;
  - **"a doom set in motion by the dead, crewed by the living, against a people who had never known they were the foe."** The wood adds: *"We were sent. We are still sent. No voice among us can call us home."*

### 2.7 BOOK SIX · THE BOOK OF THE GREEN WOOD

#### VI.1 · The Reading of the Green Wood
- **Teller:** the Bonded, in two inks. The leaf ends in the wood's voice. **Form:** tale and eucatastrophe, followed by *The Two Homes* (§6 V5).

- **Carries:**

  - Rhyna weeps and Halvard does not, "for she was weeping for them both" (story_bible B6 #14);

They kneel together, because the first part of the Ael'thar is kneeling and it is the only part they know. They set a **Stonwryt coin** in its bow, "trust, sealed in stone", which is the Shorelanders' holiest gift, as petrified wood was the Mystaeri's. They push it off. It turns against the current and goes into the grey, as the Aelvaren did, *"and this time it carried a gift and not a grave."*;
  - the hearth is silent for a long while. Then a child says "We remember", and everyone says it.

#### Epilogue · The Stone out of the Grey *(bound facing I.1; not counted)*
- **Teller:** Seren, years after. Stone cadence, with the wood's long sentences coming in at the end.
- **Unlock:** after VI.1 has been read (the next launch after the Finale, or at the credits).
- **Establishes:**
  - **Myststone is petrified Mystwood**: stone that was wood, the one stone that speaks. This resolves A12 #6. "Stone does not translate" still holds for ordinary stone.

## 5 · THE BONDED: HALVARD AND RHYNA AS ONE

**The rule (fiction_hooks §1).** No scene gives one of them an act the other is absent from. In each of the five acts Jack named, both are present:

| Act | Where | How they do it together |
|---|---|---|
| **The cry** | I.2 | They bow in one breath and lift their chisels as one. She cries Jack's words, whole, and he answers "Rise! Rise! Rise!" as "one cry in two throats" |
| **The Rite of the Cornerstone** | I.4 | She knew where to look and he went down, without a word. They anoint the Captain together: two hands on each oil and two on the staff |
| **The reading of the wood** | V.1 onward; every wood leaf | Her palms on the grain and his hands over hers (sometimes reversed). The wood speaks into one and the other gives it words, and neither reads alone |
| **The petrified gift, held an hour** | IV.4, II.2 | Both hands on it. She feels the voice "like water over stone", and he speaks it |
| **The last green sliver** | VI.1 | Both touch it at once, and both hear the line at the same instant |

**The supporting devices.**
- **Two inks**, no attributions (§1.4).
- **Antiphonal speech** in every quoted line: one begins and the other ends ("They learned it from us." / "We taught it them…").
- **One lineage made of two.** Her line laid the gate and his cut it (II.1). He is **Halvard Stone-Warden**, "rock-guardian" by his name, of the quarry-wardens; she is **Rhyna of the Inner Gate**. Together they are **the Bonded of the Keep**.
- **The mirror.** The fleet's root-bond is the dark mirror of the Aetherbond (V.7 Part I). The Ael'thar, the rite the Shorelanders refused at the door, is the same idea offered across a border. The Bonded are the living image of what was refused. It is stated once, in II.3, and after that only shown.
- **Their death**, together (Epilogue, optional). It is the one death in the Book that is not a defeat.
- **UI echoes** (optional; see §7.7): *Ask the Bonded*; the *Blessing of the Bonded*; the Stonewright Post drawn as two figures.

## 6 · THE VERSES

*All five are original to this plan, except V2, which revises the project's own quatrain (story_bible B6 #10). None imitates any line or verse-form of Tolkien's or of any Homer translation. For instance, V4 deliberately avoids the counted list form.*

**V1 · The Lay of the Torn Cloak.** Sung by the True Men. It sits at the end of **I.1**. Stone cadence, with strong stresses and plain rhyme.
> *The gate was broken, the lords were scattered,*
> *the grey sails gathered along the shore;*
> *one man climbed where the stones lay loosened*
> *and tore the cloak his shoulders wore.*
> *He wrote five things a man might die for,*
> *and none of us has needed more.*

**V2 · Two at the Gate.** The Stonewrights' building-song. It sits in **I.2**, and is sung during the laying. It is sung antiphonally: one voice to the comma, the other the rest. It is revised from B6 #10 so that stone "keeps faith" rather than "remembers", because in this world stone is mute and only the wood remembers.
> *Stone upon stone, and the stone keeps faith;*
> *hand over hand, and the hand holds true;*
> *two at the gate when the grey sails gather,*
> *two at the gate, and the gate stands through.*

**V3 · The Grey Boat.** A skipping-rhyme of the Eldhythe harbour-houses. It sits in **IV.5**. The children think the stranger's greeting was the strange thing, but the rhyme kept the truth: the kneeling, the gift of a stone, the cut and the touching of foreheads, the burning, the turn, and the scream.
> *Grey boat, grey boat, out of the grey,*
> *knelt at the warden's door one day;*
> *a stone for a present, a cut for a kiss,*
> *and who ever heard of a greeting like this?*
> *Burn it and shove it and give it the tide;*
> *but the grey boat turned, and the grey boat cried.*

**V4 · The Song of the Years.** A growing-song of the groves. It sits in **V.6**. Mystwood cadence: each line is longer than the one before, as rings are laid.
> *The green wood holds one word.*
> *The wood of forty summers holds a word, and waits for its brother.*
> *The wood of a long life holds the morrow, and carves it, and waits.*
> *And the eldest of us, the pillars of the breath, the hearts of the grey,*
> *hold one thing only now, and it is not a word, and it is not a morrow,*
> *and we have carved it into every hull we sent you, and it is grief.*

**V5 · The Two Homes.** The first hearth-song the Rivenmen make in the wood's cadence: short stone lines alternate with long wood lines. It sits at the end of **VI.1**. It is sung *after* the green wood speaks, so its last line takes up the wood's words and does not spoil them.
> *Our home was hewn from the mountain's bone;*
> *we forgot it, and found it, and held it in blood.*
> *Their home was breathed from the hearts of the trees, and the axes came, and the breath grew thin, and they have not found it, and they cannot hold it.*
> *Stone keeps the vow, and the grain keeps the truth.*
> *We remember our home. They remember theirs too.*

**The standing laws** (vision tests; story_bible C1):
1. **Cosmetic, and off the sim path.** No leaf changes a number, a spawn or a behaviour.
2. **Low-load moments only.** No lore appears during a live Fight or in timed Build.
3. **After, never before.** A tale about a fleet behaviour unlocks only once the player has met that behaviour. The Reading's law applies to tales too.
4. **No forced playstyle.** Every unlock is reachable by any way of holding a battle. Sinking and routing both yield wood. No leaf is earned only through a particular counter.
5. **Every EVENT unlock has a CAMPAIGN fallback**, so no player misses a leaf.

### 7.2 Unlock types

- **CAMPAIGN**: at the campaign-complete interstitial.
- **EVENT**: the first debrief after a sim-recorded event, with a fallback. Examples: the first order started in a Fight; the first Blackthorn- or Silverbark-grained Standard-Bearer met; the first verification probe met; the first carving of a named kind read.
- **READ FROM A CARVING**: a wood leaf, gated by reading depth (§7.4) and by its source having been met in a battle the Rivenmen held. Depth rises by campaign band, so it is deterministic.
- **FINALE**: one per Throne fallen, and one when all ten have fallen.

### 7.6 Battle messages: style rules

1. **When and how long.** At battle load or briefing, or during the 3-2-1 transition. Never during timed Build or a live Fight. Three seconds each, drawn at random from a pool of 5 to 8 per battlefield, and different on replay.
2. **Who speaks.** Wall-voices: Hale, Voss, Kael, other True Men, wall-hands and masons; the Bonded and Seren, rarely; **a Trade Council lord** who fled to the Keep (the old pride, turning); **an old Theolith** (the long view). The Captain never speaks. There are no modern ranks and no admirals or generals: there was no navy, and the generals were slain.
3. **Length.** At most 16 words, in one or two sentences.
4. **Register.**
   - Plain speech of their world. Humour comes from character, never from anachronism.
   - No design vocabulary. "Sortie" is allowed, in soldiers' mouths only.
   - Never name a mechanic: no AoR, no piece names, no campaign numbers.
   - At most one oath per pool, and faith is never used as a joke.
5. **Hint discipline.** If one message in a pool hints at the battle's carving, **every** message in that pool carries the same hint in a different voice. Otherwise none does. A hint may name a Tide-appropriate *tell*, never its counter.
6. **Truth.**
   - Never say what the fleet *will* do.
   - Fleet memory is within one siege only: "the wood remembers the wall, not the hand".
   - A Throne line may count groans.
   - Fear-names only, until a true name has been read.
7. **Flavour.**
   - The Tide sets the fleet's behaviour: one-word mobs, then pairs, then checking, then three roads, then seams, then morrows, then groans.
   - The theater sets the texture: salt, river, fen, canopy, cairn, ember, sand, rime, crag-wind, dark crystal.

The Epilogue, *The Stone out of the Grey* (Seren), is also a Shoreland telling, and moves into the wood's cadence at its close.

**Cadence rules.**
- **Words.** Short, old, hard-ended words: *stone, mortar, lintel, course, gate, ridge, salt, bread, oath, hand*. Verbs of labour: *laid, cut, hauled, set, swore, held*.
- **Sentences.** Declarative, with the verb early, joined by *and*. After every three or four, one short blunt sentence that carries the blow ("His men obeyed.").
- **Numbers are exact.** Time is counted in generations, courses, months and hours. *Three months. Twelve generations. Eight days, men say.*
- **Oaths.** Tellers swear to their truth: *"I swear it on the lintel of the western stair."* Vows are repeated word for word.
- **Faith is plain.** Stonewrights and soldiers name God without embarrassment and never as a joke: *"We prayed, and then we laid stone."* The narrator never asserts doctrine; the tellers hold it.
- **Self-accusation.** The Rivenmen tell their own faults flatly: *we laughed; we walked; we filed the warning away.*
- **Opening and closing.** *"Hold the stone."* / *"This I lay as it was laid for me."* At the end the stone is set back, and the hearth answers **"We remember."**
- **Never.** Long branching clauses, the present-tense "we are", abstractions ending in *-tion* or *-ity* doing the work of images, or more than one em-dash in a paragraph.

**Sample** (original; Kael, from I.1):
> I was on the gate that day, and I will tell it plain, for I have no other way of telling. The column came in at dusk. Old men, women with bundles, children asleep on their feet. We had walked a month to reach a castle, and we reached a heap of stones. Some shook. Some wept. Some fell and would not rise. I swear on the lintel of the western stair that I would have run, if there had been anywhere left to run to.

### 8.2 MYSTWOOD TELLER tales (8): *rolling, arboreal, patient, sorrowful*

| Tale | Read from |
|---|---|
| II.2 Of the Breath of the Wood | the petrified gift, deepest hearing |
| III.2 The Thrones That Walk the Sea | each Throne's heart |
| IV.2 The Axe in the Grain | a Blackthorn heart |
| IV.4 The Gift Held an Hour | the petrified gift |
| IV.6 The Voyage of the Aelvaren | a Remembering heart |
| V.3 The Wreck That Went Home | a hull sunk on its second coming |

| V.6 Of the Growing | a Joined-Tide heart |

**Cadence rules.**
- **Voice.** The first person plural, *we*: the wood and the long-lived as one kin. Memory is in the present tense, for "it is always now in the grain". The wood speaks *to* the Bonded, as *you*.
- **Sentences.** Long and branching, joined by *and*, *for* and *until*. Clauses grow like rings. A phrase returns with one word more each time.
- **Time.** Counted in rings, summers, leaf-falls and "lives of the shore-men". Never in hours, and never in exact counts.
- **Images.** Breath, sap, root, grain, leaf, rot, the light through the grey, the between-light. Stone is *the mute thing*.
- **Names for the Rivenmen.** The wood calls them **the shore-men**, which is Jack's own informal phrase, adopted as the wood's name for them (Jack's call). Only the late carvings say *the stone-deaf*.
- **Cannot lie.** The wood confesses the Mystaeri's faults plainly: the hiding, the three words, the vote.
- **Cannot see fear.** It never names a shore-man's fear. Where fear would be, it says *"we do not know why"*.
- **Names.** Carved names flow unbroken: *Aelvaren, Naelorn, Aelrhen*. The spoken thunder-forms with apostrophes appear only when the wood quotes speech.
- **Opening and closing.** It opens with *"This is held in the grain."* It closes by fading, *"…and the grain holds it still."*, never on a blow, and **never with "We remember"**, which is kept for VI.1.
- **Fragments.** Italics, gaps marked **[ ]**, no articles. This is how the early Readings sound.
- **Headnote.** Every wood leaf carries Seren's line: *"Read by the Bonded, her hands on the grain and his over hers; set down by Seren. Where the carving is broken it is marked, and not mended."*

**Sample** (original; from IV.2):
> This is held in the grain. We stood at the edge of the breath in our long ranks, and we held the grey to the earth as roots hold a bank against the river, and we did not think of it, as you do not think of your breath until it is taken; and then the iron came into us, summer after summer, and where each of us fell the sky came through, pale and hard, and the young of the long-lived turned their faces from it and did not know why they wept.

### 8.3 Seren's editorial voice, and the blended close

- **The headnotes** are scholarly and humble. They give the source, the date in the siege, and what was broken. They never interpret: *"I have kept both."*
- **The Epilogue** is the one leaf where the two cadences meet. It opens stone-plain, and at the reading of the Myststone the sentences lengthen into the wood's rhythm. Two made one, in the prose itself.

## 13 · VISION TESTS

| Jack's test | How this cycle passes it |
|---|---|
| **1 · Deterministic** | Every unlock is keyed to campaign milestones or sim-recorded events, and every event has a fallback. The only variable text is a token drawn from the sim's own record (the last Throne's name). Battle-message pools keep hint discipline. Wall-clock speed never matters |
| **2 · Another mind; the player learns to recognise it** | The fleet has a people, a history, a grammar and a temper. Readings explain *after* the battle, never before. The Tides tales make "they get better" legible in kind |
| **3 · Strategy, not skill** | No leaf depends on speed or accuracy. I.3 makes the unhurried Captain the ideal |
| **4 · Many stratagems, overlapping, simple building to complex** | The Book of Readings holds all 45 carvings by line. The arc tales land on each era's new capability |
| **5 · A cycle, not a chronicle; Tolkien's voice, Homer's frame; the crudest ships first** | Six Books and 24 tellers' tales, out of order, with songs, catalogues, epithets and a hearth. The Ender's Game arc runs through Book Five |
| **6 · Halvard and Rhyna as one** | §5: every one of the five named acts is done together, plus two inks and antiphony throughout |
| **7 · Command Time** | I.3 names it in fiction, with no mechanical meaning |

## 14 · RULINGS THIS PLAN ASSUMES (FOR JACK)

1. **The Captain never tells a tale.** He is the listener (§1.2).
2. **The GDD's wording of the challenge**, spoken by the Captain as his one quoted question (I.2).
3. **Rhyna's cry stays whole**, with Halvard's answer, as "one cry in two throats". It is not split between them (I.2).
4. **The battles are fought along the Rivenmen's line of keeps across the ten haven-lands.** The havens themselves are held by the Thrones and retaken only in the Finale, the homecoming frame (story_bible C11 #1).
5. **The Thrones came late.** The Leviathan is removed from the Fall.
6. **No Mystaeri sail.** It is a ghost fleet, and the landings are made by **root-men**.
7. **Myststone is petrified Mystwood.**
8. **Names, all proposals:**
   - the ten havens' working names (B7);
   - the Throne griefs (III.2);
   - the emissary **Aelrhen**;
   - the Mystaeri's own name **Naelaer**;
   - the garrison man **Brenn**;
   - the Bonded's lines (her forebears laid the gate, his cut it);
   - **Seren unbonded**.
9. **The wood calls the Rivenmen "the shore-men"**, Jack's own phrase. "The stone-deaf" appears only in the late carvings, if at all.
10. **The ending.** The recommended version sends the sliver home, kneeling, with a Stonwryt, and the Stone out of the Grey comes back. The fallback is the minimum canon (VI.1).
11. **The Bonded die together** in the Epilogue: optional, and recommended.
12. **Lifespan 2.5×**, and the chronology in §12.
13. **Two renames**: "the Law of the Rings" becomes **the Law of the Years**, and "the Council of the Fading" becomes **the Council Under the Thin Sky**.
14. **The torn cloak was crimson**, and every keep's pennant carries its colour.
15. **The hearth's answer is "We remember"**, and the wood gives it back once, at the end.
16. **Store and lore screens** never show the final line.
