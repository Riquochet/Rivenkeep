## 2 · THE CANON SHEET FOR v1.1.0 (both revisers hold to it)

### 2.1 Halyna, the pair-names, and "the Bonded" (N1, N2, R1, R2)

- **One name for the pair.** At the **sealing**, which is the wedding of a bonded pair, the guild gives the two of them one name made of both, and they are called by it ever after. Halvard and Rhyna were sealed as **HALYNA**. That is their normal name in every telling.
- **Where the single names stay:** the sealing itself, the genealogies and annals (his fathers cut the stone, her forebears laid it), one voice in one paragraph (II.3 marks them), and the single acts the Book already stages apart. Those acts are: Rhyna's cry and Halvard's "Rise!" (I.2), the vault in I.4 (she knew where, he went down), the first shape in V.1 (in her palms; he said *shore*), the breakthrough in V.2, the gift held an hour (IV.4), "Rhyna wept, and Halvard did not" (VI.1), and the last night (Epilogue).
- **Grammar.** *Halyna* takes a plural verb and *they*: "Halyna are", "Halyna say", "Halyna told it". Titles appear only where a title matters: "Elder Halvard and Elderess Rhyna" in I.2's introduction and in II.3.
- **"The Bonded" becomes the name of the class.** Capitalised, *the Bonded* is what the guild calls all Aetherbonded pairs (R1). The adjective stays in lower case: *a bonded pair*, *bonded children*. **Every "the Bonded" that meant Halvard and Rhyna becomes "Halyna".** The current text has about 80 of them (59 "the Bonded", 23 "The Bonded", 15 "the Bonded of the Keep").
- **"The Bonded of the Keep"** stays as Halyna's fixed epithet. Use it at most once per tale, and mostly in headnotes ("Halyna, the Bonded of the Keep").
- **Titles that stay:** *I.2 The Cry of the Bonded* keeps its name (the cry of the guild's Bonded, through Halyna), and so does *II.3 Of the Bonded and the Theoliths*.
- **Other pairs get pair-names** (R2; provisional, §0.2):
  - **Aldwena**: the bonded pair of the first builders who laid the capstone of the inner gate. They are Rhyna's forebears (II.1).
  - **Idrenna**: the Theolith pair who taught Halyna as children. They are old and still alive at the Keep. They supply the battle messages' "old Theolith" voice, which is now a pair (II.3 and MAP §6).
  - Unnamed pairs stay unnamed but become pairs. "A Stonewright of Eldhythe" who heard Brenn becomes "a bonded pair of the guild at Eldhythe" (IV.3). "Bonded master-pairs" who minted the coin stay as they are.

### 2.2 The bond: what it is, when it is whole, how it ends (N1, N8, R3, R4)

This is explained **only in II.3**. Before II.3 in book order (the foreword, I.1–I.5, II.1), the Book may *name* the Bonded, *show* them working as one, and say they are "one since the cradle". It may not explain ordination, the sealing, the Theoliths or the covenant (N1).
- **Ordained at birth, whole at the sealing.** The seers read two names together, and the children are laid in one cradle. The bond is ordained there, but it is **made whole only at the sealing**. (This is new, and it is what makes an unbonded child possible.)
- **The death (R4, N8).** When one of the Bonded dies, by sickness, by a fall, or by the sea, the other follows soon after. There is no wound and no sickness in the one left: the body stills, peacefully. They die of a broken heart, because they know the other is gone. The guild's saying is **Jack's line, kept word for word: "When the left hand dies, does not the right one die too?"** The Bonded are seldom apart and never far. "Eight days, men say" stays as a rumour, with "hours, and they should know, for they keep the count".
- "Separation, God did not permit." and "Birth united them; death could not separate them." stay verbatim.

### 2.3 Seren, Tarnel, and the unbonded (N3, N19.5, R3)

- **Who the unbonded are.** They are guild children whose ordained mate died **before the sealing**. Because the bond was not yet whole, the one left lives on. It is rare. It has grown less rare since the Fall, because the long road killed children. The term belongs to the guild only: Kael, Voss and the rest are not "unbonded". They were never of the bond.
- **The third.** By custom, an unbonded child is taken into a bonded pair's hearth as its **third**. So yes, the unbonded are "the adopted third" (Jack's question), but that is the custom, not the definition. **Halyna took Seren as their third.**
- **One name.** The unbonded keep a single name all their lives, and among the guild a single name is itself a mourning. Seren and Tarnel were never sealed, so they never received a pair-name, and the Book never supplies one.
- **Her mate (provisional name): TARNEL**, a fisher's son of Tidesmeet, called to the guild in his cradle and laid in one cradle with Seren. He died as a child in the passes on the long retreat to Rivenkeep, of the cold, in the night, and she woke beside him. The Book tells only that much. "The old masters said…" may add one original guild saying for the unbonded (STONE's choice). For example: that God does not take the one without leaving the other a work to do.
- **The loss.** She will never be whole. She cannot know the wood as the Bonded do, because a knowing is too great for one mind to hold. A bonded pair holds it between them, as two hands hold a stone that one hand cannot lift. When Seren once laid her hand on the wreck-wood, she felt something come into her too great to hold, and let go (V.1). *Recommended nuance:* she feels the weight because she was made for two. Soldiers feel only wet planks.
- **The benefit.** She will not die of another's death. She can be apart, go alone, and carry the Book on after Halyna.
- **Why Halyna chose her.** Every scribe of the guild before her was bonded, and each book they kept died with its pair, because no one was left who had heard its tales told. This Book must outlive its tellers.
- **Her epithet: Seren Two-Inks** (provisional). It replaces *Seren Reader-of-Wood* everywhere (§10). The hearth calls her that for the two inks she keeps for Halyna's alternating paragraphs: the one-named woman who writes for a pair. I did not take the two examples R3 offered. "*of the Leaf*" is the fixed epithet formula of *Naruto*'s Hidden Leaf, which N19.9 rules out. "*the Third*" reads as a regnal number. The fallback, if Jack prefers it, is **Seren who writes**, which matches "Voss who asked".

### 2.4 Knowing (N9, N13, R6)

**The rule.** Mystwood is not read, and it is not heard. It is **known**. Touch the wood and its story is known: given whole, as a memory is known, with a beginning, a middle and an end all at once. A knowing cannot be turned the way words can. The hard thing is **telling** it.
- **One touch is enough.** Only one of Halyna need touch the wood, because what one knows, the other knows. The other may be across the ward, on the wall, or asleep. In the first winter they did not yet know this, and laid four hands on the plank (V.1). The wood headnotes now vary and show it (§4.2).
- **Shape, then whole.** At first a shore-mind receives only **the shape** of a knowing, because its thoughts are a forest's and the mind is the shore's. Over the Tides, Halyna learn to hold more of it and to tell it: one shape, then two named, then sentences, then ties, then aim, then plans, then names. The fragments with **[ ]** are the shape before it can be told. They are not missing words.
- **What can be known.** Carved Mystwood (the wreck-wood, the hearts, the Thrones), the petrified gift, the Aelvaren's keel, and the living sliver. Felled, uncarved timber is dead and gives nothing: "they did not answer, for they were dead" (IV.4). That is why the Stonewrights who built with pillar-wood never knew what it was. At most, a single mind takes the shape of a knowing (the axemen's saying in V.1). Only the Bonded can hold a whole one.
- **Other pairs.** Any bonded pair might know a little. But holding a whole knowing and telling it is a craft, and at this Keep it is Halyna's. STONE says this once, in V.1, and not again.
- **The breakthrough (V.2).** By chance, Rhyna holds one plank at one end of the vault and Halvard another at the far end. The two planks are halves of one heart that split when the hull broke. Because what one knows the other knows, the two halves of the knowing meet in them and come whole for the first time. Seren sees their faces change together. In the morning she lays the two planks side by side and the broken grain meets. No one explains it. **Do not overexplain.**
- **Seren reads.** Reading by eye is the stone skill, and it stays: Seren reads a root sign from the wall, and she reads the Stone out of the Grey "with my eyes". The Epilogue now turns on that. The first Mystaeri thing that can be *read* was cut so that one person, alone, could read it.

### 2.5 The Captain, the Twelve, the batteries, the three calls, the runners (N5, R10)

- **The Captain commands. His True Men know his command.** There are **TWELVE True Men**, each a trusted leader of his troop. The True Men command the **batteries**, the groupings of guns, each by his own judgment. They can, because they know the Captain's mind. This is the fiction of auto-fire running the player's plan. The game commands one group at a time.
- **Before the assault** the Captain gives each True Man his intent: where to watch, what shot to lay, when to hold. This is the Deploy order card, told as fiction.
- **In the holding** he can call to **one battery at a time**, by horn. There are **three calls every True Man knows**, and they are the game's three Fight verbs (Cannon & Tower §6):
  1. **"Shift your ground."** The water a battery watches, not the stones it stands on (re-task the zone; the guns keep firing while the laying shifts).
  2. **"Change your shot."** For the water, the shore or the sky (switch domain; the battery is silent while the shot is changed).
  3. **"Hold, or loose."** (Fire-control.)
- **When the Captain calls, the moment seems to stretch.** That is the fiction of Command Time (§2.6).
- **The runners** (Hale among them) carry word of what is seen. They never carry commands. That is why each battery fights on its own.

### 2.6 The clock (N4, R11)

- A day has **24 hours**, and an hour **60 minutes**.
- **At the pace of the siege**, a day passes in **18 real seconds**, so an hour takes **0.75 s**. The racing hour is meant: it is the urgency.
- **Under command**, time ramps down until an hour takes **18 s**. The hour becomes as long as a day was, 24 times slower, and the minutes tick every **0.3 s**. At commit or cancel there is a dwell, then the ramp back up.
- **In the fiction (I.3)**, the Book gives no seconds and names no mechanic. In the holding, **"an hour goes by in a heartbeat, and a whole day in four-and-twenty of them"**. That is exact: a resting heartbeat is about 0.75 s. The saying **"For the Captain, an hour lasts as long as a day"** is now literally true. *Optional:* each ripple Hale counts going down the sail "was a minute, I think".
- **Every 18-hour-day reference is removed.** They are at I.3 (line 225) and MAP §7 (line 1398).

### 2.7 The wood and its people (N12, N14, R7, R8)

- **The symbiosis (R7).** The Mystaeri tended the trees and guided their growth. They pruned, sang, and led the roots. The trees breathed the mist the people lived by: **one life, in two kinds**. Jack said "controlled"; it was mutual, and the trees kept a will of their own. This guiding is how the Mystaeri could grow ships at all: "a ship is only a tree led far". The war then turned guiding into carving, and "we made that bond into a leash" (V.6, kept).
- **The regrowth and the giving up (R8).** The felled Mystholders regrew. The stumps put up new growth, and in a few summers there was a young pillar where the old one had stood. The Shorelanders cut the regrowth again and again, because young black wood is light and straight and makes good poles. At last the pillars stopped putting up new growth. **That is why the mist thinned.** The trees held their sap and waited, knowing that if the cutting stopped, the Mystholders would grow back. The elders counselled the trees' own patience. The axes did stop, about year 50, which is when the council voted. **"But war is for the short-minded and the angry young."** That is Jack's line, kept word for word, in V.4 only. *The tragic turn (optional, III.2):* the eldest pillars that were waiting to grow the sky back were the ones grown into the Thrones.

### 2.10 The burning boat haunts the ship-masters (N11, R9)

The ship-masters will not speak of the burning boat, because it is ill luck. Many have seen it, and their tellings are all the same: a small boat burning far out in the grey where no fire should be, turning into the wind. It brings foul weather. It is the boat that comes for you when you die. **The ship-masters do not laugh at it. Only the shore laughs, because the shore will never have to meet it.** *Plausibility (optional, IV.6):* the Aelvaren's voyage home passed shore hulls at the grey's edge. The wood marked them, and they turned away; "we do not know why". So the first tellings agree because they are of one thing. The legend grew from there.

### 2.11 The sending, made seaworthy (N19.1, R14)

- **Who builds it.** The Stonewrights have never built a boat. The boatwrights among the Rivenmen do: fisher-folk of the havens, who built the small boats that go out on the morning tide and come home by dark, and who carried their adzes up the mountain as the masons carried their chisels. Halyna build it with them. The fisher-folk lay the keel, bend and fasten the strakes, and caulk the seams. **Halyna key every joint as stone is keyed**, so the sea cannot work it loose.
- **What it is built of.** The wreck-wood of the grey fleet, the planks Halyna had known. That wood was grown for the sea: it knows the sea, and "even charred, even dying, the wood knows the way home" (IV.6).
- **Why it will reach the grey.** It is small and sound. It rides the swell in the lee of the point for a day and takes no water. Its own wood finds the way, and it turns into the wind and against the current as the Aelvaren did.
- **Recommended, cuttable:** the eldest boatwright is of Tidesmeet, and his son was Tarnel. It is one clause, and it earns its place.
- **Remove** "it was a poor boat, and it floated".

## 4 · THE FORM OF A WOOD LEAF (N9, N13, R6)

### 4.1 Knowings, not a tale

A wood leaf is now a **sequence of knowings**. Each is a short story point in the wood's voice ("we"), and together they have a beginning, a middle and an end. Halyna render them into the fewest and plainest words they can. Each paragraph is **one knowing**, and the paragraphs alternate between Halyna's two inks.
- **Cadence.** The long, rolling "and"-chained sentences of v1.0.0 give way to short sentences and plain words dense with images. "And"-chains survive *inside* a knowing and are shorter. The wood still counts in summers and rings, never hours. It still calls stone "the mute thing" and the Rivenmen "the shore-men", and it still cannot see fear. **It must stay beautiful.** Keep the images, and cut the connective tissue.
- **Three movements.** Beginning, middle and end are separated by a plain ornament, with no headings. The markdown carries `<!-- RING -->` between movements, and the builder draws a short grain-rule.
- **The formulas stay exactly:** it opens with *This is held in the grain.* and closes with *…and the grain holds it still.* Then comes *And no one answered.* The headnote must still begin *As the Mystwood remembers:*, because the builder keys wood leaves on that prefix.
- **Every verbatim line of Jack's in a wood leaf stays word for word**, as one knowing or inside one (§11).
- **Length.** Aim for 70–85% of the v1.0.0 length before new material is added. The new material (R7, R8, R12) goes where §5 says.
- **Fragments.** An italic line with **[ ]** is the shape of a knowing before it could be told: *"As first known, in the Second Tide, the shape only:"*.

Template:

## 7 · THE HTML SPEC (N7) (ASSEMBLER, in `build_legends_html.py`)

**The aim.** The difference between what is written in stone and what is written in wood should be felt before it is noticed. It is a little more than now, and it stays **one document**: same layout, measure, sizes, margins and ornaments. "I want to be able to send this to someone and get the feel of what the game already does."

1. **Two text roles, set per paragraph, not per leaf.**
   - **STONE role:** every Rivenmen telling; **Seren's headnotes and frames everywhere, including on wood leaves**; the hearth's answer; the III.1 Homecomings.
   - **WOOD role:** every knowing in a wood leaf; the quoted carvings inside stone leaves (V.1's *shore*, V.2's seven orders and *Burn*, V.7's three carvings and the line each was cut against); the green wood's words in VI.1 ("*We remember our home too.*" and the name); the IV.2 fragments; *The Song of the Years*; the appendix's carving column; the wood-word Whispers in the integrator section.

2. **Typeface.** STONE stays in **EB Garamond** (as now). WOOD moves to **Crimson Pro** at weight 400, the same size, with letter-spacing +0.005em. It is a Garamond-adjacent old-style: the change is barely noticeable, and nothing like a display face. The fallback is Cormorant Garamond 500 at 1.06×. Add it to the Google Fonts URL.
3. **Tint.**
   - The stone text is slightly yellow: `--stone-ink` about **#e9e1c8** (from #e6e1d6).
   - The wood text is slightly green: `--wood-ink` about **#d6e1cb** (from #dce1d1).
   - Keep the change of lightness under about 5% so that neither tint pulls the eye.
   - If the page has a light theme, mirror both (a warm ink near #3a3424 and a green ink near #2e3a2a).
4. **Two inks for Halyna (R5).** The paper tint changes with the role; the inks do not.
   - **ink-a** is the role's own ink.
   - **ink-b** is the rust (#cf8a68). On wood-role text it is mixed about 30% toward `--wood-ink`, so that it reads as the same rust on green-tinted paper.
   - The builder alternates ink-a and ink-b on **every body paragraph** of a leaf marked `<!-- HALYNA -->`, and restarts at each part heading.
   - It skips the headnote, frames, `inscr` Seren lines, the answer and silence lines, the laid formula, verse, and the RING ornament.
   - The current trigger for alternation (`endswith('the other finished:')`, two paragraphs) stays for quoted speech in the leaves that are not Halyna-told.

7. **Explicit marking.** Wood-role passages inside stone leaves are marked explicitly in the markdown. **Do not infer them from italics:** IV.3's clerk's report is italic and it is stone.
