## 1 · THE ONE IDEA: THE WOOD OBEYS THE WOOD

The Mystaeri do not command their fleet by voice. They cannot, for few of them are left, and none sails with it (story_bible A12 #4). The fleet is commanded **by carving**. An order cut into living Mystwood is not a message the hull reads. It is a thing the hull *becomes*, "as a hand obeys the mind, for the wood of the hull and the wood of the command were one" (Legend V).

Three truths follow, and every tale that touches the fleet should rest on them:

1. **The heart carves the morrow.** Each fleet sails under a **Standard-Bearer**, a hull grown from a single chosen tree. Its heartwood is the fleet's mind. In the hours between assaults, while the Rivenmen mend and lay their guns, the heart carves the next day's orders into every hull of the fleet through the **root-bond**. The root-bond is the old kinship of trees grown from one grove, which the war turned into a chain of command. "Both minds think at once": the Captain at his table, and the Heartwood in the grey.
2. **The carving says what, not whether.** Each hull still feels its own fear and its own wounds. A hull ordered into a sea that burns may balk at the edge of it, and a hull that has lost its bearer may turn back or turn savage. The carving is the will of the fleet. The hull's own grain is its courage. This is why the fleet seems at once one mind and many: "working together but also independent."
3. **The wood cannot lie** (Legend II: "no word could be twisted"). A carving is always a true order. When the late Tides deceive, they do it with *true orders meant to mislead* (*"Show them fear where you are strong"*), never with false words in the wood. That is the tragedy of §4.

> **FOR WRITERS · THE HEARTWOOD IS NOT A PERSON.** It has a temper (§5), but no face, no voice and no name of its own. It is the will of the council that carved it, carried on by wood long after the carvers died: "a doom set in motion by the dead" (Legend V). If Jack takes up the optional **Last Carver** (story_bible C2), he is the one living exception, and he should arrive late.

## 2 · THE GROWINGS: WHY THE CRUDEST CAME FIRST

The council did not grow one armada. It set the groves growing and **sent each hull as soon as it was ready**. The fleet therefore crossed the sea in **Tides**, strung out across years. Wood grows slowly, and old wood holds what young wood cannot.

> **THE LAW OF THE RINGS** (the fiction's reason for the Ender's Game ordering). A sapling-hull holds one sign. A hull of forty rings holds a phrase. A hull grown through a whole life of men holds a sentence, and the eldest pillars of the mist hold a judgement. So the first ships to come were the youngest and the plainest. They were cut in a single season by angry hands, each with one order and no thought of any other ship. The finer wood came later because it took longer to grow.

The Tides, as the Rivenmen name them. The Mystaeri name is the carved form; the spoken form breaks with the thunder-tongue's apostrophe (§9).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Hasty Tide** | *Leathae* / *Lea'thae* | "green tide" | **one sign** per hull; no hull waits for another | "Eight sails, and not one keeping station with another." They come each alone and fix on the first wall they touch. When a carving fails, they repeat it, because the young wood knows no other word | 1–6 |
| **The Second Tide** | *Vaelthae* / *Vael'thae* | "the tide with grain" | **a phrase**: two signs, and one hull may wait upon another | The first **answering sails**: grey-green canvas blanching pale as one hull wakes to another's deed. The first Standard-Bearers with a *temper* (§5) | 7–14 |
| **The Tide of Remembering** | *Ralenthae* / *Ra'lenthae* | "root-tide", the memory that runs back through the roots | **a sentence with a memory in it** | "They came to see whether the gun was still there." The fleet looks before it trusts | 15–22 |
| **The Joined Tide** | *Aelthae* / *Ael'thae* | "bond-tide": *ael*, the word in *Ael'thar* | **one sentence across many hulls**: three roads, one hour | Fleets that arrive on three bearings as if one hand had flung them. **The dark mirror of the Aetherbond** (§8) | 23–26 |

**The Fall and the Tides fit together this way** (resolves story_bible A12 #2). The first Tides took the ten havens easily, because the havens had "no standing navy, no war fleet, no defensive doctrine" (Legend VI), and one sign was enough: *Burn.* Rivenkeep is the first place anyone **fought back**. So the Rivenmen first meet the Hasty Tide there, the last of the young wood, sent to finish a shore the council believed already won. Every Tide after it is finer. The Thrones never sailed in the Fall. Remove the Leviathan from the Crystal spire's night; at most, "a shadow in the fog greater than any hull" was glimpsed. They came last, and they sit in the havens still. The Rivenmen must go out to them (the homecoming frame, story_bible B2.2).

## 3 · THE MYSTWOOD REMEMBERS

"But Mystwood remembers" (Legend IV). The Aelvaren sailed itself home carrying the whole of the broken rite. That same property is the fleet's learning:

- **Within one siege, the fleet is one wood.** What a hull sees (the grey-sailed read its sight), what strikes it (*the dread of the wood*) and where it was struck (*the wood remembers where it was struck*) passes hull to hull along the root-bond. It passes slowly, and fastest through the Standard-Bearer. Sink the hull that saw before it can pass it on, and the knowledge sinks with it. Kill the heart, and the knowing crawls. These are the fleet's three senses (Fleet Memory §2: sight, memory, fear), given names the Rivenmen can say.
- **Across the war, every wreck teaches its kin.** Wood grown from one grove is one wood at any distance, as the Aetherbonded feel each other's pain across a city. When a hull dies at Rivenkeep, what it felt flows back through the roots to its kin still at sea and still in the groves. The next Tide's Heartwoods are therefore carved *knowing* what the wall did to the last. So the fleet the Rivenmen meet "begins as a mob and becomes an army" (story_bible C2).

> **FOR WRITERS · THE WOOD REMEMBERS THE WALL, NOT THE HAND.** Across the war, the wood learns *kinds* of things: that walls hide guns, that silence can kill, that a fleeing ship draws pursuit. It does **not** remember a particular Captain's habits from one siege to the next. In play, what a fleet learns of *you* lasts one siege only (framework §6, CALL-OUT 7). Do not write lines like "It remembers that you ran" across battles (GDD AI §5 has one). Within a siege they are true: "They marked that the north holds its fire."

**The fleet reads the Rivenmen: the Shore-Reading.** Within a siege, the Heartwood keeps a small count of how the Rivenmen fight. Those who write the dispatches may put it in the fleet's voice, as a lookout would guess it:

| The fleet's reading (as the lookouts put it) | What it means in play |
|---|---|
| "They marked that the north holds its fire." | Silent Quarter |
| "They know we guard the east road." | Favoured Bearing |
| "They have learned we strike first and far." | Early Fire |
| "They have seen our reach move when pressed." | Re-tasker |
| "They know we move the guns they find." | Relocator |
| "They know we mend what they break." | Mender |
| "They know we kill the bearers first." | Core-Hunter |
| "They know we are ready for the sky." | Sky-Ready |

These lines appear **only** between assaults, and only as what the fleet *learned*. **Never** write one that says what it *will do* (framework §9c red line).

## 4 · THE WOOD CANNOT LIE, SO THE LATE TIDES LEARNED TO MISLEAD

The Mystaeri trusted wood for anything binding because it rendered intent truly (Legend II). The Shorelanders trusted stone, and stone carried nothing (Legend III). The war begins with that mismatch. In its last years it closes in the cruellest way: **the wood learned deceit from the stone-folk.**

The Captain feints. The Rivenmen hold their guns silent to draw ships into kill-zones. They leave a breach open that looks shut, and they move the gun the fleet came to find. Every wreck from those deceptions flowed back through the roots. By the Tide that Learned Deceit, the Heartwoods carved orders that are each **perfectly true** and together **a lie**:

> *Show them fear in the east.* (and the east is where they are strongest)
> *Do the same thing thrice; the fourth time, mean it.*
> *Show them straw until they stop shooting; then send the men.*

Seren's line, when she first reads one: *"The wood does not lie. It was told to make us believe a lie, and it obeyed."* The Bonded answer, one beginning and the other finishing: *"They learned it from us." / "We taught it them with every silent gun."* This is the long defeat on both sides (story_bible B2.1 #7): the war made each people more like the other.

> **FOR WRITERS · THE LIE ALWAYS SHOWS ITS SEAM.** The game's fairness law is that every false show carries a counter-tell:
> - phantom ships leave no wreck and do not flinch when shells fall near them;
> - a feigned rout keeps its spacing;
> - the laden ship rides low.
>
> The tales should honour this. Rivenmen who look closely always *could* have known. The heroism is in looking.

## 5 · THE HEARTWOODS AND THEIR GRAINS

Every Standard-Bearer was grown from one tree chosen by the war-council, and **the tree's grain is its temper**. The council chose the trees in grief and anger, and the tempers show it. A grain is the carver's temperament, grown into a heart. It decides how the Heartwood prefers to fight and what it does when a carving fails. The Mystaeri word is *vael*, "grain". The Rivenmen name each grain for the tree it most resembles, and Seren writes the names down so.

| Grain | Its temper | How the Rivenmen know it | Hearts that bear it (the hull that takes the standard) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Greenwood** | none yet: the young wood of the Hasty Tide | It repeats the carving that failed until the hull that bore it is gone | every hull of the Hasty Tide |
| **Ironbark** | stubborn | It comes again where it failed, heavier | the plain bombarding hulls |
| **Willow** | cautious | It looks before it goes, and turns from the first failure | the scouts; the healing hulls |
| **Blackthorn** | vengeful | It goes where it lost the most | the hunters of guns |
| **Heartoak** | proud | It never feints, never turns aside, and returns by the same road | the shield-hulls; the rams; the great hulls |
| **Twistbough** | cunning (the *twisted boughs for ceremony*, Legend II) | It never plays the same trick twice | the raiders; the fog-hulls; the mimics |
| **Yew** | patient | It waits, burns, and waits again | the carriers; the fire-sowers |
| **Ashwood** | rash | It throws everything at once | the landing ships; the goads |
| **Silverbark** | wise (the *silver-barked elders for council*, Legend II) | It reads the Rivenmen best, and bends least | the tenders of the fleet |

**When the standard passes, the grain passes with it** (framework §7). The heart of the hull that takes up the standard carves in its own grain, and it carves more plainly, for it was not grown for command: "a lieutenant standing in." Kill a cunning heart and leave a proud one alive, and the next day the fleet comes straight and will not turn. The hearth saying: **"Choose which heart you leave them."**

**When the heart dies at sea,** the hulls keep the hour the heart foretold, but not the reason. A ship carved to strike when the feint was answered strikes on the hour whether the feint was answered or not (framework §4, from the Joined Tide on). Write this as the uncanniest thing the Rivenmen see: ships obeying a dead mind, exactly on time, into nothing.

## 6 · THE CARVINGS: A GRAMMAR THAT GROWS

A stratagem is a carving. The carvings grow the way the Tides do: **sign → phrase → sentence → joined sentence → deceit → judgement**. Every carving belongs to one of **ten lines**. Each line keeps one **root sign**, the first stroke Seren learns to recognise. So a Rivenman who has read the young wood can read the root of the old, even when he cannot read the rest.

| Line | Root sign (as Seren names it) | What the sign looks like in the wood | Its first word (Hasty or Second Tide) | Its last (framework §15) | Master (the Throne) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flood | **the Wave** | one stroke curling forward | *To the shore.* | *Give them the middle; take the sides; close the hand.* | Thaesaen (the "Leviathan") |
| Sounding | **the Lone Stroke** | a short cut running ahead of a long one | *Go until struck.* | *Come as the young wood came.* | Naelthar (the "Storm King") |
| Screen | **the Bar Before** | a bar across a stroke | *Before the bearer.* | *Strike one head; two will answer.* | Leavaren (the "Hydra") |
| Hunt | **the Spark** | a cut ending in a star-notch, the flash | *Where it flashed.* | *Every gun that speaks, the mountain answers.* | Rhenvael (the "Colossus") |
| Siege | **the Mute Square** | a closed square: how they write *stone*, the thing that does not speak | *Break the wall.* | *Go under what you cannot go through.* | Ralensaen (the "Ancient Treant") |
| Feint | **the Two Mouths** | two strokes, one of them hollow | *Loud in one place; land in another.* | *Show them the host; come up beneath the stone.* | Senneir (the "Sandworm") |
| Lure | **the Turned Stern** | a stroke that hooks back on itself | *Home when wounded.* | *Burn as you go; let them chase the fire.* | Esthaer (the "Magma Titan") |
| Bleed | **the Breath** | a gap left between two cuts | *Spend their fire on wood of no worth.* | *Rot the wall; wait for the mending to fail.* | Vaelress (the "Plague Herald") |
| Patience | **the Knot** | a stroke that stops in a knot of the grain | *Wait in the grey.* | *Wait in the white; strike the stone that cannot mend.* | Eirlenth (the "Frost Wyrm") |
| Mask | **the Smoothed Cut** | a carving filled and smoothed over, so it can be felt and not seen | *Grey before the landing.* | *Show them themselves.* | Neivaere (the "Crystal Lich") |

The full list of 45 carvings, with their English "carven" lines, is framework §14. Writers may quote those carvings freely. They are the text the Reading shows.

**The Readings, as they sound at each Tide.** These are original examples. The generator will produce the real ones from what the fleet actually did.

- **Hasty Tide.** One sign, unread: the Bonded give one word and cannot say more.
  > *…shore…* · "One sign, and we are not sure even of that. The young wood had one word, and it said it at our wall until it drowned."
- **Second Tide.** Named, with gaps.
  > *Screen … bearer.* · "The front hull was told to die first. It obeyed."
- **Tide of Remembering.** Sentences, with doubtful words marked.
  > *Where the stone fell [silent?], look before you trust.* · "They remember the gun we moved. They came to see whether it was still there."
- **Joined Tide.** Fluent, with the links.
  > *The west waited for the east to be struck.* · "Three roads and one hour. They move as the Bonded move."
- **Tide that Learned Deceit.** Fluent, with the aim.
  > *Show them fear in the east.* · *Cut against: they hold the north silent.* · "They carved it against *us*. Against what we do."
- **Last Tide.**
  > *When the bearer falls, the second takes the standard; when the second falls, go home.* · "They have carved their own defeat into the plan. They know us now." (story_bible C3)
- **Thrones.** The true name, the haven, and the wrong remembered.
  > "Here is the name it gave itself, and the name of the harbour it sits in, and the name of the grief it was grown to carry."

**Late readings in the fight itself** (from the Tide that Learned Deceit). When a hull of the carving sinks in mid-assault, Seren can now read its **root sign** from the wreck as it floats: *the Two Mouths*, *the Turned Stern*. Only the root, never the rest. Write it as a skill hard-won: she learned to read the first stroke in the time it takes a wreck to roll over.

## 7 · HALVARD AND RHYNA LEARN TO READ THE WOOD

The Stonewrights could not read the Mystaeri warnings, because stone carried nothing (Legend III). The wreck-wood is different. It is the one Mystaeri writing that still *speaks*, for a carving in living wood "would interpret the writer's intent and render meaning clear to any who touched it" (Legend II). It does so slowly, and only to hands that are patient, as the petrified gift did (Legend IV).

**How the Reading is done (the rite as the tales should show it).**
- It is performed on the shingle below the wall after an assault, on wreck-wood hauled out of the surf.
- **Rhyna lays her palms flat on the grain, and Halvard lays his over hers.** The wood speaks slowly into her hands. Through the bond, Halvard *hears* what she feels, and he gives it words. The Aetherbonded share pain and thought (Legend I), and so they share this.
- Sometimes it goes the other way: his hands on the wood, hers over his, and she speaks. Either way, **neither reads alone**. The tales should say plainly that the wood gave its meaning to no single Stonewright who tried.
- **Seren Reader-of-Wood, their apprentice, sets it down.** Where the carving is broken she says so, and she does not mend it with guesses (story_bible B6 #11).

**The Readings run backwards from the emissary's words.** The Kneeling Guest came with three true words and no sentences: *"Stop… Sky… Dying…"* Now the builders are the ones fumbling fragments. The first wreck gives the Bonded one sign they cannot read. The Hasty Tide's Readings should feel exactly like the emissary's speech, only turned around: the right sign, read truly, and a sign without a sentence is only a mark.

**The arc of the Reading across the war** (it rises with the Tides, §2):

| Tide | What the Bonded can do | A moment worth a tale |
|---|---|---|
| Hasty | Feel that the wood *means*, and give one word | **The First Glyph.** The first wreck. Rhyna feels one sign burn in her palms, and Halvard says *shore* and cannot say why he knows it |
| Second | Read two signs; name the carving | **The Night of the Naming.** They go back to every Hasty Tide wreck kept in the vault and read them all at last: one word each, repeated at the wall until the wood drowned |
| Remembering | Read sentences, and see that the wood *remembers* | **The Wreck That Went Home.** A wreck they did not burn drifts off on the tide. The next day the fleet comes to the very gun that sank it |
| Joined | Read the ties between hulls | **Three Roads, One Hour.** Halvard reads *the west waited for the east* and goes pale, for it is how the Bonded work a wall |
| Deceit | Read the aim of a carving | **What We Taught Them** (§4) |
| Last | Read whole plans | **The Plan That Carved Its Own Defeat.** *"When the second falls, go home."* The first time the enemy is seen to *hope* |
| Thrones | Read true names | **The Thrones that Walk the Sea** (story_bible B8 #16), one reading per haven retaken |

**The last reading is the green sliver** (Legend IX; story_bible B8 #17, B9 #6). After the last Throne falls, the Bonded find a sliver of *living* Mystwood, still green and still aware. **Both touch it**, and both hear *"We remember our home too"* in the same instant. It is the only Reading in the war that is not an order. Stage it as the turn (story_bible B2.1 #8, eucatastrophe), not as a footnote.

**A lay, for the hearth** (original; embed it the way story_bible B6 #10 suggests):
> *Two hands on the grey wood, and the grey wood waking;*
> *one to feel the carving, and one to give it tongue;*
> *what the young ships knew was a single word, unbreaking;*
> *what the old ships knew, we learned from them too young.*

## 8 · THE FLEET AS THE DARK MIRROR OF THE AETHERBOND

This is the spine to hold under every fleet tale (story_bible finding 1, B8).
- The **Aetherbond** made two Shorelanders one: wordless and in step, their work "multiplied twelvefold".
- The **Ael'thar** offered to make two peoples one front, "harm to one was harm to both", and it was refused at the outpost door.
- The **root-bond** of the fleet is that same idea turned to war. Hulls that feel each other's wounds (resolve contagion) and wake to each other's deeds (the answering sails) work together without a word, and by the **Joined Tide** they move as the Bonded move.

So when Halvard and Rhyna read *three roads, one hour* in the wreck-wood, they are reading a description of themselves. The tales should let that land quietly and never explain it. The Mystaeri word for the Joined Tide, *Aelthae*, is built on *ael*, the same root as *Ael'thar*. What the Kneeling Guest offered in peace came back as a fleet.

In play, each has a **build-up**: the Throne's great hull **groans** once for every assault in which it has not yet carved its Judgement, and on the third groan the Judgement falls (framework §14, Thrones). The Rivenmen count the groans. Write them as the most dreaded sound of the war.

**Unexploited tragedy, now load-bearing** (story_bible A12 #23). The Thrones were grown from the last living pillars of the fog. Making them finished killing the Mystaeri sky. The Heartwoods of the Thrones carry that too: every Judgement was cut by a people spending the last of their air on vengeance.

Each seed stands alone at 250–600 words, has a named teller, and follows the register rules (story_bible B3). The *unlock* follows play (framework §13) and is reachable by any way of surviving (story_bible C5).

| # | Tale | Teller | Form | Unlocks | The seed |

## 11 · RULES FOR EVERYONE WRITING THE FLEET

1. **The Reading is always true.** It explains *this* battle and never hints at the next (story_bible C3; framework §9b).
2. **Dispatches carry what the fleet learned, never what it will do** (framework §9c).
3. **Hint discipline for battle messages.** If one message in a battlefield's pool hints at the battle's carving, every message in that pool must carry the same hint, or none may (story_bible C6). Otherwise two players get unequal information from the same seed.
4. **The wood cannot lie.** Deception is true orders meant to mislead, and every false show has a seam (§4).
5. **The Bonded always read together.** No tale gives the reading, the cry, the Rite or the sliver to one of them alone (Jack's ruling).
6. **The wood remembers the wall, not the hand** (§3). Do not write fleet memory of the player's past sieges.
7. **The Hasty Tide is crude in kind, not merely weak.** It is one sign, no waiting, the same wall, the same failure repeated. That is Jack's "the first are not organized."
8. **No design vocabulary in any in-world text.** "Sortie" is the one exception, and only in soldiers' mouths (story_bible B3). Never write *stratagem*, *AoR*, *contact*, *hinge*, *link*, *grain weight* or *era*. Write *carving*, *reach*, *ship*, *the moment*, *the answering sail*, *temper* and *Tide*.
9. **Register:** the Silmarillion mode for narration, the Odyssey frame for the collection (story_bible B2–B3). Take the methods, never the sentences. Do not quote or closely paraphrase Tolkien or any Homer translation, and do not borrow invented names from either.
10. **Never resurrect superseded variants**: Mystborn, Kiera, Rak'thol, Hale-as-translator (story_bible A5).
