# THE TEN THEATERS AGAINST THE STORY

*Jack's note N15, analysis only. Nothing here changes a haven, a Throne, a map, a rule or a number. Read against GDD v6.7.0 (Grid, DMZ, Terrain, Theaters, the theater detail cards, Boss Mechanics), the archived boss table (GDD_Removed), Ships §4, Campaign §2, the Map View Briefs, the AI image prompts, the journal's theater rulings, legends_final.md (III.1, III.2, V.6, §6, §9, §10) and fiction_hooks.md. 2026-09-26.*

---

## 1 · The short answer

The ten theaters work as games. Each Layer-A mechanic earns its place, and none needs replacing. The trouble is that the theaters were decided before the Legends, and part of each theater is its *premise*: what the place is, and what comes against it. Three of those premises do not belong in a mountain realm on an eastern sea, attacked by a fleet grown from wood.

- **Seven fit already:** Coastal, River, Swamp, Forest, Mountain, Desert and Frozen. Four of them need the same small skin fix. The GDD's enemy "vehicles" (war wagons, mule carts, camel caravans, sled cavalry) become grown hulls that come in from the sea.
- **Two need a new premise with the same mechanic: Sky and Crystal.** The Legends have already done half the work. III.1 puts Highreach on crags, not in the sky, and drops Glasspire into its own cellars. The GDD never caught up.
- **One needs a light touch: Volcanic.** A harbour cannot stand in an ocean of lava, and a fleet made of wood that burns should not sail on one.
- **Nothing needs REPLACE.** The labels "Sky" and "Crystal" can even stay.

The Campaign doc already says theaters are "freshness — scenery and feel, never punishment," and the journal's ruling is that "theaters are locations, not mechanics." So every change below is a change to text, art or enemy skins. The sim, the 190 battlefields, the five-layer matrix, the boss chassis and the leaderboards are all untouched.

## 2 · What a theater has to pass

Five tests, all drawn from the story as it now stands.

1. **It is a haven-land on the one coast.** The Shorelands are mountains and high hills "tumbling down to meet the sea" on an eastern coast, with ten harbour-cities about their natural havens. The builders feared the open water and kept to sheltered shores. Every theater is one stretch of that coast.
2. **The fleet comes by water.** The fleet was grown to sail, and it came out of the grey across the sea. So every theater's waterway begins at the sea. Where the waterway runs on over land (dune, forest floor, scree), it becomes ground that living wood can cross, and the Legends already allow that: the root-men, Ralenorn who "walked, as a pillar would walk home if it could," and the crawling root-hulls of V.6. Horses, mules, camels, sleds, propellers, dirigibles and golems fail this test in every theater they appear in.
3. **Stone, wood and faith, and no other wonder.** The Shorelanders' arts are building and belief. The one wonder in this world is the Mystwood: it grows ships, breathes the mist, obeys carvings and walks as root-men. A theater may use any wonder of the wood, but no other magic. That rules out floating stone, crystal that glows by itself, and clockwork.
4. **Keeps in the haven-land, the haven in the Finale.** The campaigns are fought along the Rivenmen's line of keeps across the ten haven-lands, and the havens themselves are retaken only in the Finale (Legends §10.8, I.5). So a theater has to be a whole country that can hold a line of keeps for every campaign battle set there. The haven itself is the boss map.
5. **The mechanic is the fixed point.** Each Layer-A rule is kept exactly. Only the reason the rule is true changes.

**One finding runs through all ten.** The GDD treats the advancing DMZ as enemy ground: the waterway grows, and a docked transport "redocks at the new friendly-zone edge." So whatever the DMZ turns into has to be something the hulls can cross. The GDD's skins manage this with lava, void and sand. The proposals below manage it by turning the lost ground into sea (the sea-stacks, the caverns, the burning shore) or into ground the root-hulls can crawl over (dune, the forest's new growth).

## 3 · The seven that fit

### 1 · Coastal — Eldhythe, the Eldest Haven · KEEP

- **Identity:** Cam 1, 60:40, no Layer-A modifier, static DMZ. It teaches the game.
- **Mystarch:** the Leviathan, Thaelorn, tide-heart, Heartoak. It submerges every two sorties, resurfaces near a keep, and its tidal wave pushes walls one grid outward.
- **Fit:** It is the story's own ground: a harbour under the mountains, the grey sails on open water, and the harbour-houses where the pleading stones hung as ornament (III.2).
- **Skin only:** The GDD card still lists "wooden galleons, rowboats, rope-netted transports, fire barges", which are built ships with crews. The hero art prompt already has the right thing ("dark organic Mystwood hulls that look grown not built"), so the card should follow the prompt. Art nit: the palm trees pull the look toward the Mediterranean in a realm of Norse and Old English names that has a frozen north. Wind-bent pine and gorse would hold the palette. This is Jack's call, and palms are not wrong for a warm middle coast.

### 2 · River — Tidesmeet, where the river meets the tide · KEEP

- **Identity:** Cam 3. River channels split the build zone into two or three islands joined by narrow land bridges that act as chokepoints. Static DMZ.
- **Mystarch:** the Hydra, Selvaren, bearer of branches, Ironbark. It has three heads worth a third of its HP each, it dives, and it regenerates 5% per sortie if left undamaged.
- **Fit:** Excellent. A mountain river reaching the tide makes a mouth full of low islands, and the fleet sails up from the sea. The land bridges rhyme with Tidesmeet's three spans, which were Mystholder kin "laid flat and walked upon." The Hydra's regrowth now carries N12 as well: the pillars would have grown back if the Shorelanders had not cut the regrowth, and Selvaren already says "as our kin would have grown again, if the axe had let them." Press it every sortie or it heals, as the wood would have.
- **Skin only:** "green lowlands" can become the tidal flats at the river's mouth under the mountains. This is optional.

### 3 · Swamp — Fenholm, the town that was always sinking · KEEP

- **Identity:** Cam 5. Bog grids take −3 HP per sortie and allow no cannons. Static DMZ. Nature is the first enemy.
- **Mystarch:** the Plague Herald, Vaelsorn, the grain that rots, Yew. Its poison cloud wears down walls, it dissolves into the water, and it spawns lesser troops.
- **Fit:** Very good. A coastal fen at a delta mouth is real ground. The GDD's "half-submerged ancient stone ruins" are exactly Fenholm's older courses: "every generation laid a new course on the old." Rot is the haven's own grief, since Fenholm stood on Mystholder pilings "because our wood does not rot."
- **Skin only:** The "disease barges trailing green fog" become rot: a spore-mist off grown wood going soft. The wood makes rot; the Mystaeri do not make plague. "Plague" can stay in the soldiers' fear-name. N16 hook: the Herald's lesser troops can be root-men that sprout from the rot, the clearest place to show how the landings are grown. Art nit: mangroves and Spanish moss are subtropical. Alder carr, sedge, reed-beds and sphagnum would suit this coast better. Known issue, not new: "Herald" collides with the Herald lineage name (story_bible A11).

### 4 · Forest — Holtward, the keep under the canopy · KEEP (skin fix)

- **Identity:** Cam 7. The densest trees in the game. Fog of war: ships stay hidden until they fire, are hit, or take a near miss. DMZ advance, the "forest growth" (per the specialties table and the DMZ section; the detail card omits it, see §9).
- **Mystarch:** the Ancient Treant, Ralenorn, root-heart, Ironbark. Its roots grow one grid per sortie through walls, it hides as a tree, and it spawns trees.
- **Fit:** The haven fits: a wooded southern coast ("in the deep woods of the south," III.1), "roofed so close among the trees that a traveller could pass it by." The enemy does not fit. The GDD's waterways here are "forest paths" carrying war wagons, foot scouts, horse-drawn log transports and fire carts, none of which a fleet with no crew could have.
- **Skin fix:** The waterways become sea-lochs and tidal inlets that run in under the canopy: black water in green shade. The fog is the canopy's shade, with the sea-mist caught beneath it. The DMZ advance becomes the wood itself coming on, new growth and root pushing into the clearings, and the root-hulls crawl through it. This is the best place in the game to show N14, the Mystaeri tending the trees as one kind with them, with that tending turned to war. The Treant, which hides as a tree and spawns trees, is that same art at its greatest. N16 hook: the treelings are most at home here.

### 5 · Mountain — Carnhold, the stronghold of the cairns · KEEP (skin fix)

- **Identity:** Cam 9. The troop map. The highest cliff density gives much free enclosure, but troops climb at half speed and come over from every side. Heavy troop compositions. Walls serve as troop barriers as well as enclosure. DMZ advance ("troop pressure").
- **Mystarch:** the Colossus, Rhenvael, stone-grain, Blackthorn. It throws boulders (2×2), climbs to high ground for range, uses caves as stealth (in by one, out by another), and is vulnerable while throwing.
- **Fit:** The realm is mountains, and Rivenkeep itself is a mountain hold, so this is home ground. But as a *harbour*, the GDD's "mountain pass" with its armoured wagons on tracks, mule carts, siege trains and wheeled catapults has no sea to come by.
- **Skin fix:** The pass becomes a **fjord**, a deep arm of the sea between sheer walls. The fleet sails up it, and the root-men land at the waterline and climb (Norway's fjords are the art reference). Everything the mechanic needs is what a fjord landing already looks like: cliffs everywhere, troops climbing, walls sealing the ledges. The art prompt already has "a mountain lake visible through the pass opening where ships approach", so make it the sea. The Colossus's caves become Carnhold's old quarry-galleries and cairn-tombs, since the haven was "cut into the mountain among the burial-cairns." That is a set-piece that means its haven. N16 hook: this is where the root-men's story (climbing, numbers, weight) is told.

### 7 · Desert — Sandreach, the citadel carved in the canyon wall · KEEP (skin fix)

- **Identity:** Cam 13. Permanent +1 wind drift ("Desert Canyon is never calm"). Fissure grids (−2 HP per sortie). Sand fog. DMZ advance (sand). 45:55 falling to 35:65.
- **Mystarch:** the Sandworm, Senneir, the deep-beneath, Willow. It burrows (stealth), charges in a line, erupts under a keep, and is vulnerable just after eruption.
- **Fit:** Better than it first looks. Deserts do meet mountain coasts: where the mountains take the rain, the coast below them stays dry, and the wind spilling over them never stops. The Atlantic coast of Patagonia is exactly this: dry, cut by canyons, famously windy. Sandreach, "not built so much as dug, gallery below gallery into the red rock," is the GDD's canyon fortress already.
- **Skin fix:** The canyons open onto the sea through drowned canyon-mouths, and the fleet sails in. At the canyon heads, where the dunes spill down to the water, the root-hulls of V.6 crawl over the sand, and here they earn their line. The DMZ advance is the dunes marching in, which happens on real coasts, over ground the crawling hulls can cross. The Sandworm needs no magic: Senneir "came up beneath, by the roads our dead had held open," through the old galleries shored with Mystholder timber. The GDD's sand skiffs, dune runners, camel caravans and trebuchets go. Art nit: cacti become thorn-scrub. The oases can stay.

### 8 · Frozen — Rimewatch, the watch on the frozen mere · KEEP (skin fix)

- **Identity:** Cam 15. Ice grids (−3 HP per sortie, no cannons). The ice sheet advances one grid per sortie. Blizzard fog pulses. Permanent +1 wind.
- **Mystarch:** the Frost Wyrm, Eiravel, the long winter, Yew. Its ice breath freezes walls so they cannot be repaired next Build, it hides in the blizzard, and it makes ice grids.
- **Fit:** Good, with one word changed. The GDD's "frozen lake surrounded by snow-covered mountains" is not a harbour. Make the mere a sea-mere: a firth or tidal lagoon that freezes each winter, with the fleet coming through leads in the sea-ice. The art prompt already has "dark open water where icebreaker ships approach."
- **Skin fix:** The advancing ice becomes **ice shove**, wind-driven sea-ice riding up over the shore. It is a real and frightening thing on northern coasts, and the ground it takes becomes broken ice the hulls can reach. The Wyrm's ice breath becomes freezing spray: brine thrown over the walls that freezes where it lands. Spray icing is how ships and harbour walls ice up in a northern winter, so a wooden Throne needs no dragon's breath. *Wait in the white* is the blizzard itself. The GDD's sled cavalry and transports on runners become V.6's hulls that go white in the snow. The great hall standing out on the ice all winter stays exactly as it is.

## 4 · Volcanic — Emberhythe, the forge on the burning shore · REINTERPRET (light)

**Identity:** Cam 11. The lava advances one grid per sortie, walls hold it back for two sorties, and the island shrinks. Crust grids take −5 HP per sortie. The build zone is fragmented. The GDD premise is "a dark volcanic island surrounded by an ocean of lava," where enemies "float freely on the lava."

**Mystarch:** the Magma Titan, Esthaer, the burning, Twistbough. It has a lava-breath cone, leaves a lava trail, sinks into lava and erupts from a flow, and its armour weakens below 50%.

**Fit:** The haven fits: "where the mountain smokes into the sea and the ground is warm underfoot in winter," its forges fed with Mystholder charcoal. The premise does not. An ocean of lava cannot hold a harbour, and wooden hulls on lava ask a great deal of a wood that burns. The Aelvaren burned, and the Fire Ships *are* the wood burning. V.6's "hulls that swim through burning rock in a skin of wet bark" was a patch for exactly this, and it shows.

**Proposal:** Emberhythe's land is a lava-built shore. The mountain has pushed black benches and fingers of new rock out into the sea, with steaming water between them.
- The **waterways** are the sea, boiling white where the flows run in. The fleet is on water again.
- The **DMZ advance** is the young rock breaking away into the sea, one grid per sortie. Lava benches really do collapse like this (Kīlauea's coast is the reference); they are the most dangerous ground on any volcanic shore. The lost ground becomes sea, which the hulls can cross, and walls still hold an edge for two sorties.
- **Crust grids** are new crust over rock still hot beneath. The **fragmentation** is the fingers of rock with sea-inlets between them.
- The orange stays on land, where the flows still glow, so the theater keeps its hellish palette.

**The Magma Titan survives whole.** Esthaer burns *itself*: "We made a trick of our own burning." Its lava trail becomes a wake of burning sap on the water, and its breath a gout of fire. It sinks under the steam where one flow meets the sea and rises at another. Its armour weakening below 50% becomes the burning eating it, which is the Throne's own tragedy made mechanical. The homecoming line ("burned out on the shore like a forge left untended, and the black crust closed over it") already fits.

**If Jack prefers the lava ocean,** it can stay. It is the smallest strain of the three. But the boiling sea costs only a paragraph and an art brief, and it retires the wet-bark line.

## 5 · Sky — Highreach, the tower of the floating stones · REINTERPRET

**Identity:** Cam 17, 45:55 falling to 35:65. Disconnected platforms with **no bridges**, each its own micro-fortress. Cannons on one island cannot cover the approaches to another. Crag grids at the edges (−3 HP per sortie). The void consumes the edges from every side, one grid per sortie, and walls hold an edge for two sorties.

**Mystarch:** the Storm King, Naelthar, blood of the sky, Ashwood. It flies between platforms, hides in a thundercloud, strikes the highest-HP cannon with lightning first, and cannons fire at half range against it.

**Why the GDD premise fails.** It fails all five tests at once.
- Floating stone islands need a magic the Shorelanders do not have.
- A people of stone would not build on nothing.
- There is no harbour.
- The fleet, grown to sail, would have to fly: the GDD gives it propeller barges, gliders, chain-hung carriers and dirigibles.
- The Legends have already moved Highreach: "on crags so high that the masons lifted their courses on cranes, and the stones seemed to hang upon the wind." Only the GDD still has it in the sky.

**Proposal: the stacks of Highreach.** Highreach stands on a coast of **sea-stacks**: pillars of rock rising sheer out of the sea off a high headland, each with a flat top far above the surf (the Old Man of Hoy and the Twelve Apostles are the art references). Each piece of the GDD maps onto the stacks without changing a rule.

| GDD (Sky Bastion) | The stacks of Highreach |
|---|---|
| floating stone islands | Walled stack-tops. On most days the sea-cloud lies at the stacks' waists, and from the headland the tops seem to ride on it. That is why the old songs call them *the floating stones*, and the Fight view keeps the GDD's look: pale stone standing out of a cloud-bank, the sea glimpsed through the gaps |
| no bridges | The spans and crane-lines that joined the stacks were Mystholder beam, "lighter than iron," and they burned in the Fall. Every stack stands alone now. This settles the Map View Brief's "narrow bridges are the only connections" against the GDD's "no bridges," in the GDD's favour |
| the void | The sea, far below. Ships work the channels between the stacks' feet |
| crag grids | The undercut edges, crumbling where the sea has eaten beneath them |
| void advance from all edges | The edges falling into the sea. Stacks are made and lost this way, and they erode from every side at once. The lost ground is sea, which the hulls can cross |
| cannons cannot cover another island | The channels are deep and the stacks stand far apart |
| rocks · trees · cliffs | fallen capstones and sea-worn blocks · wind-bent thorn and stunted pine rooted in the cracks · the stack faces and ledges |
| transports on chains; gliders; dirigibles | Hulls that moor under the faces while the root-men climb them (the cliff rules: half speed, fully exposed); fast skiffs threading the channels |

**The grief fits better than before.** Naelthar names the cranes of Highreach as its kin: "they raised your towers into the sky that was ours." On a stack coast those cranes are the whole city, because every stone had to be lifted from the boats below.

**The Storm King keeps its flight: the one Throne that leaves the sea.** "We came down out of that sky all at once, with the last of it in our sails." Naelthar spends the last of the Mystaeri's own breath to lift one Throne onto the crags. That is the tragedy of the Thrones (story_bible A12 #23) made visible, and Ashwood's rash temper made literal. The thundercloud is that last mist wrapped around it. The lightning is the storm striking the proudest gun on the stack-tops. Half range is shooting up into a squall. The homecoming ("came down out of its cloud onto the crags, and did not go up again") already reads this way. So the boss is untouched, and the only thing in the theater that flies is the one thing the story says flies.

**N16 hook.** On a stack coast the only way to strike a stack-top from above is from the air, so the bombers (the Skyfall lineage) matter most here. It is the natural place to show how they are grown. V.6's "hulls hung from bladders that ride the high air" is no longer needed for a sky theater, and can move to the bombers.

**Labels.** "Sky" and "Sky Bastion" can stay (bastions on stack-tops above the cloud), with no churn in the battle spreadsheet or the "Sk" abbreviation. If Jack wants a label that stops inviting floating-island art, the plain alternative is **Stacks**.

## 6 · Crystal — Glasspire, the spire that caught the sun · REINTERPRET

**Identity:** Cam 19, the last and most complex theater, 45:55 falling to 35:65.
- **Bounce:** every shot, the player's and the fleet's, bounces off the ground at 100/50/25% damage. Bosses get four bounces, at 100/60/35/20%.
- **Fog:** darkness, and each bounce clears 3×3 of it.
- **Collapse:** the DMZ advances one grid per sortie.
- **Fract grids:** −2 HP per sortie. There is no wind underground.

**Mystarch:** the Crystal Lich, Silvaere, the silvered, the mirror, Silverbark. Its beam ricochets off crystal formations, it becomes a crystal that must be identified, and it raises reflective shields that bounce the player's shots.

**Why the GDD premise fails.**
- A natural cavern of "hexagonal amethyst and quartz columns" with "luminescent moss" is a fairy-tale place that nothing in the Legends supports.
- It has no harbour and no way in for a fleet.
- Crystal golems, gem crawlers and prism cannons are not grown wood.
- A *spire* (sun, height, glass) is not a *cavern* (darkness, depth).
- The Legends' bridge ("collapsed in a single night into its own cellars, and the dark beneath it lies open still") solves the Finale, because Silvaere sits in those cellars. It does not solve the campaign. The campaign battles set here from Cam 19 on happen at the Rivenmen's keeps in Glasspire's *land*, and the Throne's own seat cannot also be their line of keeps (test 4).

**Proposal: the crystal caverns of Glasspire.**
- **"Crystal" is what the Shorelanders call their finest glass,** as we say cut crystal. Glass is stone taught to let the light through, which is why a people of stone could make it. Glasspire was the glass-makers' haven: a headland of white sand above a coast of **sea-caverns**, where seven generations of Stonewrights cut their furnaces and grinding-halls into the caves and made the great glass. The whole coast glitters with their crystal: slag, cullet and flawed lenses, the waste of seven generations spent on one work.
- **The spire was a lighthouse.** Its great glass "could be seen from a day's sail out, throwing the sun far into the grey," and Silvaere says that where it fell, "the breath burned away." The Shorelanders' proudest work was, without their knowing it, a wound in the mist. That is already in III.1 and III.2; the theater gives it a place.
- **The land:** sea-caverns, some still vaulted and dark, some fallen open to the sky (Benagil on the Algarve coast is the reference: a sea-cave whose roof has fallen in, with light pouring down and boats sailing in through the mouth). The fleet enters by the sea-mouths. The keeps stand on the cavern floors and on the headland above.

| GDD (Crystal Depths) | The crystal caverns |
|---|---|
| all shots bounce | **Ricochet.** Shot skips off glass-paved floors and still cave-water. Skipping shot across water was a real gunner's practice. The numbers stay |
| darkness fog; each bounce clears 3×3 | Dark under the standing vaults. A shot striking glass throws sparks and glitter, and the dark lights up where it strikes |
| collapse (DMZ advance) | The undercut vaults and old glass-galleries still falling in, with the sea taking each fall. The lost ground becomes water, which the hulls can cross |
| fract grids | the crazed glass floors of the old grinding-halls |
| crystal pillars · growths · ridges | Great standing shards and furnace-blocks · drifts of broken crystal that the tide heaps up again every night (which is why a damaged one "grows back") · cave-ribs and fallen courses of dressed stone |
| crystal golems, gem crawlers, prism cannons | grown hulls silvered with salt; shard-throwers whose shot bursts into glass on impact |
| no wind underground | stays: the vaults shelter the floors |

- **The Finale map is the undercroft** beneath the fallen spire, the greatest cavern of all, where Silvaere sits "one shard among a thousand."
- **The mechanic means the haven.** In this theater your own misses come back at you and "high-accuracy cannons are critical because misses bounce into friendly walls." It is the haven whose grief is the Shorelanders' own glass, and whose Throne's judgement is *Show them themselves*. No other theater rhymes this closely with its story.
- **The Crystal Lich survives whole.** Its beam becomes sunlight caught in a surviving shard of the great glass and thrown, a burning-glass, ricocheting off the standing shards: Glasspire's glass turned back on the people who made it. "Becomes a crystal (must be identified)" is already in III.2 (one shard among a thousand). The reflective shields become silvered plates of Silverbark that turn shot. The fear-name can stay, since *lich* is Old English for a corpse, and to the soldiers it is a silvered dead thing among the glass. The homecoming ("broke like a mirror, and every piece of it showed us our own lamps") already fits.
- **Labels.** "Crystal", "Crystal Depths", "Fract Grid" and the purple, prismatic palette can all stay. The rainbow caustics become light through broken glass.

*Honest limit.* Crystal stays the most fantastical theater, a battlefield in half-roofed sea-caverns. But after this change everything in it is something the Shorelanders made, or a real kind of coast.

## 7 · One rule fixes the rest: the fleet comes by water

Taken together, the skin fixes are one rule for the whole game, and it can go in the GDD's Map Zones paragraph: **every theater's waterway begins at the sea, and where the fleet leaves the water it crawls, as living wood.**

- The GDD now says "even theaters without literal water (Mountain, Forest) use theater-appropriate waterway coloring," and it lists oceans, rivers, lava, frozen seas and void. Under the rule the list becomes: sea, estuary, fen-channel, sea-loch, fjord, the boiling sea, drowned canyon, ice-leads, the sea between the stacks, and the flooded caverns. Two theaters also have ground the hulls can crawl over: the Desert's dunes and the Forest's new growth.
- **Every GDD "enemy vehicles" line needs the same pass**, not only Sky and Crystal: galleons, rowboats, war wagons, horses, mule carts, siege trains, camel caravans, sled cavalry, propellers, dirigibles, golems. V.6 already rules "no one sails in us." The GDD cards are the last place the old crews survive.
- **The Legends' hedge can go.** III.1 says "a haven among the Shorelanders was not always a harbour for ships," and §9 says "a haven need not stand on a harbour." Under these proposals every haven is a real harbour, and "Ten cities, ten harbours" is true without a footnote. That is a later Legends edit, not this pass.

## 9 · Where the GDD disagrees with itself

These need reconciling whatever Jack decides about the premises.

1. **Forest and the DMZ.** The specialties table and the DMZ section give Forest a DMZ advance ("forest growth"), but its detail card lists fog only.
2. **Sky's crag grids.** The Fragile Grids table says 3 HP per sortie. The Sky card says "10% chance of losing 1 block per sortie." A chance roll breaks the determinism law (same battle, same result, for everyone), so use the table.
3. **Sky's bridges.** The GDD says none. The Map View Brief says "narrow bridges are the only connections," and the art prompt draws "narrow stone bridges and rope bridges." §5 takes the GDD's side.
4. **Frozen's "Ice Physics."** It still heads Frozen's Layer-A line, but ice drift was removed on 2026-04-06 ("wind drift covers all drift").
5. **The fog list.** The GDD table gives fog to Forest, Desert, Frozen and Crystal. The 2026-04-06 journal entry also gave Mountain a cloud fog. The GDD says the list is archived with the theater and Ships detail. Settle it there.
6. **Crystal's bounce.** The journal first had missed shots bounce "3 grids along the floor," then "ALL shots bounce 100/50/25." The GDD carries the second, but the Map View Brief's "Mirror Cannon" framing belongs to the first era.
7. **Volcanic card.** It has a broken sentence: "walls take 5 HP heat damage per sortie and The stable basalt areas…"

## 10 · Beyond the ten

- **Theater 11, the "Clockwork Citadel"** of the Future Release section, would also strain belief. Gears and timed wall segments belong to neither a stone-and-faith realm nor a living wood. The story has exactly ten havens and ten Thrones, so an eleventh theater should be a place with *no* Throne, not an eleventh haven. One candidate that fits and brings a genuinely new mechanic is a **tidal causeway**: a stretch of an existing haven-land where islets are joined to the shore only at low water. Its DMZ would *breathe*, advancing on the flood sortie and falling back on the ebb, deterministically and legibly. This is only a seed, and it adds rules.
- **The Winter Siege's seasonal boss, "the Frost Titan,"** should not be a Throne. The Thrones are ten, and the Finale depends on it. Call it a great hull of the white. Its mechanic (freezing walls into fragile grids mid-fight) is fine.

## 11 · A side flag: the fear-names (N19.9)

The fear-names are the GDD's own, and in-world they are the soldiers' beast-names, shown only until each true name is read (§9 of the Legends), so changing any of them is cheap. N19.9 asks that nothing be exactly traceable to other works. Measured against that:

- **"Treant" is the one exact trace.** Dungeons & Dragons coined it in the 1970s as its substitute for Tolkien's Ents. It is worth replacing. A plain soldiers' name in the Shoreland palette would be *the Root-Giant*.
- **"Frost Wyrm"** is an exact creature name in Warcraft. Optional alternative: *the Rime-Serpent*, from the same root as Rimewatch.
- **"Sandworm"** (Dune) and **"Storm King"** (the Storm Kings of *A Song of Ice and Fire*) are strongly associated but are also ordinary English. It is a soft flag, and Jack's call.
- **Leviathan, Hydra, Colossus and Titan** come from myth and scripture and are free to use. *Lich* is Old English.
- The true-name roots *orn* and *sil* are already flagged in Legends §10.9 and are handled there.

## 12 · Optional: one coast, north to south

This is a coherence check for a future world map, not a proposal. The Legends fix only two points: Holtward is "in the deep woods of the south," and Eldhythe is nearest the Keep ("the first masters to come down from the Keep laid its walls"). One arrangement makes every climate honest on a single eastern coast:

**Rimewatch** (the far north; the firth freezes) · **Highreach** (the northern storm coast of stacks) · **Carnhold** (the fjords, where the high mountains stand straight out of the sea) · **Eldhythe, Tidesmeet, Fenholm** (the middle coast under the Keep's ridge, around one great river-mouth) · **Emberhythe** (the burning mountain on the shore) · **Glasspire** (white sand and sea-caverns) · **Holtward** (the southern woods) · **Sandreach** (the far south, where the mountains take the rain and the wind never stops).
