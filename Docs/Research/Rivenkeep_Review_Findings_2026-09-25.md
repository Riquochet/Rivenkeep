# Rivenkeep — Verified Findings (appendix)

*70 findings from 7 critic lenses. Each was adversarially checked against the source docs by a separate skeptic. "VERIFIED CLAIM" is the skeptic-corrected wording, and the severity shown is the post-verification severity. Out-of-date docs (GDD, SDD, Balance Analysis) were counted as upkeep, not design.*

# LENS: core-loop-fun

CRITIC VERDICT: What the fun is supposed to be: a calm Rampart ring-closing and castle-triage puzzle that feeds a plan → watch → fix loop against a readable, blind fleet, repeated 3–10 times a battle. The core is real. The live enclosure floor, pricing the rebuild in gaps rather than cells, and watching the wall crack mid-Fight are good ideas that suit a phone. But the docs do not yet deliver moment-to-moment fun on a phone, and several locked decisions work against it. Build is the only phase with proven hand-feel. It lost Rampart's stakes along with its speed, most of all where new players meet it (69% free time, a Ready button, ending below the required count is "a wound"). Its touch spec was never re-derived for the 128×80 board or for the late heptomino re-plugs. The Fight takes roughly 40–50% of every sortie and is specified as a hands-off resolution. Its signature decision fires automatically. There is no speed control, even on replays. Early battles have almost no verbs. Its only "active" mode is performing a memorised plan in real time, with no way to schedule it. At session scale, late battles run 17–25 min, five times the Why's own 2–5 min battle loop. There is only a whole-battle checkpoint, in a campaign meant to be failed. By design the back half of each battle deflates into mop-up plus an unopposed Last Stand. None of this is tuning. Each item is a structural choice that the locked "the prototype only tunes feelings" rule forbids a prototype from changing. So they must be reopened on paper now. A Build-touch prototype and a bare-bones Fight (AoRs, ships, hold-fire only) are the fastest ways to learn whether the bones hold.

## core-loop-fun-1 [critical | partially] The Fight is a 54–70 s resolution you mostly watch, its signature decision fires by itself, and there is no speed control
VERIFIED CLAIM: By design the Fight takes roughly 40–50% of steady-state sortie time. It is specified as read-dominant: two act-beats, 'hands off' through the steady watch, and no pause or slow-mo. Speed control is not considered at all, not even for replays. The held alpha can be released manually, but Cannon §6-C auto-releases a hold when any ship reaches the centre, so escorts can trip it before the valuable target the decision exists for. Current docs also disagree on which verb is the Fight's main activity: Deploy §5 and Cannon §6:235/252 say AoR, Fight §2, Fleet Memory §... and Cannon §6:225 say fire-control. Early-Fight passivity has been flagged since May (Balance P0-6, GDD watch-item) and has no design answer in the module docs.
ALREADY MITIGATED: Only in part. Fight §3b (wall-watching) adds a reading job. Fight §3 lets a repel close a Fight at about 25 s. MiniGames §15's Induction, which comes before Cam 1 and is skippable, teaches fire-control timing (levels 6–8, atom F·c) and one full sortie (level 10). Early-Fight passivity survives only as a GDD Known-Unknowns watch-item (GDD.txt:754); journal :839 chose to keep it there. Nothing addresses the Fight's share of time, the auto-release rule, or speed control.
FIX DIRECTION (critic): Decide the Fight is a resolution phase and design it like one.
(a) Add a fast-forward (2×/4×). The simulation is seeded, so speed cannot change outcomes. Have it drop back to 1× automatically on the two eye-seizing events Fight §5 already defines: a valuable target committing into a bright core, and a flank going wrong. Default it on for replays.
(b) Replace hold-fire's auto-release with a trigger the player sets in Deploy on the existing field: release on first contact / first core / flagship / manual. That turns a half-second timing window into a decision without adding a field.
(c) Teach fire-control first in the on-ramp, not fourth. It is the only verb with a visible payoff against a 2-contact wave.
(d) Settle, in one sentence across Cannon §6 and Fight §2, which verb the Fight is about.
(e) Consider giving Deploy, the decisive phase, generous or untimed time from sortie 2 on. The bank can run on Fight time alone.
If you would rather the Fight be a command phase, the proven alternatives are FTL's pause or Bad North's slow-time while a unit is selected. Both are compatible with determinism.

## core-loop-fun-2 [critical | partially] Late battles run 17–25 min on a whole-battle checkpoint in a campaign built to be failed — five times the Why's own battle loop
VERIFIED CLAIM: Derived clocks put late battles at 17–25 min (about 10–15 mid). The Why, which governs and is current, still calls a battle 2–5 min. The one preference-set input, Jack's 3–10 sortie range, was chosen in sorties when a sortie was roughly 1–1.3 min; a sortie is now 2–3 min. Combined with a battle-only checkpoint, a campaign meant to be failed, and a Strike-3 drop-back, one late loss costs about 20 min and three cost about an hour plus a battle. The journal logged this twice and never queued it. The auto-save already snapshots every phase boundary, so a sortie rewind is technically trivial.
ALREADY MITIGATED: Interruption only. GDD auto-save captures full state and RNG seeds at every phase boundary (GDD.txt:2494-2496). Victory §2 makes a mid-Build impossibility an immediate clean loss. Victory §4b lets skill shift sortie count by -1 to +2. The GDD adds a 3-loss hint, and MiniGames §14 has a Coach prescription. None of this changes retry granularity. Journal :254 and :365 log the risk as a 'Campaign-structure question'; no queue item owns it. Journal :522/:626 say Why §7 'owes a correction', and it has not been made.
FIX DIRECTION (critic): State the felt battle length in minutes and derive the sortie count from it. For example, a late battle of 10–12 min or less gives about 4–5 sorties at ~150 s; alternatively, make later sorties shorter. Then re-derive Q and the core counts against that.
Independently of that:
- Add a limited sortie rewind, modelled on Into the Breach's one 'Reset Turn' per battle. Determinism makes a sortie-start snapshot exact.
- Offer unscored 'practice from sortie N' on replays.
- Fast-forward replayed Fights by default.
- Scale or waive the drop-back so a single failure never costs more than about 15 minutes of play.
Put 'battle length' in the queue as a named item.

## core-loop-fun-3 [major | partially] Build's touch spec was written for the old 12-pt grid, and late Builds are precision heptomino re-plugs on a ring that won't fit on screen
VERIFIED CLAIM: Build's touch and camera spec has not been re-derived for 128×80. There is no Build framing or zoom rule, no finger offset (only a magnify halo), an explicit no-edge-auto-scroll rule, and an ambiguous tray tap (rotate vs select). Most importantly, pan time between separated gaps is unpriced in the per-piece times that justify the 54 s clock. The heptomino-in-re-plug concern is already answered on paper by Build §11's rule that late gap size tracks the late piece mean. It needs prototype confirmation, not redesign.
ALREADY MITIGATED: Build §12:506 specifies 'a magnify halo appears if you are zoomed too far out to place precisely', plus the off-screen recall frame (§12:516). Build §11 directly answers the heptomino point: 'late gaps get larger and worse-shaped, not more numerous. Gap size tracks the tier's mean piece, so the piece curve and the tax curve point the same direction'. Its late-stage row reads 'a 6-cell run with a jog a heptomino almost fits'. Build §9 grows spackle to 3×3 from Cam 17, and storage exists as a plug valve.
FIX DIRECTION (critic): Make Build touch the first prototype: one screen, the real 128×80 board, a tier-16 draw, with wind.
Change the spec now:
(a) Lift the dragged piece above the finger, as 1010! and its successors do, and show the landing ghost separately.
(b) Add a Build auto-frame that fits the required rings plus a margin at the largest possible scale, plus edge auto-pan while dragging. This replaces the no-auto-scroll rule.
(c) Settle whether a tray tap rotates or selects.
(d) Make the late draw serve the Build that happens most often. Apply tier escalation to sortie-1 and Last Stand draws, and cap re-plug Builds at hexominoes (or give them a standing small-piece plug slot).
(e) Specify how drift reads under the finger, and whether rotating a soft-locked piece drifts it again.

## core-loop-fun-4 [minor | partially] The Rampart rebuild lost its stakes along with its speed, most of all where new players meet it
VERIFIED CLAIM: Rivenkeep moved Rampart's buzzer-loss stakes into a slower chain: Lock-In nurture, exposure in the next Fight, and a 3-sortie degradation clock. As a result the Why's designed near-miss ('one block short at the buzzer') no longer bites in the moment, and early Builds (69% free, Ready, one gap optional) carry little immediate consequence. The bank rewards finishing fast, which sits awkwardly beside the 'never move fast' framing; that is an intended mastery layer but is not stated as one. Build §3 step 6 and the Ready rule still describe a buzzer defeat and need updating to Victory §2.
ALREADY MITIGATED: Partly.
- Build §3 has a visible castle degradation clock (3/3 → 0/3).
- Deploy §10's Lock-In 2 rewards enclosure in the same sortie (+6% enclosed with castle vs +1% exposed).
- An un-enclosed castle and its guns are exposed to the fleet in that same sortie's Fight.
- Fight §3b makes the resulting damage visible live.
- Build §3 step 6 ('If they are not → defeat') and Build §2's Ready rule ('you cannot skip your way into an un-enclosed defeat') still describe a buzzer loss. Victory §2 overrides both, so this is upkeep.
FIX DIRECTION (critic): Restore stakes, not speed.
(a) Make the buzzer bite on screen. When the clock hits zero with a required castle open, play its degradation tick and its exposure at that instant rather than at the next cleanup, so 'one block short' visibly costs something this sortie.
(b) Raise early decision density rather than time pressure. Author Cam 1–6 boards so the two tax gaps fall on different rings; with a 2-slot rack and 1 trash, every Build then forces a triage choice.
(c) Rule on the bank. Either stop Build time from scoring (Build → Deploy only; unspent Deploy time never banks), or openly make 'finish early' the optional mastery layer and stop describing speed as out of bounds.

## core-loop-fun-5 [major | partially] Cam 1–6 (12 battles, about the first hour) lacks every tension the game claims, then Cam 7 turns several dials at once
VERIFIED CLAIM: The first ~12 battles (about an hour, plus a ~15-min Induction) have no coverage allocation, no core-hunting and naive cognition. They are played on the full 128×80 board, which exists to support three approaches the on-ramp never fields, so new players pay the pan and cell-size cost with none of the benefit. Triage and the motion read are present, so the on-ramp is thin rather than empty. Cam 7 clusters several new things: 3/6 castles, piece group D, the first pairs campaign, the decapitation debut and a second approach. That is a pacing risk, but it does not clearly breach the contacts-per-approach law as written.
ALREADY MITIGATED: Partly.
- Campaign §4:119 designs the on-ramp to add one order-card field per battle, and argues 'simple to read, not thin' (6–8 hulls, a real motion-signature read from battle 1).
- Deploy §2:141 acknowledges that the core tension 'structurally does not exist yet'.
- Castle triage (1/4, then 2/4) exists from battle 1.
- MiniGames §15's ~15-min Induction precedes Cam 1.
- Campaign §4:134-135 stages the decapitation debut as an isolated single-core battle 'at the front of Cam 7–22' with a 'loud' tell.
FIX DIRECTION (critic): Make the on-ramp a small but complete game, not a sparse version of the big one.
- Clip the playable area of each base map for Cam 1–6 to the one live approach plus a 4-castle fort. That roughly doubles cell size for touch and cuts panning, with no new base maps (castles are already hidden the same way).
- Stage one isolated 'hunt this core' battle and one 2-approach battle inside the first 12 battles.
- Teach fire-control first.
- Spread the Cam 7 package over Cam 5–9 so no boundary turns more than one of approaches, contacts or win-route.
Into the Breach and Slay the Spire both put their complete core decision in the very first fight; aim for that.

## core-loop-fun-6 [major | partially] 'Conduct a known score' turns the active Fight into memorised real-time performance, with no way to schedule it
VERIFIED CLAIM: The Fight's only specified 'active' mode is performing a plan held in the player's head, in real time. There is no scheduling or conditional-order mechanism, no pause, and zooming in to command blinds the other bearings. Its signature skill is defined by a half-second release window, which directly conflicts with Why §2's ban on 'execution precision under time pressure'. The 'known score' is also less known than claimed, because the fleet reacts to deviations. So the payoff is weaker than the Fight doc says, and it requires replaying whole battles.
ALREADY MITIGATED: Partly.
- Fight §6's spacing law (boards must space threats so that a planned crawl plus reload fits).
- Fight §5 schedules crawls 'into a gap where that blindness is affordable'.
- Deploy §7 adds a twin of the ≥2-viable-deployments law, precisely so 'the second attempt' is not 'the first attempt typed faster'.
None of these supplies a scheduling mechanism.
FIX DIRECTION (critic): Move the 'score' into Deploy as data. Let each existing order-card field hold one conditional change, for example 'when a contact enters sector X' or 'after the first core dies': re-task to Y, or switch to Air. The simulation executes it at the same costs as a live verb.
This keeps the verb and field budget (nothing new is added), makes the plan something you can inspect and debug, removes the dexterity layer, and turns the Fight into watching your own program run: the Frozen Synapse / Gratuitous Space Battles pleasure. Keep the live verbs as the correction path for surprises.

## core-loop-fun-7 [major | partially] The battle deflates: pressure peaks at sortie 1–2, then the back half is mop-up and an unopposed Last Stand
VERIFIED CLAIM: Every quantified within-battle mechanism lowers pressure for a winning player: a pool drained by design, cores exhausting, the tax dropping on core kills, slots and veterans growing, a shrinking Deploy. The only rising curve, fleet learning plus the cognition tick, is asserted but unquantified. The battle is therefore likely to peak early and end on an unopposed Last Stand of up to about 170–220 s, against Fight §8's 'ramps across the whole battle' and the 'always just barely overwhelming' target. Fight §3 and Victory §5 disagree on the Last Stand's length.
ALREADY MITIGATED: The stated counter-curve is Fleet Memory :68 ('the intra-battle ramp… memory accumulating sortie over sortie'), plus the per-sortie cognition tick on survivors (Victory §4:578, §3:81). Build §5 calls the rebuild tax 'repeated, permanent, and the entire source of ongoing pressure'. Victory §4b's composition law keeps a convoy in every sortie 'while one is willing'. All of this is unquantified.
FIX DIRECTION (critic): Back-load the fleet.
- Add a reserve law: the flagship's command convoy and the heaviest payload sail in the final one or two sorties as 'the last push'.
- Tie intensity to pool depletion, moving toward Fanatical as the pool drains, so a draining fleet gets desperate before it breaks. Victory already notes it becomes 'more desperate and more brittle'; make desperate come first.
- Make the Last Stand a short, fixed denouement (for example 30 s or less) or have the remnants contest it, and settle the 70 s versus 170–220 s contradiction.
- Quantify the memory ratchet so the curve can actually be checked.

Otherwise, keep the Fight in Map View and spend the saved effort on Build touch.

# LENS: enemy-mind-legibility

CRITIC VERDICT: As written, the enemy mind is coherent and well argued: three channels, walls that hide muzzle flashes, a coarse fear map, and a plan made between sorties. But the player is shown almost none of it where decisions get made. Fear appears only as a soft haze during the real-time Fight. The Build/Deploy "bait window" shows nothing of what the fleet believes. Resolve is hidden by rule. The update rules form a network that is deterministic but chaotic, and a touch-screen player cannot replay it tick for tick. On a 6-inch screen, what a first-time player will reliably learn is "they avoid where it hurts" and "an exposed gun gets hunted." That is a good AI. It is not a mind that is the difficulty. The difficulty curve also pushes rational players away from the signaling game. Each smarter lineage is answered by "just enclose / don't re-task." Fanatical fleets and every boss ignore the fear grid. A turtle wins at ★★, and ★★★ comes from core-hunting rather than from manipulating beliefs. The bones are real, but the central claim will not survive as specified. The fix is not more tells. (1) Move the mind game into the planning phase and show the fleet's beliefs and intent there. The architecture already allows this without cheating, because the plan is fixed at sortie end. (2) Specify that plan. (3) Cut the player-facing rules to about seven. (4) Run a cheap greybox legibility test before writing any more roster.

## enemy-mind-legibility-1 [major | partially] The bait window shows nothing the fleet believes
VERIFIED CLAIM: In Build/Deploy, where baits are set, the authoritative docs specify no render of the fleet's fear map, of formation end-states, or of fleet resolve. Deploy :255 implies the twilight/noon exposure layer carries into Map View, but that is unspecified. The player must remember the Fight's soft haze to plan a hold-feint, relocate or hidden breach, which strains FM :46's predictability requirement. FM :280's reason for withholding is a deliberate drama choice that conflicts with :46; it is not a sender/receiver confusion. Because the next-sortie plan is a pure function of memory fixed at sortie end (FM :368), a Deploy intel layer (fear tiers at sortie end, believed ghosts and stale negatives, routed vs withdrew) would not break blindness. The GDD debrief schema (:3179) carries no belief data.
ALREADY MITIGATED: Partly. Deploy :255 says that in Deploy 'your own fort sits in mind-fog twilight', and Fleet_Memory §8 (:289) describes the saturation register as 'over your own fort' without limiting it to a phase. So an exposure/Contact render in Map View is implied but never specified. It is not clearly absent. The fear haze exists only live (Fight :115). Deploy :273, the coverage-holes toggle, already shows the design accepts planning-phase reading aids 'because nothing is moving'. That precedent supports the fix.
FIX DIRECTION (critic): Add a Fleet Intelligence layer to Deploy's Map View, shown in the 'state read' beat: (a) the fleet-wide fear map at sortie end in the four named tiers; (b) your fort in twilight/noon as the fleet has it, with ghosts (believed but gone) and stale negatives (removed but still believed) marked; (c) each formation's end-state (routed vs withdrew) and a quantised fleet-will state; (d) optionally 'intercepted orders': each formation's intended lane and objective. (d) is honest because the plan is a pure function of memory fixed at sortie end, so revealing it tells the fleet nothing. This is the Into the Breach telegraph moved to the planning table. If intel should stay contested, make (d) earned (sink the Recon, hold a Spotter). Keep the Fight's live read soft. Extend the debrief schema with the same belief data so the post-battle coach can say what they believed and why.

## enemy-mind-legibility-2 [major | partially] Reproducible is not predictable: the update rules are chaotic to a human
VERIFIED CLAIM: Determinism is necessary but not sufficient for baiting. The resolve network is a threshold/contagion system (continuous mid-Fight rout, cascades, anchor succession, plus seeded wavers per Why :30) that is likely too sensitive to inputs for a human to predict, so Repel/Held outcomes and rout timing may read as noise even though they are reproducible. The fear and contact channels, which most baits use, are coarse and discrete by design and so are more stable. Before trusting the resolve layer, test it: perturb one input by a tick in a headless sim and measure divergence. Add hysteresis or quantisation where small changes flip outcomes.
ALREADY MITIGATED: Partly, by construction. The channels the four baits run on are coarse and discrete: a ~10×10 tiered fear grid (FM :157-183) and a contact ledger revised only by contradiction (FM :192). FM :108 argues explicitly that the player learns a rule, not a trajectory. Spawn order and timing are seeded and ordered by the queue (Why :30; Victory :578). The SDD (older, upkeep only) already specifies a headless fixed-step pure-Dart core (SDD :104, :498), which makes the proposed perturbation test cheap.
FIX DIRECTION (critic): Design the rules for stability, then test for it. (1) Let belief change routing only at sortie boundaries, i.e. in the plan. Within a Fight the fleet executes, jinks and breaks, but does not re-plan. (2) Quantise fear writes to tier steps and make decay exactly one tier per silent sortie. (3) Add hysteresis to rout thresholds and a minimum cascade size, so a one-tick timing difference cannot flip a Repel into a Held. (4) Give each formation its own RNG stream. (5) Snap fire-control releases to a coarse beat. Then run a headless-sim sensitivity check: perturb one input by one tick and measure how far fleet paths and outcomes diverge. Accept the mind only if small perturbations produce small, explainable changes.

## enemy-mind-legibility-3 [critical | confirmed] Legibility of the mind was declared resolved by ruling, not tested
VERIFIED CLAIM: The key perceptual claims (the motion signature separating fear from nerve, the four shape reads, loose-for-uncertain vs loose-for-naive) are recorded as settled design facts, and the locked method lets a prototype adjust only magnitudes, never the kind of tell. These are facts about human perception at about 27 pt on a phone. They can be tested cheaply and should be tested before more derivation is built on them. Amend the rule so the prototype also verifies perception, with a pass bar fixed in advance.
ALREADY MITIGATED: Only for magnitudes. Fight :224 and :239 say the felt targets 'await prototype confirmation'. Ships :1275 flags shape-reading as the claim 'most likely to come back from prototype needing help'. Ships :1320 says 'Prototype confirms the magnitude; the kind is settled.' Under the locked rule the prototype may confirm but cannot change the kind of tell, and a perception failure would require exactly that kind of change.
FIX DIRECTION (critic): Build a throwaway 2D greybox (no art, no Build phase, even a web canvas): one approach, two or three formations, one or two AoRs, hold-fire, the fear haze and the motion signature, fully deterministic. Put it on a phone in front of 5–8 people who have not read the docs. After each sortie ask three questions: where will formation X go next sortie; is formation Y skirting or wavering; which of your guns do they know about? Fix a pass bar in advance (e.g. ≥70% correct by the third battle). If it fails, change the tell or the rule, not the tester. # LENS: philosophy-coherence

CRITIC VERDICT: The Why is the strongest document in the set. Taken one at a time, its laws are sharp and some can be tested. But they were added one ruling at a time and never checked against each other, and in current design they collide in five places. First, the felt target ("always just barely overwhelming", "slack is the bug") works against No Forced Playstyle, Wide Solution Space and the Why's own flow section. It also names no reference player or attempt, which is the central question in a deterministic retry game. Victory even argues the 8-contact ceiling is safe because "the wave has been met before". Second, the Edge-of-Tomorrow replay loop ("conduct a known score", "time the held alpha you fumbled") rewards the memorised execution that the One Test and tension #2 name as failure. Third, determinism-as-fairness and the Battle Score "fairness engine" are undercut by several systems: carried veterancy that can feed up to half the score, a time bank that pays for placement speed, adaptive thresholds, and a GDD meta layer (Assist, streak buffs, Stonwryt boosts, ad time, volume-based boards) that the Why never governs. Fourth, "difficulty is cognition, not stats" holds only in the narrow HP/DPS sense. What the player will feel ramping is quantity, while the headline cognition creep has no numbers and is designed to be nearly invisible. Fifth, the corrected win condition is better design but is not yet a rule a player can read. The fleet's clock is hidden, the battle ends "when the fleet stops committing", and the 10-sortie cap has no stated outcome. None of this needs a new mechanic. It needs three decisions: rank the laws, name the reference player and attempt, and add an economy law. These are cheap now and very expensive after 190 boards are authored against the current wording.

Difficulty levels (GDD-owned) were never re-derived against the contact and hull bounds: the GDD's ±ships modifiers and Victory §3's 'slot grant' description disagree, and the level count in Victory is stale.
ALREADY MITIGATED: Partly. Victory §4 (line 578) grounds 'you have seen this wave' inside one attempt: surviving formations return next sortie 'in the same order they came before'.
