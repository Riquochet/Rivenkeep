# RIVENKEEP NAME MAP: ORIGINALITY AUDIT

> **VETOED 2026-09-26 (Jack):** row 7, *the Mistlands → the Smother*, is withdrawn. "For now keep Mystlands. That was my own invention and not copied." The Book uses **the Mystlands** (spelled *Myst-*, as Jack wrote it). Everything else in this map stands.

*Jack's rule N19.9: original names only, nothing that can be exactly traced to other works. Tolkien's influence is unavoidable; lifted words or names are not allowed. 2026-09-26.*

**Scope.** This audit checks every coined name and word-root in `legends_final.md` (the §9 names table, §10, and the tales), in `fiction_hooks.md` (the Tide table, the Throne table and the §9 lexicon), and in `admiral_final.md` (the Tides, the Thrones, the grains, the Heartwood, and the carving lexicon). It also checks the GDD's own names. It does not cover the English piece names in the Ships doc (Reaver, Wraith, Bulwark and the rest). Those are ordinary words, not coinages (see §d.5).

**Method.** Each root was checked against Tolkien's languages (Quenya, Sindarin, Khuzdul, Adûnaic) using Tolkien Gateway, Parf Edhellen and Eldamo. Each whole name was web-searched as an exact string, and the hits were checked against the major franchises: Warcraft, Elder Scrolls, Dragon Age, Wheel of Time, A Song of Ice and Fire, D&D, Magic: The Gathering, Final Fantasy, Guild Wars, RuneScape, Heroes of Might and Magic, and Valheim.

**Status key.**
- **APPLY:** change it in this pass.
- **APPLY (confirm):** change it, but it follows from one of Jack's rulings, not from an originality problem, so he should nod.
- **JACK'S CALL:** flagged with a proposal, but not applied (a haven under N15, or a GDD-owned fear-name).
- **KEEP:** checked and kept, with a note.

**The short answer.** Two roots and five names are traceable. Replacing them, plus Jack's NAELEAR respelling, touches **eleven words** in total. Everything else is kept.

**The two new roots (*ress*, *lenth*) and the extended one (*lea*).** Each was checked the same way. None is a Tolkien word, and none turned up as a published name. The nearest things found are *saen* beside Sindarin *sain* ("new"), where the meaning differs, and *lenth*, which is Scots dialect for "length". That second one is a quiet fit for "the long winter". Every whole new name returned **no hits** on an exact search: Saenvael, Naelsaen, Thaesaen, Ralensaen, Vaelress, Neivaere, Leavaren, Eirlenth, Naelear, Rhenear.

**Why these shapes.** The Throne list has to read as ten distinct words at a glance. The old list had three near-twins: *Selvaren*, *Silvaere* and *Senneir*. The new list has none: **Thaesaen · Leavaren · Vaelress · Ralensaen · Rhenvael · Esthaer · Senneir · Eirlenth · Naelthar · Neivaere**. Four alternatives were rejected because they made new look-alikes:
- *Leinvaere* for silver sat beside *Leavaren*.
- *Vaelthess* for rot sat beside *Vaelthae*.
- *Thessvael* sat beside *Thaesaen*.
- *Eirvaen* put a *vaen* beside every *vael*.

**Rhenaer → Rhenear is APPLY (confirm).** This is not an originality problem. *Naelaer* and *Rhenaer* share the one suffix *-aer*, "those of". Jack's respelling of the first word respells that suffix, so leaving the second as it is would give the wood-tongue two spellings of one ending. N19.3 keeps the passage itself. The respelled word also reads as "stone-ear", which suits "the stone-deaf". If Jack meant only the one word, keep *Rhenaer*, and the lexicon then treats *-aer* and *-ear* as two different endings.

### b.5 · How to apply without breaking anything

- **Replace whole words only.** Never run a blind substring replace:
  - *orn* sits inside *born*, *corn*, *morning* and **Blackthorn**.
  - *sil* sits inside *silver*, **Silverbark** and *silent*.
  - *sel* sits inside *self* and *vessel*.
  - *aer* sits inside **Mystaeri** and **Esthaer**.
- **The exact pairs to replace,** carved and spoken:
  - `Ornvael`/`Orn'vael` → `Saenvael`/`Saen'vael`
  - `Naelorn`/`Nae'lorn` → `Naelsaen`/`Nael'saen`
  - `Thaelorn`/`Thae'lorn` → `Thaesaen`/`Thae'saen`
  - `Ralenorn`/`Ra'lenorn` → `Ralensaen`/`Ra'lensaen`
  - `Vaelsorn`/`Vael'sorn` → `Vaelress`/`Vael'ress`
  - `Silvaere`/`Sil'vaere` → `Neivaere`/`Nei'vaere`
  - `Selvaren`/`Sel'varen` → `Leavaren`/`Lea'varen`
  - `Eiravel`/`Ei'ravel` → `Eirlenth`/`Eir'lenth`
  - `Naelaer` → `Naelear`
  - `Rhenaer` → `Rhenear`
  - `the Mistlands` → `the Smother`
- **Possessives follow automatically** (`Thaelorn's` → `Thaesaen's`, and so on).
- **The lexicon cells** `*orn*` and `*sil*` become `*saen*` and `*nei*`.
- **Order.** Change fiction_hooks first, then the Admiral docs, then the Book, as §10.9 asked. Afterwards, grep for every old string. The expected count is zero outside the archive.

## (d) Jack's calls: flagged, not applied

1. **Fear-names.** These are the GDD's boss names; see `theaters.md` §11, which raises the same point.
   - **the Ancient Treant → *the Root-Giant*** (recommended). *Treant* is D&D's 1970s coinage, its stand-in for Tolkien's Ents, and "Ancient Treant" is word for word the Sylvan unit in *Heroes of Might and Magic V*. It is the one lifted coinage among the fear-names.
   - **the Frost Wyrm → *the Rime-Serpent*** (optional). "Frost Wyrm" is an exact Warcraft III / WoW unit name, but both words are plain English.
   - **Storm King** (the Storm Kings of *A Song of Ice and Fire*; D&D's *Storm King's Thunder*; the villain of the 2017 *My Little Pony* film) and **Sandworm** (*Dune*, though it is also a real English word for a marine worm) are soft flags. Keep them.
   - **Leviathan, Hydra, Colossus, Titan, Lich, Herald:** myth, scripture and English. Keep them.
   - **Crystal Lich, Plague Herald, Magma Titan:** no exact published use found.
   - Neither replacement name turned up anywhere. If Jack takes either, the swap runs across the GDD, Ships §4, Campaign, Victory, the Map View Briefs, the AI image prompts, the Legends (III.2), the Admiral and fiction_hooks.
2. **Havens** (N15: analysis only, so no change now).
   - **Highreach** is the exact title of VestGames' 2026 Early Access survival RPG, set on a continent of **floating sky islands**. That is the GDD's own Sky-theater premise. Proposal: ***Highholm*** (*holm*, islet, from the naming kit). It fits `theaters.md`'s sea-stack coast, where every stack is a high islet.
   - **Sandreach** is the desert kingdom of "the Sandreach" in *Dragalia Lost* (Cygames/Nintendo). Same name, same desert. Proposal: ***Sandward***.
   - Neither proposal turned up anywhere. The *-reach* suffix is crowded in any case (WoW's Skyreach, Eberron's Stormreach, ASOIAF's the Reach).
3. **Treelings** (N16). If Jack wants it as the soldiers' word for the land troops, it is a plain English *-ling* diminutive. It is also a Tibia creature ("Haunted Treeling") and the title of a small 2020 board game. That is a soft flag and fine as slang. *Root-men* stays the Book's word.
4. **Kael and Ael'thar together.** Each name is fine alone, and neither is a lift. Side by side, a Warcraft player may hear *Kael'thas*. Nothing changes unless Jack wants the distance. If he does, the cheaper side to move is the soldier's name, not the rite.
5. **Ship-piece names** (Reaver, Wraith, Corsair, Bulwark, Breacher, Linebreaker, Skyfall, Sower, Chirurgeon, Aegis, Mimic and the rest). These are English words, not coinages, so they are outside this map. The most franchise-coloured is *Reaver* (*Firefly*; the Reaver Titan of Warhammer 40,000). It is noted only.
