Balance Risks — Only Playtesting Can Settle These

These are the two balance questions that have survived every analysis pass because they cannot be resolved on paper. They require a working prototype and real combat data.

BAL-1 Lightning Cannon Dominance (was Q-10, BAL-5)
Lightning Cannon outputs 9.0 eDPS vs Standard's 5.3 eDPS — 70% higher. Even with the per-type deployment limit, a mixed loadout of Lightning + Standard significantly outperforms pure Standard. The GDD says "encounter design should counter this," but no specific encounter that punishes Lightning is designed. Lightning's 10-grid range (vs Standard's 12) means it sits closer to the action, not further — so range-based counters don't naturally apply. Resolution path: During prototype, log per-cannon-type win contribution across 100 battles. If Lightning appears in >60% of winning loadouts, either raise its unlock cost, lower its eDPS to ~7.0, or design Crystal/Fog encounters where its short range is a genuine liability. ✎ v6.2.0: now surfaced in the GDD → Unit & Structure Statistics → “Open Balance & Flow Questions.” Still an open playtest item.
BAL-2 Chain Shot eDPS Variance (was BAL-2)
Chain Shot's effective damage ranges from ~4.7 eDPS single-target to ~9.2 eDPS against 3+ clustered ships — roughly a 2× swing — depending entirely on enemy clustering (derived from the GDD stats: 22 dmg × 85% accuracy ÷ 4-tick reload single-target, rising with the 60% per-chain decay across up to 3 targets). Even at ~2× it is either a mediocre or a best-in-class cannon with little middle ground. A player who learns to bait ships into clusters gets a broken weapon; everyone else gets a dud. Resolution path: Either (a) add a minimum-targets floor so Chain Shot always hits at least 2, compressing the low end upward, or (b) accept the variance as a skill expression and ensure the tutorial/onboarding teaches clustering. Decide during prototype — do not ship without choosing. ✎ v6.2.0: surfaced in the GDD balance section. v6.2.1: eDPS reconciled to the GDD’s verified 4.7–9.2 — the earlier 4.3–12.8 estimate is superseded.

Flow Concerns — Early-Game Pacing

Two pacing issues flagged in the original Flow State analysis remain worth watching. Both are about the early game (Campaigns 1–5) where the player has few cannons and few enemies. Neither is fatal, but both could cause early churn if not tuned.

FLOW-1 Deploy Phase Is Thin in Early Campaigns
In Campaign 1, the player places 2 cannons in a 12-second Deploy phase. That is barely an interaction — drag, drag, done, wait. The Deploy phase doesn't become a genuine strategic decision until the player has 4+ cannons and multiple types to choose from (Campaign 8+). Mitigation already in GDD: cannon persistence and the move bonus give early Deploy some texture. Watch during playtest: if early Deploy feels like dead air, consider front-loading a second cannon type unlock or adding a brief "reposition for bonus" prompt in Campaign 1–3. ✎ v6.2.0: noted in the GDD → Unit & Structure Statistics → “Open Balance & Flow Questions.”
FLOW-2 Fight Phase Passivity in Early Campaigns
With 2 cannons on auto-fire against 2–3 slow ships, the early Fight phase can feel like watching rather than playing. The player's directed-fire skill isn't tested because auto-fire handles the trivial threat. Mitigation already in GDD: the 2.5D transition and explosions provide spectacle; the Build puzzle is the real early hook. Watch during playtest: if early Fights feel passive, introduce one Sapper (a priority kill that demands directed fire) one campaign earlier, or tighten the early Fight timer so auto-fire alone isn't quite enough. ✎ v6.2.0: noted in the GDD → Unit & Structure Statistics → “Open Balance & Flow Questions.”

Policy & Compliance Gaps — Close Before App Store Submission

These three items are not gameplay issues — they are App Store requirements and operational policies that have never been specified. They will block submission or create legal exposure if ignored.

POL-1 Privacy & Data Policy (was P3-6)
The game collects: Game Center ID, region, purchase history, custom piece names (user-generated content), Featured Fortress screenshots, and analytics. Both stores require a privacy disclosure at submission — Apple's App Privacy "nutrition label" and Google Play's Data Safety form. The GDD specifies what data flows where (Firestore, Firebase, plus the AI cloud-fallback tier) but not the user-facing privacy policy, consent flow, or data-deletion mechanism (GDPR/CCPA right-to-delete). Action: Write a privacy policy (including the AI cloud-fallback disclosure). Implement account-data export/delete from Firestore. Complete Apple's App Privacy questionnaire AND Google Play's Data Safety form. Required before first external test build on either platform. ✎ v6.2.0: skeleton folded into the GDD → Production → “Privacy & Data Policy.”
POL-2 Push Notification Strategy (was P3-7)
The game has natural notification hooks (daily challenge reset, streak-about-to-break, Weekend Race ending soon, challenge package ready to open, piece name approved/rejected). But none are specified, and notification permission timing/copy is undefined. Over-notifying causes uninstalls; under-notifying loses the retention value. Action: Define the notification taxonomy (which events, what copy, what frequency cap), the permission-request timing (after first ★★★, not on launch), and a settings toggle per category. Maps to the existing Settings Menu section. ✎ v6.2.0: folded into the GDD → Settings → “Notification Strategy” + a Notifications settings row.
POL-3 Monetization & Gameplay Analytics (was P3-10)
The GDD mentions server-side tuning (star thresholds, economy) via Firebase Remote Config, but no analytics event schema is defined. To tune the economy, balance cannons (see BAL-1/2), and verify session-length targets, you need instrumented events: battle_start, battle_end (with stars/Battle Score/cannon types used), purchase, ad_watch, stonwryt_earn, stonwryt_spend, challenge_complete, churn_point. Action: Define ~20 analytics events with parameters. Firebase Analytics (free) covers this. Without it, you're tuning blind — and BAL-1/BAL-2 can never be resolved. ✎ v6.2.0: event schema folded into the GDD → Backend Architecture → “Analytics event schema.”

Future Consideration — Not Required, Worth Noting

FUT-1 Spectator / "Watch a Pro" Discovery (was V-8)
Featured Fortresses show top players' builds, but there's no way to watch a top player's battle. The replay system already produces shareable clips — a curated "Replay of the Day" or a browsable gallery of top-scoring battle replays (not just fortress screenshots) would be a strong discovery and aspiration mechanic. Deterministic battles make this especially powerful: a player can watch a ★★★! run on a battle they're stuck on, then attempt the exact same battle. Recommendation: Consider for a post-launch update. Low priority — the replay infrastructure already exists, this is just surfacing it. ✎ v6.2.0: now documented in the GDD → Future Release Framework → “Spectator / Watch a Pro.”
FUT-2 Endless Mode (was GAP-4)
After 190 battles + Boss Finale + War II, the deterministic content is finite. Endless Mode (infinite escalating waves on a rotating map, unlocked at 570 stars) is listed in the GDD as "under evaluation." It would provide infinite replayability with near-zero content cost. Recommendation: Keep under evaluation. The War system and daily/challenge/race loops already provide strong retention; Endless is a nice-to-have, not a launch requirement.

Infrastructure — The Real Work Ahead (Permanent Checklist)

These ten items are not bugs or design gaps — they are the content-production and engineering tasks that turn a complete GDD into a shipped game. They remain as a permanent checklist for the development phase. This is, honestly, where the multi-year effort actually lives.

INFRA-1 Server Architecture
Largely specified in the v6.3.0 Backend Architecture section (cross-platform games_services + in_app_purchase + Firebase). Remaining work: building the 8 Cloud Functions, defining Firestore security rules, mirroring leaderboard/achievement IDs across Game Center + Play Games, and load-testing Weekend Race matchmaking at scale.
INFRA-2 Firestore Sync Data Schema
The 7 player-state record types are named, but the Firestore field-level schema, conflict-resolution strategy, and migration plan across app updates need full specification before save/restore is built. Firestore is the single cross-device store on both platforms.
INFRA-3 Cannon Carry vs Determinism (marketing nuance)
Cannon HP carry means "every battle is identical for every player" is only true for the first battle of each campaign. The marketing claim needs the honest framing: "same starting conditions = same outcome." Not a code issue — a messaging precision issue for the App Store listing. ✎ v6.2.0: caveat folded into the GDD → Design Philosophy (Deterministic Puzzles).
INFRA-4 380 Battlefield Overlays (~95 hrs)
190 environmental overlays + 190 enemy overlays, hand-designed. The single largest content bottleneck. Strongly consider a constrained procedural generator with human curation rather than hand-placing every rock and ship.
INFRA-5 800+ Sprite Production Pipeline
198 pieces × 4 rotations + cannons + ships + terrain × 10 theaters, in both Map View (flat) and World View (2.5D). AI-generation + curation workflow needs documenting: prompts, resolution, cleanup, sprite-sheet format.
INFRA-6 950+ Flavor Messages
190 battlefields × 5–8 messages. AI-generated with human curation, organized by theater and campaign stage, with a tonal style guide.
INFRA-7 10 Boss Encounter Designs
Framework specified (all 10 Mystarchs have theater, attack, HP tier, special mechanic). Remaining: exact HP-per-sortie progression, attack timing in ticks, phase-transition triggers, and vulnerability windows for each.
INFRA-8 Sound Design Asset List
The Audio section has mood direction per phase/theater but no concrete asset list (how many cannon-fire variants, explosion types, UI clicks, ambient loops). Needed to brief a contractor or AI audio tool.
INFRA-9 32 Campaign Names + 320 Soldier Quotes
The 20-soldier roster needs personality definitions first, then 32 campaign names and 10 quotes each. Writing task with a consistency style guide.
INFRA-10 Lore Progressive Revelation Map
Rich lore exists (Ael'thar ritual, Mystaeri, Elderess Rhyna, Stonewrights, Aetherbond, Rivenmen). Needs a timeline mapping which beats unlock at which campaign milestone and how they surface in-game (loading screens, victory messages, campaign intros).
